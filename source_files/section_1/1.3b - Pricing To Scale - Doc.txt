Pricing For Scale
Price:Value Rule Of Thumb
Your price should be 1/10th of the value/return your clients receive.


If you charge $1k/m, clients should be generating $10k/m in value.


If you charge $5k/m, clients should be generating $50k/m in value.


If you charge $10k, clients should be generating $100k in value.


I promise you, if the minimum return your clients make is 10x, you will never have a retention problem.


I’ve found that getting beyond 10x is more a function of fulfilment vs pricing.


Price at 10x and hyper iterate/improve on your fulfillment to get your price:value ratio to 20x, 30x, 40x and so on (more on this in later trainings).
New Offers:
If you have a relatively new offer which you don’t have much proof with, here’s how I recommend pricing (to start):


If any type of agency:
* $2.5k - $3k/month w/ 3-4 month minimum


Coaching/consulting:
* If biz opp:
   * $4800 for 12-16 weeks 
* If health/weight loss/relationships:
   * $2800 - $3800 for 12-16 weeks


Essentially, you want to price low enough initially to where price isn’t an objection. In the early days of any new offer you always want to optimize for flow (new clients) over profit.


Once you sign your first few clients you’re then able to use their results to determine your true pricing (1/10th of the value you provide).
Pre-existing Offers:
Pretty simple. Look at your past client results who all did exactly what you require them to do and got results (do not base your pricing off clients who don’t take any action/didn’t get results).


Take 5-10 of them and figure out the average monthly return… whatever that number is, divide it by 10 (again, this is where I would start).


If any type of agency:
* $3k/m+ for 4 months min


Coaching/consulting:
* If biz opp:
   * $6800+ for 12-16 weeks 
* If health/weight loss/relationships:
   * $4800+ for 12-16 weeks 
Increasing Your Prices
The above are where I would recommend you start your pricing with the goal of eventually increasing.


You want to increase your prices when:
* Your close rate is above 30%
* Your cash collected is above 50%


If your close rate + cash collected are above those 2 KPIs then it’s clear your prices are too low.


If you’re in this position, I recommend increasing your price by $2k (on the 3-4 month package).


So if you charge $20k for 4 months, bump to $22k.


Find your new closing + cash collected baseline at $22k.


Work it until you’re above 30% close rate + 50% cash collected and then increase to $24k and repeat.


(Just remember as you’re increasing your prices, you still need to maintain 1/10th price to value. Meaning, as you increase price, you may also need to increase the value of your product.)
Accelerate Cash Flow
You would have noticed above I say “X months minimum”, that’s because when pitching your pricing (upfront), never pitch a monthly amount (“it’s $3k/m”), rather you want clients to pay for 3-4 months (most common) in full and then they retain on a monthly basis.


So if you charge $5k/m, you would pitch prospects “$20k for 4 months.”


You will be shocked at how many clients PIF. And what this does is scale your business without you needing to sign any more clients.


If you close 10 clients at $3k/m each, that’s $30k cash collected.


If you close those same 10 clients and 50% PIF for 4 months and the other do month to month, that’s $75k cash collected.


$30k vs $75k from the same 10 clients closed. As you can imagine, this acceleration of cash flow is HUGE when it comes to scaling and adspend.


If you’re wondering what you would do in the instance where a prospect can’t PIF… you simply downsell them the payment plans (which is what you would have pitched them on anyway).
To Roll It Out…
Get your highest performing closer (or you) to use the new pricing on their sales calls whilst you keep the rest of the team at the normal price point.


Once the highest performing closer hits KPI, roll it out to the remainder of the team and get the higher performer to give some coaching points on what he’s found different with the price increase (usually there’s not too much difference).


This is the easiest way to roll it out whilst not having any major hit to revenue (if it doesn’t work out).


It also gives you the ability to have a ‘case study’ for the rest of the team if they can’t close on the higher price point.
