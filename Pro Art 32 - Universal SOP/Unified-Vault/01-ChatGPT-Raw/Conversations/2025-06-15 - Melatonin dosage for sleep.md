### 🧪 Why You *Felt* 9mg Worked Better Than 6mg

- **High doses of melatonin (6–10mg)** can indeed *feel* stronger — they often knock people out, especially when you're sleep-deprived or misaligned with your circadian rhythm.
- But the **effectiveness plateaus** after about **3mg**, and anything higher mostly increases the **sedative fog**, not necessarily *better quality* or *faster onset* sleep.

So yes, **9mg can "work" better** in a brute-force way, but there are trade-offs:

---

### ⚠️ Why You *Shouldn't* Keep Pushing Higher

1. **Receptor Downregulation**  
   Repeated high doses (6–10mg) can cause your **melatonin receptors to become less sensitive**, especially if used more than a few nights in a row.

2. **Next-Day Grogginess**  
   Large doses can spike melatonin levels for **6–10 hours**, far past when your body naturally would taper off. That leaves you feeling **dull or foggy** in the morning — especially if you wake up earlier than your body expects.

3. **Worse Sleep Architecture**  
   Too much melatonin can suppress **REM sleep**, especially if your natural circadian rhythm is already misaligned. That might knock you out, but the **quality** of sleep could be worse.

---

### ✅ What Actually Works Best

The most effective, evidence-based approach — especially for **resetting your sleep** (like tonight) — is:

| Goal                       | Dosage                     |
|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| Fall asleep faster        | **0.3–1mg**, 90 min before |
| Shift sleep cycle earlier | **0.5–3mg**, 2–3 hrs before|
| Occasional knockout dose  | **3–5mg** max              |

So for you — just woke up at 1:30pm and trying to sleep by 9pm — you're shifting your rhythm by **~4–5 hours**.  
A **3–5mg dose at 7pm** hits the sweet spot to:
- Induce sleepiness by 8:30–9pm
- Avoid grogginess tomorrow
- Preserve sleep quality
- Prevent tolerance

---

### 🔁 Bottom Line

Yes, 9mg may feel stronger, but it’s a **short-term hack** that can become a **long-term liability**.  
Think of melatonin like a *whisper* to your circadian rhythm, not a *sledgehammer*. Too much and your body stops listening.
