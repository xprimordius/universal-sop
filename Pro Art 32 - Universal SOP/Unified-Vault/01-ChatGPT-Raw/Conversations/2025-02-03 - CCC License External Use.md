### **Key Aspects of a Country Schedule:**

This clause from the **Copyright Clearance Center (CCC)** licensing agreement clarifies how copyright restrictions apply to individual articles within a journal or periodical. Here’s what it means in practical terms:

### **Key Breakdown:**
1. **Each Issue is a Separate Work:**  
   - A full journal issue or periodical is treated as an independent copyrighted work.  
   - Each article within that issue is considered a separate piece of content.

2. **Restrictions on Reproducing "Substantially All" of a Work:**  
   - Normally, copyright laws prevent reproducing or distributing "substantially all" of a copyrighted work without proper licensing.  
   - CCC extends this restriction to **individual articles within a larger work** (e.g., a journal issue containing many articles).

3. **Implication:**  
   - Even though an article is part of a journal issue, it **cannot** be freely copied or distributed just because the issue itself is licensed.  
   - The rule prevents users from circumventing copyright protections by claiming an article is just a small part of the journal issue.  
   - If you want to reproduce an entire article, you must obtain proper permissions **separately** for that article.

### **Practical Example:**
- If you have permission to use an entire **journal issue**, that does **not** mean you can reproduce and distribute an **individual article** from that issue without additional licensing.
- Similarly, if you license an **individual article**, that does **not** give you rights to the entire journal issue.

This ensures that copyright protection applies both at the **issue level** and the **article level**, preventing unauthorized mass reproduction of content.

### **Summary of the Grant of License (CCC Agreement Section I)**  

This section outlines the **non-exclusive** rights granted by the **Copyright Clearance Center (CCC)** to the **User** (your organization) regarding both **hard copies** and **electronic reproductions** of copyrighted works.  

### **Key Points:**
1. **Hard Copy Rights (Physical Reproduction):**  
   - You are allowed to **make photocopies** of works within the U.S. for **internal use** only.

2. **Electronic Rights (Digital Usage):**  
   - You can **store, distribute, and print** digital copies of authorized works **within your organization**.  
   - Employees and contractors can **access, read, and annotate** these digital copies via the company’s internal network (intranet).  
   - If a **digital version** of the work is unavailable, you may **digitize** a paper copy and treat it as an authorized reproduction.  
   - Digital copies may be **stored as part of work product** and retained for compliance purposes, even after the license ends.  
   - You may **send copies to government agencies** if required for regulatory filings.

### **Does This Mean You Only Need to Purchase One Copy?**  
**No, purchasing one copy does not automatically cover unlimited use.**  

- **Your rights are limited to “portions” of the work** rather than full works.  
- **Distribution is restricted to internal use** (employees and contractors).  
- **You can only make digital copies from lawfully obtained originals** (either directly from the publisher or a licensed source).  
- **External sharing (beyond regulatory filings) is not allowed** unless explicitly stated in the license.  

### **Bottom Line:**  
You must **lawfully acquire** the original work, and then you can **store, share internally, and print portions** as long as you comply with the CCC’s restrictions. If **full external use** is needed, additional permissions may be required.
