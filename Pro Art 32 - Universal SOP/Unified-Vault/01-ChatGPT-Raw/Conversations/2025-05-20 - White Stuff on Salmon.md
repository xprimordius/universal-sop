## 🤖 ChatGPT

The white stuff you're seeing on the salmon is called **albumin** — a natural protein that coagulates and comes to the surface when salmon is cooked, especially at higher temperatures or when it's cooked too quickly.

### Key Facts:
- **Safe to eat:** 100% safe and normal.
- **Texture:** It can look unappetizing but is harmless.
- **Why it appears:** Common when salmon goes from frozen to cooked too fast or is cooked at too high a temp.
- **Reduce next time:** 
  - Cook lower and slower (e.g., 275–300°F).
  - Brine salmon for 10–15 mins in saltwater (1 tbsp salt in 1 cup water) before cooking.
  - Let it fully thaw and come closer to room temp before cooking.

You can gently scrape it off before serving for better presentation, but no safety concerns here.

Yes — it's **completely healthy**.

### Breakdown:
- **Albumin** (the white stuff) is just a water-soluble **protein** in salmon.
- When heated, it solidifies and leaks out. This is **not fat, pus, or spoilage** — it's natural.
- It doesn't affect nutrition and may even indicate **lean, high-protein content**.

If you want better texture/presentation:
- Brine before cooking.
- Lower the temp (e.g., 300°F for ~20–25 min).
- Always fully thaw and pat dry before baking.
