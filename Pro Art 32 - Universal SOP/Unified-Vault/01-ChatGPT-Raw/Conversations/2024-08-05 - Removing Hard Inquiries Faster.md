1. **Verify Unauthorized Inquiries**:
   - Check your credit report for any unauthorized inquiries. If you find any, you have grounds to dispute them.

2. **Contact the Creditor Directly**:
   - Reach out to the creditor or company that initiated the inquiry. Request them to send a removal request to TransUnion if the inquiry was unauthorized or a mistake.

3. **Submit a Dispute with TransUnion**:
   - Go to the TransUnion website and file a dispute for each unauthorized inquiry. Provide any supporting documents that prove the inquiry was unauthorized.

4. **Follow Up via Phone**:
   - After submitting the dispute online, call TransUnion’s customer service to expedite the process. Be polite but persistent, emphasizing the urgency and the potential error.

5. **Send a Certified Letter**:
   - Write a formal dispute letter and send it via certified mail with a return receipt requested. Include copies of your credit report with the inquiries highlighted and any supporting documents.

6. **Leverage Consumer Protection Laws**:
   - Mention the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) in your dispute. Highlight that unauthorized inquiries should be removed promptly under the law.

7. **Request a Reinvestigation**:
   - If the initial dispute does not result in removal, request a reinvestigation. Provide additional evidence if available.

8. **Escalate if Necessary**:
   - If you face delays, escalate the issue by contacting the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) or your state’s Attorney General’s office.

By taking these steps, you can potentially speed up the removal of hard inquiries from TransUnion. While it might not be as quick as overnight, these actions can significantly reduce the time needed for resolution.
