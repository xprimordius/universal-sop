## 20 TB HDD vs SSD — Reliability Reality (Precision, Data-Backed

**Key reliability truth:**  
- **SSDs have no moving parts → inherently fewer mechanical failure modes.** HDDs contain spinning platters and actuators that fail over time. citeturn0search12turn0search24  
- **Backblaze real data (cloud scale): SSDs show slightly lower failure rates than HDDs.** ~0.98% vs ~1.64% annualized. citeturn0search25  
- **SSDs can fail without warning** (power loss, controller failure, NAND wear) whereas HDDs often show warning signs (noise, SMART). citeturn0search9

**Lifespan nuance:**  
- SSD longevity tied to **write cycles** (large writes at high capacity arenas can stress NAND).  
- HDD longevity tied to **mechanical wear**, heat, shock.  
- Both can fail — no media is “bulletproof.” citeturn0search37

**Capacity & failure correlation:**  
- Larger capacity storage (both HDD & SSD) historically shows **higher relative failure risk** vs smaller units due to more components and complex manufacturing. citeturn0search16  
- Consumer-grade large HDDs (like 20 TB/22 TB externals) may report **mixed real-world reviews**, even with strong star ratings. citeturn0search2turn0search30

**Enterprise vs Consumer drives:**  
- Enterprise HDDs often have higher workload ratings (e.g., 550 TB/year, MTBF in millions of hours) and longer warranties — real reliability advantage for heavy use. citeturn0search34  
- Enterprise SSDs have better endurance, power-loss protection, and data integrity features versus consumer SSDs. citeturn0search28

## When to Choose What

### Best for **long-term storage / backups**
- **HDD** is cost-per-TB champ — affordable massive space.
- But you **must backup multiple copies** because failure risk isn’t zero.  
- External HDD models are unpredictable in isolated reviews; one failure anecdote doesn’t reflect population statistics. citeturn0search2

### Best for **reliability + speed + critical data**
- **SSD** wins if budget allows — fewer moving parts and generally lower annual failure rates. citeturn0search25  

### Best for **24/7 heavy workloads**
- **Enterprise HDD / Enterprise SSD** — purpose-built, backed by workload ratings and warranties.

## SSD Note

Large **20 TB SSDs exist** (very expensive) but aren’t mainstream consumer picks yet — cost is often an **order of magnitude higher than HDD** per TB. citeturn0news43  
If budget isn’t the limiter and reliability + speed is highest priority, enterprise SSD arrays with redundancy (RAID, erasure coding) are the gold standard — but complexity and cost rise accordingly.

---

## Practical Reliability Strategy (Master-Grade)

**Never trust single media:**  
- Always use **redundancy + backups** (RAID + offsite/cloud).  
- Track SMART data for HDD, endurance metrics for SSD.  
- Refresh drives based on age/workload, not just star ratings.

**Star rating ≠ reliability predictability**  
- Even well-rated drives can fail; large capacity drives have wider variance in real-world failure. citeturn0search30
