How is average daily ledger balance calculated

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Here’s the breakdown:

---

## **Average Ledger Balance (Business Accounts)**

**Formula:**  

\[
\text{Average Ledger Balance} = \frac{\text{Sum of each day’s ending ledger balance in statement cycle}}{\text{Number of days in cycle}}
\]

---

### **Key Points**
- **Ledger balance** = your balance at the **end of each business day** (after posting all credits/debits that cleared that day).  
- **Pending transactions** don’t count until posted.  
- Every calendar day in the statement cycle counts (including weekends/holidays).  
- The daily balances are added up, then divided by the total number of days in the cycle.  

---

### **Example**
- Statement cycle: 30 days  
- End-of-day balances:
  - Days 1–10: $1,200  
  - Days 11–20: $800  
  - Days 21–30: $400  

\[
\frac{(10 \times 1200) + (10 \times 800) + (10 \times 400)}{30}
= \frac{12,000 + 8,000 + 4,000}{30}
= \frac{24,000}{30} = 800
\]

**Average ledger balance = $800.**

---

### **Why it Matters**
- Wells Fargo (and most banks) use **average daily ledger balance** to decide if you waived monthly fees (e.g., $1,000 for Initiate Business Checking).  
- A single low-balance day pulls the average down.
