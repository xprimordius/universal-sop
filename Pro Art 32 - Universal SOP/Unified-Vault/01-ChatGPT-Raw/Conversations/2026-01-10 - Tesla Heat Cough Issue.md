**Tesla cabin heat can dry and irritate your airways.** That dryness + airflow is the trigger.

### What’s Actually Happening (Most → Least Likely)
1. **Extremely Dry Air**  
   Tesla’s HVAC heats without adding humidity → dries throat, vocal cords, bronchial lining → **dry cough, scratchy throat**.

2. **Direct Airflow on Face/Chest**  
   Vents blowing warm air straight at you strip moisture fast → irritation reflex = cough.

3. **Cabin Air Filter Irritation**  
   Dirty or overloaded HEPA/carbon filter can release fine particulates → throat irritation.

4. **Residual Off-Gassing / Dust**  
   Heated interior plastics + dust = mild chemical or particulate irritation (more noticeable in sensitive people).

5. **Hidden Sensitivity (Common)**  
   Heat exposes **silent post-nasal drip, reflux, or mild asthma** you don’t feel otherwise.

### Why Tesla Feels Worse Than Other Cars
- Very **tight cabin seal**
- **High airflow efficiency**
- Strong **recirculation**
- No humidity control

### Fast Fixes (Ranked)
**Do these first:**
- Aim vents **away from face/throat**
- Lower temp + increase fan speed slightly
- Switch to **feet + windshield only**
- Turn **recirculation OFF**

**If still happening:**
- Replace cabin air filter (HEPA if equipped)
- Run AC for 2–3 min before heat (adds slight moisture)
- Keep water + take small sips while driving

**If you’re sensitive:**
- Use seat heaters instead of cabin heat
- Crack a window slightly
- Use a personal humidifier at home to reset dryness (not in car)

- Wheezing
- Chest tightness
- Cough lasts hours after driving  
→ suggests **airway hyper-reactivity or reflux**, not just dry air.
