# FCRA Violation Inquiry

---

## 👤 You

this credit card has been improperly reporting my balance hurting my credit at 100% false utilization for 2 months now, and upon dispute they said an additional 30-60 days. isn't this in violation of FCRA federal law?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, this could potentially be a violation of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). The FCRA is designed to promote accuracy, fairness, and privacy of information in the files of consumer reporting agencies. Here are some key points regarding your situation:

1. **Accuracy of Information**: Under the FCRA, credit reporting agencies and the entities that furnish information to these agencies (such as credit card companies) are required to report accurate information. If they knowingly report false information, they are in violation of the FCRA.

2. **Dispute Process**: When you dispute an error on your credit report, the credit reporting agency is required to investigate the dispute, usually within 30 days. If the credit card company continues to report inaccurate information after a dispute, it could be a violation of the FCRA.

3. **Correcting Errors**: If the investigation finds that the information is indeed inaccurate, the credit reporting agency must correct or delete the inaccurate information promptly.

4. **Timeframe for Disputes**: While 30-60 days might be a reasonable timeframe for some disputes, continually reporting incorrect information without correction after a dispute has been initiated could be seen as non-compliance with the FCRA.

### Steps You Can Take:

1. **Document Everything**: Keep a record of all communications with the credit card company and credit reporting agencies.

2. **Follow Up**: Ensure that you follow up with the credit reporting agencies and the credit card company within the specified timeframe.

3. **File a Complaint**: If the issue is not resolved, you can file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB). The CFPB oversees compliance with the FCRA and can investigate your complaint.

4. **Consult an Attorney**: If the issue persists and is severely impacting your credit, consider consulting an attorney who specializes in consumer rights and the FCRA.

By taking these steps, you can protect your rights and work towards resolving the incorrect reporting on your credit report.
