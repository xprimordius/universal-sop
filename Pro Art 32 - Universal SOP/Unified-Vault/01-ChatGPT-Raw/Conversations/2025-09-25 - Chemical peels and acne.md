## 👤 You

Do chemical peels help acne

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes—chemical peels can help acne, but with limits:  

**How They Help:**  
- **Exfoliation:** Remove dead skin cells, unclog pores, and reduce blackheads/whiteheads.  
- **Oil Control:** Reduce excess sebum, which lowers breakouts.  
- **Surface Renewal:** Light to medium peels (like salicylic acid or glycolic acid) smooth texture and fade post-acne dark spots.  
- **Inflammation Reduction:** Salicylic acid peels especially target active acne because they penetrate oil glands.  

**Limits & Risks:**  
- Not a cure—peels manage acne, not the root cause (hormones, diet, stress, etc.).  
- Risk of redness, irritation, or hyperpigmentation, especially on sensitive or darker skin if done too aggressively.  
- Multiple sessions usually needed; results aren’t instant.  

**Best Use Cases:**  
- Mild to moderate acne.  
- Acne scars and post-inflammatory marks.  
- Combined with other treatments (retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, antibiotics, lifestyle).  

👉 If your acne is severe, cystic, or linked to hormones (e.g., IUD, PCOS, spironolactone), a dermatologist usually recommends medical therapy first, with peels as a supportive treatment.

## 👤 You

How often should you do them with a lot of acne

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Frequency with active acne:**  

- **Light chemical peels (salicylic, glycolic, lactic):** every **2–4 weeks**.  
- **Medium-depth peels (Jessner’s, TCA):** every **6–8 weeks**, sometimes longer.  
- **Strong/deep peels:** rarely used for active acne—too harsh.  

**Key points:**  
- Start conservative (every 4 weeks), see how your skin responds.  
- Overdoing it → irritation, rebound breakouts, hyperpigmentation.  
- Works best as part of a full acne plan (topicals, diet, hormones, lifestyle).  

👉 With *a lot of acne*, a dermatologist usually suggests a series of lighter peels spaced about **monthly** for safety and consistency.
