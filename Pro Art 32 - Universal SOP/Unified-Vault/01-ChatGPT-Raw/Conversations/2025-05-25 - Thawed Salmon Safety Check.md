Pre frozen salmon thawed in the fridge for some days turning yellow, rinsed and smells decent… safe?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The salmon in the photo shows a yellowish tint on the edges and a slightly mushy texture. Here's a precise breakdown for safety:

### ✅ Safe If All the Following Are True:
- **Smell:** Clean, ocean-like or neutral (not sour, ammonia-like, or strong).
- **Texture:** Firm and resilient when pressed.
- **Color:** Slight yellowing may occur from oxidation, but not moldy, gray, or green.
- **Storage Time in Fridge (after thawing):** ≤ 2 days ideally, **max 3 days** if kept cold and sealed.

### ⚠️ Caution:
- **Yellowing + mushiness** = possible fat oxidation or spoilage beginning.
- Even if the smell is fine, degradation may already be underway.
- Risk increases after **48 hours thawed in the fridge.**

---

### ✅ Recommendation:
If it's been **≤ 2 days thawed**, still smells clean, and you're cooking it thoroughly (internal temp **≥ 145°F / 63°C**), it’s likely safe.

If it's **3+ days thawed** or feels **very soft/mushy**, **discard** to avoid risk. Bacterial spoilage can occur without smell.

---

If you’re unsure, **don’t risk it** — fishborne illness is not worth it.

## 👤 You

It has been more than 2 days and smells like sardines… what happens if I cook and eat it

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

If it **smells like sardines** and has been **thawed for more than 2 days**, it’s **starting to spoil** — even if it’s not overtly rotten. That sardine-like smell often indicates **oxidized fats or bacterial activity**.

---

### ⚠️ What Can Happen If You Eat It:
- **Best case:** Slight stomach discomfort or nothing.
- **Common case:** **Food poisoning** symptoms within 6–24 hours — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps.
- **Worst case:** **Scombroid poisoning** (if histamine levels have built up), especially with fatty fish like salmon:
  - Symptoms: flushing, headache, sweating, dizziness, rapid heartbeat, and GI distress.
  - It **cannot be cooked away** — heat doesn't destroy histamines or toxins from spoilage.

---

### ✅ Bottom Line:
**Don’t eat it.** Even if cooked, the risk of foodborne illness — especially from histamine or bacteria — outweighs any benefit. **Toss it.**
