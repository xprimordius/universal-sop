- **Cheeks and chin:** This is typically **hormonal acne pattern**, so both spironolactone and IUD hormones are likely involved.  

**2. Normal vs Concerning**  
- **Normal:** Breakouts still happening at 3 months, but slowly improving or stabilizing.  
- **Concerning:** Worsening week by week, painful cystic breakouts, or no sign of improvement by month 6.  

**3. What Helps**  
- Stick with spironolactone — most dermatologists recommend **6 months** before judging results.  
- Pair with a gentle but consistent skincare routine (non-comedogenic cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen).  
- Sometimes a **topical retinoid** or **oral antibiotic short-term** is added to bridge the gap while spironolactone takes effect.  
- If it’s the IUD causing flares, only switching/removing it will truly solve that piece.  

**4. What to Do Now**  
- Track whether breakouts are plateauing or worsening.  
- Book a derm follow-up — they may adjust your spironolactone dose (commonly goes up to 100–200mg/day).  
- Ask if your IUD is contributing (they’ll know which types have the strongest link to acne).
