### 1. **Reasonable Investigation Requirements**:
Under **15 U.S.C. § 1681i**, when a consumer disputes the accuracy of information in their credit file, the CRA must:
   - Conduct a **reasonable investigation** into the disputed information.
   - **Verify the information with the furnisher (e.g., a bank)** or remove it if it cannot be verified.
   - Notify the consumer of the results of the reinvestigation.

If the CRA claims it verified the information but refuses to provide details of that verification or directs you to deal with the bank yourself, they may be in violation because:
   - The FCRA holds the CRA—not the consumer—responsible for investigating and verifying disputes with the furnisher of the information.
   - Failing to provide the source or documentation supporting their verification may demonstrate bad faith or non-compliance.

---

### 2. **Failure to Respond to an Affidavit of Truth**:
Your **notarized affidavit of truth** carries legal weight as a sworn statement, and failure to address it properly could indicate negligence or bad faith. While the FCRA does not specifically address affidavits, the CRA must still reinvestigate disputes and cannot ignore or dismiss valid evidence you provide.

### 3. **Willful Non-Compliance Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n**:
To prove willful non-compliance:
   - **Knowledge of wrongdoing:** The CRA knowingly failed to fulfill its obligations under the FCRA, such as refusing to provide verified information or ignoring clear evidence of inaccuracy.
   - **Reckless disregard:** The CRA acted with reckless disregard for the accuracy of the information or your rights under the FCRA.

If the CRA continues to report the inaccurate information without resolving your dispute and fails to provide documentation supporting their claim of verification, this could qualify as **willful non-compliance**.

---

### 4. **Penalties for Willful Non-Compliance**:

   - Actual damages or statutory damages of $100 to $1,000 per violation.

3. **Consult an Attorney**:
   - If the CRA refuses to comply, consult a consumer protection attorney who can help you pursue a case for willful non-compliance.
