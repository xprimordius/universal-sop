# Affidavit vs Police Report

---

## 👤 You

what law says an affidavit can be used in place of a police report legally, in regards to identity theft reporting for account / inquiry removal with the bureaus

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The law that allows an affidavit to be used in place of a police report for identity theft reporting, particularly in relation to account or inquiry removal with credit bureaus, is found in the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)**. Specifically, **Section 605B** of the FCRA, which was added by the **Fair and Accurate Credit Transactions Act (FACTA) of 2003**, permits the use of an identity theft affidavit in lieu of a police report.

Under Section 605B of the FCRA:

- Consumers have the right to block information resulting from identity theft on their credit reports.
- To initiate this block, consumers can submit an identity theft report, which can be either a police report or an identity theft affidavit, typically completed through the Federal Trade Commission's (FTC) IdentityTheft.gov website.
- The affidavit serves as legally valid documentation of identity theft when submitted along with other required documents.

This means that when dealing with credit bureaus to remove fraudulent accounts or inquiries, you can submit an identity theft affidavit instead of a police report, and the credit bureaus are required by law to comply with your request to block or remove the fraudulent information.

### Relevant Statutory Language
- **FCRA Section 605B**: "[A consumer] may submit to a consumer reporting agency an identity theft report that consists of... a copy of an official, valid report filed by a consumer with an appropriate Federal, State, or local law enforcement agency, including the United States Postal Inspection Service, or such other government agency as the Commission shall by rule determine is appropriate, and that alleges the consumer is a victim of fraud or identity theft." This includes identity theft affidavits as determined by the FTC.

This law ensures that victims of identity theft can use a simpler, more accessible method to initiate the removal of fraudulent information from their credit reports, without necessarily involving law enforcement.

The requirement for credit reporting agencies to remove or block accounts related to identity theft when provided with an affidavit or a police report is established under the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)**, specifically in **Section 605B**.

### Section 605B of the FCRA - **Blocking of Information Resulting from Identity Theft**
- **Obligation to Block Information:** Under this section, consumer reporting agencies (CRAs) are required to block the reporting of any information that a consumer identifies as resulting from identity theft.
- **Consumer Requirements:** The consumer must provide:
  1. **Proof of Identity**: Sufficient identification information as required by the CRA.
  2. **Identity Theft Report**: This can be a police report or an identity theft affidavit, typically completed through the FTC’s IdentityTheft.gov website.
  3. **Statement of the Identity Theft**: The consumer must specifically identify the information in the credit report that is the result of identity theft.
  
- **Timeline for Action:** The CRA must block the information within four business days of receiving the necessary documentation from the consumer.
  
- **Permanent Blocking:** Once the information is blocked, it is not to be reported again unless the CRA later determines that the block was made in error or that the information is accurate.

### Summary
- **Legal Basis:** Section 605B of the FCRA.
- **Action Required:** Credit reporting agencies must remove or block information that is confirmed to be related to identity theft when a consumer provides an affidavit (or police report) along with other required documentation.
- **Consumer Protection:** This ensures that victims of identity theft can have fraudulent accounts and inquiries removed from their credit reports efficiently and legally.
