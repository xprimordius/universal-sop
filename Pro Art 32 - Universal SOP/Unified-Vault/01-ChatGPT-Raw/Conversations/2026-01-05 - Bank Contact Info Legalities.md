Not *automatically illegal* for a bank (like First Interstate) to decline to give contact info to another *private legal department* — **unless specific laws, contracts, or valid legal process require it.**  

**Why:**  
- U.S. banks must strictly protect **nonpublic personal information** under federal privacy law (e.g., Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act/Regulation P). They generally **can’t disclose customer details to nonaffiliated third parties** without proper authorization or an exception. citeturn0search5turn0search11

**When they *must* provide information:**  
1. **Valid Legal Process:** A court-issued, properly served subpoena, summons, or other enforceable legal process compelling disclosure. citeturn0search1  
2. **Customer Authorization:** Written consent from the account holder directing the bank to disclose the information.  
3. **Applicable Exception:** Certain statutory exceptions (e.g., law enforcement requests with proper certification under Right to Financial Privacy Act). citeturn0search3turn0search13

**When they *can* refuse legally:**  
- If the request is informal (just an email or phone call from another legal department) with no subpoena or customer authorization.
