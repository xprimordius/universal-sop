---

## The Key Distinction (This Clears the Confusion)

### 1️⃣ **Exterior sealing = keeps OUTSIDE water out**
Homes are designed to manage:
- Rainfall
- Groundwater
- Snowmelt

They do this with:
- Grading (soil slopes away)
- Foundation waterproofing/damp-proofing
- Drain tile + sump pump
- Window well drainage

**This system assumes water is outside the foundation.**

---

### 2️⃣ **Interior plumbing leaks = NOT what sealing protects against**
A leaking hose spigot:
- Is **inside the building envelope**
- Can drip **continuously for days**
- Can overwhelm flooring, seams, drains, and framing

**No house is sealed to be “waterproof from the inside.”**  
That would require the home to be built like a boat or pool — which houses are not.

---

## Why Your Rainfall Comparison Feels Logical (But Isn’t Equivalent)

Your thought:
> “If it can’t handle a hose leak, how can it handle heavy rain?”

The difference is **pressure, duration, and routing**:

| Scenario | How water is handled |
|------|------|
| Heavy rain | Routed *around* the house via grading & drains |
| Hose spigot leak | Water is *released directly inside* the structure |
| Slow drip | Worse than a burst — keeps soaking materials |

A slow, steady leak is actually **more damaging** than a storm because:
- It bypasses all exterior defenses
- It soaks framing, seams, and subfloor
- It can spread laterally across basement floors

---

## When sealing *would* be considered defective
Sealing **may be at fault** if:
- Water entered from **foundation walls**
- Water came through **window wells**
- Water seeped in during rain **without plumbing leaks**
- There are known grading or drain failures

But if the root cause is:
> ✔️ Hose spigot  
> ✔️ Interior plumbing  
> ✔️ Slow leak over time  

Then sealing is **not the failure point**.
