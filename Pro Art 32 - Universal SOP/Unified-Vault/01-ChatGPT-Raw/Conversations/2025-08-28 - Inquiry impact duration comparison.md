Why was my banker saying hard inquiries fall off after 120 days when the bureaus say 2 years

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Your banker and the bureaus are talking about **different things**:  

- **Credit Bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion):**  
  Hard inquiries stay **on your credit report for 2 years**. That’s the official reporting period.  

- **Scoring Models (FICO / VantageScore):**  
  Most only **count inquiries in your score for 12 months**, and in practice the “weight” drops sharply after **90–120 days**. Lenders know the “recency” matters most—an inquiry from 4 months ago signals far less risk than one from last week.  

- **Why your banker said 120 days:**  
  He’s likely referring to **underwriting guidelines or FICO’s algorithm**, where inquiries lose most of their impact after ~3–4 months. They don’t disappear from your report, but their negative effect on approvals is much weaker.  

👉 So:  
- **Visible:** 2 years (bureau reporting).  
- **Scoring impact:** ~12 months (with most effect in first 90–120 days).  
- **Practical banking impact:** Many underwriters only flag “recent” inquiries (last 90 days or 6 months).
