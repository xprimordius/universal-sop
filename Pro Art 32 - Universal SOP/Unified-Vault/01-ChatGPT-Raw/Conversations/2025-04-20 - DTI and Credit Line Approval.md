## 👤 You

if i have $40-50k in available credit, what does my income need to be to be good for automated approval odds for $10-$40k lines with respect to DTI. ($20k of student loans)

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**TL;DR:**  
To be in a *strong position for automated approvals* for **$10K–$40K** credit lines with **$40K–$50K** in existing available credit and **$20K in student loans**, your **stated income should be at least $75K–$120K** depending on how high you want to go.

---

### Strategic Breakdown:

#### 1. **DTI (Debt-to-Income) Ratio Basics:**
Most issuers like:
- **< 36% total DTI**
- **< 10–15% credit utilization**
- **< 50% revolving + installment exposure relative to income**

---

#### 2. **If You Already Have $50K in Credit Lines:**

To request **another $10K–$40K**, you want issuers to **see that your income can support ~$90K total exposure**.

---

### Income Benchmarks:

| Desired New Credit Line | Recommended Stated Annual Income |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------|
| $10K                   | $75K+                            |
| $20K                   | $90K–$100K                       |
| $30K                   | $100K–$110K                      |
| $40K                   | $110K–$120K                      |

> Assumes low utilization and no high balances reporting.

---

### Student Loan Impact:
- If you're **not in repayment yet**, most issuers count **~0.5%–1% of balance/month** as debt (i.e., $100–$200/month).
- If **in repayment**, your actual monthly payment is factored into DTI.
- Still relatively small impact vs income.

---

### Optimization Tip:
- Stated income should support **2x–2.5x** total requested revolving limits.
- Example: $100K income = comfort zone for up to ~$40K in new revolving lines.
