# Removing Hard Inquiry TU

---

## 👤 You

Can I remove a hard inquiry from TransUnion if the card doesn’t report to TU? It made an inquiry on all of the bureaus but only reports to experian

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, you can dispute a hard inquiry on your TransUnion report, even if the card doesn't report to TransUnion but does report to Experian. Here's how you can proceed:

1. **Obtain Your TransUnion Credit Report**: Get a copy of your TransUnion credit report to review the hard inquiry in question.

2. **Gather Information**: Collect all relevant information about the inquiry, including the date it was made and the name of the creditor.

3. **Contact TransUnion**: You can dispute the inquiry online through the TransUnion website, by mail, or by phone. When disputing by mail, send a dispute letter to the following address:
   ```
   TransUnion Consumer Solutions
   P.O. Box 2000
   Chester, PA 19016-2000
   ```

4. **Dispute the Inquiry**: In your dispute, clearly state that the inquiry is unauthorized and explain that the account does not report to TransUnion. Include any supporting documentation you have, such as a statement from the creditor that they do not report to TransUnion.

5. **Follow Up**: TransUnion typically has 30 days to investigate your dispute. They will notify you of the results. If the inquiry is found to be valid, it will remain on your report. If it is deemed invalid, it will be removed.

If the initial dispute is unsuccessful, you might consider contacting the creditor directly to request removal, especially if they did not have your permission to make the inquiry on TransUnion.

Account review inquiries, also known as soft inquiries, typically do not impact your credit score and are only visible to you and not to potential lenders. These inquiries are used by creditors to review your existing accounts and are not considered negative.

However, if you wish to have them removed, you generally cannot remove legitimate account review inquiries from your credit report since they are standard practice for creditors monitoring existing accounts. Here are some steps you can take if you believe there are inaccuracies or unauthorized inquiries:

### Steps to Handle Inaccurate or Unauthorized Inquiries

1. **Check for Unauthorized Inquiries**: Review your credit report carefully to identify any inquiries you believe are unauthorized.

2. **Contact the Creditor**: If you find an unauthorized inquiry, contact the creditor directly. Explain that you did not authorize the inquiry and ask them to remove it.

3. **Dispute with TransUnion**: If contacting the creditor does not resolve the issue, you can file a dispute with TransUnion. You can do this online, by phone, or by mail.

   - **Online Dispute**: Visit the [TransUnion Dispute Center](https://www.transunion.com/dispute) and log in to your account to initiate a dispute.
   - **Phone Dispute**: Call TransUnion’s customer service at 1-800-916-8800.
   - **Mail Dispute**: Send a dispute letter to:
     ```
     TransUnion Consumer Solutions
     ```
