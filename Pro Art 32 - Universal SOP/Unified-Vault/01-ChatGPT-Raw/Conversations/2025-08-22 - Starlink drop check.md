## 🤖 ChatGPT

Starlink Standard is fairly durable, but whether it’s “okay” depends on a few things:  

**1. The Drop Impact**  
- If it only slipped out of the trunk onto pavement/ground and landed flat, chances are high it’s fine.  
- If it landed on a corner/edge, especially the dish or connector port, inspect carefully.  

**2. What to Check Right Now**  
- **Dish surface:** Look for cracks, dents, or chips. Even hairline cracks can compromise waterproofing.  
- **Cables & connectors:** Make sure no pins are bent or housings cracked.  
- **Mount/stand:** Confirm no warping.  
- **Electronics:** Plug it in and check if it powers on, orients, and connects to satellites normally.  

**3. What Usually Fails**  
- The biggest risk is internal damage to the phased array antenna or cracked housing that lets water in. If you see physical cracks, seal them immediately (temporary: waterproof tape, long-term: replacement).  

**4. What To Do Next**  
- Test immediately with Starlink app → run “Speed Test” and check “Obstructions.”  
- If it works normally (connects, stable speeds), it likely survived.  
- If you notice unusual noises, failure to orient, or error codes, contact Starlink support.  

⚡ Bottom line: If no visible cracks and it powers/connects normally, it should be okay. Just monitor for weatherproofing issues in the next rain.
