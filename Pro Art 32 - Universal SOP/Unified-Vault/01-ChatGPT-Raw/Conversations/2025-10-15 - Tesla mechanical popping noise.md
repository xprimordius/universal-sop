| **Thermal management / cooling system residual action** | Just after driving, or even for some time after shutdown | Whirrs, hums, or short bursts | The car may still run pumps or fans to manage battery temperature or equalize pressures. citeturn0search8turn0search2turn0search1 |

If the popping happens right after turning the car off, or in the first few minutes, it’s often just the car settling into its off-state protocols.

## ⚠️ Causes to be cautious about / investigate

If the noise is **repeated, loud, irregular, location-specific**, or happening long after shutdown, that’s a red flag. Possible issues:

- **Loose bolts or control arm fasteners**: Some owners report popping when control arms or suspension bolts back out under stress. citeturn0search3turn0search0  
- **Battery pack thermal stress / structural expansion**: Metal parts expand/contract; over time tolerances shift. If it’s louder than usual, or changing, it’s worth checking. citeturn0search8  
- **Faulty cooling / HVAC fan bearings winding down**: If a fan or pump bearing is worn, the shutdown noise can be odd or harsh. citeturn0search8turn0search1  
- **Something out of place under the car**: Loose bracket, shield, debris, etc., might shift when things settle.  
- **After long shutdown**: If the popping continues many minutes after power off, that suggests the system is doing something unexpected (leak, electrical, mechanical).

---

## 🔍 What to do (diagnostic checklist)

1. **Record when/where**  
   - When (immediately after off, 5 min later, 30 min later)  
   - Under front / rear / side / center  
   - Is the car hot (just driven) or cold (after sitting)  

2. **See if noise changes under conditions**  
   - Cold vs after long drive  
   - After software update  
   - In different ambient temperatures

3. **Inspect undercarriage / suspension**  
   - Look for loose bolts, shields, panels  
   - Check subsystems that mount near the floor or battery enclosure

4. **Push to Tesla Service**  
   - Play them your recording  
   - Ask them to inspect battery contactors, suspension fasteners, cooling system, and underbody hardware  
   - Use existing warranties / service bulletins

5. **Compare to known “normals”**  
   - Try to find a same-model Tesla and listen when it powers off  
   - Ask Service whether the sound you hear is “within spec” for your build date / version

---

Bottom line: a **light pop** or click during shutdown is often harmless — the car is doing what it must to go dormant.
