# Tire Maintenance Sequence

---

## 👤 You

Alignment before balance and rotation? Or after? And where does oil change best go in the sequence

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Alignment should be done **after** balancing and rotating the tires. Here's the optimal sequence for vehicle maintenance:

1. **Oil Change:** This can be done at any time, as it's unrelated to tire or wheel maintenance. However, doing it first can ensure the engine is in good condition while the rest of the services are being performed.
2. **Tire Rotation:** This comes next, ensuring even tire wear.
3. **Tire Balancing:** After rotating the tires, balancing ensures they spin smoothly without vibration.
4. **Alignment:** Finally, alignment is done to ensure the wheels are properly aligned after the tires have been balanced and rotated.

This sequence ensures optimal tire wear and performance.

## 👤 You

If I create a fund, how large of a section 8 apartment complex real estate deal would I need to do in order to personally profit $10K+/mo after satisfying ROI goals with investors

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

To calculate the size of a Section 8 apartment complex deal that would generate $10K+/month in personal profit after satisfying investors’ ROI goals, several factors need to be considered:

### Key Variables to Calculate
1. **Target Profit:** $10K/month = $120K/year.
2. **Investor ROI:** Assume an ROI of 8-10% for investors.
3. **Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate):** The net income yield on the property, which varies by location but is typically 4-8% for multifamily real estate.
4. **Operating Expenses:** Typically 35-50% of gross rental income.
5. **Financing Terms:** Down payment, interest rates, and debt service if you're using leverage.
6. **Vacancy Rate:** Assume some vacancy, likely 5-10% for Section 8 housing.

### Example Calculation (Hypothetical)

#### Step 1: Estimate Gross Rental Income
- Assume a cap rate of 6%.
- To personally profit $120K/year after paying investors and covering expenses, the property would need to generate significant income.

#### Step 2: Estimate Total Net Operating Income (NOI)
- Let’s assume you need to achieve a $120K/year NOI **after** all expenses and debt service.
- If the investors are promised an 8-10% ROI, and you keep $120K/year, the total NOI needs to be higher.
- Let’s assume $300K/year total NOI to account for both your profit and investors’ returns.

#### Step 3: Calculate Property Value
- With a cap rate of 6%, the required property value to generate $300K in NOI would be:

\[
\text{Property Value} = \frac{\text{NOI}}{\text{Cap Rate}} = \frac{300K}{0.06} = 5M
\]

#### Step 4: Financing and Equity
- If you raise 30% equity from investors, that would be $1.5M, and the rest could be financed with a loan.
- Investors would expect their ROI (8-10%) on the $1.5M, which is $120K - $150K annually.

#### Step 5: Cash Flow and Profit
- Assuming the property generates $300K/year in NOI:
  - Investors get their $120-150K (their 8-10% ROI).
  - After debt service and expenses, you keep $120K annually ($10K/month).

### Estimated Deal Size
- To achieve this profit, you’d likely need to structure a deal with a property value around **$5M+**, assuming a 6% cap rate, investor ROI, and typical operating expenses.

### Additional Considerations
- **Higher Cap Rate:** If the cap rate is higher (e.g., 7-8%), you can achieve the same results with a smaller property, maybe around **$3-4M**.
- **Leverage:** If you use more leverage, your personal stake might be lower, but debt servicing will affect the cash flow.
- **Tax Benefits:** Depreciation and tax strategies (like using a cost segregation study) can increase your personal profit while reducing taxable income.

In summary, a **$5M+ Section 8 apartment complex deal** would be a reasonable estimate, depending on the cap rate and financing structure, to personally profit $10K+/month after meeting investor ROI expectations.
