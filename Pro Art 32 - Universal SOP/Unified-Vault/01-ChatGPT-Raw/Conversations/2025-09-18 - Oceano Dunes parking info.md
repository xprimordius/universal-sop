## 💣 Why 42 PSI is risky in sand

| Factor | What Happens |
|--------|--------------|
| **Smaller tire footprint** | Less surface area → higher pressure per square inch → tire **sinks** in soft sand. |
| **Reduced flotation** | High PSI causes tires to “cut” into the sand, especially in dry or loose patches. |
| **Higher chance of spinning** | Traction control kicks in more → tires spin → dig a hole → you're stuck. |
| **Bumpy ride and possible bounce** | High PSI reduces tire flex, so you feel every bump and might “hop” over soft spots, losing control. |

---

## ✅ Recommended: Air down

| Vehicle Type | Recommended PSI for Sand |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| AWD Tesla (Model Y, Model X) | **18–25 PSI** depending on load and conditions |
| Light 2WD cars | 15–20 PSI |
| Trucks/SUVs (heavier) | 12–18 PSI (if tires allow) |

*This allows tires to “float” on the sand instead of digging in.*

---

## ⚙️ Best Practice

- **Air down to ~25 PSI** as a starting point for firm sand.
- If you hit softer areas, go lower to **18–20 PSI** (watch sidewall bulge, don’t go below tire’s rated minimum).
- Carry a **portable air compressor** (12V or plug-in to EcoFlow) to reinflate when leaving the beach.
- Monitor **tire temperature** — deflated tires run hotter.
