No federal laws or guidelines explicitly require **Chase Bank** or any financial institution to delete **internal account notes** beyond 30 days, even if those notes negatively impact your ability to obtain credit or make major purchases. ### **Relevant Federal Guidelines:**
1. **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA):**
   - Governs how information is reported to credit bureaus.
   - If Chase Bank reports incorrect or outdated negative information to a credit bureau, you can dispute it.
   - **Key Obligation:** Chase must investigate disputes within 30 days and correct inaccuracies.

2. **Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA):**
   - Prohibits discrimination in lending.
   - Notes that improperly reflect bias or inaccuracies affecting your ability to access credit may violate this act.

3. **Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices (UDAP):**
   - If Chase is using internal notes in a way that is unfair or misleading, it could violate consumer protection laws under the **Federal Trade Commission Act (FTC Act)**.

4. **Privacy and Data Practices:**
   - **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA):** Regulates how financial institutions manage customer data, but it does not require deletion of internal notes.

---

1. **Request a Review:**
   - File a formal written request with Chase to review and correct any inaccurate or outdated internal notes.
   - Ask them to provide the exact reason why the notes remain and how they are being used.

2. **Dispute Errors:**
   - If the notes are being reported to credit bureaus, file a dispute under the **FCRA**.

3. **Legal Review:**
   - If the notes result in unfair denial of credit or harm your creditworthiness, consult legal counsel for potential violations of the **ECOA** or other consumer protection laws.

4. **Escalate to Regulatory Agencies:**
   - If Chase is uncooperative, you can file a complaint with:
     - The **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)**.
     - The **Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)** (if Chase's practices are regulated by them).
