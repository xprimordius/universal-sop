# Other Credit Bureaus Overview

---

## 👤 You

tell me more about these 50-60 other credit bureaus / reporting agencies

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

In addition to the three major credit bureaus—Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion—there are numerous other credit reporting agencies and specialty bureaus that collect and report various types of financial and personal information. These agencies often focus on specific aspects of consumer behavior, such as rental history, utility payments, insurance claims, and employment history. Here are some notable examples:

### Specialty Credit Bureaus and Reporting Agencies:

1. **Innovis**: Another general-purpose credit reporting agency like the big three. It's less known but used by some lenders.

2. **ChexSystems**: Specializes in checking and savings account activity, particularly focusing on negative information such as bounced checks and overdrafts.

3. **Certegy**: Provides check verification and risk management services, often used by retailers to prevent check fraud.

4. **CoreLogic Teletrack**: Focuses on subprime lending, including payday loans, installment loans, and rent-to-own agreements.

5. **LexisNexis Risk Solutions**: Offers a wide range of data solutions, including public records, insurance claims, and legal information.

6. **SageStream (formerly IDA)**: Provides consumer credit information, often used by lenders as a secondary credit check.

7. **Clarity Services**: Specializes in alternative credit data, often used by subprime lenders to evaluate non-traditional credit histories.

8. **MicroBilt**: Offers credit and alternative data reporting, including consumer and commercial credit reports.

9. **Tenant Screening Services**: These agencies, such as RentBureau (part of Experian) and First Advantage, provide rental history reports to landlords and property managers.

10. **Employment Screening Services**: Companies like HireRight and SterlingBackcheck provide background checks, including credit history, for employment purposes.

11. **Insurance Claims Reporting**: Agencies like CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange) by LexisNexis collect information on insurance claims for auto and property insurance.

12. **Utility Payment Reporting**: Services like the National Consumer Telecom & Utilities Exchange (NCTUE) report on utility and telecom payment histories.

### Functions and Uses:
- **Credit Scoring**: Many of these bureaus contribute data used to calculate various credit scores beyond the FICO score, such as VantageScore.
- **Fraud Prevention**: Agencies like Early Warning Services help financial institutions detect and prevent fraud.
- **Risk Assessment**: Specialty bureaus provide additional data points for lenders and insurers to assess risk more accurately.
- **Tenant and Employment Screening**: These services help landlords and employers make informed decisions by providing detailed background information.

### Consumer Rights:
Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), consumers have rights regarding their credit reports from all reporting agencies, including:
- **Free Annual Credit Report**: Consumers can obtain a free credit report annually from each of the major credit bureaus and many specialty bureaus.
- **Dispute Inaccuracies**: Consumers can dispute inaccurate information found on their credit reports.
- **Disclosure**: When adverse actions are taken based on a credit report, consumers must be notified and given the opportunity to request a copy of the report.

### How to Access Reports:
- **AnnualCreditReport.com**: The official site to obtain free annual credit reports from the three major credit bureaus.
- **Direct Requests**: Many specialty bureaus offer free reports upon request, often available through their websites or customer service channels.

Understanding the role and scope of these additional credit reporting agencies can help consumers better manage their financial profiles and protect against potential inaccuracies or fraud.

For banks and lenders, the most commonly used credit reporting agencies are typically the three major credit bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. However, there are also some specialty credit bureaus and agencies that are frequently used for specific purposes. Here are the most common ones:

### Major Credit Bureaus:
1. **Equifax**
2. **Experian**
3. **TransUnion**

### Specialty Credit Bureaus and Reporting Agencies:
1. **Innovis**: While less commonly used than the big three, Innovis is sometimes checked by lenders for additional credit information.

2. **ChexSystems**: Widely used by banks to screen individuals opening new checking or savings accounts, focusing on past account management and any issues like bounced checks or overdrafts.

3. **Early Warning Services (EWS)**: Another important agency for banks, used to detect and prevent fraud in checking and savings accounts.

4. **Clarity Services**: Frequently used by subprime lenders to gather alternative credit data, especially for consumers with limited or poor credit histories.

5. **CoreLogic Teletrack**: Used mainly by subprime lenders to assess credit risk in alternative lending markets, including payday loans and installment loans.

6. **LexisNexis Risk Solutions**: Provides a broad range of data including public records, used by lenders for deeper background checks and fraud prevention.

7. **SageStream**: Utilized by some lenders as an additional source of credit information, similar to Innovis.

### Insurance and Utility Reporting:
While not as commonly used by traditional lenders, these agencies can play a role in specific lending scenarios:
1. **National Consumer Telecom & Utilities Exchange (NCTUE)**: Reports on utility and telecom payment histories, sometimes used for underwriting purposes in lending.
2. **Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (CLUE)**: Maintains information on insurance claims, which can be relevant for loans requiring insurance (e.g., mortgages).

### Usage Context:
- **Traditional Loans and Credit Cards**: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion are the primary sources for credit reports and scores.
- **Checking and Savings Accounts**: ChexSystems and Early Warning Services are most relevant for banks evaluating new account applicants.
- **Subprime and Alternative Lending**: Clarity Services and CoreLogic Teletrack are more commonly used to assess risk for higher-risk lending products.
- **Fraud Prevention**: LexisNexis and Early Warning Services provide critical data for preventing identity theft and fraud.

### Access to Reports:
Lenders typically have agreements with these bureaus to access the necessary data for underwriting and risk assessment. Consumers can access their reports directly from these bureaus to review their credit histories and dispute any inaccuracies.

By understanding which credit bureaus and agencies are most commonly used by banks and lenders, consumers can better manage their credit profiles and anticipate the factors that might influence lending decisions.
