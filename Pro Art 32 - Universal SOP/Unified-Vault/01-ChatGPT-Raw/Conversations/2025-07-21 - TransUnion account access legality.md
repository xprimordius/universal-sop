---

## 1. FCRA § 605B – Blocking Fraudulent Items  
- Under § 605B, if you provide proof of identity, an identity theft report (police or FTC), identify the fraudulent item, and affirm it wasn’t yours, TransUnion **must block that information** within **4 business days** citeturn0search2turn0search13.  
- They **can only refuse** if they reasonably conclude it was a misrepresentation, blocked in error, or that you benefited from the fraud citeturn0search2.

---

## 2. Legal Precedents: You Have Leverage  
- In **Kaplan v. TransUnion (2024)**, a court rejected TransUnion’s technical “impossibility” argument and reaffirmed that § 605B must be honored for any identity-theft-related information citeturn0search10turn0search11.

---

## 3. Freezing, Locking & Online Access  
- You have the **right to view** your disputes and credit report online.  
- They **cannot legally deny access permanently** simply because you dispute something.  
- **Freezes** must be placed or lifted **promptly**—they were fined $8M for delays citeturn0search6.

## 4. If They're Locking You Out  
That could violate:  
1. **§ 605B** if they ignore your proper dispute.  
2. **Online access rights**—blocking your access permanently, especially on a valid dispute, could be considered non-compliance or deceptive practice.  
3. Their own freeze/lock policies—they’re required to act promptly and transparently.

2. **Request in writing** (certified mail or secure portal):

   - File a complaint with **CFPB or FTC**.

4. You can also sue for statutory damages and attorneys’ fees if they violate § 605B or the FCRA.
