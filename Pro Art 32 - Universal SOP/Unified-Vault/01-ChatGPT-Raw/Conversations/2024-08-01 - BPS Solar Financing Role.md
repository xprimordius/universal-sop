Building Performance Standards (BPS) do not play a major role in financing solar installations in the United States. Instead, financing for solar installations primarily comes from a variety of other sources, including federal and state incentives, tax credits, and private investments. The U.S. solar industry has seen substantial growth driven by utility-scale projects, which accounted for the majority of the 11.8 GW of capacity installed in the first quarter of 2024【8†source】【9†source】.

Key financing mechanisms include:
1. **Federal and State Incentives:** Programs such as the Investment Tax Credit (ITC) and various state-level incentives help reduce the cost burden for solar installations.
2. **Private Financing:** Banks, financial institutions, and solar companies often provide loans and financing solutions tailored to solar projects.
3. **Third-Party Ownership Models:** These include Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and solar leases, where a third party owns the solar system, and the customer pays for the electricity generated.

BPS, which focuses on improving energy efficiency and reducing carbon emissions from buildings, plays a complementary role in the broader context of energy policy but is not a primary source of financing for solar installations.
