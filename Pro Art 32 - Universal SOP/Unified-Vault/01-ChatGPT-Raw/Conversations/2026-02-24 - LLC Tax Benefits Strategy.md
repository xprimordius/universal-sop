### 2️⃣ **Two Separate Revocable Trusts**

- Allows clearer separation of assets

**Best for:** second marriages, asset protection planning

---

### 3️⃣ **Joint Trust That Splits at Death (Most Common for Married Couples)**
At the **first spouse’s death**, the trust splits into:
- **Survivor’s Trust** (revocable, full control)
- **Bypass / Credit Shelter Trust** (irrevocable, estate-tax planning)

---

## What a Revocable Trust Does (and Doesn’t)

### ✅ Does:
- Avoid probate
- Keep control while alive
- Allow incapacity planning
- Simplify inheritance
- Coordinate with wills

### ❌ Does NOT:
- Reduce income taxes
- Protect assets from your creditors (while revocable)
- Lock anything in permanently

1. You create the trust
2. Retitle assets into it (house, bank accounts, LLC interests)
3. You act as trustees (you’re still in control)
4. If incapacitated → successor trustee steps in
5. At death → assets pass per trust terms (no probate)

- Clean transfer to surviving spouse

- Avoid court involvement
- Keep family matters private
- Coordinate business ownership (like your LLC)

## ⚠️ First Principle (Non-Negotiable)
**Do NOT create or fund a joint trust if divorce is pending or likely.**

## Best Trust Structures During or Anticipating Divorce

### ✅ **Option 1: Two Separate Revocable Living Trusts (Most Common)**
Each spouse creates **their own revocable trust**.

**Why this is best:**
- Keeps assets **clearly separated**
- Preserves flexibility during negotiations
- Avoids accidentally gifting or commingling assets
- Easy to amend after divorce is final

**How assets are handled:**
- Separate property → goes into each spouse’s trust
- Joint property → usually stays **outside trust** until divorce is finalized

**Best for:**  
Divorce in progress, mediation, uncertainty

---

### ✅ **Option 2: Revocable Trust + Divorce Hold**
One or both spouses create revocable trusts **but do not fund contested assets yet**.

Used when:
- Divorce timing is unclear
- You want incapacity planning now
- You’ll fund assets **after settlement**

---

### ⚠️ Option 3: Testamentary Trusts (Temporary Measure)
You rely on:
- A **will**
- Testamentary trusts for kids

This is sometimes used **during litigation** when trusts could complicate disclosure.

---

## What to Do With Kids During Divorce

### 👶 **Children’s Trusts Still Make Sense**
But structure matters:

- Each parent can name a **children’s sub-trust** in their own trust
- Avoid naming the other spouse as sole trustee
- Consider a **neutral or professional trustee** if conflict is high

---

## What NOT to Do (Critical)

❌ Do not create a new joint trust  
❌ Do not retitle the marital home into a trust mid-divorce  
❌ Do not move assets without legal advice  
❌ Do not name your spouse as trustee if separation is active  

These can:
- Be viewed as hiding assets
- Trigger court scrutiny
- Complicate settlement

---

## After the Divorce Is Final (Clean Reset)

Once the decree is entered, you can:
- Amend your revocable trust
- Fund it properly
- Create long-term children’s trusts
- Add asset-protection planning if needed

## Simple Decision Guide

- **Married & stable** → Joint Revocable Living Trust  
- **Divorce pending / uncertain** → **Separate Revocable Trusts**  
- **Divorce finalized** → Revocable Trust tailored to new reality
