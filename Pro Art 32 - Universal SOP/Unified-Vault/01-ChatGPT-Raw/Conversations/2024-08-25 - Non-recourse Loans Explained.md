# Non-recourse Loans Explained

---

## 👤 You

What is non recourse / without recourse

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Non-recourse (or without recourse)** refers to a type of loan or financial agreement where the lender's recovery is limited to the collateral securing the loan. If the borrower defaults on the loan, the lender can seize the collateral but has no claim on the borrower's other assets or income. 

In other words, in a non-recourse loan, the borrower is not personally liable beyond the collateral pledged. If the collateral's value is insufficient to cover the outstanding loan amount, the lender cannot pursue the borrower for any deficiency. 

This is in contrast to **recourse loans**, where the lender can go after the borrower's other assets or income to recover any remaining debt after the collateral has been seized and sold. 

Non-recourse financing is often used in real estate, project finance, or certain types of business loans.

## 👤 You

How does this work with recision

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Recision, or **rescission**, is the legal act of canceling or reversing a contract, returning both parties to the positions they were in before the contract was executed. In the context of non-recourse and recourse loans, rescission typically involves undoing the loan agreement and returning the parties to their pre-contractual state.

### How Rescission Works with Non-Recourse Loans:
- **Returning the Collateral:** If a borrower rescinds a non-recourse loan, the lender would typically return any payments made by the borrower, and the borrower would return the loaned amount or the collateral. Because it's a non-recourse loan, the lender's ability to pursue the borrower is limited to the collateral. The borrower would not be liable for additional payments or obligations beyond the collateral.

- **Effects on the Borrower:** Since the borrower's liability is limited to the collateral, rescission of a non-recourse loan generally means that the borrower would only need to return the collateral or repay the loan amount if they still hold the funds or assets provided by the loan.

### How Rescission Works with Recourse Loans:
- **Returning the Loan Amount:** In a recourse loan, if the borrower rescinds the contract, they would need to return the loaned amount or the collateral. However, because the loan is recourse, if the borrower cannot fully repay or return the loaned amount, the lender could pursue the borrower’s other assets to recover the remaining balance.

- **Additional Liabilities:** Rescinding a recourse loan may leave the borrower liable for additional payments if the lender determines that there are costs or penalties associated with the rescission. The borrower’s other assets could be at risk if the loan amount is not fully recovered.

### Key Points to Consider:
- **Timing and Grounds for Rescission:** Rescission is typically time-sensitive and may require valid grounds, such as misrepresentation, fraud, or mutual mistake. The ability to rescind a loan agreement depends on the contract terms and applicable laws.

- **Mutual Agreement or Court Order:** Rescission can occur by mutual agreement between the lender and borrower or through a court order if one party claims the right to rescind and the other party disputes it.

In summary, rescission in the context of non-recourse loans primarily affects the return of collateral without further liability for the borrower, whereas rescission of recourse loans may involve additional obligations or risks for the borrower if the lender seeks to recover more than the collateral.

### Legal Protection Under Non-Recourse Loans:
1. **Collateral as Sole Recourse:** Under a non-recourse loan, the lender's only option for recouping losses in the event of default is to seize and sell the collateral (e.g., a property in the case of a mortgage). Once the collateral is taken, the borrower has no further financial obligation to the lender, regardless of whether the collateral's sale covers the full amount of the loan.

2. **No Deficiency Judgment:** In the event that the collateral’s sale does not cover the outstanding debt (i.e., there is a deficiency), the lender cannot obtain a deficiency judgment against the borrower. This means they cannot sue the borrower or pursue other assets to recover the remaining balance.

3. **State Laws and Contracts:** Non-recourse protections are often enshrined in state laws, particularly in certain real estate transactions. For example, in some states, laws specifically prohibit lenders from pursuing borrowers for deficiencies in non-recourse loans. These laws are especially common in real estate transactions, where the property itself is often the collateral.

4. **Contractual Terms:** The terms of the non-recourse loan are typically spelled out in the loan agreement, and both parties agree to these terms upfront. The law enforces these terms, preventing lenders from going after the borrower’s other assets if they default.

### Rescission and Legal Implications:
If a borrower rescinds a non-recourse loan, they would typically return the borrowed funds or the collateral, but because of the non-recourse nature of the loan, the lender cannot pursue any additional action beyond what was originally agreed upon. 

- **Limited Recourse After Rescission:** Even in the case of rescission, the borrower is protected from further claims by the lender, as the law upholds the non-recourse nature of the loan. The lender cannot claim more than the return of the loaned amount or the collateral.

### Summary:
Non-recourse loans offer significant legal protection to borrowers, ensuring that their liability is strictly limited to the collateral. This protection is upheld even in the case of loan rescission, where the borrower cannot be pursued for any additional payment beyond the collateral. The law enforces this limitation, preventing lenders from taking further legal action against the borrower once the collateral has been surrendered or the loan is rescinded.
