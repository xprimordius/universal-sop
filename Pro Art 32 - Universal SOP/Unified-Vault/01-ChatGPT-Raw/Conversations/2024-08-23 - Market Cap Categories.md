# Market Cap Categories

---

## 👤 You

if small cap is $25 billion, what are the other terms and amounts ?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

In financial markets, companies are often categorized based on their market capitalization, which is the total market value of a company's outstanding shares. The classifications vary slightly depending on the source, but here's a general breakdown:

1. **Micro Cap**:
   - **Market Capitalization**: Typically less than $300 million.
   - **Example**: Small emerging companies, startups.

2. **Small Cap**:
   - **Market Capitalization**: Approximately $300 million to $2 billion.
   - **Example**: Companies with growth potential but higher risk compared to larger companies.

3. **Mid Cap**:
   - **Market Capitalization**: Approximately $2 billion to $10 billion.
   - **Example**: More established companies with potential for growth, often seen as a middle ground between small and large caps.

4. **Large Cap**:
   - **Market Capitalization**: Approximately $10 billion to $200 billion.
   - **Example**: Well-established companies with a significant market presence, often considered safer investments.

5. **Mega Cap**:
   - **Market Capitalization**: Typically over $200 billion.
   - **Example**: The largest and most dominant companies in the market, like Apple, Microsoft, or Google.

These categories help investors assess the size and risk level associated with different companies when making investment decisions.
