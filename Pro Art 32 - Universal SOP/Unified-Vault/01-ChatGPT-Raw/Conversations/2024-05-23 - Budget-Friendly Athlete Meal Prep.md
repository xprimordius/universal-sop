Yes, as a sole proprietor, you can deduct business losses from your overall taxable income, which includes your W-2 income. This is possible because sole proprietorships report their income and expenses on Schedule C, which is part of the individual tax return (Form 1040).

### How It Works:

1. **Reporting Business Income and Expenses**:
   - As a sole proprietor, you will report your business income and expenses on **Schedule C (Profit or Loss from Business)**.
   - If your business expenses exceed your business income, you will have a business loss.

2. **Calculating Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)**:
   - The net profit or loss from Schedule C is transferred to **Form 1040, Schedule 1**, and then to **Form 1040**.
   - Your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) on Form 1040 will reflect your total income (including W-2 income) minus any allowable deductions, including the business loss from Schedule C.

3. **Offsetting W-2 Income**:
   - A business loss from Schedule C reduces your total income, which includes your W-2 wages. This reduction lowers your overall taxable income, potentially reducing the amount of tax you owe.

### Example:

Assume you have the following income and expenses:
- **W-2 Income**: $50,000
- **Business Income**: $5,000
- **Business Expenses**: $10,000

1. **Calculate Net Business Loss**:
   - Net Business Loss = Business Income - Business Expenses
   - Net Business Loss = $5,000 - $10,000 = -$5,000

2. **Calculate Adjusted Gross Income**:
   - Total Income = W-2 Income + Net Business Loss
   - Total Income = $50,000 + (-$5,000) = $45,000

Your AGI would be $45,000, which is lower than your W-2 income alone due to the business loss. This lower AGI can reduce your taxable income and potentially your tax liability.

### Important Considerations:

1. **Hobby Loss Rules**:
   - Ensure your business is considered a legitimate business and not a hobby. The IRS has specific rules to determine if an activity is a business or a hobby. Generally, the activity should be conducted with the intent to make a profit.

2. **Record Keeping**:
   - Maintain thorough records of all business income and expenses to substantiate your deductions in case of an audit.

3. **Other Tax Implications**:
   - Be aware of other tax implications, such as self-employment tax, which applies to net earnings from self-employment.

4. **Consult a Tax Professional**:
   - Given the complexities of tax law and to ensure compliance, it is highly recommended to consult a tax professional to accurately report income and expenses and to maximize allowable deductions.

By understanding these rules and correctly reporting your income and expenses, you can legally reduce your taxable income with business losses as a sole proprietor.
