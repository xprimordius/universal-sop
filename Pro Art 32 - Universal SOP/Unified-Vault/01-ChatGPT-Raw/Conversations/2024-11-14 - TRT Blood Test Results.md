### 1. **Hematocrit and Hemoglobin:**
   - **What It Means in TRT:** Testosterone can stimulate the production of red blood cells, which increases hemoglobin and hematocrit levels. Elevated levels can improve oxygen delivery to tissues, which may initially feel beneficial (increased energy, endurance).
   - **Health Implications:** High hematocrit (and, by association, hemoglobin) levels increase the blood's viscosity, meaning it becomes thicker. This can elevate the risk of cardiovascular issues like hypertension, blood clots, and stroke, especially if it rises above the normal range.
   - **Causes and Concerns:** Prolonged high hematocrit levels can lead to "polycythemia," a condition where there are too many red blood cells. Regular monitoring is essential on TRT, as significant increases in hematocrit might require dose adjustments or interventions like therapeutic phlebotomy (a procedure to remove blood and reduce hematocrit levels).

### 2. **Absolute Eosinophils and Basophils:**
   - These cells are types of white blood cells. Your levels are within the normal range, but higher levels are generally associated with allergic reactions, asthma, or parasitic infections.
