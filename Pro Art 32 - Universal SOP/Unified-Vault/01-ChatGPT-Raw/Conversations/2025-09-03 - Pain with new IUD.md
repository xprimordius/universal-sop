Pain with sex after a new IUD is common in the first weeks, but it’s important to know when it’s normal and when it’s a red flag.  

**Normal/Expected (first few weeks):**  
- Cramping or soreness during or after sex  
- Spotting or light bleeding  
- General pelvic discomfort while your uterus adjusts

- Sharp or severe pain during sex  
- Pain that gets worse instead of better  
- Partner feels the IUD strings poking uncomfortably

- Wait a few weeks before resuming sex if pain is significant

- Use lubrication if dryness adds discomfort  
- Over-the-counter pain relief (ibuprofen, naproxen) for cramping

- Pain that is constant or worsening.  
- Fever, chills, unusual discharge (possible infection).

- NSAIDs (ibuprofen/naproxen) before sex or during cramps.  
- Heat pad to pelvic area.  
- Rest + hydration.  
- Wait for your body to adjust (many women notice big improvement after 1–3 cycles).
