**Crispy Bacon in the Oven – Simple Method**

1. **Preheat** oven to **400°F (204°C)**.  
2. **Line a baking sheet** with foil (easy cleanup) and place a wire rack on top (optional, helps fat drip off).  
3. **Lay bacon strips flat** without overlapping.  
4. **Bake 15–20 minutes** until crispy (check at 12 min for thin cut, 20+ for thick cut).  
5. **Drain** on paper towels before serving.  

**Tips for Extra Crispiness**  
- Use a wire rack if you want evenly crisped bacon.  
- For less mess, bake directly on foil, then pour off rendered fat.  
- Let bacon rest 2–3 minutes; it crisps further as it cools.

Here’s a **quick-hit glossary** of common **pipefitter terminology** (straight to the point):  

### Pipe & Connections  
- **Pipe Schedule** – wall thickness rating (e.g., Sch 40, Sch 80).  
- **NPS (Nominal Pipe Size)** – standardized pipe diameter designation.  
- **Elbow (45°, 90°)** – angled fitting to change direction.  
- **Tee** – T-shaped fitting to branch flow.  
- **Reducer** – fitting to connect different diameters.  
- **Coupling** – short sleeve joining two pipes.  
- **Union** – fitting that allows easy disassembly.  
- **Flange** – flat rim with bolt holes for bolted joints.  
- **Cap/Plug** – closes off a pipe end.  

### Tools & Fabrication  
- **Torch Cut** – cutting pipe with oxy-acetylene or plasma.  
- **Threading** – cutting helical grooves for screw connections.  
- **Beveling** – angling pipe end for welding prep.  
- **Tack Weld** – small welds to hold pieces before final weld.  
- **Spool** – pre-fabricated section of pipe with fittings.  

### Measurements & Layout  
- **Takeoff** – length added/removed for fitting dimensions.  
- **Center-to-Face** – distance from fitting centerline to face.  
- **Offset** – angled run to shift around obstacles.  
- **Travel** – true length of pipe in an offset.  
- **Rolling Offset** – diagonal offset in two planes.  

### Worksite Terms  
- **Hot Tap** – tie-in to a live (pressurized) line.  
- **Isometric Drawing (ISO)** – 3D schematic of piping system.  
- **Blueprints (P&ID)** – piping and instrumentation diagrams.  
- **Fit-Up** – aligning and holding pipe in position for weld.  
- **Hydrotest** – pressurized water test for leaks/strength.

Got it — you’re asking about **pipe welding/fitting positions** (standard terminology used in certifications and on the job). Here’s the clean breakdown:  

### **Welding Pipe Positions**  
- **1G (Rolled Position):** Pipe is horizontal, rotates, welder stays flat.  
- **2G (Horizontal):** Pipe fixed vertical, weld performed horizontally.  
- **5G (Horizontal Fixed):** Pipe fixed horizontally, welder moves around pipe (can’t roll).  
- **6G (Inclined Fixed):** Pipe fixed at 45°, weld all around — toughest/common test position.  
- **6GR:** Same as 6G but with a **restriction ring** near the joint (hardest cert).  

### **Fitter’s Pipe Orientations**  
- **Flat:** Pipe laying flat for layout/fab.  
- **Vertical:** Pipe standing upright.  
- **Horizontal:** Pipe running level.  
- **Overhead:** Pipe above head height.  
- **Inclined:** Pipe set at an angle (like a 45° run).  

👉 Welders memorize the “G” positions for certification. Fitters usually reference **flat, vertical, horizontal, overhead, or rolling offsets** when positioning pipe.
