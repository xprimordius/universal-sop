That red icon on your Canon **R5 Mark II** means **“camera shake detected — stabilization warning.”**  

Here’s what’s happening and how to fix it:

---

### 🔍 Meaning
- The **red camera icon with wavy lines** appears when **the camera detects potential blur** from hand movement.  
- It’s a **stabilization warning**, not an error — but it can **prevent the camera from taking the shot** if:
  - You’re in a **self-timer, bulb, or long-exposure mode**.
  - **Lens IS (image stabilization)** or **IBIS** is disabled.
  - The **camera senses it’s unstable** while in **A+ (Scene Intelligent Auto)** mode.

---

### ⚙️ Fix Options
1. **Stabilize the Camera**
   - Use a **tripod** or rest the camera on a stable surface.
   - Half-press the shutter to let the camera stabilize before shooting.

2. **Enable Image Stabilization**
   - Menu → **Image Stabilizer** → turn **ON** (both body + lens IS if supported).

3. **Increase Shutter Speed**
   - Go faster than **1/60** (e.g., 1/125 or 1/250) to reduce shake sensitivity.

4. **Use a Higher ISO**
   - Raise ISO so the camera can use faster shutter speeds automatically.

5. **Switch Out of A+ Mode**
   - In **A+ mode**, the camera can block the shutter if it predicts motion blur.
   - Try **Av (Aperture Priority)**, **Tv (Shutter Priority)**, or **M (Manual)** instead.

---

### ✅ TL;DR
That red icon = **shake warning**.  
Fix: stabilize, turn on IS, or use a faster shutter / different mode.  
Once stable, the red icon will disappear and the shutter will fire normally.

Good question — here’s the **clean, practical breakdown** 👇  

---

### 🎥 DIGITAL IS (Electronic Image Stabilization)
**What it does:**  
Uses **sensor cropping + digital correction** to smooth out small shakes (especially for video).

---

### ✅ Pros — When to Turn It ON
- **Handheld shooting:** noticeable improvement in stability, especially if you’re walking or panning.  
- **Telephoto lenses:** helps counter micro-shakes.  
- **Video:** combines well with optical + IBIS for ultra-smooth results.

---

### ⚠️ Cons — Why You Might Leave It OFF
- **Slight crop:** usually **~1.1×–1.3×**, meaning your frame gets tighter.  
- **Slight quality hit:** since it uses digital correction, you lose a bit of edge detail.  
- **Not ideal for tripod or gimbal:** adds unnecessary processing and can create warping.  
- **Can conflict with lens/body IS:** in some cases causes jittery correction if all are active.

---

### 🧠 Optimal Setup
| Situation | Digital IS | Notes |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| **Handheld video** | ✅ ON | Smoothest look |
| **Walking vlog / moving** | ✅ ON | Stabilizes footsteps |
| **Tripod / static shot** | 🚫 OFF | Prevents artificial movement |
| **Photography** | 🚫 OFF | Uses optical/IBIS only (sharper) |

---

**TL;DR:**  
Turn **Digital IS ON** for handheld **video** or motion.  
Keep it **OFF** for **still photos**, tripod shots, or when you want max image quality and full field of view.
