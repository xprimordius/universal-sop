## 🤖 ChatGPT

Both **Axe Apollo** and **Axe Black** — like most body sprays and strong synthetic air fresheners — contain **fragrance chemicals, solvents, and propellants** that aren’t meant to be inhaled in concentrated amounts, especially in an enclosed space like a car.  

**Key points on toxicity:**  
- **Inhalation risk:** Continuous exposure in a small, sealed cabin can irritate lungs, eyes, and skin, especially for people with asthma or fragrance sensitivity.  
- **Ingredients:** They often contain synthetic musks, phthalates, and VOCs (volatile organic compounds), which in high or prolonged exposure may have hormonal or respiratory effects.  
- **Heat factor:** In a hot car, these chemicals can vaporize faster, increasing concentration in the air.  

**Bottom line:** Not acutely poisonous in casual use, but using them heavily in a small, enclosed, unventilated car is **not healthy long-term**. If used, spray minimally and ventilate after.

Yes — in a Tesla (or any modern EV) the issue can be **worse** because:

- **Sensitive HVAC Filters:** Teslas use HEPA or high-grade cabin filters — strong synthetic scents can cling to them, making removal difficult later.  

**Impact:** Not an immediate “poison” scenario, but prolonged use can mean higher exposure to VOCs and synthetic musks — potentially irritating to lungs, causing headaches, or triggering allergies.
