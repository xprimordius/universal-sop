### 1. **Buy Land from Land Banks**
   - **Find Land Banks:** Research land banks in your area. These are typically organizations that acquire and manage vacant or abandoned properties and resell them at affordable prices.
   - **Purchase Land:** Land banks often sell land for as little as $1,000. You can reach out to them to inquire about available lots for sale in your desired location.

### 2. **Set Up or Use Your LLC**
   - **Form an LLC (if you don’t have one):** If you don’t already have a Limited Liability Company (LLC), form one. This LLC will be used to apply for a business construction loan.
   - **Build Business Credit:** Ensure your LLC has established business credit by having a business bank account, a DUNS number, and some tradelines (business credit cards, vendor accounts, etc.).

### 3. **Qualify for a Construction Loan**
   - **Apply for a Construction Loan:** Once your LLC is set up and has credit, you can apply for a business construction loan through a bank or financial institution. You'll need to show that the LLC can repay the loan, so it's helpful to have revenue or other business assets.
   - **Construction Loan Benefits:** This loan is typically given in stages, where you receive funds as the construction progresses.

### 4. **Ask for a Home Builder’s Escrow Loan Option**
   - **Discuss with Builder:** When choosing a contractor or builder, ask if they offer a “Home Builder’s Escrow Loan” option. This means the builder works with the lender to allow you to pay only as the house is being built, reducing your upfront costs.
   - **No Documents Required:** According to the post, this option may require no documentation, which makes it easier and faster to get started.

### 5. **Build Your Home and Generate Equity**
   - **Build the House:** With the loan in place, proceed with the construction. Since you’re using business credit and not personal funds, you can preserve your personal credit while building a valuable asset.
   - **Equity Gain:** Once the home is built, the post claims you could have $100,000 in equity (value minus what you owe on the loan), depending on factors like the market and how efficiently the house is built.

By using business credit to build the home, you leverage your business’s credit profile instead of using personal financing. This allows you to grow your business assets and gain equity in the property without depleting personal resources.
