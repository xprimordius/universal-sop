## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer:**  
He’s talking about **early-stage founder discipline**, not a universal law.

**What he actually means (decoded):**

1. **Cash = oxygen**
   In the first 12–24 months, every dollar should buy **product, distribution, or survival**. Founder salary reduces runway.

2. **Signal alignment**
   Not paying yourself signals to investors, partners, and your team:  
   *“I’m all-in. I win only if this works.”*

3. **Force leverage**
   If you *must* live off the business early, you’ll optimize for comfort—not scale. Hunger forces better decisions.

4. **Avoid fake profitability**
   Many founders pay themselves early and think they’re “doing well,” while the business is actually fragile.

5. **Ownership > income**
   Billionaires think in **equity and asymmetry**, not paychecks.  
   Missing $100K–$200K in salary is irrelevant if you’re building something worth $10M–$1B.

**What this does *NOT* mean:**
- It’s **not** a moral rule  
- It’s **not** required if you have outside income or funding  
- It’s **not** smart if it creates stress, debt, or poor decisions

**The real principle (use this):**
> *Delay personal extraction until the business can pay you without flinching.*

**Practical version (elite founders actually do):**
- Year 0–1: $0 or bare-minimum survival  
- Year 1–2: modest, stress-free salary  
- Scale phase: market-rate or profit-based comp  
- Wealth phase: dividends, exits, equity liquidity

**Bottom line:**  
He’s teaching **founder restraint**, not self-sacrifice.  
If not paying yourself increases focus and speed → valid.  
If it weakens you → it’s the wrong move.
