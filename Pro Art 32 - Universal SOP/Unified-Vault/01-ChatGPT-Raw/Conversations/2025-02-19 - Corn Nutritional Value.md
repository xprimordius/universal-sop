## 🤖 ChatGPT

No, corn does **not** have zero nutritional value. While it's often criticized for being high in carbohydrates and lower in protein and fat, it still provides some key nutrients:

### **Nutritional Breakdown of Corn (per 100g)**
- **Calories**: ~96 kcal
- **Carbohydrates**: ~21g
- **Fiber**: ~2.4g
- **Protein**: ~3.4g
- **Fat**: ~1.5g
- **Vitamins & Minerals**:
  - **Vitamin C**: ~5mg (supports immune function)
  - **B Vitamins** (like B6, niacin, and folate)
  - **Magnesium**: ~37mg
  - **Potassium**: ~270mg

### **Key Benefits**
- **Fiber Content**: Supports digestion.
- **Antioxidants**: Contains **lutein and zeaxanthin**, which are beneficial for eye health.
- **Energy Source**: High in complex carbs, providing sustained energy.

However, **refined corn products** (e.g., high-fructose corn syrup, corn starch) have little to no nutritional value due to processing stripping away fiber and nutrients.

So while whole corn has **some** nutritional value, it's not the most nutrient-dense food compared to other whole grains, vegetables, or proteins.
