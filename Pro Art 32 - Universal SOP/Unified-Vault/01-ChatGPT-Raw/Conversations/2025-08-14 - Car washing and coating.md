## 🤖 ChatGPT

Even **brand-new cars** pick up contamination before you ever drive them. Here’s why **clay** and **iron removal** still matter:  

---

### **Iron Removal**
- **What it targets:** Tiny ferrous particles (rail dust, brake dust) that embed in clear coat during shipping by train/truck.
- **Why it matters:** These particles oxidize (rust) over time, causing pitting under the coating.
- **Why for new cars:** Cars often sit near rail lines, on open carriers, or in ports where airborne iron lands and bonds to the paint — invisible but present.

---

### **Clay Bar / Clay Mitt**
- **What it targets:** Non-metallic bonded contaminants — overspray, sap mist, industrial fallout, shipping wax residue.
- **Why it matters:** Even a thin contaminant layer can block graphene from bonding fully, reducing durability and slickness.
- **Why for new cars:** Factory paint and dealer prep aren’t done in sterile conditions. Cars are stored outdoors, handled by port/dealer staff, and often accumulate micro-grit you can’t see but can feel (paint won’t feel perfectly glassy).

---

💡 **Bottom line:**  
- **Iron remover** = dissolves microscopic rust-causing metal particles.  
- **Clay** = removes everything else bonded above the paint.  
Both give you a **chemically and physically clean surface**, which is critical for a 5–9 year coating to bond at full strength.  

---

If you skip these, the graphene will bond **to the contaminants instead of directly to your clear coat**, making it fail prematurely.
