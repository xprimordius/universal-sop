   - **St. Peter’s Square:** A grand plaza used for papal addresses and gatherings.

### 5. **Economy**
   - **Primary Revenue Sources:** Museum fees, publication sales, donations, and investments.
   - **Currency:** Euro (€).

### 6. **Security**

### 7. **UNESCO World Heritage**

The Vatican is home to some of the most extensive, secretive, and historically significant collections of documents, manuscripts, and texts related to Christianity, the Bible, and world history. Here's a detailed overview:

---

### **1. Vatican Apostolic Library**
The Vatican Apostolic Library, founded in 1451 by Pope Nicholas V, is one of the oldest libraries in the world, containing invaluable manuscripts, books, and historical records.

- **Notable Holdings:**
  - **Biblical Manuscripts:**
    - **Codex Vaticanus:** One of the oldest and most complete manuscripts of the Greek Bible, dating to the 4th century.
    - **Latin Vulgate Manuscripts:** Early versions of the Bible translated by St. Jerome in the 4th century.
  - **Early Christian Texts:** Writings by Church Fathers like Augustine, Jerome, and Origen.
  - **Theological Treatises:** Original works and commentaries on biblical theology.
  - **Illuminated Manuscripts:** Medieval texts with detailed artistic embellishments.

- **Languages Represented:** Greek, Latin, Hebrew, Aramaic, and others.

- **Access:** While some of the library is accessible to scholars, many areas and documents are restricted.

---

### **2. Vatican Secret Archives (Now Vatican Apostolic Archives)**
Often shrouded in mystery, the Secret Archives contain documents of immense historical and theological importance, not limited to Christianity but extending to global politics and history.

- **Notable Collections:**
  - **Papal Correspondence:** Letters and decrees from popes throughout history, including diplomatic communications with kings and emperors.
  - **Council Records:**
    - Documents from the **Council of Nicaea (325 CE)** and other councils that shaped Christian doctrine.
  - **Trial Transcripts:**
    - Galileo Galilei's trial documents.
    - Inquisition records, including heresy trials.
  - **Biblical Canon Formation:** Early records detailing how the Bible’s books were selected and canonized.
  - **Historical Agreements:**
    - Treaty of Tordesillas (1494), dividing the New World between Spain and Portugal.
    - Records of alliances and conflicts between the Church and European rulers.

- **Myth vs. Reality:** While popularly thought to house "forbidden" texts or apocryphal books, the archives are more focused on Church governance and history.

---

### **3. Lost and Apocryphal Texts**
The Vatican holds texts related to Christian and biblical traditions that are not part of the canonical Bible but are significant for understanding early Christianity and its development.

- **Apocryphal Gospels:**
  - **Gospel of Thomas:** A collection of sayings attributed to Jesus.
  - **Gospel of Mary Magdalene:** Exploring her role in early Christianity.
  - **Gospel of Judas:** Offering a controversial perspective on Judas Iscariot’s role.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls (Fragments):** Ancient Jewish texts discovered near Qumran, providing context for biblical times.
- **Nag Hammadi Texts:** Gnostic writings shedding light on alternative Christian beliefs.

---

### **4. Censorship and Forbidden Texts**
The Church has historically censored certain texts deemed heretical or dangerous:

- **Index Librorum Prohibitorum:** A list of prohibited books maintained from 1559 to 1966, banning works contrary to Catholic doctrine.
- **Gnostic Texts:** Suppressed writings often found in Nag Hammadi that challenge orthodox interpretations of Christianity.
- **Scientific Works:** Documents and books challenging Church doctrines (e.g., Galileo’s works).

---

- **Textual Criticism:**
  - Vatican scholars analyze ancient manuscripts to better understand the Bible’s origins and development.
- **Preservation Efforts:**
  - Advanced techniques to preserve fragile manuscripts for future generations.
