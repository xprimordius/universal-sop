## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes — it could be the inverter.  

Here’s the breakdown:  

**1. Pure vs. Modified Sine Wave**  
- Many cheaper “pure sine wave” inverters (like some Bestek models) actually output a *simulated* or *modified* sine wave.  
- Sensitive electronics like Starlink dishes, routers, and audio gear can pick up this distortion as buzzing or whining.  

**2. Ground Loop / Interference**  
- If your Starlink is buzzing when powered from the inverter but quiet on household AC, that points to noise from the inverter’s waveform or grounding.  
- Ground loops can also cause hum or buzz, especially when the system is connected to other electronics (laptops, speakers, etc.).  

**3. Fix Options**  
- **Upgrade inverter:** A *true* pure sine wave inverter (Victron, Renogy, Aims, etc.) usually eliminates the buzz. Bestek’s “pure sine” isn’t always clean.  
- **Add filtering:** A line conditioner, ferrite choke, or power filter between inverter and Starlink can reduce noise.  
- **Test on direct AC:** If the buzz disappears when plugged into a standard outlet, the inverter is the culprit.  

👉 Bottom line: If you’re hearing buzzing, your Bestek inverter likely isn’t delivering a clean sine wave. Starlink is sensitive — upgrading to a higher-quality true pure sine inverter is usually the fix.
