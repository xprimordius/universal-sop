## 🤖 ChatGPT

**"What Got You Here Won’t Get You There" – Marshall Goldsmith**  

**Core Premise:**  
The habits and behaviors that made you successful so far may be the very ones that hold you back from the next level. Growth requires unlearning old patterns as much as learning new ones.  

---

## **Key Takeaways**
**1. The Success Paradox**
- Past success creates blind spots.
- Skills that helped you rise may become liabilities in a new context.

**2. 20 Bad Workplace Habits (Derailers)**
- Not all are about incompetence—many are about interpersonal flaws.
- Examples:
  1. Winning too much  
  2. Adding too much value (always improving others’ ideas)  
  3. Passing judgment  
  4. Making destructive comments  
  5. Starting with “No,” “But,” or “However”  
  6. Telling the world how smart you are  
  7. Speaking when angry  
  8. Negativity  
  9. Withholding information  
  10. Failing to give proper recognition  
  11. Claiming credit you don’t deserve  
  12. Making excuses  
  13. Clinging to the past  
  14. Playing favorites  
  15. Refusing to express regret  
  16. Not listening  
  17. Failing to express gratitude  
  18. Punishing the messenger  
  19. Passing the buck  
  20. An excessive need to be “me” (rigid self-identity)

**3. Feedback is Fuel**
- Seek honest feedback from colleagues, even if uncomfortable.
- Focus on perception change, not just behavior change.

**4. Feedforward, Not Feedback**
- Instead of critiquing the past, ask for suggestions for future behavior.
- This makes people more willing to help and collaborate.

**5. The Power of Apology & Thank You**
- Sincere apologies and gratitude repair relationships and build trust.
- People forgive faster than you expect if you’re genuine.

**6. Follow-Up is Non-Negotiable**
- Change is noticed when it’s visible and consistent.
- Regular check-ins with stakeholders reinforce new behaviors.

**7. Behavior Change = Reputation Change**
- People judge you based on history; you must over-communicate progress.
- Patience: perception lags behind actual change.

---

## **Action Framework**
1. **Identify** — Pinpoint specific behaviors holding you back.
2. **Confess** — Openly acknowledge them to your team/peers.
3. **Ask** — Request suggestions for improvement (feedforward).
4. **Act** — Implement changes visibly and consistently.
5. **Follow Up** — Check in regularly on progress.
6. **Measure** — Let others evaluate your improvement.
7. **Sustain** — Make new habits part of your identity.

---

**Bottom Line:**  
What got you “here” (technical skill, ambition, drive) may not get you “there” (influence, leadership, legacy). To move up, master the softer skills—humility, listening, gratitude, and letting others win.

An **American Express “companion” benefit** usually means you can bring an extra traveler on a flight (or sometimes a cruise/hotel stay) **for free or for reduced cost** when you buy your own ticket through Amex’s program.  

- **How it works (airline example):**
  - You buy a full-fare ticket through the Amex Travel portal with a qualifying Amex card (often Platinum or Business Platinum).
  - You get a **second ticket for your “companion”** on the same flight itinerary without paying the base fare — you typically just pay taxes and fees for them.
  - Often called the **Companion Certificate** or **Companion Ticket**.

- **Fine print:**
  - Usually only works on certain airlines or classes (often business/first).
  - Must book round-trip, same cabin, same dates.
  - The primary traveler and companion must fly together the whole trip.
