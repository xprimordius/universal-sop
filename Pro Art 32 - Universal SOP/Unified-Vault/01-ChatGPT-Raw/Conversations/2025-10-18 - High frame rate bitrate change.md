why does enabling high frame rate mode lower 300 to 100 mbps

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Excellent question — this confuses a lot of Canon R5 / R5 II users.  
Here’s the breakdown:

---

### 🔍 What’s Actually Happening

- **Before enabling “High Frame Rate” (HFR)**:  
  You’re set to record **4K 120 fps (119.9 fps)** in **normal (real-time)** mode at **300 Mbps**.  
  → This means full-rate capture + audio + normal playback.  

- **After enabling “High Frame Rate” mode**:  
  The camera automatically switches to **slow-motion playback mode** — it **conforms the 120 fps footage to 30 fps** (or 24/25 fps depending on settings), **removes audio**, and **drops bitrate to 100 Mbps** because:
  - It’s no longer recording full-speed video with audio.
  - Frames are encoded as a pre-slowed, lightweight file.
  - Canon’s codec prioritizes efficiency since there’s no need to preserve sync audio or full-speed motion data.

---

### ⚙️ In Short

| Mode | Bitrate | Audio | Output Type | Purpose |
|------|----------|--------|--------------|----------|
| **HFR Disabled** | 300 Mbps | ✅ Recorded | Full-speed 120 fps (for post-slow-mo) | Max quality, full data |
| **HFR Enabled** | 100 Mbps | ❌ Not recorded | Pre-slowed 30 fps playback | Quick slow-mo, smaller files |

---

### 💡 Takeaway

- **Disable HFR** if you want **maximum quality (300 Mbps)** and to slow down footage later in post.  
- **Enable HFR** only if you want **ready-to-play slow motion** straight out of camera.

---

**Summary:**  
> Enabling High Frame Rate doesn’t increase bitrate because it’s no longer capturing full-rate motion data with audio — it’s baking in slow-motion playback, so Canon drops the data rate to save space and processing load.
