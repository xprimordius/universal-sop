## 🤖 ChatGPT

Under **Minnesota Statutes § 169.79(7)**:  
> “License plates must be **securely fastened to the vehicle** to which they are assigned **in a horizontal position at a height of not less than 12 inches from the ground**, measured from the bottom of the plate, and **must be displayed on the front and rear of the vehicle**.”

### Interpretation  
- The plate must be **securely attached to the exterior front** — not placed on the dashboard or behind the windshield.  
- A plate on the dash **does not meet** the requirement because it’s not “securely fastened” nor “displayed on the front” of the vehicle.  
- Police in Minnesota often ticket dash-mounted plates under § 169.79 for **improper display**.

### Only exceptions  
- **Specialty vehicles** (e.g., certain collector, pioneer, or street-rod vehicles) that are issued **one plate** are exempt from front display.  
- Otherwise, standard passenger vehicles must have **both** plates mounted externally.  

✅ **Conclusion:**  
No, you cannot legally keep your front plate on the dash in Minnesota — it must be firmly attached to the exterior front of the vehicle.

## 👤 You

Likelihood of external HDD corrupting from a life on the road

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**High-Moderate risk**, depending on conditions. Here’s the breakdown:  

### 🔧 **Physical Vibration + Shock**
- **Most risk factor.** HDDs have moving parts (spinning platters + read heads).  
- Constant vibration, bumps, or sudden motion while driving can cause **head crashes** or **platter damage**.  
- Even if powered off, repetitive jostling shortens lifespan.  

### 🌡️ **Temperature & Humidity Fluctuations**
- Hot car interiors (>100°F) and freezing nights (<32°F) expand/contract internal parts → **alignment drift** and **bearing wear**.  
- Condensation during rapid temp swings can **corrode** contacts or platters.  

### ⚡️ **Power & Data Risks**
- Unstable power (car inverters, battery packs) or mid-transfer disconnects = **file system corruption**.  
- Use surge protection and eject properly every time.  

### 🧳 **Mitigation Strategy**
| Threat | Protection |
|--------|-------------|
| Shock/Vibration | Keep in padded SSD-style shock case; avoid mounting on metal car surfaces |
| Heat/Cold | Store in insulated case; never leave in direct sun |
| Power | Use a UPS-rated power station or quality inverter; always eject before disconnecting |
| Drive type | Prefer **SSD (solid-state)** — zero moving parts, road-safe |

### ⚠️ **Verdict**
External **HDD = poor choice for full-time road life**. Expect degradation or failure within **6–18 months** of heavy travel.  
Switch to **NVMe or SATA SSD** in a rugged enclosure — **10× shock resistance, no moving parts, nearly zero corruption risk**.
