### Ovarian Cyst Removal (Cystectomy / Oophorectomy)

**Ease of Procedure:**  
- Common surgery; usually straightforward.  
- Can be done laparoscopically (minimally invasive, faster recovery) or via open surgery if cyst is large/complex.  

**Risks:**  
- General surgical risks: bleeding, infection, injury to nearby organs (bowel, bladder, ureter).  
- Ovarian risks: loss of ovarian tissue, reduced fertility if both ovaries affected/removed.  
- Recurrence: new cysts can form even after removal.  
- Rare but possible: reaction to anesthesia, blood clots.  

**Recovery Experience:**  
- Laparoscopic: 1–2 weeks back to light activity; 2–4 weeks full recovery.  
- Open surgery: 4–6 weeks recovery.  
- Pain and bloating common for several days post-op.  
- Most patients resume normal life without long-term issues.  

**Percentages (approximate):**  
- **Success rate of removal:** ~95–99%.  
- **Complication rate:** 1–2% for laparoscopy, slightly higher for open surgery.  
- **Recurrence rate:** ~10–15% (depends on cyst type, age, hormones).  
- **Fertility preservation:** High if only the cyst is removed and ovarian tissue is spared.  

👉 Bottom line: For most women, ovarian cyst removal is safe, effective, and low-risk, especially laparoscopically. Major complications are rare.
