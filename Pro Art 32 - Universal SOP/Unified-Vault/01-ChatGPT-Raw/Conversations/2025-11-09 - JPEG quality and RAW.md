## 👤 You

if I reduce my canon r5 mark ii JPG quality from 10 to 8, does it reduce my raw quality?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

No — reducing the **JPEG quality from 10 to 8** on your Canon R5 Mark II **does not affect RAW quality at all**.  

RAW and JPEG are saved as **separate files**:
- **RAW**: contains full, uncompressed sensor data — completely unaffected by JPEG settings (quality, color profile, etc.).  
- **JPEG**: uses compression; lowering from 10→8 just applies more compression, reducing file size and slightly image detail in the JPEG preview.

✅ **TL;DR:** RAW = untouched. JPEG = smaller/lower quality only.

Here’s the breakdown:  

### 📸 JPEG Quality 10 vs 8 (Canon R5 Mark II)
| Setting | Compression | Typical File Size | Visible Detail | Use Case |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|----------------|-----------|
| **10 (Fine)** | Minimal compression | ~12–20 MB per image | Virtually lossless — preserves fine textures and gradients | Professional delivery, prints, editing |
| **8 (Normal)** | Moderate compression | ~6–10 MB per image | Slight softening and minor artifacts visible under zoom or heavy editing | Everyday shooting, previews, web use |

### 🧠 Key Takeaways
- Expect **30–50% smaller files** at quality 8.  
- Visual difference is **barely noticeable** unless you pixel-peep or post-process aggressively.  
- RAW remains 45 MB + unchanged.  

**Recommendation:**  
If you shoot RAW + JPEG and only use JPEGs for quick previews or social uploads, quality 8 is totally fine. Stick to 10 only if you rely on the JPEGs for professional delivery or print.
