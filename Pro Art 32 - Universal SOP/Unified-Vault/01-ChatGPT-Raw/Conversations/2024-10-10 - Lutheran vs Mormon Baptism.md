## 🤖 ChatGPT

Lutheran and Mormon (Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints) baptisms differ significantly in their theology and practice. Here’s a comparison of the two:

### **Lutheran Baptism:**
- **Purpose:** Baptism is viewed as a sacrament through which God grants grace, forgives sins, and incorporates the individual into the body of Christ (the church). It is considered necessary for salvation.
- **Infant Baptism:** Lutherans practice infant baptism, believing that God's grace is given regardless of the individual’s understanding, and infants should be included in God’s covenant.
- **Mode:** Baptism is typically performed by sprinkling or pouring water on the individual’s head.
- **Trinitarian Formula:** Lutherans baptize "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit," as commanded by Jesus in the New Testament (Matthew 28:19).
- **Effect:** Baptism is believed to bring regeneration and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit.

### **Mormon Baptism:**
- **Purpose:** Mormons see baptism as a requirement for salvation and a covenant with God. It symbolizes repentance, the cleansing of sin, and entry into the church, but does not of itself bestow grace. Mormons believe that it must be performed by someone with proper authority (priesthood authority).
- **No Infant Baptism:** The LDS Church does not practice infant baptism. Instead, they baptize children at the "age of accountability" (usually age 8), when they are believed to be old enough to understand and make a choice to follow Christ.
- **Mode:** Baptism is performed by full immersion, symbolizing the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.
- **Trinitarian Distinction:** Mormons baptize "in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost" but have a different understanding of the Godhead compared to traditional Christian views. They see the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as distinct beings rather than one essence in three persons.
- **Effect:** Baptism is an essential step toward salvation, but it is followed by receiving the Holy Ghost through the laying on of hands and continuing obedience to God's commandments.

In summary, while both faiths consider baptism essential for salvation, Lutherans emphasize grace and the sacramental nature, whereas Mormons view it as a covenant and an ordinance requiring authority and personal responsibility.
