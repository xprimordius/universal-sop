> CFPB Complaint: TransUnion Willful Noncompliance with 
> FCRA 605B Identity Theft Blocking Requirements 
> Subject: Repeated Refusal by TransUnion to Block Unauthorized Inquiries Under FCRA 605B 
> Despite Receiving All Legally Required Documentation 
> 
>  
> Summary of Issue: 
> TransUnion has willfully and repeatedly violated FCRA § 605B by refusing to block 
> unauthorized inquiries from my credit report, despite having received all federally required 
> documentation. Their repeated denials contradict the plain language of the law, constitute 
> reckless disregard, and result in ongoing consumer harm. 
> 
>  
> Key Facts and Timeline: 
> June 5th and June 11th, 2025: I submitted all necessary documentation required by FCRA § 
> 605B to TransUnion, including: 
> 
> ● Valid FTC Identity Theft Report 
>  
> 
> ● Notarized Affidavit declaring the inquiries were unauthorized 
>  
> 
> ● Written Statement fully detailing the identity theft 
>  
> 
> ● Government-issued ID and supporting verification 
>  
> 
> TransUnion acknowledged receipt and validity of these documents multiple times, yet 
> continues to deny the blocking of inquiries. 
> Instead, they claim they are "unable to remove inquiries this way" and only offer to "notify 
> the creditors," which does not fulfill their legal obligation under § 605B to block the information 
> within 4 business days. 
> I have directly spoken with four different supervisors over the past 11 days, all of whom have 
> refused to comply, citing undisclosed “internal policies” that contradict federal law and are 
> not disclosed upon request. 
> 
>  
> Violations Committed: 
> 
> ● FCRA § 605B(a) – Failure to block disputed information within 4 business days upon 
> receipt of valid documents. 
>  
> 
> ● FCRA § 605B(e) – Willful refusal to block information after receiving all required 
> documentation. 
>  
> 
> ● 15 U.S. Code § 1681n – Civil liability for willful noncompliance; includes actual 
> damages, punitive damages, and attorneys’ fees. 
>  
> 
> ● 15 U.S. Code § 1681o – Civil liability for negligent noncompliance. 
>  
> 
> 
> 
> ● FTC Act § 5 – Deceptive Trade Practices – Misleading statements that internal policy 
> overrides federal law (violates UDAP). 
>  
> 
> ● FCRA § 611(a)(6)(B)(ii) – Failure to provide the method of investigation and reasoning 
> when a block or dispute is denied. 
>  
> 
> TransUnion’s refusal to explain the basis of their denials upon repeated direct 
> requests — while citing non-statutory "internal policies" or "privacy rules" — 
> constitutes an independent FCRA violation. There is no legal authority allowing a 
> credit bureau to override FCRA § 605B compliance based on internal procedure. 
> Their obstruction impairs transparency, violates due process under the FCRA, and 
> amplifies consumer harm. 
> 
>  
> Quantified Violations & Damages: 
> 
> ● Minimum 5–10 violations per supervisor, across at least 4 documented interactions 
>  = 20–40 discrete violations of § 605B and related provisions 
>  
> 
> ● Under FCRA § 1681n: 
>  
> 
> ○ $1,000 per willful violation = $20,000–$40,000+ 
>  
> 
> ○ Plus punitive damages, emotional distress, and legal costs 
>  
> 
>  
> Requested Relief from CFPB: 
> 
> 1. Immediate enforcement of FCRA § 605B – Require TransUnion to block and 
> permanently remove all unauthorized inquiries, retroactive to June 5, 2025. 
>  
> 
> 2. Written confirmation of deletion – TransUnion must issue a signed, dated letter 
> verifying: 
>  
> 
> ○ That the inquiries were removed under FCRA § 605B. 
>  
> 
> ○ The date of removal and legal justification. 
>  
> 
> ○ Confirmation that this letter may be used for submission to TransUnion’s Special 
> Handling Team or legal authorities. 
>  
> 
> 3. Explanation for prior denials – CFPB should compel TransUnion to: 
>  
> 
> ○ Disclose the exact reason and method used to deny the 605B block, per FCRA § 
> 611(a)(6). 
>  
> 
> 
> 
> ○ Cease citing any undisclosed “internal policies” or “privacy procedures” not 
> authorized by law. 
>  
> 
> 4. Internal retraining and policy correction – Require TransUnion to: 
>  
> 
> ○ Retrain all dispute agents and supervisors on proper § 605B compliance. 
>  
> 
> ○ Provide confirmation of procedural updates that eliminate the unlawful use of 
> “internal policy” to override federal statute. 
>  
> 
> 5. Referral to FTC and Attorney General – For pattern of willful noncompliance and 
> deceptive internal practices. 
>  
> 
> 6. Consideration of civil penalties – Under FCRA § 621 and applicable consumer 
> protection laws. 
>  
> 
>

>  
> 
> ● Call logs and supervisor names 
>  
> 
> ● Evidence of repeated willful noncompliance 
>  
> 
>  
> Final Statement: 
> This is a clear-cut case of willful and reckless noncompliance with FCRA § 605B and related 
> provisions. TransUnion continues to deny federally protected consumer rights using evasive 
> tactics, undisclosed internal policies, and legally unsupported privacy claims. Their actions 
> represent a documented pattern of obstruction. 
> I am requesting immediate CFPB enforcement, corrective oversight, and formal accountability 
> to ensure full compliance and prevent ongoing harm to myself and others facing similar abuse.
