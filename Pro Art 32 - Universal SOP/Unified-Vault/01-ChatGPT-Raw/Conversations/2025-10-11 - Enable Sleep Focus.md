## 🤖 ChatGPT

Apple Health (or Apple Watch) didn’t record sleep **stages** even though it logged **time asleep (2 hr 38 min)** — this happens for one of these exact reasons:

---

### 🔹 1. **Wrist Detection or Sensor Lost Contact**
If your Apple Watch was **loose**, **not worn**, or **lost skin contact** during part of the night, it can’t read heart rate or movement accurately — which are required for stage tracking (REM, Core, Deep).

**Fix:**  
Tighten strap slightly and ensure the green heart-rate sensor lights stay in full contact overnight.

---

### 🔹 2. **Watch Not in “Sleep Focus” Mode**
Sleep stages only record when Sleep Focus and Sleep Schedule are enabled.  
If you manually started sleep or just fell asleep without enabling Focus, you’ll see time asleep but no stages.

**Fix:**  
Go to **Health App › Browse › Sleep › Full Schedule & Options** → turn on **“Use Schedule for Sleep Focus.”**

---

### 🔹 3. **Heart Rate or Motion Data Missing**
Stages require heart-rate variability and accelerometer data.  
If your **watch battery was low**, **sensor turned off**, or **Fitness Tracking disabled**, Apple Health can’t compute stages.

**Fix:**  
Check: **Settings › Privacy › Motion & Fitness › Fitness Tracking → ON**  
and **Settings › Health › Heart Rate → ON**.

---

### 🔹 4. **Data Source Conflict**
If you use multiple sleep apps (e.g., Pillow, AutoSleep, or Whoop), Apple Health may log total sleep but not stages.

**Fix:**  
In **Health › Sources › Data Access & Devices**, ensure **Apple Watch** is the primary data source for Sleep.

---

### 🔹 5. **Watch Model / iOS Bug**
Older watches (Series 2–3) don’t support stage tracking.  
If yours does (Series 4+), sometimes an iOS/watchOS update bug causes one-off missing nights.

**Fix:**  
Restart both devices and install the latest **watchOS 10+ / iOS 17+** update.
