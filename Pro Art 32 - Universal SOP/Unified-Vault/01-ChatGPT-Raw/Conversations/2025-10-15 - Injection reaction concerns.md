Here’s the concise breakdown — from a medical standpoint (general info only, not medical advice):  

**1. Muscle Twitching / Pulsing**
- That’s usually a **needle hitting or grazing a motor nerve branch**. It causes involuntary muscle contraction or twitching.  
- It’s not dangerous if brief, but **you should stop, withdraw slightly, and reposition** if it happens again.

**2. Blood During Injection**
- **Small bleeding near the needle** can occur if you nicked a capillary or small vein as you moved or injected slowly.  
- Since you aspirated and no blood came *into* the syringe, you likely didn’t inject directly into a vein.

**3. Post-Injection Soreness**
- Common from:  
  - Injecting too shallow/deep.  
  - Injecting too slowly or too fast.  
  - Slight internal bleeding or local irritation.  
- Should resolve in **24–72 hrs**. Use light massage, gentle stretching, or a warm compress after a few hours.

**4. Tendon Concern**
- Unlikely — tendons aren’t typically in the **outer mid-thigh (vastus lateralis)** zone if you injected correctly.  
- You’d know if you hit one — the pain would be sharp, immediate, and you’d have difficulty moving the leg.

**5. When to Worry**
Seek prompt medical care if:
- Pain increases instead of decreases after 2–3 days.  
- Swelling, redness, heat, or lump grows.  
- You develop fever, numbness, or persistent bleeding.
