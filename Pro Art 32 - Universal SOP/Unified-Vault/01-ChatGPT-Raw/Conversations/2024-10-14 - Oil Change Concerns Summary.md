# Oil Change Concerns Summary

---

## 👤 You

How many miles do full synthetic oil changes last and how to tell by feel if you need a change? I’m 8500 miles deep, and keep getting weird almost burning smells. But i can’t tell if that’s me or the highway and other vehicles. It’s incredible inconsistent and it concerns me. There is no smells when stationary

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

1. **Mileage for Full Synthetic Oil Changes:**  
   - Most full synthetic oils are rated for **7,500 to 10,000 miles**, though some high-performance brands can last up to **15,000 miles**. However, factors like driving conditions (extreme heat/cold, stop-and-go traffic, or heavy loads) can shorten that interval.

2. **How to Check if Your Oil Needs Changing by Feel:**  
   - **Dipstick Check:** 
     - Pull out the dipstick, wipe it, reinsert, and check the oil level.  
     - Look for **dark, gritty, or dirty oil**; fresh oil will appear lighter and transparent.  
     - If it smells burnt, that’s a sign the oil has degraded.  
   
   - **Oil Texture:**  
     - Rub a bit of oil between your fingers. If it feels **thin or watery** (like it’s lost viscosity) or you feel **grit/metallic particles**, it’s time for a change.

3. **Regarding the Burning Smell:**  
   - If the smell is inconsistent and only appears when driving, it could indicate:
     1. **Oil Leak:** Oil might be dripping onto hot engine parts (manifold, exhaust), burning off, and causing the smell. Look for any oil spots beneath your car or inspect for leaks around the engine.
     2. **Transmission or Gear Oil Leaks:** Check the transmission or gear fluid levels, as leaks there can create a similar odor.
     3. **Brake Pads Overheating:** If the smell appears after braking hard or going downhill, it could be your brakes.
     4. **Other Vehicles:** It's possible the smell comes from nearby vehicles if it’s inconsistent.

4. **Diagnostics:**  
   - **Look under the hood:** Check for any signs of oil leaks around the valve cover gasket, oil filter, or pan.
   - **Inspect the exhaust:** Check if oil residue is on the exhaust manifold, indicating a leak.
   - **Monitor temperature gauge:** Ensure your engine isn’t running hot, as this could exacerbate oil breakdown and cause smells.

Since you're at **8,500 miles**, it's worth changing the oil now, especially given the smell concerns. Even if it's just the highway, it’s better to rule out potential engine issues.
