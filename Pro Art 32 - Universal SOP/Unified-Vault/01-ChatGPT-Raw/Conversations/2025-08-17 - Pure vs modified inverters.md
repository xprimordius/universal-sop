## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Pure sine wave vs other inverters (modified/ square):**

### 1. **Pure Sine Wave Inverter**
- **Output:** Smooth, clean waveform (identical to grid power).  
- **Best for:** All electronics, especially sensitive devices (laptops, medical equipment, audio gear, variable-speed tools).  
- **Pros:**  
  - Safe for all devices.  
  - No electrical noise in audio/video equipment.  
  - Higher efficiency and lifespan for appliances.  
- **Cons:**  
  - More expensive.  

### 2. **Modified Sine Wave Inverter**
- **Output:** Stepped/simplified waveform (approximation of sine wave).  
- **Best for:** Simple devices (lights, fans, older TVs, some tools).  
- **Pros:**  
  - Cheaper.  
  - Works with most basic electronics.  
- **Cons:**  
  - Can cause buzzing, flickering, or overheating in some appliances.  
  - Not ideal for sensitive electronics.  
  - May reduce efficiency and lifespan of devices.  

### 3. **Square Wave Inverter** (rare today)
- **Output:** Harsh on/off square signal.  
- **Best for:** Very basic resistive loads (old incandescent bulbs, simple heaters).  
- **Cons:**  
  - Incompatible with most modern electronics.  
  - Inefficient and obsolete.  

---

**Bottom line:**  
- Use **pure sine wave** if powering sensitive electronics, medical gear, or anything expensive you don’t want damaged.  
- Use **modified sine wave** only for basic, non-sensitive loads where cost is the main factor.
