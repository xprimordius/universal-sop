## Core Idea (Plain English)

**“Material conduct”** means **important behavior or actions that actually matter to the transaction or decision being made.**  
Not minor details — **actions that meaningfully affect what happens.**

So under **entity["law","15 U.S.C. § 45","Federal Trade Commission Act unfair or deceptive acts"]**, subsection **(4)(A)(ii)** means:

> If **important parts of the deceptive or unfair behavior happen inside the United States**, the FTC can enforce the law — **even if the business or victims are outside the U.S.**

# Simple Breakdown

### 1. What “Material” Means
From **Black’s Law Dictionary definition** you provided:

Material =  
- **Important**
- **Substantive**
- **Influences the outcome**
- **Relevant to the real dispute**

Plain meaning:

**Something that actually affects the decision or result.**

Examples of **material things**:

- Advertising claims that influence purchase decisions  
- Pricing representations  
- Key promises about a product or service  
- Contract terms  
- Sales scripts or guarantees  

Things that are **NOT material**:

- Minor typos  
- Cosmetic details  
- Irrelevant statements  
- Things that do not affect consumer decisions

---

### 2. What “Conduct” Means
Conduct = **behavior or actions.**

Includes:

- What someone **does**
- What someone **says**
- What someone **fails to disclose**
- How a company **operates or sells**

---

# Combined Meaning: “Material Conduct”

**Material conduct = important actions or behavior that influence the transaction or outcome.**

In consumer protection law this means:

> Actions that **actually influence or shape the consumer decision or the transaction itself.**

---

# How It Applies to §45(a)(4)(A)(ii)

The law says the FTC can enforce against unfair or deceptive practices involving foreign commerce if:

> **“[they] involve material conduct occurring within the United States.”**  
> — entity["law","15 U.S.C. § 45","Federal Trade Commission Act unfair or deceptive acts"]

Plain English meaning:

Even if the company is overseas, the FTC still has authority if **important parts of the deceptive scheme happened in the U.S.**

---

# Practical Example

### Example 1 — Covered by the Law

Foreign company sells a program.

But the **material conduct happens in the U.S.:**

- Sales team located in the U.S.
- Ads created in the U.S.
- Payment processing in the U.S.
- Marketing promises made from the U.S.

Those actions are **material conduct in the U.S.**

Therefore the FTC can enforce the law.

---

### Example 2 — Not Covered

Foreign company:

- Advertises abroad
- Operates abroad
- No meaningful actions occur in the U.S.

Then **(ii)** might not apply.

---

# Another Way to Think About It

The law asks:

**Did important parts of the deception happen in the U.S.?**

If yes → FTC jurisdiction applies.

Important parts include:

- Marketing
- Sales representations
- Payment processing
- Fulfillment decisions
- Corporate control

---

# Simple Test

Ask three questions:

1. **Did the behavior influence the consumer decision?**  
2. **Was that behavior important to the transaction?**  
3. **Did it occur in the United States?**

If **yes**, it qualifies as **material conduct occurring in the United States**.
