## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer:**  
Because **fraud = trust broken**, and banks are legally required to **contain risk across the entire customer profile**, not just one account.

**Why banks close *all* accounts when one is flagged for fraud ($1,500 or any amount):**

1. **Customer-level risk (not account-level)**  
   Fraud attaches to *you*, not the single account. Once flagged, every linked account is considered exposed.

2. **Regulatory obligations (KYC / AML / BSA)**  
   Banks must prevent money laundering and repeat fraud. Leaving other accounts open = compliance violation.

3. **Internal risk policy (zero-tolerance triggers)**  
   Most banks have automatic thresholds where *any confirmed fraud* → full relationship exit.

4. **Shared credentials & access risk**  
   If one account was compromised, others could be next (same login, devices, behavior patterns).

5. **Loss prevention economics**  
   $1,500 is irrelevant. The *pattern* matters more than the dollar amount.

**Key clarification:**  
- This is **not punishment**.  
- It’s **risk containment + legal compliance**.

**What matters most for outcomes:**
- Was it **confirmed fraud** vs **suspected fraud**?
- Was it **customer-initiated** (reported promptly) or **bank-detected**?
- Any **prior flags** on the profile?
