Excellent question — this distinction is critical.  

Here’s the breakdown:  

---

## 🚗 If Your Tesla Gets Totaled

### 1. **Insurance Pays Actual Cash Value (ACV)**  
They pay what the car is **worth today**, not what you owe.  
That’s based on fair market value — similar to **trade-in / retail comps**, minus deductible.

So if:
- ACV = **$40,600**
- Loan balance = **$68,000 – payments made (~$65K owed)**  
Then you’d owe the difference — about **$24–28K out of pocket.**

---

## 🧾 Unless You Have GAP Coverage

**GAP insurance** (Guaranteed Asset Protection) pays the *difference* between:  
👉 the **insurance payout (ACV)** and  
👉 your **loan balance.**

✅ If you have GAP:
- Insurance pays $40,600  
- GAP pays the $24–28K difference  
- You owe **$0**

❌ If you don’t have GAP:
- You’re responsible for the $24–28K remaining.

---

## 💡 What To Do Right Now

1. **Check your Tesla Finance or lender contract** — some Tesla or credit union loans **include GAP by default**, but not always.  
2. **Call your insurer** — verify if you added GAP or “loan/lease payoff” coverage.  
3. If you don’t have it, **add GAP immediately** — it’s cheap ($5–10/month) and critical for new EVs with fast depreciation.
