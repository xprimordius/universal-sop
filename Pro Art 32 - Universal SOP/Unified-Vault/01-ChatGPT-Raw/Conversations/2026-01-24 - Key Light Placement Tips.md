## 1️⃣ What **Negative Fill** Actually Is
**Negative fill = removing light**, not adding it.

- It’s usually **black foam board, fabric, or a flag**
- Placed **close to one side of your face**
- It **absorbs bounced light** from your key and monitors

### Why it matters
Rooms naturally bounce light everywhere.  
Negative fill *kills that bounce* → **stronger contrast, sharper jawline, cinematic depth**.

> Cinematic lighting isn’t brighter.  
> It’s **controlled darkness**.

---

## 2️⃣ Where to Place Negative Fill (Your Setup)
- **Side:** Camera-right (the shadow side of your face)
- **Distance:** 1–2 feet off your face, just out of frame
- **Height:** From chest to temple (doesn’t need to be perfect)
- **Angle:** Parallel to your face

**DIY:** black hoodie, black poster board, matte black towel = works.

---

## 3️⃣ What Negative Fill Is *Not*
- ❌ Not a light  
- ❌ Not fill light  
- ❌ Not something you “see” on camera  

You *feel* it in the contrast.

---

## 4️⃣ Rim Light (Hair Light) — Correct Placement

### Yes, it is:
- **Behind you**
- **Facing toward you**
- **Aimed at hair + shoulder**, not your face

### Ideal placement
- **Behind + camera-right**
- **Above head level**
- **Angled down ~30–45°**

**Power:** low (10–25%)  
**Goal:** thin edge of light outlining you

---

## 5️⃣ Can Rim Light Be on the Ground?
⚠️ **Technically yes, but not ideal**

### Ground rim light problems:
- Creates **uplighting** (unnatural, horror-ish)
- Misses hair → hits jaw/neck instead
- Can cast weird shadows upward

### When it works:
- If elevated at least **waist height**
- Tilted upward gently
- Kept **very dim**

**Best practice:**  
> Rim light wants to mimic **sun or overhead practicals**, not floor lamps.

---

## 6️⃣ Fast “Right vs Wrong” Snapshot

**Correct look**
- Shadowed cheek stays rich
- Hair edge glows
- Face feels sculpted

**Wrong look**
- Face evenly bright everywhere
- No depth
- Background swallows you
