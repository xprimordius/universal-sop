## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer:** *It depends — but yes, it **can** be shifted/recovered in many hit-and-run cases if handled correctly.*

### Here’s the clean breakdown 👇

## 1. **MRI + PT billed to insurance**
- If you use **health insurance**, they usually pay **now**.
- Later, they may assert **subrogation** (they want reimbursement) **if money is recovered**.
- This is normal and **managed by your attorney**.

## 2. **Hit-and-run + attorney**
- You typically pursue recovery through:
  - **UM/UIM (Uninsured Motorist)** on *your own auto policy* (most common)
  - Or later from the at-fault driver *if found*

## 3. **Can medical bills be “shifted”?**
Yes — **in 3 main ways**:

### A. **Paid by UM/UIM settlement**
- Medical bills (MRI, PT) are included as **damages**
- Attorney negotiates **reductions** with providers/insurers
- You do **not** usually repay dollar-for-dollar

### B. **Subrogation reduction**
- Health insurers rarely recover 100%
- Attorney reduces liens using:
  - *Common fund doctrine*
  - *Made-whole doctrine* (state-specific)
- You keep more of the settlement

### C. **Med-Pay (if you have it)**
- Pays MRI/PT **without subrogation** (often)
- Extremely favorable if available

## 4. **What NOT to do**
- ❌ Don’t pay out of pocket unnecessarily  
- ❌ Don’t talk settlement numbers with insurance alone  
- ❌ Don’t assume bills are “locked” just because insurance paid  

## 5. **Key reality**
- This is **standard personal-injury mechanics**
- Good PI attorneys **expect** insurance-paid treatment
- The system is built to reconcile it later

**Yes — MRI and PT billed to insurance can be recovered/offset/shifted through a hit-and-run claim**, especially via UM/UIM. The *quality of the attorney* determines how much actually sticks to you.
