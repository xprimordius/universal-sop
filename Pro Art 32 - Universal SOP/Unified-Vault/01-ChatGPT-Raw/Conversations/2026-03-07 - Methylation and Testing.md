## Short Answer  
You **already methylate** — every human does.  
The real question is **whether your methylation cycle is inefficient**.

You determine that through **specific biomarkers or genetics**, not by guessing or by symptoms alone.

---

# 1. The Most Reliable Way: Blood Biomarkers
These directly show how well methylation is functioning.

### Key Markers

| Biomarker | What It Indicates |
|---|---|
| **Homocysteine** | The most important methylation marker |
| **Vitamin B12** | Required cofactor for methylation |
| **Folate (B9)** | Converts homocysteine → methionine |
| **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)** | Detects functional B12 deficiency |
| **SAM / SAH ratio** | Direct measurement of methylation capacity (advanced test) |

### Ideal Ranges (functional medicine ranges)

| Marker | Optimal Range |
|---|---|
| Homocysteine | **6–8 µmol/L** |
| B12 | **500–900 pg/mL** |
| MMA | **Low / within lab reference** |

Source:  
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/

---

# 2. Genetic Testing (Optional but Common)
This identifies **predisposition**, not current function.

### Key Gene

**MTHFR gene variants**
- C677T
- A1298C

These variants reduce conversion of folate → **5-MTHF**, the methylated form.

Source:  
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK6561/

Important:

Having an **MTHFR mutation does NOT mean you have a problem**.  
Many people with the mutation have normal methylation.
