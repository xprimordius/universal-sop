Google Chrome on iOS does not provide an option to enable or disable hardware acceleration directly because the app relies on iOS's WebKit engine, which is optimized for hardware acceleration by default. However, if you want to address performance or display issues potentially related to hardware acceleration, you can try the following alternatives:

### 1. **Clear Cache and Cookies**
   - Open **Chrome** on your iPhone.
   - Tap the **three-dot menu** in the bottom-right corner.
   - Go to **Settings > Privacy and Security > Clear Browsing Data**.
   - Select **Cookies, Site Data, and Cached Images and Files**, then tap **Clear Browsing Data**.

### 2. **Reset Experimental Flags**
   - Enter `chrome://flags` in the Chrome address bar.
   - Tap **Reset All to Default** to ensure no experimental settings are affecting performance.

### 3. **Check for Updates**
   - Ensure your Chrome app is updated to the latest version.
   - Go to the **App Store**, search for Chrome, and update if available.

### 4. **Use Safari for Testing**
   - If you suspect hardware acceleration issues, test the same site in Safari. This can help determine if the issue is browser-specific.

If these steps do not resolve your issue, consider reaching out to Google Chrome support or Apple support for more specific assistance.
