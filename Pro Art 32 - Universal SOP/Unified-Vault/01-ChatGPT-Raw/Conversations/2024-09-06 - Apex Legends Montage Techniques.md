# Apex Legends Montage Techniques

---

## 👤 You

How are people making apex legends montages where they have the same clip playing not only from the first person perspective, but from a flying camera perspective like theatre mode, witnessing them and the enemy

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

To achieve those Apex Legends montages with both first-person and third-person "flying camera" perspectives, creators generally use a combination of advanced techniques:

1. **Multiple Replays or Recordings:**
   - The main method is that players record the first-person perspective of the gameplay while also having a friend or second account spectate the match. The spectator captures the flying camera angles, allowing for the switch between first-person and third-person perspectives in post-production.

2. **Custom Lobbies:**
   - In some cases, players organize custom lobbies where they can stage fights or key moments. A friend or secondary account spectates the match and captures cinematic camera angles while the player records their first-person view.

3. **Advanced Editing:**
   - Once both perspectives are recorded (first-person gameplay and third-person via spectate), editors use video editing software like Adobe Premiere or Final Cut Pro to synchronize both recordings. This allows seamless transitions between the two perspectives during the same action sequence.

4. **Mods or Hacks:**
   - Though less common, some players may use unauthorized mods or hacks to achieve a true theatre mode in Apex Legends. However, this is against the game's terms of service and could result in bans.

In summary, the combination of a secondary spectator recording the match and precise video editing allows creators to achieve those cinematic montages with multiple perspectives.

## 👤 You

How can it be done if it is not staged?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

If the montage isn't staged, meaning the action is happening in a live, spontaneous game, achieving the dual perspective effect with first-person and "flying camera" views is more complex but still possible through these methods:

1. **Use of Spectators After Death:**
   - One common approach is to have a teammate spectate you after they are eliminated. When they die, they can continue to spectate the match in real-time. By recording from their spectator view, you can still capture your gameplay from a third-person perspective. Later, you sync their spectator footage with your first-person view in post-editing.

2. **Post-Game Replays (If Available in Tournaments or Events):**
   - In some high-level tournaments or custom events, servers often capture replays from multiple angles for broadcasts. If you have access to this type of footage, you could download it and splice in those third-person or cinematic angles along with your recorded first-person gameplay. However, Apex Legends doesn't have a built-in theater mode for regular players.

3. **Leveraging Game Capture Cards with Multiple Perspectives:**
   - Some high-level streamers or content creators may use multiple PCs or capture setups to record different perspectives at the same time. For instance, they might run the game on one PC while streaming or recording from a second PC that spectates their game. This allows for simultaneous capturing of both perspectives in real-time.

4. **Creative Editing Techniques:**
   - Sometimes, editors simulate a "flying camera" perspective by zooming out or creating dynamic transitions using effects and clever cuts. For instance, they might freeze a frame of your first-person gameplay, cut to a pre-recorded or staged fly-by shot, and then return to the live action. This gives the illusion of a free-floating camera without actually needing a spectator.

In short, if it's not staged, the most practical and common method involves leveraging a spectator's perspective after they've been eliminated and syncing that with your first-person gameplay using advanced editing techniques.
