## 🤖 ChatGPT

TL;DR — Cost, Use Case, Diminishing Returns.

### Reasons Monitors Aren't 460 PPI:

| Factor | Why Monitors Don't Have 460 PPI |
|--------|---------------------------------|
| Cost | Insanely expensive to manufacture at large sizes (27"-57"). Mobile-sized 460 PPI panels are cheaper at small scale (phones). |
| Use Case | You view monitors 2-3x farther than phones. You don't need 460 PPI for sharpness at that distance. |
| Diminishing Returns | Human eye can't distinguish pixels >160-220 PPI at typical monitor viewing distance (~24-36 inches). Anything beyond is wasted clarity for most users. |
| GPU Performance | Higher PPI = higher resolution = heavier GPU load = lower FPS. Not practical for gaming or work. |
| Power Consumption | Ultra-high PPI panels draw more power (brightness + resolution scaling). Bad for large monitors. |

---

### What Exists Today:
| PPI Range | Typical Use Case |
|-----------|------------------|
| 90-110 PPI | 1080p-1440p at 24"-32" — Standard monitors. |
| 140-160 PPI | 4K at 27"-32" — Premium sharpness. Ideal for pros. |
| 218-460 PPI | Phones & Tablets — Needed for close viewing. |

---

### Final Note:
If you sat 12 inches from a 27" monitor — you'd want 400+ PPI.  
But monitors are designed for human ergonomics — not phone-distance clarity.

> Monitors prioritize:  
1. Size  
2. Speed (refresh rate)  
3. Brightness  
4. Power efficiency  
5. Reasonable clarity (not overkill)
