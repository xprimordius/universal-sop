Why do I have to pay the consequence of losing discounts with insurance companies because of a claim that was 100% someone else’s failtb

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Because insurance operates on **risk modeling**, not **fault morality**. Here’s the breakdown:  

### 🔹 1. **Risk vs. Responsibility**
Even if you’re **not at fault**, filing a claim means you were *involved* in an incident — statistically, insurers see that as an indicator of *higher future risk*. They price policies based on probability, not fairness.

### 🔹 2. **Claim Frequency Penalty**
Each claim (even not-at-fault) can flag you as a “higher-activity” policyholder. Too many incidents = perceived instability → loss of claim-free or safe-driver discounts.

### 🔹 3. **Underwriting Data Sharing**
Insurers share claim history via databases like **CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange)**. Any report under your name (regardless of fault) can influence rates or discounts.

### 🔹 4. **Policy-Specific Triggers**
Some discounts (e.g., accident-free, loyalty, preferred risk tiers) are **binary** — *any* claim resets them, because the discount is a *reward for no claims*, not just no fault.

### 🔹 5. **What You Can Do**
- **Ask for a “not-at-fault” exception:** Some states or insurers legally can’t raise rates if fault is 0%.  
- **File under the other driver’s insurance** directly to avoid a mark on your record.  
- **Dispute the surcharge:** Provide police reports, claim documentation proving 0% liability.
