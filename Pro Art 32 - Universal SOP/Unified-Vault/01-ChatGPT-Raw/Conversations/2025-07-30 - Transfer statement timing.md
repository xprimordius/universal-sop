## 👤 You

if I do a transfer today, but it's not gonna clear until the 4th, and my statement closes on the last day of the month; will the statement show the incoming deposit for the month or no? affecting total monthly deposits?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

No, it won’t show.  

**Key:** Banks/credit card statements are based on *cleared* transactions. If the transfer is still pending at the statement close (end of the month), it will **not** appear on that month’s statement or count toward monthly deposits. It will post in the month it actually clears (the 4th in your example).

---

## 👤 You

why

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Because statements only capture **posted (cleared) transactions**, not pending ones.  

- **Pending = Not final:** Until a transfer fully clears the banking system, the bank doesn’t legally have the funds.  
- **Statement cutoff = snapshot:** At the close date, the bank generates a snapshot of all finalized transactions. Pending items don’t count because amounts could still change (fail, reverse, or settle differently).  
- **Impact:** Only once the transfer is fully settled (cleared) will it be reflected in deposits and balances, which in your case is after the statement date.

---

## 👤 You

are you sure? is it possible?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

100% sure. It is **not possible** for a pending transfer to show on a statement.  

**Why?**  
- Statements are legally required to reflect only *posted (final)* transactions.  
- Pending items can fail, reverse, or change amounts.  
- Banks lock the statement data at the exact cutoff. If it hasn’t posted by then, it’s excluded and will appear on the next statement.  

**No exceptions:** Even if the deposit is visible in your “available balance,” it will not show on the statement until it clears.

---

## 👤 You

but my friend had a card paid with ISF that bounced after but showed up as paid on the statement even though it was pending

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

That’s a different situation:  

1. **Card payments (ISF)** – Banks often *provisionally* post payments immediately (showing as cleared) to restore available credit.  
2. **If it later bounces** – They reverse it after the fact, but because it was provisionally posted at the statement cutoff, it shows on the statement and then a reversal appears later.  

**Key difference:**  
- With transfers/deposits, pending items do **not** get provisionally posted.  
- With card payments, banks *may* front the credit because they control the account and risk.  

So your friend’s example was a **provisional posting + later reversal**, not a true pending item. Regular incoming deposits that are still pending at cutoff **cannot** show up.
