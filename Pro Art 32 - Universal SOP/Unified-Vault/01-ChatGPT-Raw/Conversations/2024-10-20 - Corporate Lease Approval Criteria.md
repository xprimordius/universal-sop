## 👤 You

Criteria to get approved for a corporate lease as a new LLC

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

To get approved for a corporate lease as a new LLC, especially with no established business credit history, you’ll need to meet certain key criteria. Here’s a breakdown of what landlords or leasing companies typically look for:

### 1. **Personal Guarantor**
   - **Personal Credit**: As a new LLC without substantial business credit, you'll likely need a personal guarantor. A strong personal credit score (usually 680+) is critical. The guarantor will be responsible if the business defaults on the lease.
   - **Income Verification**: You may need to provide proof of personal income to show your ability to cover the lease if the business cannot.

### 2. **Security Deposit and Prepayments**
   - **Larger Deposit**: Landlords may ask for a larger security deposit (typically 3–6 months' rent) if your LLC is new and has no established financials.
   - **Advance Rent**: Some may require several months of rent upfront, usually 3–12 months depending on the lease amount and location.

### 3. **Business Financials**
   - **Bank Statements**: Even if your LLC is new, you may need to show several months' worth of bank statements, demonstrating the financial stability of your business or personal accounts.
   - **Profit & Loss Statements**: If available, these documents provide insight into the revenue and expenses of your LLC.

### 4. **Business Plan**
   - Present a solid business plan that highlights your strategy, projected revenues, and expected growth. This can build confidence in your ability to fulfill the lease obligations.

### 5. **References**
   - **Professional and Landlord References**: If available, provide references from previous landlords or vendors to demonstrate reliability and payment history.

### 6. **Business Credit Building**
   - **EIN & DUNS Number**: Ensure your LLC is properly established with an Employer Identification Number (EIN) and a DUNS number to start building business credit.
   - **Trade Lines**: Establishing trade lines or vendor accounts can provide some initial business creditworthiness, which may help during the application process.

### 7. **Type of Lease**
   - Consider negotiating a **shorter lease term** (e.g., 1-year instead of 5-year) to lower the perceived risk for the landlord.

By following these strategies, you increase the chances of getting approved for a corporate lease as a new LLC.

What about for an apartment

## 🤖 ChatGPT

For an apartment lease under a new LLC, the approval process is more stringent compared to individual leasing. Here’s what you need to consider:

### 1. **Personal Guarantor**
   - **Personal Credit Check**: Since your LLC is new and has no established credit history, landlords will likely require a personal guarantor. A guarantor with a strong personal credit score (usually 680+) is essential.
   - **Income Verification**: The guarantor might need to provide proof of sufficient personal income to ensure they can cover the lease if the LLC defaults.

### 2. **LLC Documentation**
   - **Formation Documents**: You’ll need to provide proof that your LLC is properly registered (Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement).
   - **EIN**: Include your LLC’s Employer Identification Number (EIN) to show it is a legally formed entity.

### 3. **Business Financials**
   - **Bank Statements**: Even for a new LLC, landlords may request recent bank statements (typically 3–6 months) to verify financial stability.
   - **Balance Sheet and Profit/Loss Statements**: If your LLC has already generated revenue, these documents will help establish credibility.

### 4. **Lease Terms and Conditions**
   - **Security Deposit**: Expect to pay a larger security deposit if your LLC lacks a strong credit history—this could be 3 to 6 months of rent upfront.
   - **Advance Rent Payments**: Some landlords may request several months of rent in advance to mitigate the risk of leasing to a new business entity.

### 5. **Rental History and References**
   - **Previous Landlord References**: If the LLC has leased property before, providing references from previous landlords can help prove reliability.
   - **Professional References**: Including vendor or business partner references may also help build credibility.

### 6. **Personal Lease Guarantee**
   - In many cases, landlords will still require you to personally sign as a lease guarantor, even if the lease is in your LLC's name, especially for newer entities with no credit.

### 7. **Business Credit**
   - **DUNS Number**: If your LLC has established a DUNS number, provide it to show you’re working on building business credit.
   - **Business Credit Cards/Accounts**: If possible, offer any trade references or business credit activity to further show that your LLC is creditworthy.

### 8. **Rental Insurance**
   - Some landlords might also require **commercial rental insurance** for leases under LLCs, as an added layer of security.

By offering a strong personal guarantor, relevant LLC documents, and possibly prepayments, you'll improve your chances of getting an apartment lease approved under your LLC.

Is it possible to get approved with no docs? And how

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

It is highly unlikely to get approved for an apartment lease under an LLC with **no documentation**, as landlords typically need to verify the legal standing and financial viability of the LLC or the personal guarantor. However, there are a few strategies that could increase the chances of approval with minimal documentation:

### 1. **Pay a Large Upfront Deposit**
   - Offering **6-12 months of rent upfront** may convince a landlord to overlook traditional documentation, as the financial risk is reduced. Some landlords are willing to bypass verification if they receive a significant lump sum.

### 2. **Use a Co-Signer or Guarantor**
   - A **co-signer or guarantor** with a strong financial and credit background could help. They would assume responsibility for the lease if the LLC defaults, reducing the landlord's risk and potentially allowing them to forgo reviewing LLC documents.

### 3. **Offer a Short-Term Lease**
   - Propose a **short-term lease (e.g., 3-6 months)**. Landlords may be more flexible with documentation requirements if the lease term is short, as the risk is limited.

### 4. **High-Rent or Premium Properties**
   - Some landlords of high-end or luxury apartment properties may be willing to accept a lease with minimal documentation if they believe they are dealing with high-net-worth individuals. In this case, offering a large deposit or paying several months upfront will be even more effective.

### 5. **Private Landlords**
   - **Individual or small property owners** may be more flexible than large property management companies. They might waive documentation requirements in exchange for upfront payment, a strong guarantor, or a good impression during negotiations.

### 6. **Strong Negotiation**
   - If you have good personal connections, a referral from a trusted tenant, or simply negotiate well, you may convince the landlord to lease without heavy documentation.

### 7. **Use a Rental Broker**
   - A well-connected **rental broker** with established relationships can sometimes persuade landlords to be more lenient in terms of required documents.

In most cases, providing some documentation, such as a personal guarantor or upfront payments, will still be necessary, even if you bypass traditional verification methods.
