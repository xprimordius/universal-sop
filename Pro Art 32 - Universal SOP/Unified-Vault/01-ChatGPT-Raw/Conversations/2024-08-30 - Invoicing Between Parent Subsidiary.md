# Invoicing Between Parent Subsidiary

---

## 👤 You

Can you create invoices to a parent C Corp and its subsidiary DBA LLC underneath it? Back and forth between the accounts legally?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, you can create invoices between a parent C Corporation (C Corp) and its subsidiary DBA (Doing Business As) LLC, but there are some important legal and accounting considerations to ensure compliance and avoid issues, especially in the context of intercompany transactions.

### Key Points for Creating Invoices Between C Corp and DBA LLC:

1. **Legitimate Business Purpose:**
   - Ensure that each transaction between the C Corp and the DBA LLC has a legitimate business purpose. Invoices should reflect actual services rendered, goods sold, or other legitimate business activities.

2. **Fair Market Value:**
   - The invoiced amounts should reflect fair market value. Charging too much or too little could raise red flags with tax authorities, potentially leading to audits or claims of tax avoidance.

3. **Separate Accounts:**
   - Maintain separate bank accounts and accounting records for the C Corp and the DBA LLC. Even though the DBA is not a separate legal entity, it must be treated as such for accounting purposes to clearly delineate transactions.

4. **Consistent Invoicing and Payments:**
   - Invoices should be created, sent, and paid in a consistent and timely manner. This consistency helps to demonstrate that the transactions are regular business operations.

5. **Documentation:**
   - Keep thorough records of all invoices, payments, and the business purpose behind each transaction. This documentation is crucial in case of an audit or legal inquiry.

6. **Transfer Pricing Considerations:**
   - For larger operations, ensure compliance with transfer pricing rules. These rules require that transactions between related entities be priced as if they were between unrelated parties. This can require additional documentation and analysis.

7. **Avoid Circular Transactions:**
   - Avoid creating circular transactions where money simply flows back and forth without a legitimate business purpose, as this could be viewed as a scheme to manipulate financial statements or avoid taxes.

8. **Tax Implications:**
   - Be aware of the tax implications of intercompany transactions. These could affect the taxable income of both the C Corp and the DBA LLC. Consulting with a tax professional is advisable to ensure compliance with IRS regulations.
