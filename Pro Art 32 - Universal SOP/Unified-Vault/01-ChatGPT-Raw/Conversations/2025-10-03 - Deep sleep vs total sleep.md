How does the watch know what stage of sleep I’m in for how long

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Great question. Your Apple Watch (and similar trackers) **doesn’t actually “see” your brainwaves** like a medical sleep lab (polysomnography). Instead, it **infers sleep stages** from body signals using sensors + algorithms.  

### How It Works
1. **Heart Rate Variability (HRV):**  
   - During deep sleep, HRV patterns change — your parasympathetic system dominates (rest & repair).  
   - REM has more irregular HR/HRV, closer to waking state.  

2. **Motion (Accelerometer/Gyroscope):**  
   - Very little movement = deep sleep.  
   - Twitches/rolling = lighter/core stages.  

3. **Blood Oxygen (Pulse Oximetry):**  
   - Drops or fluctuations can signal sleep apnea events or arousals that break deep cycles.  

4. **Skin Temperature / Wrist Temp (newer watches):**  
   - Core body temp drops in deep sleep, rises in REM.  

5. **Algorithms & Machine Learning:**  
   - Apple trains models on large datasets comparing watch data with medical sleep lab results.  
   - The watch then “predicts” when you’re in **awake, REM, core, or deep**.  

### Accuracy
- **Pretty good for total sleep time** and **awake detection**.  
- **Less accurate for precise REM vs Deep** (estimates, not exact).  
- **Polysomnography (EEG)** is the only gold standard, but watches give a useful approximation for trends.

---

⚡So, your watch knows by **analyzing HRV + movement + temp + blood O2**, then applying algorithms to guess the stage.
