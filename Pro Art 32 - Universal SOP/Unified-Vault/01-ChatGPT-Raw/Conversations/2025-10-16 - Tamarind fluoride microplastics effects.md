Here’s the breakdown from the **major studies** that drove the “fluoride–IQ” findings:

---

### 🧠 1. **Bashash et al., 2017 (Mexico City; ELEMENT cohort)**
- **Duration:** **Prenatal and early-childhood exposure** — mothers’ urinary fluoride measured during pregnancy; children tested at **ages 4 and 6–12**.  
- **Finding:** Each 1 mg/L increase in maternal urinary fluoride ≈ **5–6 IQ-point reduction** in the child.  
- **Context:** Natural fluoride levels (0.2–1.5 mg/L).  
- **Type:** **Prospective cohort** — among the strongest designs available.

---

### 🧠 2. **Green et al., 2019 (Canada; MIREC study)**
- **Duration:** **Prenatal exposure** — maternal urinary fluoride measured once per trimester; children tested at **3–4 years old**.  
- **Finding:** Boys showed ≈ **4.5 IQ-point lower** scores per 1 mg/L maternal fluoride; girls no significant change.  
- **Exposure range:** Similar to U.S. fluoridated levels (0.3–0.9 mg/L).  
- **Type:** Prospective.

---

### 🧠 3. **Till et al., 2020 (Canada; CNFluoridation study)**
- **Duration:** **Postnatal childhood exposure** — fluoride in tap water and urine; children aged **6–12 years**.  
- **Finding:** About **1.5–2 IQ-point lower** per 1 mg/L increase.  
- **Exposure:** Lifetime residence in fluoridated vs. non-fluoridated areas.
