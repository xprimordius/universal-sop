## 👤 You

Why isn’t the $10k loan not considered a loss? Since it was an essential cost for the business

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Elite question — here’s the precise answer:

---

### IRS Rule:  
Loans ≠ Expenses.  

#### Why?  
Because borrowed money is *not* income nor an expense — it’s a liability (debt).  

> *Spending* the loan = Expense.  
> *Borrowing* the loan = Not an expense.

---

### Your Case:

| What Happened | IRS Treatment | Where It Goes |
|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| Borrowed $10K | Liability | Schedule L — Line 16 (Loans from Shareholder) |
| Spent $1K on setup (website, legal, etc.) | Expense | Page 1 — Line 26 (Other Deductions) |
| Remaining $9K Loan unused | Still liability | Remains on Balance Sheet until spent or paid |

---

### Example:

If you borrowed $10K → spent $10K fully on *deductible* startup costs → Then yes — you’d show a $10K loss.

But right now:  
- You spent ~$1K (deductible).  
- $9K is still "cash available" (even if in your pocket or unused).  

IRS cares about *what was spent* — not the loan amount.

---

### Summary:

| Action | Tax Treatment |
|--------|---------------|
| Borrowed Funds | Not a loss. Liability only. |
| Money Spent on Setup | Deductible loss. |
| Money Unspent | Remains on Balance Sheet (Assets or Loan Payable). |

---
