No — not as new debt.

- It cannot legally reappear as collectible.

  - Included in Bankruptcy
  - $0 balance

If it reappears as collectible → that’s a violation.

### What “Reaffirming” Means

When someone files **bankruptcy (usually Chapter 7)**, most debts are **discharged** — meaning they are no longer personally liable.

A **reaffirmation agreement** is:

> A voluntary legal contract signed during bankruptcy that says:  
> “I agree this specific debt will NOT be discharged. I remain personally responsible.”

---

## In Mortgage Context

If she reaffirms the mortgage:

- The debt survives bankruptcy  
- She is personally liable again  
- Chase can legally pursue her if she later defaults  
- The mortgage typically continues reporting normally  

If she does **NOT** reaffirm:

- The personal liability is discharged  
- She can still keep the house if she keeps paying  
- If she later defaults, Chase can take the house (foreclosure)  
- But cannot pursue her for deficiency balance (in most cases)

---

## Why This Matters for Credit Reporting

### Reaffirmed:
- Tradeline stays active  
- Reports as open  
- Can show “paid as agreed”  
- No “included in bankruptcy” remark  

### Not Reaffirmed:
- Usually marked “Included in Bankruptcy”  
- Balance goes to $0  
- Reporting may stop updating monthly  

---

## Important

Reaffirmation must:
- Be signed before discharge  
- Be filed with the bankruptcy court  
- Sometimes approved by a judge  

You cannot reaffirm after discharge.

---

## Strategic Note

You keep the house either way if you stay current.  
Reaffirming just restores personal liability.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

No. Freezing secondary bureaus does **not** stop a bankruptcy from showing up.

Here’s how it works:

### 1️⃣ Bankruptcy reporting source
Bankruptcies are public records filed in federal court (PACER system).

Primary credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion):
- Pull directly from court records.
- Freezes do **not** block public record reporting.

---

### 2️⃣ Secondary bureaus (LexisNexis, SageStream, etc.)

Freezing them:
- Stops lenders from pulling their background data.
- Does **not** erase or prevent bankruptcy from being recorded in their system.
- Does **not** prevent the primary bureaus from reporting it.

They may still maintain a record internally.

---

### 3️⃣ What freezing actually does

It prevents:
- Soft background risk pulls
- Insurance underwriting pulls
- Some bank pre-screening systems

It does **not**:
- Stop court data reporting
- Remove BK
- Block mortgage reporting
- Change tradeline status

---

### Bottom Line

Freezing secondary bureaus does **not** stop the bankruptcy from appearing anywhere.

If your goal is:
- Minimizing underwriting visibility  
- Controlling background data exposure  
- Or long-term credit strategy  

That’s a different tactical discussion.
