**Dharma** is a complex concept with roots in Indian philosophy, particularly Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. While it can overlap with the idea of a "life purpose," its meaning is broader and depends on the context. Here's a breakdown:

### 1. **Hinduism:**
   - **Core Meaning:** Dharma refers to the cosmic law and order that governs the universe, as well as the moral duties and obligations each individual must follow to maintain harmony.
   - **Personal Dharma:** It includes one's responsibilities based on their stage of life (ashrama), social role (varna), and personal nature (svabhava). This can align with one's **life purpose** but emphasizes living in alignment with universal principles.

### 2. **Buddhism:**
   - **Core Meaning:** Dharma refers to the teachings of the Buddha and the universal truths about existence. Following the Dharma involves practices that lead to enlightenment, such as cultivating wisdom, ethical conduct, and mindfulness.
   - **Life Purpose Connection:** In Buddhism, fulfilling one’s purpose often means progressing on the path to liberation, which is broader than an individual’s specific goals.

### 3. **Jainism:**
   - **Core Meaning:** Dharma involves ethical living, non-violence (ahimsa), and self-discipline as part of the journey toward liberation of the soul.

### 4. **Modern Interpretation:**
   - In contemporary contexts, people often interpret dharma as one’s **"life purpose" or "calling"**—the unique role or path that aligns with their abilities, passions, and the greater good. While not incorrect, this simplifies the deeper, multi-dimensional nature of the term.

### **Key Distinctions:**
- **Dharma** is more universal and duty-oriented. It emphasizes living in harmony with the world and fulfilling responsibilities to oneself, others, and the cosmos.
- **Life Purpose** is often more individualistic and focuses on personal fulfillment and goals.

In summary, **dharma** can include your life purpose, but it also encompasses broader duties, ethics, and living in alignment with universal principles. It’s both a personal and cosmic guide to living a meaningful life.
