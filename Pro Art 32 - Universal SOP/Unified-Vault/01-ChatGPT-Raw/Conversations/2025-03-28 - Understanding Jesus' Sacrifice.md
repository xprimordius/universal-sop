## 🤖 ChatGPT

You're not alone in feeling that—it *doesn’t* make intuitive sense at first glance. Here's the core idea, stripped down and clarified:

---

### **Core Concept: “He died for our sins”**

In Christian belief, humans are seen as inherently flawed (sinners). Sin separates humans from God. Justice requires that sin be paid for—like a debt or a crime demanding consequence.

- **Jesus’ death** is presented as a substitution:  
  Instead of each person paying the "debt" for their own sin (which = death/separation), Jesus voluntarily takes the punishment on himself.

- **Why Jesus?**  
  He’s seen as sinless (perfect), so his death could count as a *universal* sacrifice. Like a spotless “lamb” (a reference to ancient Jewish sacrifice customs).

- **“For our sins”** = *on our behalf*.  
  Not that we asked, but that he did it as an act of divine love—absorbing the consequence so that we could be reconciled to God without paying it ourselves.

---

### **Analogy (imperfect, but helps):**  
Imagine you’re in court, guilty, with a $10M fine you can’t pay. A stranger steps in and pays it for you. Justice is satisfied (fine paid), but *you* walk free.

---

### **Why people struggle with it:**

- It’s **not transactional** in a normal sense.
- It’s **not logical** in the way we usually understand justice (why should an innocent person suffer?).
- It’s **spiritual logic** rooted in ancient Hebrew sacrificial law + divine love + atonement concepts.
