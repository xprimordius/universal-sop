## Why the charge rate drops

- Battery charging is **non-linear**: EVs charge fastest at low SOC, then taper as SOC rises.  
- The car’s BMS (battery management system) protects battery health — it throttles current as SOC increases, temperature limits, and internal resistance rise.  
- The charger / station itself might have limits (power sharing, cable temperature, voltage limits).  
- Thermal limitations: if battery is too hot or too cold, current will be restricted.  
- Infrastructure or grid supply limitations.

So hitting ~100 kW at 55% is typical on many EVs / chargers. (See typical Tesla Y charging curves showing drop past ~30–40% SOC. citeturn0search12)

---

## Strategies to get >100 kW at 55%

You may not *always* override the tapering, but you can shift the curve to get higher rates at higher SOC. Here’s how:

| Tactic | What to do | Why it helps / caveats |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-heat battery | Drive or use “preconditioning for Supercharger” so battery is in optimal temperature window before charging | Thermal constraints often limit current |
| Use a higher-power charger / Supercharger V3 or V4 | Seek out a station with higher peak output and less shared load | Gives more headroom on the supply side |
| Avoid charger stalls that are shared | If power sharing is in effect, individual power may get limited | Use solo / unshared stalls when possible |
| Charge earlier (lower SOC) then “top up” | Start charging at maybe 10–20% instead of waiting until 50-60% | You’ll be in the flatter portion of the charging curve longer |
| Firmware / software optimizations | Tesla may release updates that allow more aggressive charging curves within safety margins | You can’t push it manually beyond BMS limits |
| Minimize voltage drop / resistance | Keep cables cool, ensure connections are solid — avoid “weak link” resistive losses | Circuit or charger cable losses eat into current |

---

## Constraints you can’t easily override

- You can’t force the BMS to charge harder beyond its built safety curves without risking battery damage or warranty void.  
- The charger infrastructure may be the limiting factor.  
- The SOC curve taper is a physical necessity — as the cell voltage moves higher, less difference remains, so current must taper.  
- Thermal and internal resistance effects are inevitable.

---

## Conclusion & Implementation

- Always **precondition your battery** before charging to raise it to optimal temp.  
- Use **highest power charger possible** (V3 / V4).  
- Don’t wait until 50–60% to plug in if you want to maintain high current.  
- Be strategic with charger stalls to avoid sharing.  
- Keep your car updated and monitor Tesla updates for improved charge curves.
