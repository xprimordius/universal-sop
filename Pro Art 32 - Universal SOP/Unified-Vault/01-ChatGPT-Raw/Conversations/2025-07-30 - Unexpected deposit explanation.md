## 🤖 ChatGPT

### Possible Explanations for the $3K Deposit  

1. **Cashback Bonus or Rewards Redemption**  
   - Unlikely unless she had enough rewards points (~300,000 points = $3,000).  
   - Usually requires manual redemption, and Discover doesn’t auto-deposit such large amounts.  

2. **Refund/Credit Balance**  
   - If she overpaid the card in the past or a large charge was reversed, Discover could issue a refund.  
   - But you mentioned the card is maxed, making this less likely unless there was a past chargeback or merchant refund.  

3. **Mistaken or Misapplied Deposit**  
   - Banks occasionally misroute ACH deposits. Discover could have deposited funds intended for someone else.  
   - This is the most likely scenario if there’s no known trigger.  

4. **Fraudulent Activity**  
   - Someone could be “testing” her account by depositing stolen funds (e.g., money mule scam).  
   - They might later ask her to “send the money back” by another method.  

5. **Loan or Cash Advance**  
   - If she has other Discover accounts (e.g., personal loans) and was approved without realizing it, they could deposit funds.  
   - But Discover would typically email/mail loan paperwork first.  

---

### What She Should Do Immediately  

1. **Do NOT Spend the Money** – If it’s a bank error or fraud, the bank can reverse it.  
2. **Call Discover’s Fraud Department** (use the number on the back of her card) and ask:  
   > “I received an unexpected $3,000 deposit into my bank account today from Discover. Can you confirm why?”  
3. **Check All Discover Accounts**  
   - Credit card, loans, savings, etc.  
   - Log into online banking and verify there’s no new loan or withdrawal.  
4. **Ask the Bank Who Originated the Deposit**  
   - They can trace the ACH ID. This will confirm if it truly came from Discover.  
5. **Monitor for Reversal or Scam Attempts**  
   - If someone contacts her claiming it was “sent by mistake” and asks her to send it back, **don’t**. Only follow instructions directly from the bank or Discover.
