**15 U.S. Code § 1666** (part of the **Fair Credit Billing Act**) provides consumers with protections for resolving billing disputes with creditors for **open-end credit accounts**, such as credit cards. This statute can be leveraged to address and potentially remove a late payment under certain conditions. Here's how it applies:

### Key Provisions of § 1666
1. **Scope**:
   - Covers **open-end credit accounts**, such as credit cards or lines of credit.
   - Applies to billing errors, which include:
     - Charges not made by the consumer.
     - Incorrect charges.
     - Failure to properly credit a payment.

2. **Billing Error Definition**:
   - If the late payment resulted from a billing error (e.g., the payment was submitted on time but not properly credited, or the creditor failed to send a billing statement), you can invoke § 1666.

3. **Dispute Process**:
   - You must notify the creditor in **writing** within **60 days** of the statement containing the error.
   - Include the following:
     - Name and account number.
     - Description of the disputed charge (e.g., "late payment reported in error").
     - Explanation and supporting evidence (e.g., proof of timely payment).

4. **Creditor Obligations**:
   - Upon receiving a timely dispute, the creditor must:
     - Acknowledge the dispute within **30 days**.
     - Investigate and resolve the dispute within **two billing cycles** (not exceeding **90 days**).
   - During the investigation, the creditor cannot report the disputed amount as delinquent.

5. **Outcome**:
   - If the creditor determines a billing error occurred, they must:
     - Correct the account.
     - Remove any associated late fees or negative marks.
   - If they find no error, they must provide a written explanation of why.

### How to Apply § 1666 for Late Payment Removal
1. **Assess the Situation**:
   - Determine if the late payment resulted from a billing error (e.g., payment misapplied, failure to credit, or incorrect processing).

2. **File a Written Dispute**:
   - Notify the creditor within 60 days of the statement containing the error.
   - Clearly outline the error and attach any supporting documents, such as:
     - Bank statements.
     - Receipts showing the payment was made on time.

3. **Follow Up**:
   - Ensure the creditor acknowledges your dispute within the required timeframe.
   - Monitor the resolution process to ensure compliance with statutory deadlines.

4. **Escalate if Necessary**:
   - If the creditor fails to comply or remove the late payment, you can escalate the matter by:
     - Filing a complaint with the **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)**.
     - Considering legal action for violations of the Fair Credit Billing Act.

### Practical Use
If the late payment doesn't fall under a billing error as defined by § 1666, you may need to rely on other laws (e.g., Fair Credit Reporting Act) or goodwill adjustments directly with the creditor.

### **15 U.S.C. § 1666a – Prompt Crediting of Payments**
This section ensures timely crediting of payments to your account, which can directly affect late payments.

#### **Key Provisions**:
1. **Timely Crediting**:
   - Creditors must apply payments **on the date they are received**.
   - If payments are misapplied due to the creditor’s error (e.g., applied late), this could lead to improper late fees or a late payment report.

2. **Exception for Payment Conditions**:
   - If the creditor specifies particular conditions for processing payments (e.g., specific payment location, cutoff times), they must disclose these clearly to the consumer.

3. **Delayed Crediting**:
   - If a creditor fails to credit a timely payment properly, the consumer has a right to dispute any resulting late fees or negative credit reporting under § 1666.

#### **Application**:
If your payment was made on time but credited late, § 1666a provides grounds to demand the creditor remove the late payment. This section protects you if the delay in processing was due to the creditor's practices or errors.

---

### **15 U.S.C. § 1666b – Timely Payments Made by Mail**
This section reinforces consumer rights for payments made via mail.

#### **Key Provisions**:
1. **Mail Payment Deadline**:
   - A payment sent by mail is considered timely **if received on or before the due date**, as long as it was properly addressed and met the creditor’s payment instructions.

2. **Mail Delivery Delays**:
   - If your payment was delayed due to **mail service issues** (e.g., not your fault), you may dispute any resulting late fees or negative credit marks.

#### **Application**:
If you mailed a payment on time but it was delayed and the creditor charged a late fee or reported a late payment, § 1666b provides a defense. You can argue the creditor improperly penalized you for a delay outside your control.

### **15 U.S.C. § 1666b(b) - Timely Billing Statements**
This subsection ensures that creditors provide sufficient notice for the consumer to make timely payments without incurring late fees.

#### **Key Provisions**:
1. **21-Day Rule for Delivery**:
   - Creditors must deliver or mail periodic billing statements **at least 21 days** before the payment due date for open-end credit accounts (e.g., credit cards).
   - This ensures consumers have adequate time to review the statement and make their payment.

2. **Grace Period**:
   - If the creditor provides a grace period (the time during which no interest or fees accrue for payments made on time), the **21-day rule applies to that period as well**. This means the statement must be delivered at least 21 days before the end of the grace period.

3. **Effect of Non-Compliance**:
   - If the creditor fails to send or deliver the statement at least 21 days before the due date (or the grace period expiration), they cannot impose late fees, penalties, or negative reporting based on that missed payment.

---

### **Practical Application**:
- If the creditor did not provide your statement at least 21 days before the payment was due, or if they failed to honor a grace period, you can:
  1. **Challenge the Late Payment**: Argue that the late payment was the result of their non-compliance with § 1666b(b).
  2. **Request Removal**: Demand removal of any late fees or negative reporting as the creditor is prohibited from penalizing you for their failure to meet the 21-day requirement.

---

### **Steps to Enforce This Right**:
1. **Check Statement Dates**:
   - Compare the statement issuance date with the payment due date to verify whether the 21-day rule was followed.
2. **Gather Evidence**:
   - Obtain statements, payment records, or communications showing non-compliance.
3. **File a Dispute**:
   - Reference § 1666b(b) and demand corrections to late fees or negative reports.
