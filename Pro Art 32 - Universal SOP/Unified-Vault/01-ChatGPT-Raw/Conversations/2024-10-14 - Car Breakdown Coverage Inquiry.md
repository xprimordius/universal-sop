## 👤 You

If i have full coverage geico insurance, am i covered if my car breaks down due to it being at 160K+ miles? Like if the engine goes out

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

No, standard full coverage auto insurance from Geico (or any insurer) typically *does not* cover mechanical breakdowns due to wear and tear, such as engine failure from high mileage. Full coverage usually includes:  

1. **Liability coverage** – Covers damage or injury to others if you are at fault.
2. **Collision coverage** – Covers damage to your car in an accident.
3. **Comprehensive coverage** – Covers non-accident-related damage (theft, vandalism, weather, etc.).

However, mechanical breakdowns like engine failure, unless caused by an accident or a covered event (e.g., fire or flooding), are considered maintenance issues and are not covered. 

If you're concerned about future breakdowns, you could explore **Mechanical Breakdown Insurance (MBI)**, which Geico offers for newer vehicles with fewer than 15,000 miles. Alternatively, extended warranties or vehicle service contracts from third-party providers might be an option for older cars.

## 👤 You

What if the mechanical failure causes the car to crash / collide into a wall

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

If a mechanical failure (like engine failure or brake malfunction) causes you to crash, **your Geico full coverage policy should cover the damages caused by the collision**, subject to your deductible. Here’s how it breaks down:

1. **Collision Coverage**  
   - This will pay for the repair or replacement of your vehicle if you collide with a wall or another object, regardless of what caused the accident (e.g., mechanical failure).  
   - You will need to pay your deductible, and the policy will cover the remaining damage, up to the car's actual cash value (ACV).

2. **Liability Coverage**  
   - If the mechanical failure results in damage to someone else's property (like another vehicle or structure) or injures someone, your liability insurance will cover those damages, up to your policy limits.

### What’s Not Covered:
- The **cost to repair the original mechanical failure** (e.g., engine or brakes) is not covered by insurance, as it falls under maintenance. 

### Summary:
Even though the insurance can cover the **consequences of the mechanical failure** (i.e., the crash), it won’t cover the **mechanical repair** that led to the incident. Be sure to document everything and notify your insurer as soon as possible if this happens.
