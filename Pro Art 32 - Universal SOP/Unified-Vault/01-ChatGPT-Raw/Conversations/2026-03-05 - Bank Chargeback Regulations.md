# 1. Truth in Lending Act (TILA) — Billing Error Law
### Statute
**15 U.S.C. §1666**

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666

### What it requires (black-letter)

If a consumer disputes a billing error, the bank **must investigate**.

**Law (verbatim):**

> “The creditor shall investigate the matter and correct the billing error or explain why the billing statement is correct.”

### Key rule

A bank **cannot simply accept the merchant’s claim**.  
It must conduct an **actual investigation**.

---

# 2. Regulation Z — Billing Error Procedures
### Regulation implementing TILA

**12 CFR §1026.13**

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.13

This is the **core chargeback investigation rule.**

---

## Mandatory investigation requirement

**12 CFR §1026.13(f)**

> “The creditor shall conduct a reasonable investigation and determine whether a billing error occurred.”

Key point:

**Reasonable investigation is legally required.**

If a bank:

• ignores consumer evidence  
• relies only on merchant statements  
• fails to examine documentation  

that **can violate Reg Z**.

---

## Mandatory resolution timeline

**12 CFR §1026.13(c)**

The bank must:

1. **Acknowledge dispute within 30 days**
2. **Resolve within 2 billing cycles (max 90 days)**

Quote:

> “The creditor shall resolve the billing error within two billing cycles… but in no event later than 90 days.”

---

## Mandatory written explanation

If the bank denies the dispute:

**12 CFR §1026.13(f)**

> “The creditor shall send the consumer a written explanation or clarification.”

Meaning:

They must explain **why the charge is valid based on the investigation.**

A denial without meaningful explanation **violates Reg Z.**

---

# 3. Regulation Z — Disputed Amount Cannot Be Treated as Owed

**12 CFR §1026.13(d)**

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.13

Quote:

> “The creditor may not treat the disputed amount as delinquent.”

Meaning:

During investigation they **cannot treat it as owed or penalize you.**

---

# 4. Regulation Z — Claims and Defenses Rule
### Extremely important in merchant disputes

**12 CFR §1026.12(c)**

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.12

Quote:

> “The cardholder may assert against the card issuer all claims and defenses arising out of the transaction.”

Meaning in plain terms:

If the merchant:

• misrepresented the service  
• failed to deliver  
• committed fraud  

the **card issuer must consider those claims.**

They **cannot ignore them**.
