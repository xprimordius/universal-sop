That’s a fair and common frustration — here’s how and why it’s structured that way:  

---

### ⚖️ Why It’s Set Up This Way  
Minnesota’s paid leave program is **insurance-based**, not savings-based.  
- Everyone pays a **flat percentage (0.88%)** of wages, like Social Security or unemployment insurance.  
- Those contributions go into a **statewide fund**, which then pays out benefits when anyone qualifies for leave.  
- The fund isn’t tied to your individual balance — it’s pooled risk.  

---

### 💰 The Fairness Logic  
It’s **proportional** rather than equal in dollar amount.  
- A person making $100,000 pays $880 a year.  
- A person making $30,000 pays $264 a year.  
Both pay the same **percentage** of their income.  
- The higher earner pays more in absolute dollars because they earn more — similar to progressive taxation.  

The benefit payout, however, **caps at a maximum weekly benefit (~$1,423)**, so higher earners don’t get proportionally more back. That’s by design — to protect low-income workers who otherwise couldn’t afford unpaid leave.  

---

### 🧩 The Policy Goal  
- Make sure **everyone** has access to paid leave regardless of income or employer benefits.  
- Avoid leaving lower-income workers behind, since they’re least likely to have employer-provided paid leave now.  
- Use pooled contributions so that those who can afford more help stabilize the system for those who can’t.
