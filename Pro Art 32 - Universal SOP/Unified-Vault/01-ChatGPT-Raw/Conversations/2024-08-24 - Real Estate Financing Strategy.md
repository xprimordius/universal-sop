This segment describes a financial strategy involving real estate and leveraging assets to generate income and access liquidity. Here's a thorough and clear breakdown:

### Scenario Explanation

1. **Initial Setup**:
   - You own a house, which you either purchased or already have, with an outstanding mortgage (let's assume $350,000 is owed).
   - You decide to transfer ownership of the house to a separate corporation that you control. This allows you to keep the property legally separate from your personal assets.

2. **Creating a Lending Opportunity**:
   - Once the house is owned by the corporation, you can create a promissory note (a legal document in which one party promises to pay a determinate sum of money to the other).
   - This note is offered to someone interested in purchasing the house. Instead of selling the house outright, you act as the lender. The buyer will make payments to you, effectively creating a mortgage-like situation where you are the bank.

3. **Income from the Note**:
   - The buyer agrees to pay you $2,500 per month.
   - From this payment, $700 goes towards servicing the existing mortgage on the house (the $350,000 owed), leaving you with a net income each month.
   - The key idea here is that you have reduced your out-of-pocket expense to cover the mortgage because the buyer's payments are helping to pay off the original loan.

4. **Leveraging the Note**:
   - The note (an agreement for the buyer to pay you over a long period, like 30 years) is valuable. For example, if the total value of the payments over 30 years is $2 million, this becomes an asset.
   - You can then approach a bank and use this note as collateral to borrow money. Since the buyer has good credit and is reliable, the bank sees this as a low-risk loan.
   - Typically, a bank might lend you 75% of the note's value. In this case, 75% of $2 million equals $1.5 million.
   - You now have access to $1.5 million in cash, which can be used for other investments or needs, while still collecting monthly payments from the buyer.

### Summary
In this strategy:
- You convert a real estate property into a financial asset owned by a corporation.
- By creating a promissory note and becoming a lender, you secure ongoing monthly income.
- The note's value can be used as collateral to obtain a substantial loan from a bank, giving you liquidity while still maintaining ownership of the underlying asset.

This scenario exemplifies leveraging real estate to generate both immediate and long-term financial benefits through strategic use of corporate ownership, lending, and collateralization.
