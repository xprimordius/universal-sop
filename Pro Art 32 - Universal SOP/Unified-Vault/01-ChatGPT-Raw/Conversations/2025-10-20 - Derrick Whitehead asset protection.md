### 1. **Lender Verification (Conventional / FHA)**
When applying for a loan, lenders verify:
- **Employment income** via **W-2s** and **pay stubs** (past 30–60 days).  
- **Employment status** via **VOE (Verification of Employment)** — they contact the company or payroll provider.  
- **Tax returns** (usually 1–2 years) to confirm consistency.  
- **Ownership interest** in the employer — cross-checked via business tax returns, W-2 employer EIN, and sometimes Secretary of State records.

If you **own the company that pays you**, lenders must reclassify you as **self-employed**, requiring 2 years of business returns and more scrutiny.

---

## 🧠 STRATEGIC STRUCTURE OPTIONS

### 1. **Operating Company (LLC / S-Corp / C-Corp)**
Runs your business, pays salaries.  
If **you are on payroll** and show up as an **officer / >25% owner**, underwriters will likely classify you as **self-employed** even if you receive a W-2.

### 2. **Holding Company**
Owns the operating entity. You can be a **beneficiary or manager** of the holding entity, not the direct shareholder of the operating company that pays you.  
→ This adds one layer of separation but does **not automatically hide ownership**; underwriters can request **K-1s or ownership disclosures** if suspected.

### 3. **Trust / Offshore Trust**
If a **trust** (even offshore) owns the holding company, you technically don’t own the business—**the trust does**.  
However:
- U.S. lenders apply **beneficial ownership tracing** under anti-money-laundering and Patriot Act rules.
- They may require **beneficiary disclosure** or **trust documents** if your name appears as trustee, manager, or if the trust pays your salary.

### 4. **Manager-Only Role**
You can legally be a **manager** or **employee** of a company you don’t directly own.  
If **your name is not on any ownership filings**, lenders typically accept the W-2 as standard income.  
⚠️ But: intentionally concealing ownership or beneficial interest can constitute **loan misrepresentation** (bank fraud under 18 U.S.C. §1014).

## 🔍 How lenders verify your income

### If you’re a **regular W-2 employee (not an owner)**  
Lenders will typically only require:  
1. **Two years of personal W-2s**  
2. **Recent pay stubs (30–60 days)**  
3. **Verification of Employment (VOE)** — a short form the employer confirms via HR or payroll provider  
➡️ They **do not** need the company’s tax returns.

---

### If you are an **owner, partner, or officer (>25%)**  
Under FHA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and most bank overlays:  
- You are treated as **self-employed**.  
- The lender must review **the business’s tax returns (Form 1120/1065/Schedule C)** to confirm that the W-2 income is sustainable and not inflated.  
➡️ Without those returns, **they cannot count the income**.

---

## ⚠️ If the business “chooses not to share” its returns  
Then the underwriter cannot verify stability of income and will **exclude** that income entirely from qualifying ratios.  
You’d need another verified source of income or wait two years showing W-2s from that employer with stable history.

If your structure is clean and the **employing company truly isn’t owned by you** (e.g., owned by a trust or holding entity where you’re not a listed owner or officer), then:
- The lender will **not ask for company returns**.  
- Your W-2 and VOE will suffice.  
- This is **legal**, provided the entity and payroll are real, taxes are paid, and there’s no intentional concealment.
