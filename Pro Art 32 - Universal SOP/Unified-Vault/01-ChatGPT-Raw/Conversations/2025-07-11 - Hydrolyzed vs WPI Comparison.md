**Hydrolyzed Whey vs. Whey Isolate – Fast Breakdown**

| **Feature**                 | **Hydrolyzed Whey Protein**                               | **Whey Protein Isolate (WPI)**                             |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Processing**              | Pre-digested (partially broken down via enzymes)           | Filtered to remove most fat, carbs, and lactose             |
| **Digestion Speed**         | Fastest (rapid absorption, minimal digestion needed)       | Fast (slightly slower than hydrolyzed but still rapid)      |
| **Lactose Content**         | Virtually none (extremely low)                            | Very low (usually <1%)                                     |
| **Best For**                | Ultra-fast recovery; sensitive stomachs                    | Fast recovery; low-fat, low-carb diets                      |
| **Taste**                   | Can be bitter (due to peptides)                           | Cleaner, smoother taste                                    |
| **Price**                   | Highest (more expensive due to processing)                | Moderate to high                                           |
| **Bioavailability**         | Extremely high                                            | Very high                                                  |
| **Main Advantage**          | Speed + digestion ease                                    | High protein purity + fast absorption                       |

**Key Insight:**  
- **Hydrolyzed whey** is ideal if you need *ultra-fast* absorption or have digestive issues.
- **Isolate** is optimal for most people—fast, pure, and more cost-effective.

**Recommendation:**  
Unless you're a pro athlete or have gut sensitivity, **whey isolate** is typically sufficient and better value.
