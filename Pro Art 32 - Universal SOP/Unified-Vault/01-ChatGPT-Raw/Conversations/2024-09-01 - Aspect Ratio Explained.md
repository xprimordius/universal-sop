# Aspect Ratio Explained

---

## 👤 You

why doesn't 16:10 for 1080 use 1080 instead of 1050

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The resolution 16:10 ratio using 1050 instead of 1080 is a matter of aspect ratio and pixel count. Here's a breakdown:

- **Aspect Ratio**: The 16:10 aspect ratio is slightly taller than the 16:9 aspect ratio commonly used in 1080p (1920x1080).
- **Pixel Count**: To maintain the 16:10 aspect ratio, the resolution needs to adjust the height to match the width correctly. If you kept the height at 1080 pixels, the width would need to be around 1728 pixels to maintain the 16:10 aspect ratio, which deviates from the common widths used in displays.

The 16:10 resolution closest to 1920x1080 (16:9) is **1920x1200**. However, the more common 16:10 resolution, often used in monitors, is **1680x1050**. This resolution maintains the 16:10 aspect ratio while keeping the pixel count within a practical range for display technology.

So, 1050 is used instead of 1080 to preserve the correct aspect ratio of 16:10, ensuring that the image doesn't appear stretched or distorted.

## 👤 You

why isn't it 1728 x 1080 then?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The resolution 1728x1080 isn't commonly used because it doesn't align with standard aspect ratios that have been widely adopted in display technology. Here’s why:

1. **Standardization and Industry Adoption**: Display resolutions are standardized for consistency across devices, content creation, and media distribution. Common resolutions like 1920x1080 (16:9), 1680x1050 (16:10), and others have been widely adopted because they fit well within the industry standards for various applications, including monitors, TVs, and content creation.

2. **Practicality in Display Manufacturing**: The 1728x1080 resolution would create a non-standard aspect ratio that doesn’t align with the typical 16:9, 16:10, or even 4:3 ratios. Manufacturers tend to stick to standard aspect ratios to simplify production, compatibility, and content creation. Non-standard resolutions could lead to scaling issues, mismatches in content display, and overall consumer confusion.

3. **Content and Compatibility**: Most content is designed for standard aspect ratios like 16:9 or 16:10. A resolution like 1728x1080 wouldn't fit neatly into these formats, leading to potential issues with letterboxing, stretching, or black bars, which detract from the viewing experience.

4. **Usage Scenarios**: 16:10 was preferred in professional environments where more vertical screen real estate was beneficial for tasks like coding, graphic design, or document editing. The 16:9 aspect ratio became the standard for most media, particularly video, as it better fits widescreen content.

In summary, while 1728x1080 would technically maintain the height of 1080 pixels with a 16:10 aspect ratio, it isn't used because it doesn’t align with standardized resolutions that offer better compatibility, easier manufacturing, and a more consistent user experience.
