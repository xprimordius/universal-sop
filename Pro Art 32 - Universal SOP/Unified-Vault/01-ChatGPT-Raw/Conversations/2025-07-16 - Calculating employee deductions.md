## 🤖 ChatGPT

### Will a Bank Flag a $2,200 Federal-Tax Line as “Fake”?  
**They might.** Under-withholding is one of the easiest tells lenders use to spot fabricated or “template” pay stubs.

---

#### 🔍 What Banks Actually Check
| Checkpoint | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|
| **Withholding Ratio vs. Gross** | For six-figure earners, federal + FICA often land **28-35 %** of gross (Single, no big deductions). $2,200 on a **$10.4 K bi-weekly check → ~21 %** looks light. |
| **Year-to-Date Consistency** | YTD tax totals must line up with YTD wages and IRS brackets. One short check can slide; a low YTD raises alarms. |
| **W-4 on File** | Lenders may ask for the employee’s W-4 or a payroll register. If no W-4 or if it shows “Single, 0,” a low figure is indefensible. |
| **Payroll Provider Verification** | Banks call ADP, Gusto, Paychex, etc. Template stubs with odd rounding or missing EINs are easy to spot. |

---

#### ⚖️ When $2,200 *Could* Be Legit
- **Married Filing Jointly** with two incomes → lower table rates.  
- **Large Pre-Tax Reductions** (maxed 401(k), HSA, §125 plans) shrink taxable wages.  
- **Mid-Year Cap Reached** for Social Security: lowers FICA slice but *doesn’t* justify low FIT alone.  
If you can document these, the bank won’t label it fake.

---

#### 🚩 Red Flags That Trigger “Fake” Suspicion
1. **No W-4 on record** but low withholding. IRS default is *high*, not low.  
2. **Perfectly round numbers** ($2,200.00) without cents on every stub.  
3. **No Additional Medicare Tax** showing after $200 K YTD.  
4. **Employer EIN or address missing / mismatch**.  
5. **YTD totals reset or inconsistent** across stubs.

---

#### 🔑 Best-Practice Playbook
1. **Default to IRS rules** (Single, 0) until employee files a new W-4.  
2. **Show real YTD figures** on every stub.  
3. **Keep copies of W-4 and benefit elections**; hand them over if lender asks.  
4. **Use a reputable payroll service**; banks trust recognizable formats.  
5. **If withholding truly is low, attach an explanation** (e.g., Married, big 401(k), dependent credits).

---

### Bottom Line
A single bi-weekly FIT of **$2,200 on a ~$10 K check looks suspicious unless it’s backed by a W-4 and hefty pre-tax deductions**. Provide documentation or withhold at the higher default rate to stay audit-proof and lender-friendly.
