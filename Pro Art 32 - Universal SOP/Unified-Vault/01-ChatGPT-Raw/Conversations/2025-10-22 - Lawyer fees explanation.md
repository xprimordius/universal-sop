## 🤖 ChatGPT

Exactly right — yes, **PIP (Personal Injury Protection)** and **Bodily Injury Liability (BI)** are *completely different* parts of auto insurance.  

Here’s the breakdown 👇  

---

### 🩹 **PIP (Personal Injury Protection)** — *“No-fault” coverage*
- **Who it covers:** You and your passengers, no matter who caused the accident.  
- **Purpose:** Pays for **your** medical bills and a portion of lost wages *immediately*, without needing to sue anyone.  
- **Typical limit:** $10,000 in Florida.  
- **Paid by:** *Your* own insurance company.  
- **Covers:**  
  - 80% of reasonable medical expenses  
  - 60% of lost wages (up to the limit)  
  - Some death benefits  
- **You can’t sue under PIP** — it’s automatic, limited coverage.  

---

### ⚖️ **Bodily Injury Liability (BI)** — *“Fault-based” coverage*
- **Who it covers:** The *other* person that you hurt (if you caused the crash).  
- **Purpose:** Pays for **the victim’s** injuries, pain and suffering, and future damages if you’re at fault.  
- **Typical limits:** $25K, $50K, $100K, or more — sometimes up to millions for commercial or umbrella policies.  
- **Paid by:** The *at-fault driver’s* insurance company.  
- **Covers:**  
  - Medical expenses beyond PIP  
  - Lost income  
  - Pain and suffering  
  - Long-term care or permanent injury  

---

### 🛡️ **So in short:**
- **PIP = your own $10K no-fault medical coverage.**
- **BI = the at-fault party’s liability coverage (where big settlements come from).**

---

If the at-fault driver has **no BI coverage**, you can then use:
- **Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage from *your* policy, or  
- File a **personal lawsuit** (though it’s often not worth it unless they have assets or umbrella coverage).
