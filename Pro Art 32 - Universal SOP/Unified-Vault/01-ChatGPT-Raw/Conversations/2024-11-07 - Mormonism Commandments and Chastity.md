### 2. **Translation Choices and Language**
   - **Translation Approach**: KJV translators aimed for a balance between literal translation and readability, focusing on both accuracy and beauty of language.    - **17th-Century English**: The KJV uses a formal and poetic style with archaic language (e.g., “thee,” “thou,” “saith”), reflecting English as it was spoken in the early 1600s. ### 3. **Textual Variations**
   - **Differences in Manuscripts**: Since the KJV translators didn’t have access to some of the oldest manuscripts available today, the KJV includes certain verses and phrasing not found in these earlier sources. For example:

   - **Later Discoveries**: Manuscripts discovered after the KJV’s creation, such as the **Codex Sinaiticus** and the **Dead Sea Scrolls**, have helped scholars create more accurate translations by providing older source material that often differs slightly from the Textus Receptus.

### 4. **Theological Interpretations**

### Summary
The KJV is a revered and historically significant translation that has influenced Christianity for centuries.
