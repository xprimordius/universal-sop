**Corporate Lease Qualification – Fast Breakdown**

### 1. **Entity Setup**
- **LLC or Corporation**: Must be active and in good standing.
- **EIN**: Required from IRS (free, instant).
- **Business Bank Account**: Shows legitimacy and financial flow.

---

### 2. **Business Credit Profile**
- **D&B Number**: Register with Dun & Bradstreet (optional but adds credibility).
- **Business Credit Cards or Net-30s**: Establish basic tradelines.
- **Business address + phone**: Consistent across all documents.

---

### 3. **Documents Landlords May Require**
- **Articles of Organization**
- **Operating Agreement**
- **Business license (if applicable)**
- **Proof of income or bank statements**: 3–6 months showing deposits.
- **Corporate resolution to lease**: If someone other than owner is signing.

---

### 4. **Financial Strength or PG (Personal Guarantee)**
- If your business is new or low-revenue:
  - You may need to **personally guarantee** the lease.
  - Some allow upfront payment (1–3 months) in lieu of credit.

---

### 5. **Types of Places That Accept Corporate Leases**
- **Furnished short-term rentals**: Like Airbnbs (use platforms like LeaseAbroad, Landing, Zeus).
- **Apartments with corporate housing programs**: Greystar, Camden, Equity.
- **Private landlords**: Often flexible if paid upfront or via trusted third party.

---

### 6. **Tips to Get Approved**
- **Professional presentation**: Website, email (e.g., alan@yourbiz.com), pitch deck.
- **Use a leasing agent** who understands corporate housing.
- Offer **security, liability insurance**, and proof of **purpose** (relocation, business use, etc.).

what if I subject to'd a place for $0 down, would the house be in my name?

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**No — not immediately.**  
If you buy a house **subject to the existing mortgage**, the **loan stays in the seller’s name**, but **you take control of the property**.

---

### 🔑 What “Subject-To” Means:
- **Deed transfers to you** → **You own the house**  
- **Mortgage stays in seller’s name** → **You make payments**  
- This is **legal** and **common in creative financing**.

---

### 🏠 Title vs. Loan
| Aspect        | In Whose Name?                    |
|---------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Title (Deed)** | **Your name** (you own the house)  |
| **Mortgage**     | **Seller’s name** (they’re liable) |

---

### ✅ Benefits
- **No bank qualification**
- **$0 down possible**
- Fast close, even with poor credit

---

### ⚠️ Risks to Know
- **Due-on-sale clause**: Lender *can* call the loan due (rare if current)  
- **Seller credit risk**: You default → their credit tanks  
- **Insurance and taxes**: Must be handled carefully  
- **Need full disclosures + paperwork** to avoid fraud claims

---

### 💼 Required Documents
- Warranty Deed / Subject-To Agreement  
- Authorization to Release Info  
- Purchase Agreement  
- CYA Letter (disclosure to seller)  
- Land Trust or LLC (often used to mask transfer)

---

### Summary:
> **Yes**, the house title can be in your name with **$0 down** via a subject-to deal — but **the loan is not**, and you must **keep payments current** to stay safe.
