## Personal Injury – Hit & Run  
### Time Constraints (U.S.)

### 1) **Statute of Limitations (lawsuit deadline)**
This is the **hard stop**. Miss it → case is dead.

- **Most states:** **2 years** from date of injury  
- **Some states:** **1 year** (e.g., TN, KY)  
- **Others:** **3–6 years** (varies)

> If you don’t file suit by this date, you lose the right to recover—**even if liability is obvious**.

---

### 2) **Settlement Window**
- Settlements can happen **anytime before** the statute expires  
- **Practically:** insurers get serious **6–12 months before deadline**
- After the deadline → **zero leverage**, no settlement

---

### 3) **Hit-and-Run Specific Factors**
- If the at-fault driver is **unknown**, claims often go through:
  - **Uninsured Motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage
- UM/UIM claims often have:
  - **Shorter notice deadlines** (sometimes **30–90 days**)
  - Separate contractual limitation periods (often **2–3 years**)

---

### 4) **Government Entity Involved?**
If a city, county, or state vehicle is involved:
- **Notice of Claim:** as short as **30–180 days**
- This is **separate** from the lawsuit deadline

---

## Bottom Line (Action-Oriented)
- **There is no settlement deadline**
- **There IS a lawsuit deadline**, usually **2 years**
- Settlement only exists **before** that deadline
- UM/UIM claims may have **earlier notice requirements**
