Short answer: **usually no, not as a standard part of insurance**, but with caveats.  

---

### ⚖️ Why it’s generally not covered

- Insurance for vehicles typically covers **glass breakage**, **collision**, **vandalism**, or **comprehensive** (hail, theft, etc.).  
- Tint film (like XPEL) is considered an **add-on or aftermarket upgrade**, not part of factory glass.  
- So if your glass is replaced, your insurer might only replace the bare glass – not the film on it – unless it’s explicitly included in your policy.

---

### ✅ When it *might* be covered (or replaced)

- If your insurer acknowledges it as part of "glass and accessories" coverage or optional “full glass & tint” add-on.  
- If you purchased special coverage or rider stating “replace with equivalent parts” (i.e. include aftermarket components).  
- If the damage is due to a covered peril (e.g. vandalism) and your insurer accepts the film as valid extra cost.  
- If glass is replaced under factory warranty and the manufacturer or supplier agrees to reapply the tint (e.g. Tesla’s approach: “Tesla will also cover the film replacement if the glass requires … replacement under their warranty” citeturn0search15).

---

- But that only applies to **manufacturing or installation defects**, not damage from impacts, collisions, vandalism, etc.  
- In regions, XPEL may offer **lifetime, transferable warranties** for window film (e.g., “XPEL Window Tint has a lifetime transferable warranty … covers peeling, cracking, bubbling, fading”) citeturn0search13  

---
