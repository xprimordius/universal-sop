In Mormonism, the commandment against adultery is rooted in the teachings of the King James Version of the Bible, particularly in the Ten Commandments. Here’s how Mormon doctrine incorporates and expands upon these principles:

1. **Biblical Commandments on Adultery**: The Ten Commandments in the King James Version state, "Thou shalt not commit adultery" (Exodus 20:14). This commandment forbids sexual relations outside of marriage, focusing on maintaining the sanctity of marriage vows and moral purity.

2. **Adultery in the New Testament**: The King James Bible also expands on adultery in the New Testament. Jesus taught that adultery is not just a physical act but can occur in one’s heart, saying, "Whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart" (Matthew 5:28). This extends the concept to include thoughts and intentions, emphasizing moral integrity.

3. **LDS Scriptures and Teachings**: In the Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and other LDS teachings, adultery is further condemned. Doctrine and Covenants 42:24 states, "Thou shalt love thy wife with all thy heart, and shalt cleave unto her and none else." This reinforces the idea that marital fidelity is central to one's relationship with God and is a fundamental commandment in LDS doctrine.

4. **Consequences of Adultery**: In Mormon doctrine, adultery is considered a serious sin. Church discipline may include repentance processes and, in some cases, excommunication, depending on the circumstances. The focus, however, is on repentance and forgiveness for those who sincerely seek to make amends.

5. **Moral Purity and Eternal Families**: Mormonism places a high value on chastity and purity as part of a covenant with God. The belief in eternal families strengthens the emphasis on fidelity, as Mormons believe that marriage, when sealed in the temple, can continue beyond this life. Therefore, adultery is seen as a violation not only of a spouse’s trust but of the eternal family covenant.

These teachings underscore the seriousness with which Mormonism views marriage and fidelity, building on the foundation of the King James Bible with additional LDS scriptures and doctrines.
