## 👤 You

Can I leave water and beef bones and onion in water simmering for days? 

It’s open top and I’m concerned about sickness

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Leaving water, beef bones, and onion simmering in an open pot for days is not recommended due to health and safety concerns. Here's why:

1. **Bacterial Growth**: Simmering at low temperatures (especially below 140°F/60°C) provides an environment where harmful bacteria, such as Clostridium perfringens and Bacillus cereus, can grow if the temperature fluctuates or parts of the pot aren't hot enough.

2. **Evaporation**: Leaving the pot uncovered allows water to evaporate, potentially concentrating contaminants and exposing food to airborne particles.

3. **Toxin Risk**: Some bacteria produce heat-resistant toxins that may remain even if the broth is later brought to a boil.

### Best Practices for Safety:
1. **Use a Slow Cooker or Pressure Cooker**: These devices maintain a consistent temperature and help prevent contamination.
2. **Cover the Pot**: Use a lid to reduce evaporation and exposure to airborne bacteria.
3. **Cool and Refrigerate**: If you're not actively cooking, cool the broth quickly and refrigerate it to prevent bacterial growth. You can reheat it later to continue simmering.
4. **Simmer Safely**: Keep the temperature above 165°F/74°C consistently.

By following these practices, you can avoid health risks while making broth safely.

## 👤 You

At what point do they create sickness and how to know

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The risk of sickness arises when **harmful bacteria multiply to dangerous levels** and/or produce toxins. Here’s what to know about the process:

---

### **Key Points When Bacteria Become Dangerous:**
1. **Temperature Danger Zone (40°F–140°F / 4°C–60°C):**
   - Bacteria like *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, and *Clostridium perfringens* thrive in this range.
   - Food left in this zone for **more than 2 hours** is considered unsafe.
   - Even simmering temperatures below 165°F (74°C) for prolonged periods may allow bacteria to multiply slowly.

2. **Toxins Produced by Bacteria:**
   - Some bacteria (*e.g.*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Bacillus cereus*) produce **heat-stable toxins**. Once toxins are present, boiling the broth won't make it safe.
   - Toxins can develop if the broth cools down to unsafe temperatures during simmering.

3. **Open Pot Risks:**
   - Dust, airborne bacteria, and contaminants can settle into the broth.
   - Long exposure (hours to days) gives bacteria time to multiply, even if the broth is hot in spots.

---

### **How to Detect Unsafe Broth:**
1. **Visual Signs:**
   - Cloudy appearance or an unexpected layer of scum forming after boiling.
   - Mold growth on the surface (if cooled and left uncovered).

2. **Smell:**
   - Sour, rancid, or "off" odors are strong indicators of spoilage.

3. **Taste:**
   - A strange or sour taste can indicate bacterial activity (but avoid tasting if you suspect it's unsafe).

4. **Symptoms After Consumption:**
   - *Foodborne Illness Symptoms*: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomach cramps, or fever typically occur **1–48 hours after ingestion**.

---

### **Preventing Sickness While Simmering Broth:**
1. **Simmer at a Consistently High Temperature:**
   - Keep the liquid at or above **165°F (74°C)** continuously.
2. **Use a Lid:**
   - Reduce contamination and evaporation.
3. **Refrigerate Overnight:**
   - If you need to pause cooking, strain the broth, refrigerate it, and reheat thoroughly the next day.
4. **Reboil Daily:**
   - If left simmering for days, boil the broth fully (above 212°F/100°C) at least **once every 24 hours** to kill bacteria.
   
By following these steps, you can minimize risks and ensure safe broth preparation. If in doubt, discard the broth to avoid sickness.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Scooping out most of the broth and adding fresh water may reduce bacterial load but **does not guarantee safety** because:

1. **Bacteria on Bones and Ingredients**:
   - The beef bones and onion can still harbor bacteria or heat-stable toxins from prior growth. These won't be eliminated by merely replacing the water.

2. **Heat-Stable Toxins**:
   - If bacteria like *Bacillus cereus* or *Staphylococcus aureus* have already produced toxins, those toxins remain in the food (bones, onion, etc.) even if the broth is replaced or boiled.

3. **Contamination Residue**:
   - Surfaces of the pot and any remaining broth may still contain harmful bacteria.

---

### **How to Proceed Safely:**
1. **Discard the Contents**:
   - For maximum safety, discard the bones and onions if they were left simmering at unsafe temperatures.

2. **Sanitize the Pot**:
   - Thoroughly clean and sanitize the pot before starting fresh.

3. **Restart with Fresh Ingredients**:
   - Use new bones, onion, and water. Ensure the broth simmers at a safe temperature (above 165°F/74°C).

4. **Boil Thoroughly**:
   - If you choose to reuse the ingredients, boil the new broth vigorously for **at least 10 minutes** to kill active bacteria. This won't remove toxins, but it can reduce bacterial load.

---

### **When It’s Safe to Reuse Bones and Ingredients**:
- If the original broth was consistently kept above **165°F (74°C)** and never left in the **danger zone (40°F–140°F)** for over **2 hours**, the bones and ingredients are likely still safe to reuse.

If there's **any doubt about safety**, it's better to discard the ingredients and start fresh to avoid risk.

## 👤 You

If I completely cover the pot, is that safe? 

I’ve left food out before and it always spoils if I keep a lid on it, vs leaving the lid partly open for air

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Covering a pot completely **does not automatically make it safe**, especially for extended cooking or storage. While a lid can help reduce contamination, other critical factors determine safety:

---

### **Why a Covered Pot Alone Isn't Always Safe**
1. **Temperature Control Is Key**:
   - If the broth isn’t kept at or above **165°F (74°C)** consistently, bacteria can grow, even in a covered pot.
   - A lid helps maintain heat, but if the pot cools below safe temperatures (e.g., when simmering too low or left to sit), bacterial growth resumes.

2. **Moist, Sealed Environments Promote Bacterial Growth**:
   - A tightly sealed pot creates a humid environment where certain bacteria (*e.g.*, *Clostridium perfringens*) can grow faster in low-oxygen conditions, even if submerged.

3. **Spoilage and Toxins**:
   - Some bacteria (*e.g.*, *Bacillus cereus*) can survive cooking and produce toxins. A lid won’t prevent toxins from forming if the conditions allow bacterial growth.

4. **Difference Between Spoilage and Safety**:
   - Leaving the lid slightly open allows moisture to escape, reducing condensation and slowing visible spoilage (like mold). However, this doesn’t eliminate bacteria or toxins.

---

### **How to Safely Simmer with a Lid**
To ensure safety when using a fully covered pot:
1. **Keep the Temperature Consistently High**:
   - Ensure the broth stays at **a constant simmer (at least 185°F/85°C)** or boils briefly every 24 hours if cooking long-term.
2. **Avoid Overnight Cooling**:
   - If you need to pause cooking, cool the broth quickly and refrigerate it (below 40°F/4°C). Reheat thoroughly before resuming.
3. **Ventilation for Long Simmering**:
   - A lid can stay slightly ajar to allow steam to escape while still keeping contaminants out. This prevents excessive condensation and helps regulate heat.

---

### **Why Your Food Spoiled with a Closed Lid**
- When food cools with the lid on, condensation creates a moist environment perfect for bacterial growth.
- Without airflow or refrigeration, the trapped heat is insufficient to prevent spoilage but warm enough to accelerate bacterial growth.

---

### **Final Tips**
- A closed lid helps maintain heat and reduces contamination during cooking, but **temperature control** is the ultimate factor in food safety.
- If leaving food out to cool, always refrigerate it within **2 hours** or discard it to avoid health risks.
