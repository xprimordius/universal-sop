## Core Differences — DSLR Context

**1) Brightness & Coverage**  
- **Key Light:** ~2800 lm panel → wider, flatter, more even light across face/scene. Excellent for key lighting even at lower output. citeturn0search0  
- **Key Light Neo:** ~700–1000 lm (USB-C / external 3 A supply). Compact size means less spread & narrower coverage. citeturn0search13  
**How it matters:**  
At ~25% brightness in a small room:  
- **Key Light** still throws broad, smooth light with easier control.  
- **Neo** will light nicely but may fall short if you want fill/edge control or coverage beyond close-in face lighting.

**2) Light Quality & Shape**  
- **Key Light:** large flat panel → smoother gradients, less hotspot, softer shadows. citeturn0search0  
- **Key Light Neo:** compact panel → soft light too but smaller area can produce harder fall-off further from subject. citeturn0search15

**3) Mounting & Flexibility**  
- **Key Light:** Desk clamp + standard thread → can mount high/angled like classic key light setup. citeturn0search0  
- **Key Light Neo:** Monitor/desk clamp; easy reposition but limited height without additional stand. citeturn0search13

**4) App & Control**  
Both use Elgato Control Center (brightness + color temp 2900–7000 K) and can be networked. citeturn0search0  
Neo also has onboard controls for quick edits. citeturn0search20

## DSLR Use Case Strategy

**Primary Key Light → Big Panel**
- Use **Key Light** as your **main key** if:  
  • You want even light across face/body.  
  • You want broader coverage and less precision tweaking to fill shadows.

**Supplement / Fill / Accent**
- Use **Key Light Neo** as:  
  • Fill light on opposite side at lower power.  
  • Hair/edge light.  
  • Portable spot when moving around a lot.

**One vs Two Lights**
- A pair (even of Neos) can fill shadow and shape better than a single light on axis. citeturn0search4

- **DSLR primary key light:** **Key Light** — wider, softer, more professional panel.
