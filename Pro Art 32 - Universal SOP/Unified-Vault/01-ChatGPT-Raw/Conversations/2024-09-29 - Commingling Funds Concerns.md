# Commingling Funds Concerns

---

## 👤 You

If I move $20K from my business account to my navy federal personal account to get a pledge loan with the intent to build my relationship with that bank to get future business capital; will the banks look at this as co mingling?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, moving $20K from your business account to your personal account for a pledge loan could potentially be viewed as commingling funds, especially if it's not properly documented or justified as part of a business transaction. Banks and auditors often look closely at how funds are transferred between personal and business accounts to ensure that there is a clear separation between the two.

To avoid the appearance of commingling:

1. **Document the transaction:** Clearly record the purpose of the transfer, such as it being a loan or a capital contribution from the business to yourself.
   
2. **Use proper channels:** If possible, avoid moving large sums between personal and business accounts without clear documentation or a legal basis, such as a formal loan agreement or dividend payment.

3. **Consult with a tax or legal advisor:** This ensures you stay compliant with both banking regulations and tax laws, as such transfers could trigger scrutiny, particularly if audited.

Doing so carefully will help you avoid potential legal or financial issues later.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

To ensure the banks green-light the transfer from your business account to your personal account and avoid issues of commingling, follow these steps:

### 1. **Establish Clear Documentation**
   - **Loan Agreement or Dividend Payment:** If you're treating the transfer as a loan from the business to yourself, draft a formal loan agreement outlining the terms of repayment, interest (if applicable), and purpose. If it's a dividend or owner's draw, ensure it's legally allowed and properly documented in your business records.
   - **Bookkeeping Records:** Record the transaction properly in your accounting system. For example:
     - If it's a loan, record it as a loan receivable on the business side and a loan payable on the personal side.
     - If it's an owner's draw or dividend, ensure it’s properly accounted for and does not exceed your business’s retained earnings.

### 2. **Use Formal Channels**
   - **Transfer through Formal Means:** Avoid simply wiring money between accounts without documentation. Instead, treat it as an official transaction. For example:
     - **Loan Documentation:** Provide your bank with a copy of the loan agreement or formal record of the transaction when applying for the pledge loan.
     - **Dividend/Draw Justification:** If the transfer is for personal use, ensure it is legally justifiable under corporate governance rules (especially for an LLC or corporation).

### 3. **Communicate with the Bank**
   - **Explain the Transaction:** When you approach Navy Federal for the pledge loan, explain that the funds come from a business transaction, whether it’s a loan from your business or a personal distribution. Be transparent about the source of funds to avoid any red flags.
   - **Provide Supporting Documents:** While banks don’t typically require documentation at the point of deposit for personal transfers, they may ask for an explanation later during their due diligence process, especially if you’re applying for business capital in the future. Having clear records upfront will streamline this.

### 4. **Separate Business and Personal Use**
   - **Avoid Personal Use of Business Funds:** Even if the money is in your personal account, ensure that it’s used for the specific purpose of the pledge loan, and not for personal expenses, to avoid the appearance of commingling.

### 5. **Seek Professional Advice**
   - **Tax and Legal Considerations:** Work with your accountant or legal counsel to ensure that the transaction complies with IRS regulations and any banking rules specific to Navy Federal.

By documenting the transaction properly and being transparent with the bank, you're more likely to avoid issues with co-mingling and ensure that the transfer is seen as a legitimate step towards obtaining future business capital.
