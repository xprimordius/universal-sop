### ⚠️ POSSIBLE TRT-RELATED CAUSES:

#### 1. **Acneiform Eruptions**
- **Why:** TRT increases sebum (oil) production, especially in androgen-sensitive areas.
- **Where:** Common on back, chest, abdomen, shoulders.
- **Look:** Small, red or skin-colored bumps. Sometimes itchy or tender.

#### 2. **Folliculitis**
- **Why:** Inflammation/infection of hair follicles triggered by increased oil or sweating.
- **Look:** Bumps may have a hair in the center, can look like acne, sometimes pus-filled.

#### 3. **Keratosis Pilaris (less likely)**
- **Why:** Dead skin clogging hair follicles. Not directly from TRT but may worsen with skin dryness or hormonal changes.
- **Look:** Rough, small bumps—more common on arms/thighs, less likely abdomen.

---

### ✅ ACTION STEPS:
1. **Avoid Irritation:** No tight waistbands or scratching.
2. **Cleanse Gently:** Use a non-comedogenic, mild antibacterial wash like benzoyl peroxide (2–5%) once daily.
3. **Topical Treatment:**
   - **For acne/folliculitis:** Try topical clindamycin or salicylic acid.
   - **If itchy:** Consider hydrocortisone (briefly) or antihistamines.
4. **Monitor Injection Technique (if applicable):**
   - Rotate sites, use alcohol swabs, avoid injecting near areas with breakouts.
5. **Hydration + Diet:** Stay hydrated, reduce processed oils/sugars that spike sebum.

- Triggers skin bacteria (like *Cutibacterium acnes*) to multiply.
- **Internally**, this reflects high androgen activity, especially at skin receptor sites.

- TRT may **disrupt your T:E2 ratio**, especially if aromatization (T → E2) spikes.
