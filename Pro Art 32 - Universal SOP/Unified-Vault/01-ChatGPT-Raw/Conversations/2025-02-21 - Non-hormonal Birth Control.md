## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, there are several **non-hormonal** birth control options that do not affect hormones:

### **Barrier Methods**  
1. **Copper IUD (Paragard)** – A long-term, highly effective option that prevents sperm from fertilizing an egg through copper’s natural spermicidal effect. Lasts up to **10 years**.  
2. **Male Condoms** – Widely available and protect against both pregnancy and STIs.  
3. **Female Condoms** – A polyurethane pouch inserted into the vagina before intercourse.  
4. **Diaphragm** – A shallow, dome-shaped device placed over the cervix, used with spermicide.  
5. **Cervical Cap** – Similar to a diaphragm but smaller, requires spermicide for effectiveness.  
6. **Sponge** – A soft, disposable foam sponge containing spermicide, inserted into the vagina before sex.  

### **Permanent Solutions**  
7. **Tubal Ligation ("Getting tubes tied")** – A surgical procedure that blocks or seals the fallopian tubes.  
8. **Vasectomy (for male partners)** – A minor surgical procedure that prevents sperm from entering the semen.  

### **Fertility Awareness Methods (FAMs)**  
9. **Tracking Ovulation** – Avoiding sex or using a barrier method on fertile days. Includes:  
   - **Basal Body Temperature (BBT) tracking**  
   - **Cervical mucus monitoring**  
   - **Calendar method**

### **Risks of Copper IUD (Paragard)**  
The **Copper IUD** is a highly effective non-hormonal birth control option, but it comes with potential risks and side effects:  

#### **Common Side Effects:**  
- **Heavier periods** – Many users experience increased menstrual bleeding and cramping, especially in the first 3–6 months.  
- **Longer periods** – Menstrual cycles may become longer than usual.  
- **Increased cramping** – Some users report more intense period cramps.  

#### **Less Common but Serious Risks:**  
- **IUD expulsion** – The device can be pushed out of the uterus (occurs in about 2–10% of cases, usually in the first year).  
- **Uterine perforation** – Rare (<0.1% of cases), but the IUD can puncture the uterine wall during insertion.  
- **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)** – A small risk (1%) if an undiagnosed STI is present at the time of insertion.  
- **Copper allergy or sensitivity** – Rare but can cause reactions in some individuals.  
- **Displacement** – The IUD can move, requiring removal or repositioning.
