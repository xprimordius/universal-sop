### ✅ Step-by-Step Action Plan

#### 1. **Refile Your Identity Theft Report (Properly)**
- File an **FTC Identity Theft Report** via [https://identitytheft.gov](https://identitytheft.gov)
- Attach a **notarized affidavit** declaring fraud
- Include proof: your ID, utility bill (proof of residence), and the **account in question**
- Check box that says: “I did not open or authorize this account.”

#### 2. **File a Police Report**
- Required for strong legal leverage.
- File in your local jurisdiction or where the fraud occurred.
- Attach the report to your identity theft packet.

#### 3. **Submit an Official ID Theft Dispute to the Bank**
- Address the fraud department in writing via certified mail.
- Include:
  - FTC report
  - Police report
  - Your affidavit
  - Proof of identity
  - Specific demand for:
    - Account closure
    - Complete disassociation from your SSN and credit
    - Rescission of any 1099-C tied to your name

#### 4. **Challenge the 1099-C with IRS**
- File **Form 14039** (IRS Identity Theft Affidavit)
- Send a letter of explanation, police/FTC reports, and 1099-C copy to the IRS.
- This flags the tax liability as fraudulently generated.

#### 5. **Dispute with Credit Bureaus Using 15 U.S.C. § 1681g(e)**
- Send full ID theft packet to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
- Demand removal of the account as fraudulent under FCRA § 605B.
- They **must block** the item within **4 business days** if properly filed.

---

### 🛑 Key Rebuttal to the Bank’s Claim

Even if someone:
- Opened the account *in person*
- Made *one payment* with a bank account…

That **does not prove it was you** under federal law.

#### Cited Violations and Legal Points:
- **15 U.S.C. § 1681g(e):** Right to request method of verification.
- **15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(6):** Furnisher must correct and delete inaccurate data.
- **15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a):** CRA must reinvestigate upon dispute.
- **15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2(a):** Mandatory block of identity theft items with proper documentation.
- Failure to provide **verification method** and **signed application copy** = violation.
- Payment made from a bank account is **not proof of identity**, especially if the account is closed/fraudulent.

If the bank refuses:
- File a complaint with the **CFPB** and **State Attorney General**
- Attach all documentation
- Clearly cite **willful FCRA noncompliance** and **false tax implication via fraudulent 1099-C**

## 👤 You

Is it a furnisher if it’s just the bank itself? No credit bureaus

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes — the **bank is a “furnisher”** under the law **if it reports to the credit bureaus**, but if no credit bureaus are involved and it’s only about the **bank’s internal records or the 1099-C**, then the bank is acting solely as:

- **a data controller (internal)**
- **an issuing institution (originator)**
- **a 1099-C issuer (to the IRS)**

---

### 🔍 Key Distinction:

| Role | Definition | Applies Here? |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Furnisher** | An entity that **reports** account info to credit bureaus | ❌ *Only if no CRAs involved* |
| **Creditor** | An entity that **issues credit/accounts** | ✅ Yes (the bank is this) |
| **1099-C Issuer** | An entity reporting debt cancellation to IRS | ✅ Yes |

---

### ✅ If No Credit Reporting Involved:
You shift focus from **FCRA** to:

1. **IRS Fraud** (Incorrect 1099-C):
   - File **Form 14039**
   - Request **correction or withdrawal** of the 1099-C
   - Attach police + FTC reports

2. **Bank Internal Fraud Dispute**:
   - Use your **ID theft affidavit + reports**
   - Send a formal written demand to:
     - Remove account from internal records
     - Retract 1099-C
     - Provide *full evidence* of identity verification (video, ID scan, signature, IP logs)

3. **Consumer Protection Violations**:
   - If the bank refuses to disassociate the debt *after evidence of fraud*, it's a potential **violation of the Fair Credit Billing Act** or **state consumer fraud statutes**

---

### ⚠️ Bottom Line:

If there’s **no credit reporting**, the bank is **not a furnisher**, but it **can still be challenged under fraud, identity theft, and IRS laws**. Focus shifts to:

- IRS (for tax impact)
- Internal fraud investigation failure
- State and federal consumer fraud laws
