In The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (commonly known as Mormons), Jesus Christ is recognized as the central figure of their faith, and they view Him as the literal Son of God, the Savior, and Redeemer of the world. The name "Jesus" in Hebrew is **Yeshua**, which means "salvation," and this aligns with their understanding of Jesus as the one who brings salvation to humanity.

Mormons identify Jesus as **Christ**, which comes from the Greek word *Christos*, meaning "the Anointed One." This title reflects their belief that Jesus was chosen and anointed to fulfill the divine mission of atonement, enabling humanity to return to God through His sacrifice, resurrection, and teachings.

Some key points of their belief about Jesus include:
1. **Pre-Mortal Existence**: Mormons believe Jesus existed before His mortal birth, serving as Jehovah in the Old Testament and playing a central role in God's plan for humanity.
2. **Literal Son of God**: They teach that Jesus is the literal Son of God the Father and that His divine nature allows Him to fulfill His role as Savior.
3. **Atonement and Resurrection**: Jesus' atonement is central to their faith. They believe He suffered for the sins of humanity in Gethsemane and on the cross, and through His resurrection, He overcame death, making it possible for all to be resurrected.
4. **Second Coming**: Mormons believe that Jesus will return to earth during the Second Coming to reign and complete His work as the Redeemer.

In essence, while Mormons recognize Jesus as **Yeshua** (in His original Hebrew context) and **Christ** (as the Greek title for "Anointed One"), their views emphasize His divinity, His role in God's plan, and His centrality to their faith.
