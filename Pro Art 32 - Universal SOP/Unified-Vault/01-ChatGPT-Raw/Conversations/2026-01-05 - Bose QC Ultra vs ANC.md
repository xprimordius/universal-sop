## Sound-System Architecture — Planar vs Dynamic vs Consumer Tuned

| Feature | Planar Magnetic | Dynamic Drivers (AirPods / most headphones) | Consumer Tuned Systems (Tesla / ANC phones) |
|---------|------------------|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Driver Type** | Flat diaphragm with magnets | Cone/voice coil | Typically dynamic drivers with DSP tuning |
| **Bass Character** | Tight, accurate, extended | Punchy, often colored | Emphasized, “boomy” |
| **Detail / Transparency** | High | Moderate | Tuned for impact |
| **Frequency Response** | Wide, flat potential | V-shaped to please | Sculpted for enjoyment |
| **Distortion** | Low at high SPL | Higher at high SPL | Often boosted |
| **ANC / Noise Control** | *None by default* | Possible with ANC tech | DSP cancels noise and boosts impact |

### What *planar* actually means
Planar magnetic drivers use a **thin diaphragm with evenly distributed magnets** (no traditional cone). That gives you:
- **More accurate bass control** — less boom, more *defined sub-bass*  
- **Lower distortion at loud levels**  
- **Better micro-detail** — you hear tiny reverb tails, layering, transients  
- **Consistency across ear surface** — fewer “sweet spots” in sound

Dynamic drivers (AirPods / ANC headphones) are:
- **Small cones** that inherently color sound  
- Tuned to **boost bass and treble** for fun — not flat for mix translation

Tesla audio (DSP-rich car sound) is:
- **Multi-speaker spatial optimization** with heavy EQ shaping  
- Very pleasing, not reference-true
