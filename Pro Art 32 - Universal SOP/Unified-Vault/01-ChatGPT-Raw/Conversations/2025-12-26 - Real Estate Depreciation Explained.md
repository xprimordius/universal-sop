**What that claim is talking about (plain English):**

**Headline:** *Real estate depreciation can offset W-2 income — but only if you qualify.*

### The Core Idea
- Rental real estate gives you a **paper expense** called **depreciation**.
- Depreciation reduces **taxable income**, even if the property makes cash.
- Those losses are called **passive losses**.

### The Catch (This Is Where Social Media Lies)
Normally:
- **Passive losses ≠ W-2 income**
- You **cannot** offset W-2 wages with rental losses **by default**.

### When It *Does* Work
You can reduce or eliminate W-2 taxes **only if one of these is true**:

1. **Real Estate Professional Status (REPS)**  
   - >750 hours/year in real estate  
   - More than 50% of working time in real estate  
   → Losses become **non-passive** and can offset W-2 income

2. **Short-Term Rental (STR) Loophole**  
   - Avg stay ≤ 7 days (or ≤ 30 with services)  
   - Material participation (100+ hrs, or more than anyone else)  
   → Losses treated as **active**, can offset W-2

3. **High-income phase-out exception (small)**  
   - If AGI < $100k (phases out by $150k)  
   - Up to **$25k** of losses may offset W-2  
   → Most high earners don’t qualify

### How Depreciation Gets So Big
- **Cost segregation** accelerates depreciation  
- Front-loads 5–15 years of deductions into year 1  
- Often used with STRs or REPS to wipe out taxes

### The Truth Bomb

- The **Instagram version is incomplete**
- Without REPS or STR rules, **you cannot touch W-2 income**
