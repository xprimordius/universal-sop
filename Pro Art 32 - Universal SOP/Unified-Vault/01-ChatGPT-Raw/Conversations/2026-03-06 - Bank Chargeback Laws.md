**Statute:** 15 U.S.C. §1666i  
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666i  

This section allows a consumer to raise **claims or defenses against the card issuer**.

**Direct quote**

> “A cardholder may assert **against the card issuer all claims and defenses arising out of any transaction** in which the credit card is used as a method of payment.”

Meaning:

If the merchant committed:

- fraud  
- misrepresentation  
- bait-and-switch  
- non-delivery  

those **same claims can legally be asserted against the bank**.

# 4. Regulation Requiring Banks to Investigate Disputes
### entity["law","Regulation Z","12 CFR §1026.13"]

Source:  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.13  

**Direct quote**

> “If, after conducting a **reasonable investigation**, a creditor determines that no billing error occurred… the creditor shall mail or deliver to the consumer an explanation.”

This regulation implements the **Fair Credit Billing Act dispute process**.

---

# Key Legal Principle Combining These Laws

Under federal law:

1. **Fraud or deceptive conduct by merchants is illegal**  
   (FTC Act §45)

2. **Banks cannot engage in unfair or deceptive practices themselves**  
   (Dodd-Frank UDAAP §5531)

3. **Consumers can assert merchant fraud against the card issuer**  
   (TILA §1666i)

4. **Banks must conduct a reasonable investigation before denying disputes**  
   (Regulation Z §1026.13)

---

# Simple Legal Framework

If a bank:

- ignores fraud evidence  
- relies only on merchant claims  
- denies disputes without investigation  
- refuses to correct charges for non-delivery or deception  

those actions can potentially violate:

| Law | Purpose |
|---|---|
FTC Act §45 | prohibits deceptive practices |
CFPA / UDAAP §5531 | prohibits unfair or abusive financial conduct |
TILA §1666i | allows claims against the card issuer |
Regulation Z §1026.13 | requires dispute investigation |
