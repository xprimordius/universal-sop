**Yahweh** is the name of the God of Israel in the Hebrew Bible (Old Testament). It is considered the most sacred name of God in Judaism and is often represented as **YHWH** (the Tetragrammaton) because ancient Hebrew did not include vowels. Due to its holiness, observant Jews traditionally do not pronounce the name and instead use substitutes like **Adonai** ("Lord") or **HaShem** ("The Name"). 

The name **Yahweh** is derived from the Hebrew root **"hayah" (היה)**, meaning **"to be" or "to exist,"** often interpreted as "I Am Who I Am" (Exodus 3:14). This signifies God’s eternal, self-existent nature.

In Christianity, Yahweh is recognized as the God of the Old Testament, and some Christian groups use the name in worship. However, in most Christian traditions, **"Lord"** is used instead of Yahweh in translations of the Bible.
