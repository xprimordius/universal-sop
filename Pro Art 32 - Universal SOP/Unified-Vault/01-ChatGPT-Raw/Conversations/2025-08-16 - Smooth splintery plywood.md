Here’s the fastest way to smooth splintery plywood to a solid, clean finish:

### 1. **Surface Prep**
- **Inspect**: Mark areas with splinters or rough edges.  
- **Trim**: Use a sharp utility knife to shave off sticking splinters.

### 2. **Sanding Sequence**
- Start with **80–100 grit** sandpaper (orbital sander preferred) to knock down roughness.  
- Progress to **150–180 grit** for smoothing.  
- Finish with **220 grit** for a clean, paint- or stain-ready surface.  
*(Always sand with the grain to avoid tear-out.)*

### 3. **Fill Voids / Gaps**
- Apply **wood filler** or **wood putty** to knots, chips, or rough layers.  
- Let it dry fully, then sand flush (start 150 → finish 220 grit).  

### 4. **Seal the Surface**
- Wipe with a **damp rag** (raises grain slightly). Let dry, then do a light sand with 220 grit.  
- Apply a **sanding sealer or primer** (optional but best for paint/stain).  

### 5. **Final Finish**
- For **paint**: Use primer → sand lightly → apply 2–3 coats paint.  
- For **clear finish**: Apply polyurethane, lacquer, or varnish (lightly sand 220 grit between coats).  

👉 Key tip: Don’t skip grits, and always finish at least to 220. That’s what makes plywood feel like solid wood, not splintery sheet.
