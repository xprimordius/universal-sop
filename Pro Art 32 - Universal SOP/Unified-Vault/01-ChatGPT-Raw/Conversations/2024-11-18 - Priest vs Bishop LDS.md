In the **Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS)**, a **priest** and a **bishop** hold distinct roles and responsibilities, both within the **Aaronic Priesthood** and broader church leadership. Here’s a concise comparison:

### **Priest**:
1. **Priesthood Office**: Aaronic Priesthood (the preparatory priesthood).
2. **Responsibilities**:
   - Administer the sacrament (bread and water).
   - Baptize individuals.
   - Confer the Aaronic Priesthood and ordain others to offices within it (e.g., deacon, teacher, or priest).
   - Preach, teach, and exhort members of the church.
3. **Eligibility**:
   - Male members aged 16 and older, considered worthy by their bishop, are ordained as priests.
4. **Scope**: Local congregational duties, typically within their ward or branch.

---

### **Bishop**:
1. **Priesthood Office**: Melchizedek Priesthood (higher priesthood), though bishops are also part of the **Aaronic Priesthood presidency** for their ward.
2. **Responsibilities**:
   - Oversee the spiritual and temporal welfare of all ward members.
   - Preside over meetings, including sacrament services.
   - Provide counseling and guidance to members.
   - Oversee youth and Aaronic Priesthood programs.
   - Manage tithes and church finances for their ward.
   - Perform callings, interviews, and recommend ordinances (e.g., temple recommends).
3. **Eligibility**:
   - Male members holding the Melchizedek Priesthood who are spiritually mature and called by revelation through church leadership.
4. **Scope**: Presides over an entire ward (congregation) and serves as its spiritual leader.

---

### Key Differences:
- **Priests** serve within the Aaronic Priesthood, performing basic ordinances and preparing for higher responsibilities.
- **Bishops** are part of the Melchizedek Priesthood leadership, tasked with presiding over a ward, guiding its members, and managing both spiritual and temporal affairs.

Both roles are vital to the functioning of the church, but bishops have a much broader scope of authority and responsibility.
