## 🤖 ChatGPT

The controlling law is the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** — specifically **15 U.S.C. § 1681b(a)(3)(A)** and **§ 1681b(f)** — which governs when a company is legally allowed to pull your credit (create a “hard inquiry”).  

### ⚖️ Legal Basis
A company **may only obtain your credit report** if they have a **“permissible purpose.”**  
> **15 U.S.C. § 1681b(f):**  
> “A person shall not use or obtain a consumer report for any purpose unless… the consumer report is obtained for a purpose for which the consumer report is authorized to be furnished under this section.”

### 🚫 No Product or Service = No Permissible Purpose
Under **§ 1681b(a)(3)(A)**, a creditor can only pull your credit **in connection with a credit transaction that you initiated** or for which you gave **written authorization**.  
If:
- You **did not authorize** the inquiry, and  
- **No credit, product, or service** was provided or even legitimately applied for,  

then the inquiry **lacks permissible purpose** and must be **removed** as an **impermissible pull** — a direct FCRA violation.

### 🧾 Supporting FTC & CFPB Interpretations
The FTC and CFPB have both affirmed that:
- **“A creditor may not obtain a consumer report unless the consumer initiates a transaction or authorizes it.”**  
- “If a consumer report is obtained without a permissible purpose, it constitutes a violation of the FCRA.”

> **Law:** FCRA §1681b(a)(3)(A) + FTC LeFevre Opinion (1998)

### 2️⃣ **Misrepresentation of Purpose (“Hard” vs. “Soft” Pull)**
> If you were told or led to believe it was a *prequalification*, *soft pull*, or *business-only check*, but it reported as a *hard inquiry*, that’s **misrepresentation and impermissible use.**

> **Law:** FCRA §1681b(f); FTC Hinkle Opinion (1999)  
> *A report may only be obtained for the purpose expressly authorized by the consumer.*

> **Result:** Must be reclassified or deleted as an impermissible hard pull.

### 4️⃣ **No Personal Benefit or Liability Materialized**
> If the PG never derived personal benefit, never received funds, or the guarantee was never triggered, the inquiry **does not reflect a true consumer liability or benefit**.

> **Law:** §1681e(b) (accuracy) + CFPB Bulletin 2012-09
