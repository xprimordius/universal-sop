## 👤 You

Does lemon law for cars apply to Mn or CA only

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The **lemon law applies in both Minnesota (MN) and California (CA)** — but each state has its own version and criteria.  

### ⚖️ Minnesota Lemon Law
- **Covers:** New passenger vehicles, pickup trucks, vans, and SUVs under **2 years or 24,000 miles** (whichever comes first).  
- **Qualifies if:**  
  - The same defect is repaired **4+ times** or  
  - The vehicle is out of service for **30+ cumulative days**.  
- **Administered by:** Minnesota Attorney General’s Office.  
- **Statute:** Minn. Stat. §§ 325F.665–325F.70.

### ⚖️ California Lemon Law (Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act)
- **Covers:** New and certain used vehicles still under **manufacturer warranty**.  
- **Qualifies if:**  
  - The same defect is repaired **2–4+ times** (depending on safety risk), or  
  - The vehicle is out of service for **30+ cumulative days**.  
- **Administered by:** California Department of Consumer Affairs.  
- **Statute:** Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1793.2 et seq.

### ✅ Summary
| Feature | Minnesota | California |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Applies to | New vehicles | New + certain used (under warranty) |
| Mileage limit | 24,000 mi | None (while under warranty) |
| Time limit | 2 years | Duration of warranty |
| Out of service | 30+ days | 30+ days |
| Repair attempts | 4+ | 2–4+ |
| Governing body | MN AG | CA DCA |

## 🤖 ChatGPT

If the manufacturer or dealer says *“the EMF sound is how the car is”* — here’s how lemon law applies in both states:  

---

### ⚖️ 1. **Legal Core Principle**
A car qualifies as a *lemon* if a **manufacturer defect substantially impairs use, value, or safety** — even if the dealer claims it’s “normal.”  
- The key test: *Would a reasonable consumer consider the issue acceptable?*  
If the answer is no, it can still qualify.

---

### ⚙️ 2. **When They Claim “That’s Normal”**
- This statement does **not exempt them** from lemon law.  
- You can demand written confirmation that “this is normal operation.” That record is crucial — if they refuse to repair, it counts as a **repair attempt** under lemon law.  
- You must document every service visit and communication.

---

### 🧭 3. **If They Cannot Fix the EMF Defect**
- **Minnesota:** If 4+ attempts or 30+ days out of service and the defect still exists → eligible for **refund or replacement**.  
- **California:** If 2+ attempts for a *serious safety issue* or 4+ attempts for any issue, or 30+ days out of service → same outcome (refund or replacement).  

---

### 🔍 4. **Next Steps**
1. **Request a “Manufacturer Field Engineer Inspection.”** This triggers higher-level documentation.  
2. **Keep all service records.** Each visit strengthens your claim.  
3. **File a lemon law claim** with your state AG (MN) or Department of Consumer Affairs (CA) if they refuse further action.  
4. **Optional:** Hire a lemon law attorney — fees are paid by the manufacturer if you win, not you.
