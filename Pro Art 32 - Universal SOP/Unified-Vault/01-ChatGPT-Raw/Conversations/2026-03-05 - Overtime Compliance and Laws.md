## 👤 You

Is it illegal to have to work from after after hours to stay within compliance at work standards because they over burden you at work training people in a different department

Work is strict about not allowing over40 hours per week and the person is not getting at home work

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short Answer:**  
Yes — this **can be illegal** in the U.S. if the employer **knows or should reasonably know** the employee must work off-the-clock to meet required duties.

---

## Key Law: entity["law","Fair Labor Standards Act","United States wage and hour law"] (FLSA)

Under the FLSA:

- **All hours worked must be paid**.
- This includes **work performed outside scheduled hours** if the employer **suffers or permits it**.

Official rule (U.S. Department of Labor):

> “Work not requested but suffered or permitted is work time that must be paid.”  
Source:  
https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/22-flsa-hours-worked

---

## When It Becomes Illegal

It becomes unlawful if **all three occur**:

1. **Workload cannot realistically be completed in 40 hours**  
2. Employee must perform work after hours to meet expectations/compliance  
3. Employer **knows or should know** it is happening

If so, the employer **must pay for that time**, even if they have a policy against overtime.

Courts consistently rule:

> **Employers cannot avoid paying overtime by telling employees not to work overtime while still assigning excessive work.**

---

## Common Violations

Illegal scenarios include:

- Requiring employees to:
  - finish reports at home
  - answer emails after hours
  - prepare training materials off-clock
  - perform compliance work off the clock

while also saying:

- “You cannot record overtime.”

This is known as **off-the-clock work violations**.

---

## Important Distinction

| Situation | Legal Status |
|---|---|
Employee voluntarily works extra without employer knowledge | Usually legal |
Employer workload makes overtime unavoidable | **Illegal if unpaid** |
Employer forbids overtime but expects work finished | **Illegal if unpaid work occurs** |

---

## Key Court Principle

From U.S. Department of Labor enforcement:

> If an employer **knows or has reason to believe** work is being performed, they must count the time as hours worked.

Source:  
https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/hoursworked/sufferpermit.asp

---

## Example That Matches Your Scenario

Employee:

- Works full 40 hours
- Must **train another department**
- Still required to meet normal duties
- Cannot record overtime
- Finishes work **after hours at home**

If management knows this is necessary → **wage violation**.

---

## What the Employee Should Do (Strategic)

1. **Document workload**
   - tasks assigned
   - training responsibilities
   - time required

2. **Notify manager in writing**

Example:

> “Based on current workload and training responsibilities, completing all required tasks within 40 hours may not be possible. Please advise how you would like priorities handled if overtime is not permitted.”

3. **Do not work unpaid hours** without documenting.

---

An employee can recover:

- **Unpaid wages**
- **Overtime pay**
- **Liquidated damages (double wages)**
