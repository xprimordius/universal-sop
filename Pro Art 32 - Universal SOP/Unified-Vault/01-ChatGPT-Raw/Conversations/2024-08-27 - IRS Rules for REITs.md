It seems like you're asking about IRS rules related to Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs). REITs are companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate. They are a popular investment vehicle because they offer the benefits of owning real estate without having to actually manage properties, and they provide liquidity since shares of many REITs are traded on major stock exchanges.

Here’s a brief overview of key IRS rules regarding REITs:

1. **Income Distribution Requirement**: REITs must distribute at least 90% of their taxable income to shareholders annually in the form of dividends. This is one of the primary reasons REITs are attractive to investors seeking regular income.

2. **Income Sources**: At least 75% of a REIT's gross income must come from real estate-related sources, including rents from real property, interest on mortgages financing real property, or sales of real estate. Additionally, at least 95% of a REIT's gross income must come from these real estate sources or other passive income like dividends and interest.

3. **Asset Composition**: At least 75% of a REIT’s assets must be invested in real estate, cash, and government securities.

4. **Shareholder Requirements**: A REIT must have at least 100 shareholders, and no more than 50% of its shares can be held by five or fewer individuals during the last half of the taxable year.

5. **Corporate Taxation**: REITs can avoid corporate income tax at the federal level by adhering to the above requirements, particularly the distribution of 90% of taxable income. If they fail to meet these requirements, they may lose their REIT status and be taxed as a regular corporation.

6. **Taxation of Dividends**: For shareholders, dividends received from REITs are generally taxed as ordinary income, although a portion may be taxed as capital gains or qualify for the Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act.
