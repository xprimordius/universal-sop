## 👤 You

Is it safe to be simmering rotisserie chicken bone soup for2 and a half days?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, but only under **strict conditions** to ensure food safety:

1. **Keep it at a Safe Temperature** – The soup must be kept at **above 165°F (ideally 190–210°F)** continuously. Bacteria multiply rapidly between **40°F and 140°F**, so never let it cool to room temperature.

2. **Boil it Periodically** – If you're simmering for 2+ days, bring it to a full boil **at least once every 12 hours** to kill potential bacteria.

3. **Use a Clean Lid** – Keep the pot covered to prevent contamination and excessive evaporation.

4. **Watch for Signs of Spoilage** – If it smells sour, has an unusual texture, or has any slimy residue, **discard it immediately**.

5. **Proper Storage If Not Cooking Continuously** – If you stop simmering for any period, immediately **refrigerate (≤40°F)** or freeze it.

If done correctly, **bone broth can be simmered safely for days**, but **constant heat and sanitation** are critical.

Yes, you can keep it **uncovered**, but you need to manage evaporation and contamination risks carefully:

1. **Maintain a Rolling Simmer (190–210°F)** – The constant heat will prevent bacterial growth.
2. **Add Water as Needed** – Since leaving it uncovered leads to evaporation, top it off with **hot water** periodically to maintain volume.
3. **Keep the Cooking Area Clean** – Avoid dust, insects, or anything falling into the pot.
4. **Skim Impurities Regularly** – Foam and scum can build up; skim them off to maintain broth clarity and cleanliness.
5. **Check for Spoilage** – If it develops an off smell, slimy texture, or mold, discard immediately.

If you’re keeping it uncovered for **over 48 hours**, ensure it's **boiled thoroughly every 12 hours** to kill bacteria.

If using a lid makes your broth go bad, it likely means **too much moisture is being trapped**, leading to potential **anaerobic bacterial growth** (which thrives in low-oxygen environments). This could also create an environment where the broth **ferments** or develops off smells. 

### **Why This Happens With a Lid:**
1. **Condensation Drips Back In** – This can lead to unwanted bacterial buildup if the heat isn’t high enough.
2. **Trapped Heat Without Evaporation** – If the broth isn’t at a consistent high-enough simmer, bacteria can survive.
3. **Lower Oxygen Exchange** – Some bacteria thrive in low-oxygen, moist environments.

### **How to Prevent Spoilage Without Drying Out:**
- **Partially Cover the Pot** – Leave a small gap for **steam to escape** while reducing evaporation.
- **Monitor Heat Levels** – Keep it at a **constant simmer (190–210°F)** but not boiling aggressively.
- **Add Water Regularly** – Check levels every **4–6 hours** and top off with hot water as needed.
- **Use a Larger Pot** – More surface area can slow down excessive evaporation without needing a lid.

If you’re experiencing **spoilage with a lid**, it means **something in the cooking process needs adjusting**—usually heat, airflow, or sanitation. Try the **partially covered** method and monitor results.
