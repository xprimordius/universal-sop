## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer: No.**  
This panel **does NOT directly test for heavy metal toxicity**.

### Why
None of the listed biomarkers measure **specific heavy metals**. A true heavy-metal screen would explicitly include elements like:
- **Lead (Pb)**
- **Mercury (Hg)**
- **Arsenic (As)**
- **Cadmium (Cd)**
- (sometimes Aluminum, Nickel, Chromium)

Your list is **metabolic, hormonal, cardiovascular, liver, kidney, and inflammatory**—but **not elemental toxicology**.

### What this panel can do (indirect only)
Some markers may **hint at stress or organ burden**, but they **cannot diagnose heavy metals**:
- **ALT / AST / ALP / Bilirubin** → liver stress (many causes)
- **Creatinine / eGFR / BUN** → kidney function
- **CRP / Homocysteine** → inflammation / oxidative stress
- **CBC abnormalities** → nonspecific (many causes)

These are **non-specific** and **not reliable** for heavy metal detection.

### What you need for heavy metals (direct)
Ask for **one of these**:
1. **Blood Heavy Metals Panel** (most clinically accepted for lead, mercury)
2. **Urine Heavy Metals (with or without provocation)**  
3. **Hair Mineral Analysis** (screening only; confirm with blood/urine)

### Bottom line
- ❌ This test **does not check heavy metal toxicity**
- ✅ You need a **dedicated heavy metals panel**
- ⚠️ Do not infer heavy metals from liver/kidney markers alone
