### TL;DR — Why There Are No Big Cities on the Oregon/NorCal Coast

**1. Rugged Geography**  
- The coastline is steep, mountainous, and heavily forested.  
- Limited flat land for large-scale urban or industrial development.  
- Building infrastructure (roads, ports, airports) is expensive and difficult.

**2. Harsh Weather & Isolation**  
- Frequent rain, fog, and wind reduce appeal for large populations.  
- Historically poor transportation access — winding highways and few direct routes inland.  
- Isolation from major trade corridors limits economic scale.

**3. Economic Structure**  
- The main industries (fishing, timber, tourism) don’t require large cities.  
- No deep-water harbors comparable to San Francisco or Seattle for global shipping.

**4. Earthquake & Tsunami Risk**  
- The Cascadia Subduction Zone makes the entire coast highly seismic.  
- Major cities avoided the immediate coastline for safety and infrastructure resilience.

**5. Urban Growth Elsewhere**  
- Population and investment concentrated inland:  
  - **Portland**, **Eugene**, and **Sacramento** have better terrain and transport links (I-5 corridor).  
  - The coast serves more as a scenic, residential, or tourist region.

**Summary:**  
Nature, geography, and economics all conspired to keep the Oregon and NorCal coastlines stunning—but sparsely urbanized.
