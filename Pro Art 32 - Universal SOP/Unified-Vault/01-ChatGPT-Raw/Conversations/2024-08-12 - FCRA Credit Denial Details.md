# FCRA Credit Denial Details

---

## 👤 You

Does the FCRA say that I am entitled to know the details of why I was denied for credit?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) entitles you to know the details of why you were denied for credit. Specifically, under the FCRA, if you are denied credit (or any other adverse action is taken against you based on information in your credit report), the creditor must provide you with an "adverse action notice." This notice must include the following:

1. **The specific reason(s) for the denial**: The creditor is required to provide the precise reason(s) for the denial. If the denial was based on information from your credit report, they must explain which information led to the adverse decision.

2. **Information about the credit report**: The notice must include the name, address, and telephone number of the credit reporting agency (CRA) that supplied the report used in the decision.

3. **Your right to a free credit report**: You are entitled to a free copy of your credit report from the CRA that provided the information if you request it within 60 days of receiving the adverse action notice.

4. **Your right to dispute inaccurate information**: The notice will also inform you of your right to dispute any inaccurate information on your credit report with the CRA.

These provisions are designed to ensure transparency and give you the opportunity to understand and address any issues that may have led to the credit denial.

Certainly! Here are the relevant sections of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) that relate to your rights when you are denied credit and your right to information:

### 1. **15 U.S.C. § 1681m(a) - Duties of Users Taking Adverse Actions**
   - **Summary**: This section outlines the duties of those who take adverse action based on information in a consumer report. If a creditor or lender denies you credit based on your credit report, they must:
     - Notify you of the adverse action.
     - Provide you with the specific reasons for the denial or explain how you can obtain these reasons.
     - Inform you that the decision was based on information in your credit report.
     - Provide the name, address, and phone number of the credit reporting agency (CRA) that supplied the report.
     - Inform you of your right to obtain a free copy of your credit report from the CRA within 60 days.
     - Inform you of your right to dispute the accuracy or completeness of any information in your credit report with the CRA.

### 2. **15 U.S.C. § 1681g - Disclosures to Consumers**
   - **Summary**: This section gives consumers the right to access their credit report and understand the contents of the report. Specifically:
     - You have the right to request and obtain all the information in your credit file from a credit reporting agency.
     - You are entitled to receive information on the nature of the information in your report, including the sources of the information and the recipients of any reports provided in the last year (for most purposes) or two years (for employment purposes).

### 3. **15 U.S.C. § 1681j - Free Annual Disclosures**
   - **Summary**: This section entitles consumers to receive a free credit report once every 12 months from each of the three nationwide credit reporting agencies (Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion).
     - Additionally, if you are denied credit or another adverse action is taken against you, you have the right to a free copy of your credit report if requested within 60 days of receiving notice of the adverse action.

### 4. **15 U.S.C. § 1681i - Procedure in Case of Disputed Accuracy**
   - **Summary**: This section outlines the process for disputing inaccurate information on your credit report. If you find inaccuracies on your report, you have the right to dispute the information, and the CRA must investigate your dispute.
     - The CRA must correct or delete inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information within 30 days.
     - The CRA must notify the furnisher of the information about your dispute.

### Key Points:
- **Adverse Action Notice**: Creditors must provide specific reasons for credit denial and information about the credit report used.
- **Right to a Free Credit Report**: You can request a free credit report within 60 days of receiving an adverse action notice.
- **Dispute Rights**: You have the right to dispute inaccurate information on your credit report, and the CRA must investigate.

These provisions ensure that consumers are fully informed about the reasons for any adverse credit decisions and have the means to address any potential inaccuracies in their credit reports.
