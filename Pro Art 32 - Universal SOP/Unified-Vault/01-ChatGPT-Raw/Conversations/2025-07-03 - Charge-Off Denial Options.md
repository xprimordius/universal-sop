## 👤 You

Are there any laws that allows consumers to get credit approvals from institutions if the only reason for denial is prior account history, referencing charge off accounts at Chase from 8 years ago ago

They’ve refused all written disputes and reconsideration requests

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**No Law Forces Approvals Based on Past Charge-Offs Alone**  
There is **no U.S. law** that *forces* a lender (including Chase) to approve credit applications even if the only reason for denial is prior account history (e.g., charge-offs, defaults).

### Key Points:
1. **Lenders Have Discretion:**  
Lenders can legally deny credit based on *legitimate prior account history*, including charge-offs—even if they’re old or paid. They’re allowed to weigh risk as they see fit.

2. **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA):**  
Negative items like charge-offs *must* be removed from credit reports after **7 years**. However:
- Internal bank records **aren’t bound** by FCRA time limits.  
- Chase can retain internal records indefinitely and deny based on them.
  
3. **Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA):**
You have the right to a specific reason for denial—but there's no law requiring reversal of their decision based on charge-offs.

4. **Reconsideration Exhausted:**  
If Chase has denied disputes and reconsideration requests, you’ve likely exhausted all reasonable internal remedies.

---

### **Your Only Options:**
1. **Switch Banks:**  
Apply elsewhere—many banks won’t see Chase’s internal notes.

2. **Executive Office Escalation (Final Attempt):**  
Send a formal, factual goodwill request to Chase’s **Executive Office** (not customer service) asking for manual reconsideration.

3. **CFPB Complaint (Low Odds but Pressure Tactic):**  
File a CFPB complaint alleging *unfair treatment* or *lack of clear reconsideration process*. This won’t force approval but may trigger review.

---

### **Important:**  
Chase is legally allowed to permanently deny based on *internal history*. No law overrides their internal lending policy.

---

**Summary:** No law forces Chase to approve you after past charge-offs, even if they’re 8+ years old and off your credit report. Their denial is legal. Solution: Move to lenders without that internal history or escalate one last time via their Executive Office.
