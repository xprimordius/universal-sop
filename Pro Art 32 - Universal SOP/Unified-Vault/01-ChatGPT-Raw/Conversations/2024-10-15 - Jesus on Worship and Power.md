# Jesus on Worship and Power

---

## 👤 You

Where does Jesus say not to worship him, as everything he can do, we can do too; as the infinite light and power of god is within us all

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

There are several passages in the Bible where Jesus emphasizes the power within his followers and points to a deeper relationship with God rather than focusing solely on worshiping him. Although Jesus never explicitly tells people *not* to worship him, he consistently redirects attention to faith, inner transformation, and unity with God. Some key references include:

1. **John 14:12**  
   > *"Very truly I tell you, whoever believes in me will do the works I have been doing, and they will do even greater things than these, because I am going to the Father."*  
   - Here, Jesus emphasizes that his followers can perform the same miracles he did—if not greater—through faith and alignment with God’s power within.

2. **Luke 17:21**  
   > *"Nor will people say, ‘Here it is,’ or ‘There it is,’ because the kingdom of God is within you."*  
   - This suggests that the divine presence or power is not external but already present within each person.

3. **Matthew 6:9-10 (The Lord’s Prayer)**  
   > *"Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven."*  
   - Jesus teaches his followers to pray directly to God, focusing on aligning themselves with God’s will, reinforcing the idea that power flows from God through them.

4. **John 10:34-36**  
   > *"Is it not written in your Law, 'I have said you are "gods"'? If he called them 'gods,' to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be set aside—what about the one whom the Father set apart as his very own and sent into the world?"*  
   - Jesus quotes Psalm 82:6 to illustrate the divine potential within all people, further emphasizing that they are connected to God's power.

In these verses, Jesus shifts the focus from himself to faith, inner divinity, and unity with God. His teachings suggest that his followers can access divine power within themselves, implying that worship should be directed toward God and lived out through love and action.
