## 🤖 ChatGPT

No, it is not legal for a bank to deny your credit application and then refuse to provide specific reasons for the denial under the guise of "security purposes."

Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and its implementing Regulation B, creditors are required to provide applicants with a notice of adverse action that includes either the specific reasons for the credit denial or a notice of the right to request those reasons within 60 days. citeturn0search2 This requirement ensures transparency and allows applicants to understand and address the factors that led to the denial.

Similarly, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) mandates that if a credit decision is based on information from a consumer reporting agency, the creditor must inform the applicant of this fact and provide the name and contact information of the agency. citeturn0search12

If a bank fails to comply with these requirements, you have the right to file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) or consult with a legal professional to explore further action.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

This denial letter **does not meet the full specificity requirements** under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) Regulation B [12 CFR §1002.9(b)(2)].

### Why:
- **"Unable to verify information on application"** is **too vague**. The law requires either:
  1. Specific reasons (e.g., "unable to verify stated income via third-party database"), or
  2. A **clear notice** that the applicant may request those specific reasons within 60 days.

This letter **fails to do both**:
- No actionable path is given for how to request further detail.
- Upon calling, they reportedly **refused** to clarify — which contradicts regulatory guidance.

### Legal Compliance Required:
- Under **Reg B**, you must be told:
  - The **principal reasons** (not codes or vague terms) for denial.
  - Your right to request a **detailed explanation**.

### What You Can Do:
1. **Request a Written Statement of Reasons**:
   - Send a certified letter or email explicitly invoking your rights under *12 CFR §1002.9(a)(2)*.
   - Demand a specific explanation of which item(s) could not be verified.

2. **Report Noncompliance**:
   - File a complaint with the **CFPB**: [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/)
   - Reference their failure to provide “specific reasons” as required under ECOA.
