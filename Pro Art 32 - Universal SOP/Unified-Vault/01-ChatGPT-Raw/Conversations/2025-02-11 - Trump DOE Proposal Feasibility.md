## 🤖 ChatGPT

An **incumbency agreement** (or **incumbency certificate**) is a corporate document that certifies the identities and authority of a company’s officers or directors. It does **not** legally bind an individual to the company but confirms their official role and authority to act on behalf of the company, especially in matters like signing contracts or executing transactions.

### **Does an Incumbency Agreement Apply to Electronic Signatures?**
Yes, but only in specific contexts:
1. **Authorization to Sign Electronically** – If an officer is listed in the **incumbency certificate** as having the authority to sign agreements, that authority typically extends to **electronic signatures**, provided it complies with company policies and relevant e-signature laws (e.g., **ESIGN Act** in the U.S.).
   
2. **Proof of Validity** – In disputes over electronic signatures, an incumbency certificate can serve as **evidence** that the signer had the legal authority to execute the document.

3. **Third-Party Verification** – Banks, investors, and other entities may request an incumbency certificate to **confirm the legitimacy** of electronic agreements signed on behalf of the company.

### **Limitations:**
- **Does not create new obligations** – It only verifies authority.
- **Company policies may require additional approvals** – Some companies require dual signatures or board approvals for certain contracts, even if electronic signing is used.
- **Laws governing electronic signatures still apply** – The ESIGN Act (U.S.), eIDAS (EU), or other local laws dictate the enforceability of e-signatures.

### **Bottom Line:**
An incumbency certificate supports **electronic signatures** by confirming an individual’s authority to sign, but it does **not** inherently "bind" someone to the company in a contractual sense beyond their corporate role.
