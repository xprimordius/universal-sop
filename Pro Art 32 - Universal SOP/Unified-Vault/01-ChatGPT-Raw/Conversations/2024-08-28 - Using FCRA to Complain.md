# Using FCRA to Complain

---

## 👤 You

can you tell me more about writing to the attorney general with FCRA 617 to get their help to remove stubborn bureaus and creditors that are negatively reporting without providing sufficient validation / verification / methods of verifications upon multiple requests

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Writing to the Attorney General (AG) with reference to FCRA Section 617 can be an effective strategy when dealing with stubborn credit bureaus and creditors who fail to provide proper validation or verification of disputed items on your credit report. Here's how you can approach this:

### 1. **Understand FCRA Section 617:**
   - **FCRA Section 617 (15 U.S.C. § 1681o)** primarily deals with civil liability for negligent noncompliance with the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). If a credit bureau or creditor negligently fails to comply with the requirements of the FCRA, they can be held liable for actual damages, legal fees, and, in some cases, punitive damages.

### 2. **Identify the Violations:**
   - Before writing to the Attorney General, clearly identify how the credit bureaus or creditors are violating the FCRA. Common violations include:
     - **Failure to Provide Validation:** When a creditor does not validate the debt upon request.
     - **Insufficient Verification:** When the credit bureau or creditor fails to provide proper verification methods.
     - **Lack of Response to Disputes:** When the credit bureau or creditor does not respond to disputes within the required 30-day period.
     - **Continuing to Report Inaccurate Information:** When inaccurate information is still being reported despite repeated disputes.

### 3. **Gather Evidence:**
   - Collect all documentation related to your disputes, including:
     - Copies of your dispute letters sent to the credit bureaus and creditors.
     - Any responses (or lack thereof) received from them.
     - Evidence of inaccurate or unverifiable information being reported.
     - Proof of mail delivery (e.g., certified mail receipts) to demonstrate that they received your dispute letters.

### 4. **Drafting the Letter:**
   - **Header:**
     - Include your full name, address, and contact information.
     - Address the letter to the Attorney General’s office in your state.
   - **Introduction:**
     - Briefly explain that you are writing to request their assistance in enforcing the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) due to ongoing violations by credit bureaus/creditors.
   - **Body:**
     - **Detail the Violations:** Outline the specific sections of the FCRA that have been violated (e.g., Section 611 regarding dispute procedures, Section 617 for negligent noncompliance).
     - **Describe the Issues:** Clearly state the problems you've encountered, such as the failure to validate or verify the disputed items, and provide a summary of your attempts to resolve the matter directly with the bureaus/creditors.
     - **Attach Evidence:** Mention that you’ve included copies of all relevant correspondence, dispute letters, and evidence of their non-compliance.
   - **Request for Assistance:**
     - Request that the Attorney General’s office intervene to ensure that your rights under the FCRA are upheld.
     - Ask them to take appropriate legal action to compel the credit bureaus/creditors to comply with the FCRA, including the removal of any inaccurate or unverified information.
   - **Closing:**
     - Thank the Attorney General for their time and assistance.
     - Include a formal closing (e.g., “Sincerely,”) followed by your signature.

### 5. **Follow Up:**
   - After sending the letter, follow up with the Attorney General’s office to ensure they received it and inquire about any potential actions they may take. Be prepared to cooperate and provide any additional information they might request.

### 6. **Consider Additional Steps:**
   - If the Attorney General’s office is unable to resolve the issue, you may consider escalating the matter to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) or seeking legal advice to pursue further action against the credit bureaus or creditors.

### Sample Letter Structure:

```plaintext
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[City, State, ZIP Code]
[Email Address]
[Phone Number]
[Date]

Attorney General [Name]
Office of the Attorney General
[State of Your Residence]
[Office Address]

Dear Attorney General [Last Name],

Subject: Request for Assistance in Enforcing FCRA Section 617 - Ongoing Violations by Credit Bureaus/Creditors

I am writing to seek your assistance in enforcing my rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), specifically Section 617, due to ongoing violations by [Credit Bureau/Creditor Names]. Despite multiple requests, these entities have failed to properly validate or verify disputed information on my credit report, which continues to negatively impact my creditworthiness.

[Provide a brief overview of your disputes, including dates of correspondence and any responses received.]

I have made every reasonable effort to resolve these issues directly with the credit bureaus and creditors involved, but they have either refused to comply or have ignored my requests altogether. As a result, I am left with inaccurate and unverified information on my credit report, in clear violation of the FCRA.

I have enclosed copies of all relevant correspondence, dispute letters, and evidence of these violations for your review. I respectfully request that your office take the necessary legal action to ensure compliance with the FCRA and to compel these entities to remove the inaccurate information from my credit report.

Thank you for your time and attention to this matter. I look forward to your prompt response.

Sincerely,

[Your Full Name]
[Signature]
```

This approach should increase the likelihood of getting the attention of the credit bureaus and creditors, and it may lead to a more favorable outcome in your dispute resolution efforts.
