# Late Payments Reporting Duration

---

## 👤 You

Closed accounts stay on your credit report for up to 10
years since the date of last activity. Negative information such as late payments or collections, generally stay on your Equifax credit report for up to 7 years from the date of first delinquency.

In what situation would these late payments or charge offs report for after 7 years?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Late payments or charge-offs generally remain on your credit report for up to 7 years from the date of the first delinquency. However, there are some situations where this negative information could appear on your credit report for longer:

1. **Bankruptcy**: Chapter 7 bankruptcies can remain on your credit report for up to 10 years from the filing date. Chapter 13 bankruptcies typically stay for 7 years, but can sometimes appear for up to 10 years.

2. **Judgments**: Civil judgments can remain on your credit report for up to 7 years from the date the judgment was filed, but they can be renewed and potentially stay on longer if they are not resolved.

3. **Multiple Delinquencies**: If an account has multiple delinquencies, the 7-year period can restart with each new delinquency, potentially extending the time the negative information remains on the report.

4. **Re-aging**: Some creditors might re-age an account, changing the date of first delinquency to a later date, which can extend the reporting period.

5. **Credit Reporting Errors**: Sometimes errors occur, and negative information can stay on your report longer than it should. It's important to review your credit report regularly and dispute any inaccuracies.

If you find that negative information is lingering on your credit report beyond the allowed timeframes, you can dispute it with the credit reporting agency to have it removed.
