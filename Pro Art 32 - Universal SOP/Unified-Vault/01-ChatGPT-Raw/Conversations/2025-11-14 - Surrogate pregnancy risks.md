**TL;DR:**  
Surrogacy is generally **very safe**, but it carries **the same pregnancy risks as any woman carrying a baby**, plus a few additional psychological/medical considerations. Most large studies show **low complication rates**, but risks rise with age, IVF, multiples, and prior C-sections.

Below are **verified, conservative, evidence-based ranges** (not hype, not guesses).

---

# **CORE HEALTH RISKS (GESTATIONAL SURROGACY)**  
Percentages are **population averages** from major obstetric studies of IVF pregnancies and surrogate cohorts.

## **1. Pre-eclampsia / Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension**
- **5–10%** typical risk in all pregnancies  
- **10–15%** in IVF pregnancies  
**Why:** IVF slightly elevates hypertensive disorder risk.

## **2. Gestational Diabetes**
- **6–9%** normal population  
- **8–12%** with IVF or higher BMI  
**Why:** IVF + older surrogates = slightly higher incidence.

## **3. Preterm Birth (<37 weeks)**
- **10%** general population  
- **15–20%** IVF singleton  
- **>50%** in twins (but reputable agencies avoid twins now)

## **4. C-Section**
- **30%** U.S. average  
- **35–45%** for IVF pregnancies  
**Why:** Higher monitoring + planned deliveries.

## **5. Placenta Complications**
- **Placenta previa:** **0.5–1%** → increases to **1–2%** with IVF  
- **Placental abruption:** **<1%**  
- **Placenta accreta:** **0.2–0.5%** (higher in surrogates with prior C-section)

## **6. Miscarriage**
- **10–20%** natural pregnancies  
- **10–15%** gestational surrogacy using screened embryos  
**Why:** Genetic screening significantly lowers miscarriage.

---

# **RISKS TO THE BABY**
## **1. Low Birth Weight**
- **8%** general pregnancies  
- **10–15%** IVF singletons  

## **2. Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) Admission**
- **8–10%** general  
- **10–15%** IVF  
Mostly due to preterm or monitoring caution, not severe illness.

## **3. Birth Defects**
- **2–3%** baseline human rate  
- IVF slightly increases to **3–4%**, mostly minor anomalies  
PGT-A testing further reduces major risks.

---

# **PSYCHOLOGICAL / SOCIAL RISKS**
(Not percentages, but well-documented patterns.)

### For the surrogate:
- Hormonal mood shifts
- Rare postpartum emotional attachment (agencies screen heavily)
- Stress from intended parent expectations

### For the baby:
- No known long-term health disadvantages  
- No difference in cognitive or emotional development  
- Bonding normal with intended parents

# **WHY SURROGACY IS GENERALLY SAFER THAN AVERAGE PREGNANCY**
**Surrogates are extremely screened:**
- Age **21–38**  
- At least one uncomplicated prior birth  
- No major medical history  
- Non-smokers, stable environment  
- Body-mass-index within specific range

This lowers catastrophic risk dramatically.

# **TWO TYPES OF SURROGACY (IMPORTANT DISTINCTION)**

## **1. *Gestational Surrogacy* (99% of modern cases)**  
**Egg + sperm = embryo (IVF)**  
→ Embryo is transferred into surrogate.  
→ **Surrogate has zero genetic connection.**  
→ She is only the *gestational carrier.*

**This is the safe, ethical, and standard method today.**

## **2. *Traditional Surrogacy* (rare and mostly illegal)**  
Sperm is inserted into surrogate’s egg.  
→ Surrogate *is the genetic mother.*  
→ Creates legal and emotional complications.  
→ Almost never done today.

---

# **HOW GESTATIONAL SURROGACY WORKS (THE ONE YOU MEAN)**

### **Step 1 — The embryo is created in the lab**
- **Someone’s sperm** (intended father or donor)  
- **Someone’s egg** (intended mother or donor)  
These combine outside the body (IVF).

### **Step 2 — The embryo is transferred into the surrogate**
- A single embryo is placed gently into her uterus.
- She becomes pregnant but **the baby is not biologically hers**.

### **Step 3 — She carries and gives birth**
- Baby’s DNA = egg + sperm providers  
- Surrogate contributes **no genetics**  
- Only environment (nutrients, hormones, womb)

---

# **RISKS FROM USING SOMEONE ELSE’S EMBRYO?**

**Medically:**  
No increased risk **to the surrogate** simply because the embryo isn't hers.  
The risks come from:
- IVF itself  
- Pregnancy (normal risks)  
- Surrogate’s health

**Emotionally:**  
Gestational carriers rarely bond as “mother” because:
- They know from the start it’s not their baby  
- They’ve already had their own kids  
- They pass psych/mental screening first

This is why the emotional complication rate is **<1%** in reputable programs.

---

# **RISKS TO THE BABY?**
Using someone else’s uterus **does not increase genetic defects**.  
The womb environment affects growth, but not DNA.

When embryos are PGT-tested, defect risk often becomes **lower than natural pregnancy**.

# **Can a surrogate breastfeed the baby?**  
**Yes, physically she can — but 95% of the time she *does not*.**  
Most intended parents choose **not** to have the surrogate breastfeed because of:
- boundaries  
- bonding concerns  
- legal clarity  
- logistics after birth

Instead, surrogates often **pump colostrum + milk for a few weeks** and ship it.  
This is very common and medically safe.

---

# **OPTIONS (REAL-WORLD COMMON)**

## **1. Surrogate breastfeeds in the hospital only**  
**Purpose:**  
- Baby gets colostrum (“liquid gold”) immediately  
- Surrogate doesn’t continue after discharge  
**Most common short-term method.**

## **2. Surrogate pumps milk and ships it**  
- 4–12 weeks on average  
- Pays her a pumping compensation  
- Maintains boundaries  
- Protects legal clarity

## **3. Intended mother induces lactation (yes, it works)**  
Non-pregnant women *can* breastfeed using:  
- hormone protocol  
- pumping routine  
- domperidone or similar medications (depends on country)

This lets the **intended mother** bond and nurse the baby.

---

# **RISKS / CONCERNS**

## **If the surrogate breastfeeds directly:**
- Creates stronger emotional bonding  
- Can blur boundaries if not managed  
- Rarely recommended beyond hospital stay

## **If surrogate pumps:**
- No bonding issue  
- Milk is safe  
- Surrogate stops whenever needed

## **If intended mother induces lactation:**
- Works for many, not all  
- Requires commitment  
- Baby may need supplemental milk at first
