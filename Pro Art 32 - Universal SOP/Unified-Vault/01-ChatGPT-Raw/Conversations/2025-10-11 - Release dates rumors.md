**APS-C** = “Advanced Photo System – Classic.”  

It refers to the **sensor size** — smaller than **full-frame**, but larger than **micro four thirds**.  

---

### 🔍 Breakdown
| Type | Size (approx.) | Crop vs Full-Frame | Typical Use |
|------|----------------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Full-Frame** | 36 × 24 mm | 1.0× | Professional photo/video; best depth, low light |
| **APS-C** | ~24 × 16 mm | 1.5× (Sony/Nikon) or 1.6× (Canon) | Lighter, cheaper, smaller lenses |
| **Micro 4/3** | 17.3 × 13 mm | 2.0× | Compact systems, vlogging, travel |

---

### 📸 Practical Impact
- **Crop Factor:** A 24 mm lens on APS-C looks like a **36 mm** lens on full-frame (1.5× narrower field of view).  
- **Low Light:** Smaller sensor → slightly less light → more noise at high ISO.  
- **Depth of Field:** Harder to get ultra-shallow blur (bokeh).  
- **Portability:** Smaller lenses and bodies, more affordable.  

---

**In short:**  
APS-C = smaller sensor → cropped image, cheaper, lighter.  
Full-frame = larger sensor → better image quality, shallower depth, higher price.

## 👤 You

Wha is over sampling for 4k60

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Oversampling** means the camera captures video from the **entire sensor at a higher resolution** (like 6K or 8K) and then **downscales it to 4K** — instead of just reading a 4K-sized portion of the sensor.  

---

### ⚙️ **Example: Canon R5 II 4K/60**
- The sensor is **45 MP (~8K resolution)**.  
- For 4K/60 recording, it reads the **full 8K frame** and then downsamples it to 4K.  
- Result: every 4K pixel contains real image data averaged from multiple higher-res pixels → more detail, less moiré, cleaner edges, and lower noise.  

---

### 🎥 **Oversampled vs. Non-Oversampled**
| Mode | Sensor Readout | Image Quality | Crop / View |
|------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Oversampled 4K60** | Full 8K → 4K downsample | Sharper, richer detail | Full-frame (no crop) |
| **Native 4K Readout** | Reads only 4K-sized area | Softer, less detailed | May crop the frame |
| **Line-Skipped / Pixel-Binned** | Skips pixels to reduce processing | More artifacts, less true resolution | Often cropped |

---

### 🧠 **In Short**
Oversampling =  
> “Record in 8K, deliver in 4K.”  

It’s like shooting a high-res photo and resizing it — you keep clarity but reduce file size and noise.  

That’s why **4K60 oversampled** from a high-MP sensor (like Canon R5 II or Sony A1) looks **crisper** than native 4K60 from a 24 MP sensor.

### ⚙️ **What Happens at 4K 120 fps**
- The sensor must read **120 full frames per second**, which is *4×* the data rate of 30 fps.  
- To sustain that, cameras **reduce data load** by doing one (or more) of the following:  
  1. **Pixel binning or line skipping** — combines or skips pixels instead of reading all 8K lines.  
  2. **Partial readout / crop** — reads only part of the sensor (e.g., 5.8K window instead of 8K).  
  3. **Lower detail / higher noise** — image is still good, but not full-sensor oversampled quality.
