Here’s the truth in headlines:  

**1. Desire vs. Dependency**  
- Wanting to be someone’s one-and-only is natural and deeply human.  
- But if it comes with *pressure and expectation* for them to constantly prove it, it shifts from healthy desire into dependency.  

**2. Why It Feels “Needy”**  
- When love is tied to *validation* (“I’m only okay if they prioritize me above all else”), partners can feel smothered.  
- Real, mature love thrives on freedom, not control.  

**3. High School Love vs. Adult Love**  
- High school love often feels “all-consuming” because it’s your first exposure to that intensity.  
- Adult relationships are often steadier, less intoxicating, but potentially *more real, more enduring*.  
- If everything else feels pale, it may be nostalgia for that first “flood of chemicals” more than a lack in partners.  

**4. Healthy Expression of the Desire**  
- It’s not wrong to want depth, devotion, and being someone’s #1.  
- The healthy version: “I want to share a bond where we choose each other fully and consistently.”  
- The unhealthy version: “I need them to constantly prove I’m their everything or I don’t feel safe.”  

**5. What to Do with This Desire**  
- **Own it:** It’s part of your truth—you want deep devotion.  
- **Balance it:** Ground it in self-worth, so you *invite* love instead of *demanding* it.  
- **Communicate it:** Share it as a vision (“I thrive in a relationship where we prioritize each other as #1”) instead of a test or condition.  

👉 Bottom line: Your desire is real and valid. The key is learning to hold it without grasping—so the right partner feels magnetized, not pressured.
