I blew something from using my PC and a space heater

After restarting the power by switching off and on the panel, the electricity in the room isn’t coming back on but the other rooms are on

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

It sounds like you overloaded the circuit, which likely caused the breaker to trip. Since power isn't returning to the affected room after resetting the breaker, here’s what to check:

### **Step 1: Confirm the Breaker is Fully Reset**
1. **Turn the breaker all the way off** (firmly push it to the OFF position).
2. **Then switch it back ON** with a solid push.
   - Some breakers need to be completely turned off before they can reset.

### **Step 2: Check for a Tripped GFCI Outlet**
- If the room has **GFCI-protected outlets** (common in bathrooms, kitchens, and sometimes bedrooms), one may have tripped.
- **Find any GFCI outlets and press the RESET button** (usually in the middle of the outlet).

### **Step 3: Test Another Outlet**
- Plug a lamp or phone charger into different outlets in the room to see if **any** work.
- If some outlets work but others don’t, you may have a tripped **AFCI breaker** (modern circuit breakers with arc fault protection).

### **Step 4: Check for a Blown Fuse (If Applicable)**
- If you have an **older home with a fuse box**, one of the fuses may have blown and needs replacement.

### **Step 5: Check for a Partially Failed Breaker**
- If the breaker seems on but there's still no power, the breaker itself **might be damaged** and not properly delivering power.
- If you feel comfortable, **test voltage at the breaker** using a multimeter (120V expected).

### **Step 6: Call an Electrician If Needed**
- If resetting the breaker and GFCI doesn’t work, **the wiring or breaker may have been damaged** due to the overload.
- **An electrician may need to replace the breaker or inspect the circuit wiring.**

## 👤 You

How to find the gfci

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

To locate the **GFCI outlet** in your room, follow these steps:

### **Step 1: Look for Outlets with "Test" and "Reset" Buttons**
- GFCI outlets have two small buttons, usually labeled **"TEST"** and **"RESET."**
- They are often **different colors** (e.g., black and red, or both white).
- If the "RESET" button is popped out, the outlet has tripped.

### **Step 2: Common Locations to Check**
1. **Nearby Rooms** – Even if your room doesn’t have one, a GFCI in a nearby **bathroom, hallway, garage, or kitchen** might control your room’s power.
2. **Bathroom Outlets** – Check all bathroom outlets first.
3. **Kitchen and Laundry Areas** – These areas often have multiple GFCIs.
4. **Garage and Basement** – Sometimes, GFCIs are placed there and can control multiple rooms.
5. **Outdoor Outlets** – If your home has exterior outlets, one might be linked to indoor circuits.
6. **Behind Furniture** – If an outlet is blocked by furniture, it could still be a GFCI.

### **Step 3: Reset the GFCI**
1. Press **"TEST"** (it should click and cut power if it wasn’t already tripped).
2. Press **"RESET"** (it should click again, restoring power).

### **Step 4: If You Can’t Find One**
- Some older homes have **GFCI breakers in the electrical panel** instead of individual outlets.
- Check your **breaker box** for a breaker with a "TEST" button and reset it.

Since the **GFCI in the kitchen and bathroom aren't affecting the room that lost power**, the issue is likely with the **breaker, a hidden GFCI, or a damaged circuit**. Here’s what to do next:

### **1. Double-Check for Another GFCI Outlet**

  - **Garage**
  - **Laundry room**
  - **Another bathroom** (even if it's far from the affected room)
  - **Basement**
  - **Exterior outlets**
  - **Hallway outlets near the affected room**

### **2. Reset the Breaker Again (Fully Off → Then On)**
- Go to your electrical panel.
- **Find the breaker that controls the affected room.**
- **Turn it all the way OFF, then firmly back ON.**
- If it **feels loose or doesn’t click**, the breaker might be damaged.

### **3. Test Multiple Outlets in the Room**
- Plug a lamp or charger into **every outlet** in the room.
- If **some work and others don’t**, there could be a loose wire or a tripped AFCI.

### **4. Look for AFCI Breakers in the Panel**
- Some homes use **AFCI breakers** (similar to GFCI, but they protect against arc faults).
- If any breaker has a **TEST button**, press it, then reset the breaker.

### **5. Check for a Blown Fuse (If Your Home Uses Fuses)**
- If your home has an **older fuse box instead of breakers**, check for a blown fuse.
- A blown fuse needs to be replaced, but only with the **same amperage**.

### **6. If Power Still Won’t Come Back:**
- The **breaker itself may be damaged** and need replacement.
- **A wire may have burned out or become loose** at one of the outlets in the affected circuit.
- If comfortable, **check for voltage at outlets and breakers with a multimeter** (expect 120V).
- Otherwise, call an electrician to inspect the wiring and breaker.
