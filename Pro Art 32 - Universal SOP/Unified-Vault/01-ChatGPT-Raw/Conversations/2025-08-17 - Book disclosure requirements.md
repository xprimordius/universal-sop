## 🤖 ChatGPT

Not legally, unless you’re marketing it in a way that would be deceptive.  

### Key Points:
- **Fiction:** You don’t need to disclose whether it’s “true” or not. Authors often base fiction on real experiences but present it as a novel without explanation.  
- **Memoir / Nonfiction:** If you’re selling it as a “true story” (memoir, biography, investigative nonfiction), then the expectation is honesty. Misrepresenting fiction as fact could lead to credibility loss or, in extreme cases (like fabricating a memoir for profit), legal/contract issues.  
- **Best Practice:** Many authors add a disclaimer if parts are fictionalized—e.g., *“Based on true events”* or *“Some names and details have been changed.”* This protects you and sets proper expectations for readers.  

👉 TLDR: No disclosure required for fiction. For memoir/nonfiction, disclose truthfully—or risk consequences.
