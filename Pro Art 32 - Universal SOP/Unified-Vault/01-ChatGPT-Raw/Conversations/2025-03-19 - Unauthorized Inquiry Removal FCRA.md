## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, under the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** (15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.), an inquiry must have a **permissible purpose** under **§ 1681b**. If you did not receive **any credit, service, or benefit** from the inquiry and did not authorize it, then the inquiry is **unauthorized** and must be removed upon dispute.

### **Key Legal Points:**
1. **Permissible Purpose Requirement (15 U.S.C. § 1681b)**
   - A company must have a valid reason (e.g., credit application, account review, employment background check with consent).
   - If the inquiry lacks a permissible purpose, it's a **violation**.

2. **Dispute Process (15 U.S.C. § 1681i)**
   - You can dispute unauthorized inquiries.
   - The credit bureau must investigate within **30 days**.
   - If the creditor **cannot verify** that you applied for credit or authorized the inquiry, it must be **deleted**.

3. **Burden of Proof**
   - The **furnisher (creditor)** must prove they had authorization.
   - If they **fail to provide proof**, the inquiry must be removed.

### **What to Do:**
- File a **dispute with the credit bureau** stating:
  - You never applied for or received credit.
  - The inquiry lacks a permissible purpose.
  - Demand removal per **15 U.S.C. § 1681b & 1681i**.
- If they fail to remove it, you may have grounds for an **FCRA lawsuit** with statutory damages up to **$1,000 per violation**.
