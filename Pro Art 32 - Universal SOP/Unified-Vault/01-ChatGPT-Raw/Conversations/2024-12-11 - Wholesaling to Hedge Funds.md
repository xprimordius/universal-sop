### **1. Wholesaling to Hedge Funds**
**Overview:**
- You act as a middleman, contracting properties at a discount and assigning the contract to a hedge fund buyer for a fee.
- Hedge funds seek volume deals, typically focusing on specific property types and markets.

**Numbers:**
- **Average Assignment Fee:** $10,000–$50,000+ per deal (higher than typical wholesaling due to hedge fund budgets).
- **Time to Profit:** 30–90 days per deal.
- **Costs:** Low upfront cost (marketing and lead generation, <$1,000/month).
- **Energy Required:** High initially (networking with sellers and hedge fund buyers), low once systems are in place.

**Pros:**
- High profit per deal.
- Reliable buyers with repeat business potential.
- Minimal capital risk (no property purchase).

**Cons:**
- Competitive market.
- Requires deep market research and precise execution to meet hedge fund criteria.

---

### **2. Standard Wholesaling**
**Overview:**
- Similar to wholesaling to hedge funds but deals are assigned to smaller investors, landlords, or flippers.

**Numbers:**
- **Average Assignment Fee:** $5,000–$20,000 per deal.
- **Time to Profit:** 30–60 days per deal.
- **Costs:** Low (marketing and lead generation, <$1,000/month).
- **Energy Required:** Moderate (negotiating, marketing, and finding buyers).

**Pros:**
- Low startup costs.
- Accessible for beginners.
- Flexible deal structure.

**Cons:**
- Smaller profit margins.
- Requires strong buyer and seller network.

---

### **3. Fix-and-Flip**
**Overview:**
- Buying, renovating, and selling properties for profit.

**Numbers:**
- **Profit per Flip:** $30,000–$70,000 on average (depending on market and property).
- **Time to Profit:** 3–12 months.
- **Costs:** High upfront costs (down payments, renovation budgets).
- **Energy Required:** High (managing contractors, project timelines).

**Pros:**
- Higher profit per deal.
- Control over deal outcomes.

**Cons:**
- High financial risk (market downturns, renovation overruns).
- Time-intensive.

---

### **4. Buy-and-Hold for Rental Income**
**Overview:**
- Acquiring properties to generate passive rental income and long-term appreciation.

**Numbers:**
- **Cash Flow per Property:** $200–$500/month after expenses.
- **Time to Profit:** Immediate cash flow, long-term appreciation (5–10 years for significant ROI).
- **Costs:** High upfront costs (down payments, property management).
- **Energy Required:** Low once stabilized.

**Pros:**
- Passive income stream.
- Long-term wealth building.

**Cons:**
- Capital-intensive.
- Slower path to significant cash flow.

**Overview:**

**Numbers:**
- **Profit per Property:** $1,000–$2,000/month.
- **Time to Profit:** 1–3 months.
- **Costs:** Moderate (furniture and deposits, ~$5,000/property).
- **Energy Required:** High (management, customer service).

**Pros:**

**Cons:**
- Legal and market restrictions.
- Operational intensity.

- **Energy Required:** Moderate (research and due diligence).

**Pros:**

**Cons:**
