Short answer: **iOS doesn’t natively let you record the iPhone screen *and* turn on the camera at the same time** — the system blocks camera access while screen recording runs. That’s why Loom (desktop) can do facecam + screen, but iPhone’s built-in recorder can’t. citeturn0search22

**Workarounds & Apps that *do* it (screen + face camera overlay):**

1. **Third-party screen recorder apps with face-cam support**  
   (will record screen + front camera at once):
   • *Screen Recorder＋Face Cam* — screen + face cam overlay. citeturn0search10  
   • *Screen Recorder with face cam* — screen + face camera + audio. citeturn0search11  
   • *Screen Recorder: Dual Cam* — records front & rear camera with screen (varies by version). citeturn0search14  
   • *Go Record* — screen recording with face cam, editing tools. citeturn0search15  
   • *Record It!: Screen Recorder* — screen + face cam (free with watermark). citeturn0search4turn0search5

2. **Screen + Facecam apps/web tools**  
   • *Screen.studio* (browser/app) reported by users for similar use. citeturn0search6

**If you can’t get a good direct recorder:**

- **Record separately then edit:** iOS built-in screen recording + Camera app → combine in iMovie or CapCut. citeturn0search16

**Note:** Some *dual cam* apps exist (e.g., *MixCam*, *2Camera*) for front-behind video, but they don’t capture the iOS screen itself — only camera feeds. citeturn0search18turn0search21

**How to set up with a third-party app (general steps):**

1. Install your chosen app from the App Store.  
2. Grant screen + camera + mic permissions.  
3. Start screen capture *through the app’s built-in recorder*, not the iOS Control Center.  
4. Position the face-cam overlay (corner, etc.).  
5. Record.
