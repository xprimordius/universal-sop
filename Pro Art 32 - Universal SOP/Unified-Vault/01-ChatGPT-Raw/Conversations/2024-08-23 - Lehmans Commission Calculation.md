The JOBS Act (Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act) is a U.S. federal law that was enacted in April 2012 to encourage funding for small businesses and startups by easing various securities regulations. The law aimed to stimulate economic growth and job creation by making it easier for small companies to raise capital. Below are the key components of the JOBS Act:

### 1. **Title I: Reopening American Capital Markets to Emerging Growth Companies**
   - **Emerging Growth Companies (EGCs):** The JOBS Act created a new category called "Emerging Growth Companies," defined as companies with annual gross revenues of less than $1 billion during their most recent fiscal year. These companies can take advantage of reduced regulatory and disclosure requirements for up to five years after going public, making the IPO process more accessible.

### 2. **Title II: Access to Capital for Job Creators**
   - **General Solicitation:** This provision allowed companies to engage in general solicitation and advertising when seeking funding, provided they only sell securities to accredited investors. Previously, companies were restricted from publicly soliciting investment opportunities.

### 3. **Title III: Crowdfunding**
   - **Equity Crowdfunding:** The JOBS Act introduced equity crowdfunding, allowing small businesses to raise capital from a large number of small investors through online platforms. Companies can raise up to $1 million annually from non-accredited investors under certain conditions, with oversight from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

### 4. **Title IV: Small Company Capital Formation (Regulation A+)**
   - **Regulation A+:** This section expanded and updated Regulation A, which allows companies to offer and sell securities to the general public without having to register with the SEC, up to $50 million in a 12-month period. This regulation provides a streamlined process for smaller companies to raise capital.

### 5. **Title V: Private Company Flexibility and Growth**
   - **Shareholder Cap:** The JOBS Act increased the threshold for mandatory SEC registration from 500 shareholders to 2,000 shareholders, or 500 non-accredited investors. This change allows private companies to have more shareholders before being required to publicly report financial information.

### 6. **Title VI: Capital Expansion**
   - **Banks and Bank Holding Companies:** This provision increased the threshold for registration for banks and bank holding companies from 500 to 2,000 shareholders, making it easier for smaller banks to operate without the costs associated with SEC registration.

### Impact of the JOBS Act
The JOBS Act has been credited with significantly changing the landscape for startups and small businesses by expanding access to capital. It allowed more companies to seek funding from a broader range of investors and provided new opportunities for investors to participate in early-stage financing.

Overall, the JOBS Act was designed to reduce the regulatory burden on small companies, making it easier and less expensive for them to go public and raise capital, which in turn was expected to foster innovation and job creation.
