> 01.05 GEOLOGICAL SETTING, MINERALIZATION, AND DEPOSIT 
> 
> The gold reserve is hosted in sedimentary rock and gravels eroded from the Arrow Canyon range, southern 
> end of Meadow Valley Mountains, and consists of alluvium of Quaternary age, underlain by Tertiary 
> sedimentary deposits of Muddy Creek formation.  The underlying host rock is from the Ordovician to 
> Permian eras.  The carbonate is from the Bird Spring formation.   
> 
> One stratigraphic, Site-wide, gold-rich “zone” thirty feet thick has been identified by Nuclear Magnetic 
> Resonance (NMR, Figure 07-1) and confirmed by sampling and assay (“HH Survey”,  Figure 07-5). The same 
> NMR survey identifies a “horizon” buried 450 feet below grade, suggesting either “hard rocks” (“basement”) 
> or a prominent stratigraphic horizon (possibly an erosional unconformity). Up to 450 feet of possible placer 
> deposits have been interpreted from the available drilling, sampling, and satellite data beneath the Claim. 
> The same NMR survey identifies multiple linear features, possibly veins or faults (or both) , at approximately 
> 1300 feet below grade. A second series of linear features is identified at approximately 1400 feet below 
> grade.   
> 
> The Yellow Jacket 2A is part of the Yellow Jacket Claim Group and the placer deposit occupies an area of 
> one square mile (640 acres or about 28 million square feet) and may be as much as 1,200 feet thick.
