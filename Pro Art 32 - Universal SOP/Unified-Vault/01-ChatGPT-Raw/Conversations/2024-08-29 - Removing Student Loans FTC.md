# Removing Student Loans FTC

---

## 👤 You

Can student loans be removed from the credit bureau reports via an FTC report & affidavit

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Student loans generally cannot be removed from credit bureau reports simply by using an FTC identity theft report and affidavit unless the loans are the result of actual identity theft. The FTC report and affidavit are used to address fraudulent accounts opened in your name due to identity theft. If the student loans were legitimately taken out by you, they are legally required to remain on your credit report.

However, if you believe that the student loans are on your credit report due to identity theft, you would need to:

1. **File an Identity Theft Report**: This includes filing a report with the FTC and creating an affidavit, which is a sworn statement that you did not open the account.

2. **Contact the Lender and Credit Bureaus**: Send the FTC identity theft report and affidavit to the lender and all three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). They are required to investigate and, if the claim is valid, remove the fraudulent accounts from your report.

If the loans are valid and there is no identity theft, other methods like dispute processes based on inaccuracies, payment history corrections, or rehabilitation programs for defaulted loans would be the appropriate steps to manage the loans on your credit report.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Given your situation, here’s how you might proceed:

1. **File a Dispute with the Credit Bureaus**: If the student loans were refinanced and reopened without your authorization, you should file a formal dispute with each credit bureau. Clearly state that you did not authorize the refinancing and that the loans are connected to an address that is not yours. Provide any supporting documentation that can help your case.

2. **Submit an FTC Identity Theft Report**: Even if you don’t consider this traditional identity theft, the unauthorized refinancing and linking to an incorrect address could be treated similarly. Filing an FTC report and submitting an affidavit could strengthen your dispute by demonstrating that you did not authorize these actions.

3. **Contact the Loan Servicer**: Reach out directly to the loan servicer(s) who managed the refinancing. Request a full account history, documentation of your consent to the refinance, and explain that the loans were connected to an address that is not yours. If they cannot provide evidence of your consent, you may be able to argue that the refinancing was unauthorized.

4. **Escalate the Issue**: If the credit bureaus and loan servicers do not take action, you can escalate by filing a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and your state's Attorney General's office. These complaints can often lead to further investigations and potential resolution.

5. **Consider Legal Action**: If the issue is not resolved through these steps, consult with an attorney who specializes in consumer law or credit disputes. They can help you understand your legal rights and potential actions, including possibly seeking removal of the unauthorized accounts through a court order.

These steps aim to remove the unauthorized accounts from your credit report and disassociate them from the incorrect address.
