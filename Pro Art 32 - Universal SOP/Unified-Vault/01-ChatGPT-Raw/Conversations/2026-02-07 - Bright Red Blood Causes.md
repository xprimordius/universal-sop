### Most common causes (benign)
- **Hemorrhoids** – painless bright red blood on toilet paper, dripping, or coating stool.
- **Anal fissure** – sharp pain/burning with bowel movement; small streaks of bright red blood.
- **Constipation/straining** – can trigger either of the above.

### Other possible causes (need evaluation)
- **Infectious or inflammatory colitis** – blood + diarrhea, cramps, fever.
- **Diverticular bleeding** – sudden painless bleeding (often more volume).
- **Colon polyps or colorectal cancer** – bleeding without pain, change in bowel habits, anemia.

### When to get **urgent** care
- Large or ongoing bleeding
- Dizziness, weakness, fainting
- Black/tarry stools (upper GI bleed)
- Severe abdominal pain or fever
- Bleeding plus weight loss or anemia

### What to do now (practical)
- If mild and likely hemorrhoids/fissure: increase **fiber + fluids**, avoid straining, warm sitz baths.
- If it **persists >1–2 weeks**, recurs, or you’re **>45**, schedule a clinician visit (rectal exam ± colonoscopy).

**Catheter + bright red blood (hematuria): what it usually means**

### Most common causes
- **Insertion trauma** (urethral irritation/tear) — very common, especially right after placement or movement.
- **Catheter friction or pulling** — blood appears after repositioning or walking.
- **UTI** — blood + burning, urgency, cloudy urine, fever.
- **Prostate irritation** (men) — especially after catheterization.

### Less common but serious
- **Bladder or kidney bleeding** (stones, tumors)
- **Coagulation issues** (blood thinners)
- **False passage / urethral injury** — increasing pain, resistance, worsening bleeding

### What’s concerning (seek urgent care)
- **Heavy bleeding** or **clots** blocking urine flow
- **Rapidly increasing blood**
- **Severe pain**, fever, chills
- **Little/no urine output** despite fluid intake

### What to do now
- **Do not remove the catheter yourself.**
- Ensure tubing isn’t kinked; keep the bag below bladder level.
- If bleeding is **light and improving**, monitor and hydrate.
- If it **persists >24 hours**, worsens, or you have symptoms above → **contact a clinician or go to urgent care/ER**.
