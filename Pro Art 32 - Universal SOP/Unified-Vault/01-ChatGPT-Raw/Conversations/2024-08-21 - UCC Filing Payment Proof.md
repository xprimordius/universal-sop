# UCC Filing Payment Proof

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

When Corp 1 sells an asset (like a car) to Corp 2 and writes a UCC (Uniform Commercial Code) filing, the following steps and considerations are involved:

### 1. **Writing the Initial UCC-1 Filing:**
   - **Corp 1 as the Secured Party:** Corp 1 files a UCC-1 statement indicating that they have a security interest in the asset (the car) sold to Corp 2.
   - **Corp 2 as the Debtor:** Corp 2 is listed as the debtor in this UCC-1 filing, acknowledging that they owe Corp 1 for the asset.

### 2. **Creating a Promissory Note or Agreement:**
   - **Document the Sale:** Corp 1 should document the sale with a promissory note or another form of agreement, detailing the terms, including the payment schedule, interest rate (if any), and collateral (the car).
   - **Proof of Sale:** This document serves as proof that Corp 1 sold the asset to Corp 2, creating an obligation on the part of Corp 2 to repay Corp 1.

### 3. **Writing a UCC-3 Termination Statement:**
   - **When the Debt is Paid Off:** Once Corp 2 has fully paid off the debt, Corp 1 should file a UCC-3 termination statement. This terminates the security interest claimed in the original UCC-1 filing.
   - **Tradeline Impact:** This action can positively impact Corp 2's credit as it shows that they have successfully paid off a debt, thereby strengthening their corporate credit.

### 4. **Proof of Payment:**
   - **No Legal Requirement to Submit Proof with UCC Filings:** There is no requirement to submit proof of payment when filing a UCC-3 termination. The UCC system relies on the parties involved to maintain their records.
   - **Internal Documentation:** However, it is crucial for both Corp 1 and Corp 2 to maintain records of the transaction, including proof of payment (e.g., bank statements, canceled checks, or receipts) in case of any disputes or audits.

### 5. **Key Considerations:**
   - **Accuracy in Filings:** Ensure that all UCC filings are accurate and reflect the actual transaction.
   - **Internal Record-Keeping:** Both corporations should retain all relevant documents to substantiate the sale and payment, even though they are not required to file these with the UCC-3 termination.

By following these steps, Corp 1 and Corp 2 can effectively use the UCC system to build and reflect their corporate credit profiles. Proper documentation and filings are crucial to avoid potential disputes and ensure the integrity of the credit-building process.
