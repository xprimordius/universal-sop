Eligibility for a mortgage primarily depends on factors like income, debt-to-income (DTI) ratio, credit score, down payment, and loan term. Here’s a breakdown based on common lender standards:

### 1. **Income and DTI Ratio Analysis**
   - **Monthly Gross Income**: $3,500
   - **Standard Front-End Ratio (Housing Expense Ratio)**: Generally, lenders like this to be no more than 28% of gross income. This includes principal, interest, taxes, and insurance (PITI).
     - **Max Housing Payment at 28%**: $3,500 * 0.28 = $980
   - **Standard Back-End Ratio (Total DTI)**: Lenders usually prefer a back-end DTI below 36-43%, including all debt payments (e.g., credit cards, car loans).
   
2. **Estimation of Loan Amount**
   - Assuming minimal or no other debts, a maximum monthly housing payment (mortgage + insurance + taxes) of around **$980** is likely manageable.

3. **Loan Amount Based on a 30-Year Term with Interest Rate Assumptions**
   - **Example Assumptions**: 6% interest rate, 30-year term.
   - With a $980/month cap, this translates approximately to a **$160,000 - $180,000 mortgage**, depending on specific rates and additional factors like insurance and property taxes.

### Note
- **Down Payment**: A larger down payment could increase buying power.
- **Credit Score**: With a strong credit score (e.g., 770+), you may qualify for more favorable terms.
  
For a precise eligibility estimate, reaching out to a mortgage lender with your detailed financial profile would be best.
