can a contract be easily broken if the individual didn't read it? and just trusted what the sales rep was saying via word

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

In general, **not reading a contract** before signing it does not make the contract easily voidable. Courts typically uphold contracts under the legal principle that a person is responsible for understanding the terms they agree to. However, there are exceptions based on the circumstances, such as:

### 1. **Misrepresentation or Fraud**
   - If the sales rep knowingly made **false statements** or misrepresented the terms of the contract, this can be grounds to void the contract.
   - **Key Evidence Needed:** Proof of what was said (e.g., written communication, witnesses).

### 2. **Duress or Coercion**
   - If the individual was pressured or coerced into signing, the contract might be invalid.
   - **Key Factor:** Evidence of undue influence or threats.

### 3. **Unconscionable Terms**
   - If the contract is deemed grossly unfair or exploitative, it might be voidable.
   - **Example:** Hidden fees or extreme penalties that were not disclosed.

### 4. **Lack of Informed Consent**
   - If the sales rep actively **prevented the individual from reading** the contract or rushed them into signing without explanation, it could weaken the enforceability.

### 5. **Violation of Consumer Protection Laws**
   - If the sales rep’s actions violated laws such as the **Federal Trade Commission Act** or state-level consumer protection laws, the contract might be voidable.
   - **Example:** Using deceptive practices or failing to disclose critical terms.

### Action Steps for the Individual:
1. **Review the Contract Terms:** Carefully read the contract to identify inconsistencies with what the sales rep said.
2. **Gather Evidence:** Compile any written communication, audio recordings, or witness statements that contradict the contract terms.
3. **Consult an Attorney:** Legal advice can clarify if there are sufficient grounds to void the contract based on the specific facts.

While verbal statements can be used as evidence, written contracts typically take precedence unless there's clear proof of misconduct or illegality.
