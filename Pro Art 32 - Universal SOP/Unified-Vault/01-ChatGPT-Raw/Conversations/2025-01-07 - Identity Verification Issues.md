---

### **1. Issues with Secondary Bureaus or LexisNexis Data**  
Even though your secondary bureaus are unfrozen, inaccurate or incomplete data in **LexisNexis**, **SageStream**, **CoreLogic**, or **ChexSystems** may cause verification failures. These institutions rely heavily on secondary bureaus for identity verification.

- **Solution**:  
  - Request a full consumer disclosure report from LexisNexis, SageStream, and CoreLogic.
  - Review for errors or outdated information (e.g., old addresses or mismatched SSN).
  - Dispute any incorrect data.

---

### **2. Address or Identity Mismatch**  
If your current address or identity details don’t match exactly with what’s on file across all databases, it can trigger verification issues.

- **Solution**:  
  - Ensure your address, phone number, and name are consistently updated across your driver's license, passport, and credit reports.
  - Check if your current address is correctly reflected on your credit bureau files.

---

### **3. Thin or Fragmented Credit File**  
If your credit file lacks sufficient history or appears fragmented (e.g., due to a lack of recent account activity), it may affect identity verification.

- **Solution**:  
  - Ensure you have active, open accounts reporting to the bureaus.
  - Consider adding positive data by using services like **Experian Boost** or **UltraFICO**.

---

### **4. Fraud Flags or Identity Alerts**  
Even if you didn’t place fraud alerts, sometimes flags can be added by third-party fraud prevention services if they detect unusual activity, such as frequent address changes.

- **Solution**:  
  - Contact your primary and secondary bureaus to check for fraud or identity alerts.
  - Request information from NASA FCU and Apple Card on **which third-party service** flagged your identity and attempt to contact them directly.

---

### **5. OFAC or Watchlist Screening**  
Certain banks and financial institutions use watchlists (e.g., OFAC) to screen applicants. A false positive can occur if your name closely matches someone on a government watchlist.

- **Solution**:  
  - Ask the institutions directly if the issue is related to OFAC or a watchlist match.
  - If yes, request a secondary review to clear your name.

---

### **6. Biometric Verification System Error**  
Sometimes, errors in the biometric verification process, such as mismatched facial recognition, can cause issues.

- **Solution**:  
  - Ensure you are in a well-lit environment and follow biometric verification instructions precisely.
  - If issues persist, request an alternative verification method.

---

### **7. Data Sharing Issues Between Verification Providers**  
Financial institutions may rely on third-party vendors for identity verification (e.g., Plaid, ID.me). If these vendors can't retrieve or match your information correctly, it results in denials.

- **Solution**:  
  - Ask which vendor they use for verification (they may not disclose, but it’s worth trying).
  - Reach out to major ID verification services like **ID.me** or **Plaid** to ensure your identity data is correct.
