Learning Latin in the context of money, business, and law has significant importance for several reasons:

### 1. **Foundation of Legal Terminology:**
   - **Legal Terms**: Latin forms the basis of much of the legal terminology used in modern legal systems, particularly in countries with legal traditions influenced by Roman law. Understanding Latin helps in grasping the meaning of complex legal terms and phrases such as *habeas corpus*, *subpoena*, *pro bono*, *de facto*, and *prima facie*.
   - **Precision**: Legal language requires precision and clarity, and Latin terms are often used because of their exactness and historical consistency.

### 2. **Historical Influence on Western Law:**
   - **Roman Law**: The legal systems of many countries, especially in Europe and the Americas, are deeply influenced by Roman law, which was originally codified in Latin. Learning Latin allows for a better understanding of these roots and the principles that underlie modern legal systems.
   - **Canon Law**: In addition to secular law, much of the historical development of church law, or canon law, was written in Latin. This has influenced the development of Western legal thought.

### 3. **Understanding Financial and Business Terminology:**
   - **Economic Terms**: Latin also influences many terms used in economics and finance. Phrases such as *quid pro quo* (something for something), *caveat emptor* (let the buyer beware), and *bona fide* (in good faith) are rooted in Latin and are used in business transactions and contracts.
   - **Classical Education**: Historically, those who studied Latin were often in positions of power and influence, particularly in business and finance. Understanding Latin was a marker of education and sophistication, which could be advantageous in business dealings.

### 4. **Cultural and Intellectual Prestige:**
   - **Language of the Elite**: For centuries, Latin was the language of the educated elite. Legal documents, scholarly works, and religious texts were written in Latin. Learning the language gave individuals access to a wealth of knowledge and allowed them to communicate with others in the upper echelons of society.
   - **Legacy of Authority**: Many of the foundational texts in law, philosophy, and economics were written in or heavily influenced by Latin. Understanding Latin gives one direct access to these texts without relying on translations, which can sometimes alter the meaning.

### 5. **Global Legal System Understanding:**
   - **Common Legal Language**: In international law, many foundational principles and legal maxims are derived from Latin. Knowledge of Latin can aid in understanding these concepts across different legal systems, promoting better communication and negotiation in international business and legal contexts.

### 6. **Mental Discipline and Analytical Skills:**
   - **Rigorous Training**: Latin is a highly inflected language, meaning it has a complex system of endings to denote case, number, and gender. Learning Latin requires discipline and attention to detail, skills that are directly applicable to the meticulous nature of legal work and business analysis.
   - **Logical Structure**: The logical structure of Latin grammar can also enhance one's ability to think critically and analytically, which is essential in law and business strategy.

### 7. **Connection to Etymology and Modern Vocabulary:**
   - **Etymological Roots**: A large portion of the English language, particularly in law, medicine, and theology, has Latin roots. Understanding Latin can greatly enhance vocabulary, comprehension, and the ability to decode unfamiliar words.

In conclusion, learning Latin provides a deep understanding of the language and concepts that underpin much of modern law, finance, and business. It also connects learners to a vast intellectual tradition, enhancing both their knowledge base and their ability to engage with complex concepts in these fields.

For the concepts of "star born" and "alien from the heavens," the Latin translations would be:

1. **Star Born**: **"Stelligenitus"** - This is a constructed term combining "stella" (star) and "genitus" (born), meaning "born of the stars" or "star-born."

Another variation could be **"Exter caelestis,"** where "exter" also means foreign or alien, but with a slightly more external or otherworldly connotation.
