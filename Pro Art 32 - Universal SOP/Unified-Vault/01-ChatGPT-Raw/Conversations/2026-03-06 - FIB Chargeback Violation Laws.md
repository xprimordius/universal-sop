# 1. Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) — Investigation Requirements
Primary law governing credit-card billing disputes.

### Statute
entity["law","Fair Credit Billing Act","U.S. consumer credit dispute law"]  
15 U.S.C. §1666

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666

### Key rule (verbatim)
> “If a creditor receives a written notice of a billing error… the creditor shall investigate the matter and report the results of such investigation to the obligor.”  
— 15 U.S.C. §1666(a)

### Additional requirement
> “Not later than two complete billing cycles… the creditor shall… correct the account or explain why the creditor believes the billing statement was correct.”  
— 15 U.S.C. §1666(a)(B)

### Meaning
A bank **must conduct an investigation** and provide the **results and reasoning**.

If they:

- ignore evidence
- rely solely on merchant statements
- close a case without investigation
- deny based on procedure instead of facts

that can violate **FCBA investigative duties**.

---

### Regulation

The bank must perform a **reasonable investigation**.

# 7. When a Chargeback Investigation Becomes “Unfair”
Based on regulators’ interpretation of the above statutes.

A bank investigation can become **unfair or deceptive** if it:

1. **Ignores material evidence**
2. **Relies solely on merchant claims**
3. **Fails to conduct a reasonable investigation**
4. **Misrepresents reasons for denial**
5. **Fails to disclose investigation results**
6. **Denies based on internal procedure instead of facts**

These fall under:

- FCBA investigation duty
- Reg Z reasonable investigation rule
- FTC Act deception standards
- Dodd-Frank UDAAP

# 8. Simple Legal Framework Applied to Your Situation

To prove a **UDAAP violation**, regulators look for:

### Element 1 — Material Conduct
Important behavior affecting the transaction.

Examples:

- ignoring submitted evidence
- refusing to review documentation
- relying solely on merchant statements

### Element 2 — Consumer Injury
Financial harm.

Example:

- refusal to refund fraudulent or misrepresented charge

---

### Element 3 — Not Reasonably Avoidable
Consumer cannot fix it themselves.

Example:

- bank controls chargeback investigation process.

---

If all three exist → **unfair practice under federal law.**
