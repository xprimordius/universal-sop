The "B1" service reminder on your Honda Pilot indicates that maintenance is required. Specifically:

- **B** indicates a need for an oil and filter change, along with other services such as brake inspection, fluid checks, etc.

Even though you had an oil change 1,100 miles ago, the system may not have been reset at that time, which is why it's still showing 10%.

To resolve this, you can manually reset the Maintenance Minder system. Here's how:

1. Turn the ignition to "ON" (position II) without starting the engine.
2. Press the "Select/Reset" knob until the oil life percentage is displayed.
3. Press and hold the "Select/Reset" knob for about 10 seconds until the oil life starts blinking.
4. Release the knob, then press and hold it again for another 5 seconds until it resets to 100%.

This is especially common if the vehicle experiences high engine loads or extreme temperature changes.

2. **Faulty Oil Life Sensor or System**: It's possible that the oil life sensor or related components are malfunctioning, causing an inaccurate reading. In this case, the system might be prematurely signaling that maintenance is needed.

3. **Oil Quality or Type**: While you had a synthetic oil change, if the oil used was not of the recommended specification or quality for your Honda Pilot, it could degrade faster than expected, leading to a shorter oil life reading.

4. **ECU or Software Issue**: Occasionally, a glitch in the vehicle’s ECU (Electronic Control Unit) or software can cause erroneous oil life readings. In such cases, a reset or software update may be necessary.

Given that this is unusual for such low mileage after a reset, it might be worth having the vehicle inspected by a mechanic to ensure the oil life monitoring system is functioning correctly and that the oil quality is appropriate for your driving conditions.
