When considering the use of shares to safeguard a company from being taken over, it's crucial to understand the distinction between *"novelous"* and *"innocuous"* shares, assuming you might be referring to strategic and protective measures often employed in corporate governance. Let’s break it down:

### 1. **Issuing "Novilous" Shares:**
   - **Definition:** While "novilous" is not a standard financial term, it could be interpreted as creating a class of shares with unique characteristics that give certain rights or powers to shareholders. These could include rights that are typically defensive in nature.
   - **Purpose:** Such shares could be used to dilute the voting power of potential hostile acquirers or to empower existing shareholders, such as founders or management, with more voting rights per share.
   - **Mechanism:** You might issue preferred shares with enhanced voting rights, or "poison pill" shares, which trigger additional shares or rights in the event of an attempted takeover, making the takeover prohibitively expensive or complex.

### 2. **Issuing "Innocuous" Shares:**
   - **Definition:** "Innocuous" shares likely refer to shares that are designed to be non-threatening or harmless to current shareholders' control, yet still serve a strategic purpose.
   - **Purpose:** These shares could be used to distribute ownership more widely without diluting control or to create a perception of fairness and inclusiveness in the shareholder structure, thereby reducing the likelihood of hostile actions.
   - **Mechanism:** This might involve issuing non-voting shares to employees or small investors, which helps align their interests with the company without impacting control.

### **Strategies to Safeguard Against a Takeover:**

1. **Dual-Class Share Structure:** Implementing a dual-class share structure, where one class has significantly more voting power than the other, allows founders or key management to retain control even if the majority of shares are sold.

2. **Poison Pill Strategy:** A shareholder rights plan that triggers the issuance of more shares when someone buys a certain percentage of the company, diluting the acquirer's stake.

3. **Golden Shares:** Issuing a type of share that grants the holder veto power over key decisions, which could be given to a government entity or a trusted individual.

4. **Staggered Board of Directors:** Setting up the board so that only a portion of the directors are elected each year, making it harder for a hostile bidder to quickly gain control.

5. **White Knight Defense:** Finding a more favorable company (a white knight) to merge with or sell to, instead of allowing a hostile takeover.

### Key Considerations:
- **Legal Compliance:** Ensure any measures taken are compliant with corporate laws and regulations in your jurisdiction.
- **Shareholder Relations:** Communicate effectively with shareholders to ensure they understand the reasons for these measures and support them.
- **Impact on Market Perception:** Consider how these actions might affect the company’s market perception and stock price.

Implementing these strategies carefully can help in safeguarding your company from being taken over while maintaining operational integrity.
