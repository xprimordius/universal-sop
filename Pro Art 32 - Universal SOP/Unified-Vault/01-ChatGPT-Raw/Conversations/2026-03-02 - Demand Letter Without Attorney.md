## Short Answer  
An attorney **cannot personally “force” payment**.

Only a **court** can order someone to pay.

---

## What an Attorney *Can* Do

1. **Send Formal Demand Letter**  
   - Cites Florida law  
   - Demands accounting + payment  
   - Creates legal record  

2. **File Lawsuit** (if refusal continues)  
   Possible claims in Florida:
   - Breach of Operating Agreement  
   - Breach of Fiduciary Duty  
   - Conversion (if money diverted)  
   - Accounting action  
   - Judicial Dissolution  

3. **Seek Court Orders**
   - Court-ordered accounting  
   - Forced production of records  
   - Monetary judgment  
   - Forced buyout  
   - In extreme cases, forced sale or dissolution  

Under Florida LLC law (Chapter 605), members owe fiduciary duties. If other members misused funds or withheld distributions improperly, a judge can award damages.

- **Reinvested legitimately** → harder to force payout  

Ownership % ≠ automatic right to distributions unless agreement says so.

Yes.  

A **Minnesota attorney can send a demand letter to a Florida business.**

There is **no requirement** that a lawyer be licensed in Florida just to send a demand letter. A demand letter is not considered appearing in court.

---

## Where Licensing Matters

Licensing becomes required if the attorney:

- Files a lawsuit in Florida  
- Appears in a Florida court  
- Gives formal legal representation in Florida litigation  

To sue in Florida state court, the attorney must:
- Be licensed in Florida, or  
- Work with Florida counsel (pro hac vice admission)

- A Florida attorney is ideal
