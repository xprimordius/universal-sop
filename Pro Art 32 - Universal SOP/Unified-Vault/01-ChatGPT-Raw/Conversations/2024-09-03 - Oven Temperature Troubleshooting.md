# Oven Temperature Troubleshooting

---

## 👤 You

[image]

Stove bake is not going to 375, tried resetting it off and on 3 times it’s stuck at 175 and 176 for over 20-30 minutes

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

It seems like your oven is having trouble reaching the desired temperature of 375°F and is getting stuck around 175-176°F. Here are a few potential reasons and troubleshooting steps you can try:

1. **Check the Oven Calibration:**
   - The temperature sensor or thermostat might be out of calibration, leading to inaccurate temperature readings. Check your oven's manual for instructions on how to recalibrate the temperature.

2. **Inspect the Heating Elements:**
   - The heating elements (both the bake and broil elements) might be faulty or damaged. If one of the elements isn't working properly, it can prevent the oven from reaching higher temperatures.

3. **Clean the Temperature Sensor:**
   - The temperature sensor inside the oven may be dirty or faulty. A dirty sensor can cause incorrect readings, which might lead to the oven being stuck at a lower temperature.

4. **Ensure Proper Air Circulation:**
   - Make sure there is nothing obstructing the air vents inside the oven. Proper air circulation is necessary for the oven to reach and maintain the correct temperature.

5. **Check the Door Seal:**
   - A faulty or damaged oven door seal could be letting heat escape, preventing the oven from reaching the desired temperature. Inspect the seal for any cracks or gaps.

6. **Power Cycle the Oven:**
   - Although you've already tried turning it off and on, try unplugging the oven from the power source for a few minutes and then plugging it back in. This might reset the control board.

7. **Look for Error Codes:**
   - Check the oven's display or the manual for any error codes that could give you more specific information about the problem.

If the problem persists after trying these steps, it might be necessary to contact Samsung support or a professional appliance repair technician to diagnose and fix the issue.

If the broil function works but the bake function does not, this typically indicates an issue with the bake element or the associated circuitry. Here’s what might be happening:

### 1. **Faulty Bake Element:**
   - **Cause:** The bake element is likely damaged or burned out. When this happens, the oven won't reach the desired temperature for baking, as it relies heavily on this element for heat.
   - **Solution:** Inspect the bake element for any visible signs of damage, such as blistering or cracks. If you see any, the element will need to be replaced.

### 2. **Faulty Wiring or Connections:**
   - **Cause:** There could be a loose or damaged wire connected to the bake element, preventing it from receiving power.
   - **Solution:** Check the wiring connections to the bake element (if you are comfortable doing so) to ensure they are secure and intact. If there’s any sign of damage, it’s best to call a professional to repair or replace the wiring.

### 3. **Control Board Issue:**
   - **Cause:** The control board might be malfunctioning, preventing it from sending power to the bake element while still functioning correctly for the broil element.
   - **Solution:** If the bake element and wiring appear fine, the control board could be the culprit. This issue usually requires replacement of the control board, which is best handled by a professional.

### 4. **Thermostat or Temperature Sensor Malfunction:**
   - **Cause:** A faulty temperature sensor might be causing the oven to shut off the bake element prematurely or not activate it at all, while the broil element is still working properly.
   - **Solution:** You can test the temperature sensor with a multimeter for continuity or check if it’s properly calibrated. If the sensor is faulty, it will need to be replaced.

### Immediate Action:
Since your broil function works, it strongly suggests that the bake element itself is the most likely issue. I would recommend replacing the bake element. You can often find replacement parts online, and it's a relatively straightforward job if you’re comfortable with basic repairs. If not, a professional technician can replace it for you.

How to replace back element and trouble shoot

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Replacing the bake element in your oven is a fairly straightforward process if you're comfortable working with basic tools and handling electrical components. Here’s a step-by-step guide to troubleshooting and replacing the bake element:

### **1. Safety First**
- **Turn off the Power:** Before starting any repairs, turn off the power to the oven by unplugging it or turning off the circuit breaker that supplies power to the oven.
- **Confirm Power is Off:** Double-check that the oven is not receiving any power before proceeding.

### **2. Access the Bake Element**
- **Locate the Bake Element:** The bake element is usually located at the bottom of the oven interior.
- **Remove the Oven Racks:** Take out any racks to give yourself room to work.
- **Inspect the Bake Element:** Look for visible signs of damage, such as blistering, burns, or breaks. If any of these signs are present, the element likely needs to be replaced.

### **3. Remove the Bake Element**
- **Unscrew the Element:** The bake element is typically held in place by a few screws, either at the back of the oven or at the bottom.
- **Carefully Pull the Element Forward:** Gently pull the bake element toward you. You’ll see that it’s connected by two wires at the back.
- **Disconnect the Wires:** Before disconnecting, take note (or a picture) of how the wires are connected. Then, carefully disconnect the wires from the element. These might be connected via screws or slide-on connectors.

### **4. Test the Bake Element (Optional)**
- **Use a Multimeter:** If you want to confirm that the element is faulty, you can use a multimeter to test it for continuity. Set the multimeter to the lowest ohms setting and touch each probe to one of the terminals on the bake element.
- **Check for Continuity:** A reading of zero or near zero means the element has continuity and is likely not the issue. A high reading or no reading at all indicates a break in the element, meaning it needs to be replaced.

### **5. Install the New Bake Element**
- **Connect the Wires:** Attach the wires to the new bake element exactly as they were on the old one. Make sure the connections are tight.
- **Secure the Element:** Push the element back into place and secure it with the screws you removed earlier.
- **Reassemble the Oven:** Replace the oven racks.

### **6. Test the Oven**
- **Restore Power:** Turn the power back on by plugging the oven in or turning on the circuit breaker.
- **Test the Bake Function:** Set the oven to bake and see if it heats up properly. Monitor the temperature to ensure it reaches and maintains the desired setting.

### **7. Further Troubleshooting (If Needed)**
- **If the Bake Element Doesn’t Work:**
  - **Check the Wiring:** Ensure that the wires connected to the element aren’t damaged or loose.
  - **Inspect the Control Board:** If the new element doesn’t solve the problem, the issue may be with the control board or thermostat. This usually requires a professional diagnosis and repair.

### **Tools You May Need**
- Screwdriver (usually Phillips)
- Multimeter (for testing continuity)
- Replacement bake element (specific to your oven model)

### **Final Note**
If you’re not comfortable working with electrical components or if the issue persists after replacing the bake element, it’s best to call a professional technician to avoid any potential hazards.
