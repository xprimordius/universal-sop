If I have new whites that are also blue and purple, do these go in the darks? So they don’t stain the whites?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes — to protect your pure whites:

**Treat these shirts (white with blue/purple tie-dye or print) as *colors*, not whites.**  
Here's the breakdown:

### Why:
- **New dyes bleed**, especially blue and purple.
- If washed with pure whites, they can tint them (especially if water is warm or hot).

### What to do:
- **Wash separately the first 2–3 times**, ideally in cold water.
- After that, you can group them with **light colors** or **colors**, but not with bright whites.

### Sort like this:
- **Whites:** All-pure whites only.
- **Lights:** Pastels or light prints on white (after bleed test).
- **Darks:** Black, navy, red, saturated colors.

✅ Cold wash minimizes bleeding  
✅ Inside-out helps preserve prints  
✅ Vinegar or color-catcher sheets help protect if you're mixing carefully

Won’t hang drying mold

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Great question — **hang drying will not cause mold** if you follow these quick principles:

---

### ✅ Prevent Mold While Air Drying:

1. **Hang in open air** (ventilated room, closet rod, shower bar, etc.)
2. **Avoid damp piles** — spread them out or hang individually
3. **Flip inside-out** to speed drying + protect prints
4. **Use a fan or crack a window** for airflow if room is humid
5. **Dry same day** — most shirts dry in 4–8 hours indoors

---

### ❌ Mold Risks Only Happen If:
- Clothes stay **balled up** and damp for 24+ hrs
- You leave them **in the washer wet overnight**
- Drying is attempted in **sealed, unventilated rooms**

---

### 🧠 Pro Tip:
Lay the shirts flat on a towel near a window or fan for speed + safety.
