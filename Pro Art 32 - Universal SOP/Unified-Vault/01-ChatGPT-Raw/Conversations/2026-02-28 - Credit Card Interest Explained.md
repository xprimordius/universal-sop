- The application flow suggested it was revolving  

If none of that occurred, simply not calling it a “charge card” is not automatically deceptive.

---

### 3️⃣ The Legal Reality

Under federal law:

- “Credit card” = access device for open-end credit.
- It does **not** require revolving functionality.
- A pay-in-full card can still legally be called a credit card.

So the absence of the phrase “charge card” alone is not a violation.

---

### 4️⃣ Where Liability Would Exist

There would only be regulatory exposure if:

- Marketing said “carry a balance”
- Marketing showed minimum payments smaller than full balance
- Or underwriting described it as revolving

That would create a **material inconsistency**.

---

### 5️⃣ What This Really Comes Down To

The strongest argument (if any) would be:
“Did the marketing create a reasonable expectation of revolving credit?”

That’s a fact-based question.

Not a definition question.
