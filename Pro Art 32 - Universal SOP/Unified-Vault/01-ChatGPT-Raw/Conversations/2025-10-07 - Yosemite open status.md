## 🤖 ChatGPT

Excellent question — and one most Americans ask every time it happens.  

Here’s the **real breakdown:**

---

### 🎯 The Purpose (In Theory)
A *government shutdown* is designed as **leverage** — a pressure tactic built into the U.S. budgeting system.  
- Congress must pass 12 funding bills each fiscal year.  
- If they can’t agree, the government legally loses authority to spend money on “non-essential” operations.  
- The goal is to **force negotiation** by making political gridlock painful and visible.

---

### ⚙️ What Actually “Shuts Down”
Only **non-essential discretionary services** pause — things like:
- National parks (partially closed)
- Passport processing
- Federal research programs
- Many federal employees furloughed without pay

Essential services continue:
- Military, air traffic control, Social Security, Medicare, IRS basics, postal service.

So, the government isn’t truly “off.” It’s *limping* — crucial systems run, but progress, projects, and paychecks stall.

---

### 💰 Why It Feels Pointless
Because 80–85% of federal spending is **mandatory** (by law: Social Security, Medicaid, debt payments, etc.), shutdowns only affect a small slice of operations — but cause **massive ripple effects**:
- Billions lost in economic productivity  
- Delays in services and permits  
- Federal workers going unpaid temporarily  
- Erosion of public trust

---

### 🧩 Bottom Line
A shutdown doesn’t save money — it **costs** billions — but it’s used as a **political weapon** to gain leverage in funding battles.
