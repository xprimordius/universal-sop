**Headline:** There is **no single law** that outright bans a funding company from “guaranteeing” a minimum funding amount — **it becomes illegal based on *how* the guarantee is made and *what role* the company is actually playing.**

Below is the **clean, legally accurate breakdown** 👇

---

## 1. FTC Act — Deceptive or Unfair Practices  
**entity["organization","Federal Trade Commission","us consumer protection agency"] – FTC Act §5 (15 U.S.C. §45)**

**Trigger:**  
A funding company **guarantees a minimum funding amount** when:
- They **do not control the lender’s underwriting**, or  
- Approval depends on **third-party banks**, or  
- The result depends on the consumer’s **credit profile as PG**

**Why illegal:**  
The guarantee is a **material misrepresentation** of certainty and control.

**Key principle:**  
> You cannot guarantee an outcome you do not control.

This is the **most common enforcement hook** used by the FTC.

---

## 2. State UDAP Laws (Every State)  
**Unfair & Deceptive Acts and Practices statutes**

**Trigger:**  
- “Guaranteed $100K+ funding”
- “Approved or your money back” (without true lender control)
- Guarantees tied to **shelf corps + PG credit stacking**

**Why illegal:**  
States prohibit **false promises of financial results** and **illusory guarantees**.

**Reality:**  
State AGs enforce this **far more aggressively** than people realize.

---

## 3. Securities Law — If It’s Framed as an Investment  
**entity["organization","Securities and Exchange Commission","us securities regulator"]  
Securities Act of 1933 + Howey Test**

**Trigger:**  
If the shelf corp + funding is marketed as:
- “Passive”
- “Turnkey”
- “We do everything”
- “Guaranteed capital to deploy for profit”

**Why illegal:**  
That can become an **unregistered security**, and guarantees of returns or capital access are **strictly prohibited**.

---

## 4. Credit Services / Loan Broker Statutes (State-Level)  
Often called:
- Credit Services Organization (CSO) laws  
- Loan broker / financing broker statutes  

**Trigger:**  
- Upfront fees **plus**
- Guaranteed funding amounts
- Especially when using **consumer credit as PG**

**Why illegal:**  
Most states prohibit:
- Guarantees of credit results  
- Fees tied to promised financing outcomes  

Some states require **licensure** just to broker credit.

---

## 5. Contract Law — The Guarantee Is Often Void  
**Common Law Fraud / Unconscionability**

Even if not prosecuted:
- The “guarantee” is often **unenforceable**
- Courts void contracts where outcomes depend on **independent third parties**

---

## Critical Distinction (This Is the Line That Matters)

**Legal:**  
> “We will *attempt* to secure funding using best efforts. Results vary. No guarantees.”

**Illegal:**  
> “We guarantee you will receive at least $___ in funding.”

If the funding relies on:
- Banks
- Issuers
- The client’s PG credit
- Automated underwriting

👉 **A guarantee is legally indefensible.**

---

## Bottom Line  
There is **no magic statute number**, but guarantees fail under:

- **FTC Act §5 (15 U.S.C. §45)**  
- **State UDAP laws**  
- **State loan broker / CSO statutes**  
- **Federal & state securities law (if framed as passive/turnkey)**  
- **Common law fraud**
