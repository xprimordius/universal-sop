## 👤 You

Is there any extra compensation due since I have to chase them down and do all this work where they hit and ran? Is that a crime?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes — a **hit-and-run is a crime**, and while you don’t get paid *just* for the hassle of chasing it down, there are indirect ways you can be compensated depending on how you handle it:  

---

### ⚖️ **1. It’s a Crime (Hit-and-Run)**
- In every U.S. state, **leaving the scene of an accident** without stopping is illegal.  
- Even if it’s only property damage (like your cargo box), it’s typically a **misdemeanor**, escalating to a **felony** if injury or major damage occurs.  
- Police can **cite, fine, or arrest** the driver once identified.

---

### 💰 **2. Your Compensation Options**
You can pursue financial recovery through:

#### a. **Insurance Claim**
- File under your **collision** or **uninsured motorist property damage (UMPD)** coverage.  
- Your insurer can **subrogate** (go after the at-fault driver or their insurer) for repayment — including your deductible.

#### b. **Civil Damages (Small Claims Court)**
If the driver is identified:
- You can sue for:
  - Repair or replacement costs (cargo box, car, etc.)
  - **Loss of use** (if you couldn’t drive or use the box)
  - **Incidental damages** (e.g., towing, time, effort, paperwork)
- Some states allow **“pain and suffering” or “inconvenience”** claims in small claims if you can show clear impact or lost time.

#### c. **Restitution (Criminal Case)**
If prosecuted, you can request **restitution** through the criminal court — this compensates you for **all out-of-pocket losses** tied to the incident.

---

### 📋 **3. What You Should Do**
1. File a **police report** immediately (include the video).  
2. File an **insurance claim** — mark it as a **hit-and-run**.  
3. Ask for your **claim to include time and administrative burden** under *“incidental damages.”*  
4. Keep **all receipts and time logs** — they strengthen restitution or small-claims filings later.
