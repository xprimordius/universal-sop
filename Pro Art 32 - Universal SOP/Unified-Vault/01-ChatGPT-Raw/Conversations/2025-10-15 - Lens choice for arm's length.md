## Key Differences You’ll Notice

1. **Light gathering / low light performance**  
   - The f/1.4 is about **2⅔ stops** “faster” than f/4. That means in low light you can shoot at faster shutter speeds (or lower ISO) and still get usable exposure.  
   - If you shoot indoors, in dim rooms, or at night, the f/1.4 gives you a big advantage.

2. **Depth of Field & Background Separation (Bokeh)**  
   - At f/1.4 you can get much shallower depth of field. Your subject can be sharp while the background falls off nicely into blur. That gives a more “cinematic / portrait” feel.  
   - At f/4, more of the scene will be in focus. If you want everything sharp (you, background, surroundings), f/4 is more forgiving.

3. **Image Quality & Optics**  
   - Primes often have optical designs optimized for that one focal length and can exhibit **less distortion, better sharpness, less chromatic aberration** especially wide open. 4. **Flexibility & Convenience**  
   - The f/4 variant (if it's a zoom or slower version) may have advantages in weight, size, cost, and ease of use. You might be able to stop it down and still get acceptable performance in many conditions.  
   - The f/1.4 prime gives you more creative options (shallow DOF, low light) but you’ll also have to manage focus more carefully and sometimes deal with softness or aberrations wide open (depending on the lens).

5. **Edge / corner behavior & distortion**  
   - At f/1.4, depending on how “good” the lens is, you might see softness or vignetting or more aberration in the corners if you're not stopping down.  
   - At f/4, many lenses (especially quality ones) perform more uniformly across the frame.  
   - The prime often has better control over distortion because it's not trying to cover a zoom range.
