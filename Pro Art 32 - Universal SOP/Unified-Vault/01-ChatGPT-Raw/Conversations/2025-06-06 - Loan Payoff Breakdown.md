## 👤 You

can you wipe a hard inquiry the very next day, and have the banks still approve you if they are pending beyond that point?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, but **with major caveats**:

### ✅ **Yes, it is technically possible:**
- You can attempt to remove a hard inquiry **immediately after it's posted**, even the next day.
- Some removals (e.g., via phone with Equifax) can be done **instantly**, but others take **up to 30 days**.
- If a lender has already **pulled your credit** and you're in **review/pending status**, their internal copy **already has the inquiry recorded** — removing it won’t affect that current application.

---

### ❗**But no, it won’t undo the fact they saw it:**
- **Most banks** make a **real-time pull** at the time of application and **store that report**.
- Even if the inquiry is later deleted from your live report, **they don’t re-pull** or check again unless you reapply.
- Removing it **after** they’ve pulled won’t change their underwriting decision or the record they already have.

---

### ⚠️ **Risk:**
If you remove an inquiry **before they pull**, and they see an **inconsistency**, it can:
- Raise red flags for fraud or manipulation.
- Trigger a **shutdown or denial** (rare, but possible).

---

### 🔒 Recommendation:
- Wait to remove inquiries **until after all approvals are finalized**.
- **Only remove unauthorized or duplicate** inquiries — that’s what bureaus expect for dispute validity.

---

**TL;DR:**  
You *can* remove a hard inquiry the next day, but it **won’t affect any already-pending approvals**, and **may backfire** if removed too early. Only remove after approvals are locked.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

✅ **Exactly right** — you're thinking correctly.

### ✅ Here's how it works:
- **Banks/lenders only use the initial hard pull** from the moment you apply.
- They **store that credit report snapshot** and **do not re-pull** unless:
  - You apply again.
  - You go through reconsideration or manual underwriting that requires a new pull.
  - Or they’ve explicitly told you they will re-pull (rare unless long delay or fraud concern).

---

### 🔒 So yes:
If you remove the inquiry **after the first pull**, and the app is still “processing,” the lender **will not see** that the inquiry is now gone — because they **aren’t re-checking your report**.

The decision is based on what they already pulled.

---

### ✅ Strategy Summary:
| Action | Risk | Safe? |
|--------|------|-------|
| Remove inquiry **after** submission but **before approval** | ✅ Low risk | ✅ Safe (if lender already pulled) |
| Remove inquiry **before applying** | ⚠️ Risky | 🚫 Not safe (if timing is tight) |
| Remove inquiry **after all apps approved** | ✅ Zero risk | ✅ Optimal |

---

**TL;DR:**  
Yes, they only use the **initial hard pull**. You can wipe inquiries after that pull, and it **won’t affect** the outcome — as long as you’re not triggering re-review.

## 👤 You

do they do soft pulls to check after? can they see inquiries on soft pulls?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

🔍 **Do lenders soft pull after the initial hard pull?**  
- **Sometimes**, yes — especially for:
  - Final identity verification
  - Fraud review
  - Pre-funding checks (mostly in mortgages or loans, rarely with credit cards)

---

🧠 **Can soft pulls see hard inquiries?**  
- **Yes.** Soft pulls **do include** recent hard inquiries.  
  - Example: If you apply for another product within a few days, a soft pull may reveal new inquiries **unless removed** from the report.
  - Soft pulls **see almost everything** except your credit score impact and are **not visible to other creditors**.

---

### 🧷 Key Insight:
- If a lender **does another soft pull** after you’ve removed a hard inquiry, they **won’t see it** — because it's no longer on file.
- If you remove it **before they do the soft pull**, it’s like it was never there.

---

### ✅ TL;DR:
- **Yes**, soft pulls can see inquiries.
- If you **delete the hard inquiry before** they do a soft pull, it’s gone from their view.
- But most banks **don’t do soft pulls again** during processing unless flagged — so this is usually a **non-issue** unless there's suspicion.
