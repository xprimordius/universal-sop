## 🤖 ChatGPT

This is a common Tesla Model Y issue—your car’s Wi-Fi module often won’t pull anywhere near your home’s rated internet speed. Here’s why and how to fix it:

### Why It Happens
1. **Hardware Limitation**  
   - Tesla Wi-Fi hardware often caps out far below gigabit speeds, especially if it’s not Wi-Fi 6. Many users report 20–80 Mbps max.  
   - Cars prioritize stability over raw speed since OTA downloads happen in the background.

2. **Signal Interference / Range**  
   - Thick garage walls or distance from the router can drop speeds dramatically.  
   - The car’s antenna placement isn’t optimized for Wi-Fi like a laptop/phone.

3. **2.4GHz vs 5GHz Bands**  
   - 2.4GHz → more stable but much slower.  
   - 5GHz → much faster, but weaker range/penetration.  
   - Tesla may default to 2.4GHz depending on conditions.

4. **Tesla Server Bottleneck**  
   - Even if your Wi-Fi is fast, Tesla update servers often throttle OTA downloads to ~50 Mbps.

### Fixes / Optimizations
- **Force 5GHz Band** → In your router, create a separate SSID for 5GHz only, connect Tesla to that.  
- **Router Placement** → Move router closer or add a mesh/extender in the garage/driveway.  
- **Check Interference** → Other 2.4GHz devices (smart plugs, cameras) may be slowing connection.  
- **Use Phone Hotspot** → Surprisingly, LTE/5G hotspots sometimes download faster than home Wi-Fi.  
- **Hard Reset Car’s Wi-Fi** → Forget and reconnect the network in Tesla settings.  
- **Ethernet Adapter** (advanced) → Some owners use a Powerline adapter + Ethernet-to-Wi-Fi bridge for stable higher throughput.

⚠️ Reality check: Even with a 300 Mbps home plan, you’ll likely max out ~40–80 Mbps in Tesla due to server and hardware limits. That’s normal—downloads may take hours regardless.
