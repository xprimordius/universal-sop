Silicon, a semiconductor, is the primary material used in CPU manufacturing. The process begins with the growth of a pure silicon single crystal, typically using the Czochralski method, where a seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon and slowly withdrawn to form a large, monocrystalline ingot. This ingot is then sliced into thin wafers, which serve as the substrate for CPUs.

The planar process is employed to create intricate transistor patterns on these wafers. This involves photolithography, where light is projected through masks onto the wafer coated with a light-sensitive material, defining the circuit patterns. Subsequent steps include doping, to modify electrical properties, and etching, to remove unwanted material, thereby constructing the layered architecture of the CPU.

Crystals also play a crucial role in timing functions within computer systems. Quartz crystals, for example, are used in oscillators to generate precise clock signals that synchronize the operations of the CPU and other components. These crystals ensure that data processing and communication occur at the correct frequencies, maintaining system stability and performance.

In summary, silicon single crystals form the foundation of CPU structures, while quartz crystals provide essential timing signals, both integral to the functionality of modern computing devices.
