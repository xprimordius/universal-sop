### Differences Between Peptides, Amino Acids, and Proteins:

#### 1. **Amino Acids**  
- **Definition:** The basic building blocks of peptides and proteins.  
- **Structure:** Consist of an amine group (-NH₂), a carboxyl group (-COOH), and a side chain (R group) that varies among amino acids.  
- **Function:** Serve as precursors for peptides and proteins, participate in metabolism, and support various physiological processes.  
- **Examples:** Glycine, leucine, tryptophan.

---

#### 2. **Peptides**  
- **Definition:** Short chains of amino acids linked together by peptide bonds.  
- **Structure:** Formed when the carboxyl group of one amino acid reacts with the amine group of another, releasing a molecule of water (condensation reaction).  
- **Size:** Typically contain 2–50 amino acids.  
- **Function:**  
  - Act as signaling molecules (e.g., hormones like insulin).  
  - Regulate biological processes (e.g., wound healing, immune responses).  
- **Examples:**  
  - **Dipeptides:** Two amino acids (e.g., carnosine).  
  - **Polypeptides:** Longer chains (e.g., glucagon).  

---

#### 3. **Proteins**  
- **Definition:** Large, complex molecules made of one or more long chains of peptides folded into specific three-dimensional structures.  
- **Structure:** Composed of more than 50 amino acids. Includes primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures.  
- **Function:**  
  - Structural support (e.g., collagen).  
  - Enzymatic activity (e.g., amylase).  
  - Transport and storage (e.g., hemoglobin).  
  - Immune responses (e.g., antibodies).  
- **Examples:** Keratin, enzymes, hemoglobin.  

---

### Key Differences

| **Feature**      | **Amino Acids**              | **Peptides**                  | **Proteins**                   |
|-------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Size**         | Single molecules             | 2–50 amino acids              | >50 amino acids               |
| **Complexity**   | Simple molecules             | Simple chains                 | Complex 3D structures         |
| **Function**     | Building blocks              | Signaling, regulation         | Structural, enzymatic, transport |
| **Examples**     | Glycine, leucine             | Insulin, oxytocin             | Collagen, hemoglobin          |

---

### Summary  
- **Amino acids** are the basic units.  
- **Peptides** are small chains of amino acids.  
- **Proteins** are large, complex structures formed from peptides.
