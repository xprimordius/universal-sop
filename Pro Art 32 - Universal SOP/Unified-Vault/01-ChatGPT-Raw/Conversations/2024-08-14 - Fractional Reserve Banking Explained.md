# Fractional Reserve Banking Explained

---

## 👤 You

How does fractional reserve banking turn $100K into $900K

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Fractional reserve banking allows banks to lend out a portion of their deposits while keeping a fraction in reserve. Here’s how $100,000 can theoretically turn into $900,000 through the process:

1. **Initial Deposit:** Someone deposits $100,000 into a bank.

2. **Reserve Requirement:** Assume the reserve requirement set by the central bank is 10%. This means the bank must keep $10,000 (10% of $100,000) in reserve but can lend out the remaining $90,000.

3. **First Loan:** The bank lends $90,000 to a borrower. The borrower deposits this $90,000 into their bank account, which could be in the same bank or a different one.

4. **Second Loan:** The receiving bank now has $90,000 in new deposits. With a 10% reserve requirement, this bank must keep $9,000 in reserve and can lend out $81,000.

5. **Process Continues:** The process repeats, with each bank keeping 10% of the new deposits in reserve and lending out the remaining 90%. 

6. **Money Creation:** The original $100,000 deposit leads to a series of loans, which theoretically increases the money supply. If you sum all the loans created through this process, it can approach $900,000. This occurs because the $100,000 deposit is multiplied by the money multiplier, which in this example is 1 / 0.1 = 10. Thus, $100,000 * 9 (since the original deposit is not considered as loan-created money) = $900,000.

### Key Concepts:
- **Money Multiplier:** The money multiplier in this case is 10 (1 divided by the reserve ratio of 10%).
- **Credit Expansion:** The total potential money supply expansion is $100,000 * 9 = $900,000 in loans.

This process shows how fractional reserve banking can expand the money supply through repeated lending, multiplying the initial deposit many times over.
