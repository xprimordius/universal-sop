## Minnesota (MN)
- **You may qualify for a refund or replacement** if the vehicle is defective.
- **Mandatory first step:**  
  You must **notify the manufacturer in writing** and give them a **reasonable chance to repair**.
- **Before court:**  
  You must use **Tesla’s arbitration program** (run by the National Center for Dispute Settlement) **before filing suit**.
- **Magnuson-Moss (federal warranty law):**  
  Arbitration is **not required**, but still available.

---

## Montana (MT)
- **You may qualify** if:
  - Tesla can’t fix the defect after a reasonable number of attempts **or**
  - The vehicle is out of service too long.
- **Mandatory first step:**  
  **Certified written notice** to Tesla describing the defect.
- **Arbitration:**  
  Offered, but **not always mandatory** depending on the claim path.

### 1) **Use Federal Warranty Law (Primary Path)**
- **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (MMWA)**  
  If your claim is **federal warranty–based**, **mandatory arbitration is not required** unless the warranty clearly and lawfully conditions suit on it.
- Many courts hold **MMWA claims can proceed directly to court**, even when state lemon statutes require arbitration.

**Result:** You bypass Tesla’s arbitration gate entirely.

---

### 2) **File Claims Arbitration Does NOT Cover**
Arbitration programs are usually limited to **“lemon law repurchase/replacement.”**

You can proceed directly to court with claims such as:
- **Breach of express warranty**
- **Breach of implied warranty**
- **Failure to repair / unreasonable delay**
- **Deceptive or unfair practices** (state consumer protection acts)

**Result:** Arbitration becomes irrelevant.

---

### 3) **Arbitration Clause Is Procedurally Defective**
You may bypass if:
- The warranty **fails to clearly disclose** arbitration as mandatory
- The program **does not meet statutory standards**
- The notice was **not conspicuous or properly delivered**
- Tesla **failed to follow its own arbitration procedures**

**Result:** Court can deny enforcement.
