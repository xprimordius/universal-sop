If someone drives my car with me in it, do I need extra insurance for them or? What if they don’t have insurance

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Headline: You’re Covered — As Long As Your Policy Allows “Permissive Use.”**

**TL;DR:**  
If they’re driving *your* car *with your permission,* **your insurance usually covers them** — even if they personally have no insurance. But there are limits.

**Core Rules (Straight Facts):**

1. **Car insurance follows the car, not the driver.**  
   Your policy is typically primary coverage when *you* allow someone to drive your car.

2. **Permissive Use = Covered (usually).**  
   Most standard policies automatically cover anyone you **give permission** to drive.

3. **If THEY don’t have their own insurance:**  
   - Your liability coverage applies.  
   - Your collision/comprehensive applies (if you have it).  
   - Their lack of personal insurance doesn’t matter for the claim **as long as they had permission** and aren’t excluded on your policy.

4. **If they cause a crash:**  
   - Your insurance pays first.  
   - If damages exceed your limits, the driver's insurance (if they have any) may kick in second.  
   - If they have **no** insurance, you’re still primary — but you might be personally exposed if the damage exceeds your coverage.

5. **When there’s a problem:**  
   You need **extra coverage or a named-driver addition** if:  
   - They live with you but are **not listed** (most insurers require all household drivers listed).  
   - Your insurer excludes “permissive use” or lowers coverage for non-listed drivers.  
   - The driver is high-risk and your policy **specifically excludes** them.

6. **Ride-along doesn’t matter.**  
   *You being in the car does not change coverage.*  
   The insurer only cares who’s operating the vehicle.

---

**Bottom Line:**  
You **don’t** need extra insurance for someone to drive *your* car with permission, even if they don’t have insurance — unless your specific policy says otherwise.
