# Photocopied Affidavit Validity

---

## 👤 You

Does a photocopied affidavit work for disputes? Or does it need to be original

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

For disputes, particularly in credit-related matters, it is generally recommended to provide original, signed affidavits whenever possible. Photocopied affidavits may be considered less credible and could be rejected by some institutions or credit bureaus, especially if they are looking for reasons to deny your dispute. 

Submitting an original affidavit shows a higher level of seriousness and authenticity in your claim, and it’s more likely to be accepted without issues. However, in some cases, if a photocopy is all you have, it could still be submitted, but it's crucial to follow up and confirm that the institution accepts it. For critical legal disputes or cases involving identity theft, original, notarized documents are usually the standard requirement.

## 👤 You

What if I have photo copies of the affidavit, but get each one notorized?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

If you have photocopies of an affidavit but get each one notarized, they effectively become as credible as the original, provided the notarization is properly executed. A notarized document means that the notary has verified your identity and witnessed your signature, which adds a level of authenticity and legal standing to the document.

Here’s how it typically works:

1. **Notarization Process**: The notary public will confirm your identity and witness your signature on each photocopy of the affidavit. They will then add their official seal and signature to the document, indicating that it has been notarized.

2. **Acceptance**: Since each notarized photocopy is now treated as an original, it should generally be accepted by most institutions, including credit bureaus and legal entities, as valid evidence in disputes.

3. **Best Practices**: Ensure that each notarized copy is clearly legible and that the notary’s seal and signature are prominently visible. It’s also a good idea to check with the specific institution or entity you’re submitting to, just to confirm that they accept notarized copies.

By notarizing each photocopy, you significantly increase the chances of your documents being accepted without issue.

## 👤 You

Will I run into any problems? Or am I better off just printing out each form and filling them each out manually

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

While notarizing photocopies of affidavits is generally acceptable, printing out each form and filling them out manually may reduce potential issues, especially if you're dealing with institutions that are particularly stringent about document authenticity.

Here are a few considerations:

1. **Institutional Preferences**: Some institutions may prefer original documents over photocopies, even if notarized. This is especially true in legal or financial contexts where the authenticity of documents is paramount. By providing manually filled-out originals, you eliminate any doubt about the document's validity.

2. **Risk of Rejection**: Although notarized photocopies should be acceptable, there is always a small risk that they might be questioned or rejected, depending on the policies of the institution handling your dispute. Original documents are less likely to face scrutiny.

3. **Time and Effort**: Manually filling out each form can be time-consuming, but it guarantees that each document is an original. This can save time and hassle in the long run by avoiding potential back-and-forth if a notarized photocopy is rejected.

**Recommendation**: If the time and effort required to manually fill out each form are manageable, it’s generally the safer route. However, if you need to produce a large number of affidavits and notarizing photocopies is significantly more convenient, this approach should work in most cases, particularly if you confirm in advance that the receiving party accepts notarized copies. 

If absolute certainty is crucial, manually completing each affidavit might be worth the extra effort to ensure no issues arise.

## 👤 You

Is it common for credit bureaus to deny this for identity theft account removals?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

It is not uncommon for credit bureaus to be stringent when reviewing documentation for identity theft-related account removals. Credit bureaus are responsible for ensuring the accuracy of credit reports, and they may scrutinize documentation closely to prevent fraudulent claims. Here’s what you might encounter:

### Common Challenges:
1. **Verification Process**: Credit bureaus often require thorough documentation to verify identity theft claims. If they perceive any discrepancies or if the documents (like photocopied affidavits) seem less credible, they may deny the request.

2. **Documentation Standards**: Bureaus may require original or notarized documents, such as a police report, a notarized affidavit, and proof of identity, to process identity theft claims. They might be particularly strict about accepting only original documents, especially for something as serious as removing accounts.

3. **Delay Tactics**: In some cases, credit bureaus have been known to delay or complicate the process by requesting additional documentation or questioning the legitimacy of the documents provided. This is partly to ensure the validity of the claim, but it can also be a hurdle for consumers.

### Best Practices:
1. **Submit Original Documents**: If possible, submit original, signed, and notarized affidavits. This approach minimizes the risk of your claim being denied based on the documentation.

2. **Complete Package**: Ensure you provide a complete package of documents that the bureau might require, including a police report, FTC Identity Theft Report, and copies of any relevant correspondence.

3. **Follow Up**: After submitting your documents, follow up with the credit bureaus to ensure they received everything and to confirm that your case is being processed. Keeping a record of all communication can be helpful if you need to escalate the matter.

### Conclusion:
While notarized photocopies might be accepted, there's a higher chance of encountering challenges or delays compared to submitting original documents. To avoid complications, it's often best to provide original, manually completed, and notarized affidavits. This approach reduces the likelihood of your claim being denied or delayed.

## 👤 You

If the affidavit has 6 pages printed and written with blue ink, and 2 pages printed and filled out via computer text, is this okay as one whole 8 page notorized document? Or will this be a problem

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Generally, mixing handwritten and typed pages in a notarized document is not a problem as long as the following conditions are met:

1. **Consistency and Clarity**: The document should be clear, consistent, and easy to follow. The combination of handwritten and typed text should not create any confusion or discrepancies in the content.

2. **Complete Document**: All pages should be presented together as a complete document. When the notary notarizes the affidavit, they will typically notarize the entire document, ensuring that all pages are accounted for and properly attached or referenced.

3. **Notary Seal and Signature**: The notary will likely place their seal and signature on the last page of the document. However, some notaries may place a small mark or initial on each page to indicate that each is part of the notarized document. Ensure that all pages are securely attached or that the notary confirms they are part of the same document.

4. **No Alterations After Notarization**: Once notarized, the document should not be altered. Any additions, corrections, or changes should be made before notarization.

5. **Institution Requirements**: While the above general guidelines apply, it’s wise to confirm with the institution you’re submitting the affidavit to, as some may have specific requirements regarding the uniformity of the document. They may prefer either all handwritten or all typed for consistency, though this is less common.

### Recommendation:
Before proceeding with notarization, it’s a good idea to check with the notary to ensure they are comfortable notarizing a document that mixes handwritten and typed pages. If they are, and if the institution doesn’t have specific rules against it, this approach should generally be acceptable.

However, if you want to avoid any potential issues, you could consider either:

1. **Converting all pages to one format**: Either fully handwritten or fully typed, to maintain uniformity.
2. **Clearly indicating any sections that are handwritten and typed**: To avoid any confusion.

This proactive approach ensures that your affidavit is accepted without any complications.

## 👤 You

Can the credit bureaus legally deny it? Or have been known to deny this affidavit

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Credit bureaus can deny an affidavit if they believe it does not meet their requirements or standards. However, whether they *legally* can deny an affidavit depends on the specific circumstances and how the affidavit is presented.

### Legal Grounds for Denial:
1. **Inconsistent or Incomplete Information**: If the affidavit contains inconsistencies, incomplete information, or is difficult to read or understand due to the mixed format, a credit bureau might deny it. The denial would be based on the need for clarity and completeness in the documentation.

2. **Notary Issues**: If there are any issues with the notarization, such as the notary not properly witnessing the signature or any suspicion that the document has been tampered with after notarization, the credit bureau might deny the affidavit.

3. **Document Authenticity**: Credit bureaus might be more skeptical of documents that appear altered or inconsistent (e.g., a mix of handwritten and typed pages), which could lead them to question the authenticity of the affidavit. In some cases, they could deny it on the basis that it does not appear to be a genuine or complete document.

### Known Practices:
- **Strict Requirements**: Credit bureaus are known to have strict documentation requirements, especially for sensitive issues like identity theft. They may reject documents that do not meet their formatting or content standards, even if those standards are not explicitly stated.

- **Document Rejection**: There have been instances where credit bureaus have rejected affidavits due to perceived issues with the document's format, notarization, or completeness. This can happen if they suspect the affidavit is not authentic or does not comply with their guidelines.

### What You Can Do:
- **Uniform Format**: To reduce the risk of denial, it's generally advisable to provide a uniformly formatted document—either all handwritten or all typed—especially when dealing with identity theft claims. This uniformity helps ensure the document is seen as consistent and credible.

- **Confirm Requirements**: Before submitting your affidavit, you can contact the credit bureau to confirm their specific requirements for affidavits in identity theft cases. This can help you ensure that your document meets their standards.

- **Follow Up**: If your affidavit is denied, you can request a detailed explanation from the credit bureau. In some cases, you may need to revise and resubmit the affidavit or escalate the matter through a formal dispute process.

### Conclusion:
While credit bureaus have the legal right to deny an affidavit if they believe it doesn't meet their standards, this usually happens when there are issues with the document's clarity, consistency, or authenticity. To avoid problems, it's best to provide a clear, uniform, and well-prepared affidavit that meets their requirements. If you're ever in doubt, contacting the bureau in advance can help clarify what they expect.
