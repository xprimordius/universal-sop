## **What a Federal Trademark Actually Does**
A trademark—federal or otherwise—gives you **intellectual property rights** over a name, logo, or brand **in commerce**.  
- It lets you **prevent others from using your name (or brand)** in ways that would confuse consumers in specific business categories.  
- It does **not** give you control over how credit reporting agencies maintain or disclose your credit history. citeturn1search13

---

## **What Governs Credit Reporting**
The law that controls how credit bureaus (like Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) handle your credit information is the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)**—not trademark law. citeturn1search8

### **Core rules under FCRA**
- CRAs can collect and report accurate credit information about you (positive and negative) because credit reports are tied to your **Social Security number and financial history**, not trademark rights.  
- They must follow legal procedures for accuracy, privacy, and permissible purposes.  
- Consumers have rights to dispute errors, and CRAs must investigate and correct unverified or inaccurate data. citeturn0search1turn1search14

---

## **Can CRAs report negative items without permission?**
**Yes.**  
- CRAs are allowed to include negative items on your credit report if they genuinely reflect your financial history (e.g., late payments, defaults, collections), **even if you do not “consent” explicitly**—because reporting that information is part of how credit systems work under FCRA.  
- Consent is required only for certain specific uses (like employment credit checks), not for general credit reporting to lenders. citeturn0search1

---

## **Are there fines like $50,000 per incident?**
**No statutory $50,000 fine tied to trademark or general credit reporting protections exists.**  
Penalties under FCRA include:  
- Civil damages awarded by courts (actual, statutory, and sometimes punitive) when CRAs or furnishers violate FCRA rights.  
- The **FTC’s inflation-adjusted civil penalty** for knowing violations of some FCRA provisions is under **$5,000 per violation**, not $50,000. citeturn0search0turn0search20

There’s no federal rule that says *simply reporting negative credit info* is unlawful or triggers a fixed $50k penalty. Any damages depend on **specific legal violations**, not on trademark ownership.

---

## **What *can* you do for credit protection**
- Place a **security freeze** on your credit file so CRAs can’t release it to new creditors without your consent. citeturn0search5  
- Dispute errors under FCRA to compel removal of incorrect information. citeturn1search14  
- Sue under FCRA if CRAs or furnishers violate your legal rights—but recovery is based on statutory and actual damages, not trademark status. citeturn0search0

---

### **Bottom line**
**Trademark rights and credit reporting rights are separate legal systems.**  
A trademark on your name does **not give you special exclusion from negative credit reporting or a built-in $50,000 penalty for negative entries.** Credit reporting is governed by the FCRA, with its own procedures and remedies. citeturn0search1turn1search8
