# Closed Accounts Credit Report

---

## 👤 You

why are there closed accounts still on my credit profile that were closed 9 years ago? I thought it falls off in 7 years

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Closed accounts can remain on your credit report for various reasons. Here are some key points to consider:

1. **Positive Accounts:** Positive credit information, including closed accounts with a positive history (such as paid-off loans or credit cards with no late payments), can stay on your credit report for up to 10 years from the date they were closed.

2. **Negative Accounts:** Negative information, such as late payments, charge-offs, or collections, typically falls off your credit report after 7 years. However, the account itself may still be listed as closed for up to 10 years if it was in good standing when closed.

3. **Credit Reporting Errors:** Sometimes, information remains on your credit report due to errors. If you believe an account should no longer be listed, you can dispute it with the credit bureaus.

4. **Account Status:** Ensure that the accounts listed as closed are accurately reported. Sometimes, the status might not be updated correctly.

To address this issue, you can:

1. **Check Your Credit Report:** Obtain a copy of your credit report from all three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion) to see how the accounts are listed.

2. **Dispute Inaccuracies:** If you find any inaccuracies, dispute them directly with the credit bureaus. They have 30 days to investigate and respond to your dispute.

3. **Contact Creditors:** If the credit bureaus do not resolve the issue, contact the creditors directly to correct the reporting.

If you need assistance with the dispute process or want to ensure your credit report is accurate, consider consulting a credit counseling service.

## 👤 You

how can a charge off be in good standing? what creates the extra 3 year exception

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

A charge-off cannot be in good standing. However, there might be some confusion around how long certain types of information stay on your credit report. Here are the key points:

### Charge-Offs and Negative Information:
1. **Charge-Offs**: When an account is charged off, it means the creditor has written it off as a loss because you have not made payments for a significant period. This is a negative mark and stays on your credit report for 7 years from the date of the first missed payment that led to the charge-off.
2. **Negative Information**: Generally, negative information, including late payments, collections, and charge-offs, falls off your credit report after 7 years.

### Positive Information:
1. **Closed Accounts in Good Standing**: Accounts that were closed in good standing (e.g., paid as agreed, no late payments) can remain on your credit report for up to 10 years from the date they were closed. This is beneficial because it shows a history of responsible credit use and can positively impact your credit score.

### Why a Charge-Off Cannot Be in Good Standing:
- A charge-off is inherently negative because it indicates you defaulted on the debt. Therefore, it does not fall into the category of accounts that can remain for up to 10 years due to good standing.

### Disputing Old Information:
- If a charge-off remains on your credit report beyond the 7-year period, it may be due to an error. You can dispute this with the credit bureaus to have it removed.

### Summary:
- **Charge-Offs and Negative Info**: Stay for 7 years from the date of the first delinquency.
- **Closed Accounts in Good Standing**: Can remain for up to 10 years, providing a positive credit history.

If you have specific accounts that seem to be reported inaccurately, it's best to review your credit report in detail and initiate disputes for any discrepancies.

## 👤 You

how is it possible that a card can be closed on my credit report, but somehow the banks recognize it as a charge off in their records, that the bureau reports do not show

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

It is possible for there to be discrepancies between what appears on your credit report and what banks have in their records due to several reasons:

### Reasons for Discrepancies:

1. **Timing of Updates**: Credit bureaus and creditors may update their records at different times. A bank might recognize a charge-off before it gets reported to the credit bureaus, leading to a temporary discrepancy.
  
2. **Reporting Errors**: There can be errors or delays in how creditors report information to the credit bureaus. Sometimes, accounts might not be updated accurately, leading to differences between a bank's internal records and what appears on your credit report.

3. **Multiple Systems**: Banks may have multiple internal systems for tracking accounts, and discrepancies can arise if these systems are not perfectly synchronized with the data sent to credit bureaus.

4. **Partial Reporting**: In some cases, creditors might not report all details to all credit bureaus, resulting in incomplete information on your credit report.

### Steps to Resolve Discrepancies:

1. **Request Detailed Information**: Contact the creditor directly to request detailed information about the account in question. Ask for a written explanation of its current status.

2. **Check All Three Credit Reports**: Obtain your credit reports from all three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion) to see if the charge-off appears on any of them. Sometimes, not all bureaus receive the same information.

3. **Dispute Inaccuracies**: If you find that the charge-off is not reported accurately, file a dispute with the credit bureaus. Provide any documentation you have from the bank to support your case.

4. **Monitor Your Credit**: Regularly monitor your credit reports to ensure that any discrepancies are corrected and that your information remains accurate.

5. **Seek Professional Help**: If the issue is complex or not resolved through standard dispute processes, consider seeking help from a credit repair service or a financial advisor.

### Summary:

Discrepancies between bank records and credit bureau reports can occur due to timing, reporting errors, and multiple tracking systems. To resolve this, gather detailed information from the bank, check all three credit reports, dispute inaccuracies, and consider professional assistance if needed.
