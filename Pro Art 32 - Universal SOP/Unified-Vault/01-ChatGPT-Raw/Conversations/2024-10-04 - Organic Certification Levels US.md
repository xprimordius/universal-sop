# Organic Certification Levels US

## 🤖 ChatGPT

There are several organic certifications in the U.S., with different standards and levels of stringency. USDA Organic is one of the most recognized, but you’re correct in noting that it is not the most stringent compared to other certifications. Here’s a breakdown of some key organic certifications:

### 1. **USDA Organic (Certified Organic)**
   - **Overview**: The USDA National Organic Program (NOP) regulates this certification. Products with the USDA Organic label must contain at least **95% organic ingredients**, with no synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, or genetically modified organisms (GMOs).
   - **Strength**: It's widely recognized and establishes a baseline for organic practices.
   - **Criticism**: It allows certain synthetic substances in the remaining 5%, making it less stringent compared to some private organic certifications.

### 2. **Non-GMO Project Verified**
   - **Overview**: This certification focuses on verifying that products are made without genetically modified organisms. It doesn’t fully overlap with organic standards, but it complements USDA Organic by adding an additional layer of assurance regarding GMOs.
   - **Strength**: Ensures a product is free from GMOs.
   - **Criticism**: It does not require organic farming practices and allows non-organic ingredients as long as they aren’t genetically modified.

### 3. **Demeter Certified Biodynamic**
   - **Overview**: This is a **stricter certification** than USDA Organic. It not only requires that farms meet organic standards but also integrates holistic, regenerative farming practices that promote soil fertility, ecological diversity, and animal welfare. Demeter farms must be **self-sustaining ecosystems**.
   - **Strength**: Considered one of the highest levels of organic certification, ensuring comprehensive ecological farming practices.
   - **Criticism**: It can be more expensive and difficult for farmers to obtain due to the stringent requirements.

### 4. **Certified Naturally Grown (CNG)**
   - **Overview**: This grassroots alternative to USDA Organic is designed for small-scale farmers who sell locally. It holds similar standards to USDA Organic, but the certification process is less bureaucratic and costly.
   - **Strength**: Affordable for small-scale farmers, maintaining high organic standards.
   - **Criticism**: Less regulated and less recognized compared to USDA Organic.

### 5. **Regenerative Organic Certification (ROC)**
   - **Overview**: This certification goes beyond organic by adding additional requirements for soil health, animal welfare, and social fairness. The program has three tiers: Bronze, Silver, and Gold, which represent increasing levels of regenerative practices.
   - **Strength**: It promotes regenerative farming practices that restore ecosystems and address climate change.
   - **Criticism**: It's still relatively new and not as widespread.

### 6. **Food Alliance Certified**
   - **Overview**: This certification focuses on sustainability and includes some organic practices but isn’t limited to organic production. It covers environmental stewardship, fair labor practices, and safe working conditions.
   - **Strength**: Provides a broad approach to sustainability.
   - **Criticism**: It doesn’t strictly enforce organic standards across all areas.

### Summary:
- **USDA Organic** sets a good baseline, but it's not the strictest.
- **Demeter Certified Biodynamic** and **Regenerative Organic Certification** are considered more rigorous.
- Certifications like **Certified Naturally Grown** are alternatives for smaller producers, and **Non-GMO Project Verified** focuses on specific aspects like GMOs.

If you're looking for the most stringent certifications, **Demeter** or **Regenerative Organic** are generally viewed as top-tier.
