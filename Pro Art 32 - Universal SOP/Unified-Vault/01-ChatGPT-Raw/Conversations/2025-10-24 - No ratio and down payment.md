---

### 🔹 The Basic Idea
Instead of you borrowing **all the money from a bank**,

You still get a **main loan (DSCR)** from a lender,

---

### 🔹 Example
You buy a $500,000 rental property.  
The DSCR lender says:  
> “We’ll loan you 80% = $400,000.”

Normally you’d need **$100,000 down** (20%).  

But you don’t have $100K — so you negotiate with the seller:
> “Can you finance $50,000 of it? I’ll pay you back over 3–5 years with interest.”

Now you only bring **$50,000 cash**, and the seller holds a **$50,000 note** (secured by the property as a **second lien**).

---

### 🔹 What It Looks Like on Paper
| Loan Type | Amount | Notes |
|------------|---------|-------|
| DSCR lender (first mortgage) | $400,000 | 30-yr term |
| Seller loan (second mortgage) | $50,000 | 3–5 yr, interest-only or balloon |
| Your cash | $50,000 | Actual down payment |

- They get **interest income** (often 7–10%).  
- They help sell the property faster.  
- They defer capital-gains taxes.

### 🔹 What You Need to Know
- The DSCR lender must **allow** a second lien — some do, some don’t.  
- The seller must **own the property outright** or have their current lender’s written permission.  
- You’ll sign a **promissory note** and **deed of trust** for the seller’s loan.

At closing, the title company sees:
- The bank wires in **$400K**  
- You wire in **$50K**  
That’s **$450K total**.  

Since the sale price is $500K, **there’s still $50K left** owed to the seller.

But instead of paying that last $50K in cash, the **seller agrees to “carry” it**.  
So, the title company **records a $50K promissory note** in the seller’s name — that’s their lien.  
They simply **don’t receive that $50K** at closing — it becomes a **loan from them to you**.

---

### 🔹 3. You Owe the Seller Later
That $50K stays on paper as a **debt** you owe the seller.  
You might:
- Pay **monthly interest** (e.g., 7%)  
- Or make **no payments until a balloon payoff** in 3–5 years  

So, the seller isn’t giving you new money — they’re just **deferring part of the payment** for the property.

---

### 🔹 4. Why It’s Called “Seller Financing”
They’re literally **financing part of the sale** — just like a bank would — except they use their own equity instead of wiring money.
