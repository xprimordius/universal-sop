**Insolvency and Debt Cancellation**  
Insolvency is a condition where your **liabilities exceed your total assets** at a specific point in time. This concept is relevant to 1099-C debt cancellation because the IRS allows individuals to exclude some or all canceled debt from taxable income if they can prove they were insolvent when the debt was forgiven.

### **Key Points About Insolvency**

1. **How It Works for Canceled Debt:**
   - Normally, forgiven debt is treated as taxable income.
   - However, under **IRS Form 982** ("Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to Discharge of Indebtedness"), you can exclude some or all of the canceled debt if you prove insolvency.

2. **Determining Insolvency:**
   - Compare your **total liabilities** (debts) with your **total assets** (what you own).
   - If liabilities exceed assets, the difference is the amount of insolvency.

   **Example:**
   - Total Liabilities: $50,000
   - Total Assets: $30,000
   - Insolvency Amount: $50,000 - $30,000 = $20,000

   If the debt forgiven was $4,451.48 (as shown on your 1099-C), and you were insolvent by $20,000, the entire $4,451.48 can be excluded from taxable income.

3. **What Counts as Liabilities:**
   - Mortgages
   - Credit card debt
   - Loans (auto, student, personal)
   - Any other financial obligations

4. **What Counts as Assets:**
   - Cash and bank accounts
   - Real estate (market value)
   - Vehicles
   - Investments (stocks, retirement accounts)
   - Personal property (e.g., jewelry, electronics)

5. **How to Claim Insolvency:**
   - Use **IRS Form 982** when filing your tax return.
   - Attach a detailed statement listing all assets and liabilities at the time of debt forgiveness to substantiate your insolvency.

6. **Partial Insolvency:**
   - If you were only partially insolvent (e.g., your insolvency amount was less than the forgiven debt), you can only exclude the portion of the forgiven debt equal to the insolvency amount.

   **Example:**
   - Insolvency Amount: $2,000
   - Forgiven Debt: $4,451.48
   - Taxable Income: $4,451.48 - $2,000 = $2,451.48

### **Documentation Needed to Prove Insolvency**
To claim insolvency, you'll need to prepare evidence for the IRS, such as:
- Bank account statements
- Loan and credit card balances
- Mortgage statements
- Vehicle valuation (e.g., Kelley Blue Book)
- Appraisals for real estate or personal property
