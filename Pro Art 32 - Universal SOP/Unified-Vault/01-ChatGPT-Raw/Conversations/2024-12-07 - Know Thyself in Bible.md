The exact phrase **"know thyself"** does not appear in the Bible. However, the concept aligns with biblical themes of self-awareness and introspection. The phrase is more commonly associated with ancient Greek philosophy, particularly the inscription at the Temple of Apollo at Delphi and philosophers like Socrates and Plato.

In the Bible, similar ideas are expressed in verses encouraging self-examination and understanding one's spiritual state:

1. **2 Corinthians 13:5** - *"Examine yourselves to see whether you are in the faith; test yourselves."*
2. **Proverbs 4:23** - *"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."*
3. **Psalm 139:23-24** - *"Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."*

These verses reflect the idea of introspection and seeking self-knowledge in light of God's truth, aligning with the essence of "knowing oneself" in a spiritual and moral context.
