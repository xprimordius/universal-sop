**TESLA OEM TIRES vs THIRD-PARTY – Decisive Comparison**

**1) Quality & Fit**
**OEM (Michelin/Continental on Tesla)**
- Designed specifically for Model Y load, torque, efficiency.
- Optimized for range + noise + handling balance.
- Fitment, rolling resistance, speed rating tailored for Tesla.

**Third-Party**
- Many options with **higher performance**, **longer wear**, **aggressive grip**, or **all-season upgrades**.
- Not optimized for EV efficiency unless you pick EV-rated tires.

**2) Range / Efficiency**
- **OEM ≈ Best baseline** for maximizing range w/o tradeoffs.
- **Third-party EV tires** with low rolling resistance can beat OEM range.
- High-performance tires often **reduce range** (softer compounds).

**3) Wear & Longevity**
- OEM wear life is **average** for size/category.
- **Third-party touring/all-season** often last longer (65000–80000 mi).
- High-performance tires may wear faster.

**4) Handling / Grip**
- OEM balanced; competent in most conditions.
- **Third-party sport/all-weather** can give significantly better grip in rain/snow.
- Dedicated summer tires outperform OEM in dry grip.

**5) Noise & Comfort**
- OEM engineered for low NVH (noise/vibration).
- Some third-party options match or exceed comfort; others are louder.

**6) Warranty & Claims**
- OEM tire worn under normal conditions = **not covered** by Tesla (standard).
- Third-party tires come with **their own mileage warranty** (treadwear promise).
- Some brands offer **free replacement prorated**.

**7) Cost**
- **OEM = typically premium price.**
- **Third-party = wider price range**: budget to premium (Michelin/Continental/Pirelli outside Tesla OEM).

**8) Alignment Sensitivity**
- EV’s higher torque and camber setups can wear edges.
- **Choose tires with strong shoulder blocks** for longevity.
