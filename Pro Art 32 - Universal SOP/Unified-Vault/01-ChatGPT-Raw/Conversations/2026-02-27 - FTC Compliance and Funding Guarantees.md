## Short Answer  
**No.** You cannot legally *guarantee* someone will obtain $100K+ in funding — especially under FTC standards.

---

## Why (FTC Reality)

Under the entity["organization","Federal Trade Commission","us consumer protection agency"] Act (15 U.S.C. §45):

- It is unlawful to make **deceptive or misleading claims**
- You must have **competent and reliable evidence** for any objective claim
- Earnings / funding claims require:
  - Substantiation
  - Clear disclosures
  - Typicality data (what most people achieve)

If you guarantee $100K+ funding:
- You are making a **specific, measurable financial outcome claim**
- You would need proof that **virtually all qualified participants achieve it**
- “Qualified” must be objectively defined and consistently applied
- Disclosures cannot contradict the headline claim

Guarantees tied to external underwriting decisions (banks, lenders) are legally risky because:
- You do not control approval
- Lenders control underwriting
- Variables include credit, income, debt ratios, industry risk, policy changes

---

## What *Is* Compliant

You can:

### 1. Guarantee the Process  
- “We guarantee delivery of the full DFY funding preparation system.”
- “We guarantee we will submit X applications following X criteria.”
- “We guarantee compliance optimization.”

### 2. Guarantee Effort  
- “If you complete all required steps and do not receive approvals, we will continue working with you.”

### 3. Offer Conditional Performance Models (Carefully)  
Only if:
- You have documented historical averages
- You disclose typical results
- You avoid implying certainty

Example structure:
> “Clients who meet our documented qualification criteria and complete the full process have historically secured between $X–$Y in funding. Results vary based on credit profile and lender underwriting.”

---

## What You Cannot Do

❌ “We guarantee you’ll get $100,000+.”  
❌ “Everyone who qualifies gets funded.”  
❌ “If you have 700+ credit you’re guaranteed approval.”  

Unless you literally reimburse them or provide capital yourself (and even then, wording matters).

---

- **Qualification screening precision**
- **Underwriting alignment**
- **Application sequencing**

- **Support until decision**

You sell certainty of *execution*, not certainty of lender behavior.

- Written disclosures reviewed by counsel
