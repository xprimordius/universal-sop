## 🤖 ChatGPT

If you withdraw money from a **401(k)** before the age of **59½**, the IRS typically applies:  

1. **20% Withholding Tax** – Your plan administrator must withhold **20%** for federal taxes when making a withdrawal. This is a prepayment toward your overall tax liability.
2. **10% Early Withdrawal Penalty** – If you withdraw early (before 59½), an **additional 10% penalty** applies unless an exception applies.
3. **Ordinary Income Tax** – The withdrawal amount is taxed as **regular income**, meaning your final tax rate could be higher or lower than the withheld 20%.

### **Strategies to Minimize or Avoid Taxes on 401(k) Withdrawals**
1. **401(k) Loan Instead of Withdrawal**  
   - You can borrow from your 401(k) (up to **$50,000** or **50% of your balance**, whichever is lower).  
   - No taxes or penalties if repaid within **5 years**.  
   - Interest is paid back into your 401(k) account.

2. **Rollover to a Roth IRA & Use Contributions**  
   - Convert your 401(k) into a **Roth IRA** and wait **5 years** before withdrawing (to avoid penalties).  
   - Roth IRA **contributions** (but not earnings) can be withdrawn **tax-free** anytime.

3. **Substantially Equal Periodic Payments (SEPP) / Rule 72(t)**  
   - Set up IRS-approved fixed withdrawals based on life expectancy.  
   - Avoids **10% penalty**, but you must continue for **at least 5 years** or until age **59½**.

4. **Business Loss Strategy (Advanced & Risky)**  
   - If you own a business, you could offset **401(k) withdrawal income** with business expenses/losses.  
   - The IRS is strict on abusive loss deductions, so the losses must be **legitimate**.  

5. **Medical & Hardship Exemptions**  
   - If you have **unreimbursed medical expenses** exceeding **7.5% of your AGI**, you can withdraw penalty-free.  
   - Some **hardship exemptions** allow penalty-free withdrawals but still require income tax.

6. **Retire at 55 (Separation of Service Rule)**  
   - If you **leave your job** in or after the year you turn **55**, you can withdraw penalty-free from that employer’s 401(k).  

### **Best Approach?**
- If you're looking to **access funds tax-efficiently**, a **401(k) loan** or **Roth IRA rollover** are safer than trying to offset with business losses, which invites IRS scrutiny.  
- If using business losses, consult a **tax professional** to ensure compliance.
