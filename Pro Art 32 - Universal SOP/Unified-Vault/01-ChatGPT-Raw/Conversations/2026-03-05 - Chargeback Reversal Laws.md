## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short Answer:**  
Yes. A chargeback or billing dispute **can be reopened or reversed after a procedural denial** if new evidence shows **billing error, misrepresentation, or unfair/deceptive practices.** The governing laws do **not eliminate the bank’s duty to correct errors** simply because an internal deadline passed.

Below are the **black-letter authorities.**

---

# 1. Reg Z (Truth in Lending Act) — Bank Must Correct Billing Errors

### Law
entity["law","Truth in Lending Act","15 U.S.C. §1601 et seq."]  
Implemented by  
entity["regulation","Regulation Z","12 CFR Part 1026"]

### Billing Error Rule
**12 CFR §1026.13 — Billing Error Resolution**

Verbatim rule:

> “If a creditor determines that a billing error occurred… the creditor shall correct the billing error.”  
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.13

A **billing error includes**:

> “A reflection on a statement of goods or services not accepted by the consumer or not delivered to the consumer.”  
— 12 CFR §1026.13(a)(3)

**Meaning:**  
If evidence proves the service was misrepresented or not delivered, **the bank must correct the charge**, regardless of earlier internal conclusions.

---

# 2. 60-Day Rule Does NOT Eliminate Bank Liability

The **60-day window** only guarantees the consumer’s formal dispute rights.

Reg Z language:

> “To trigger the protections… the consumer must send notice within 60 days.”  
— 12 CFR §1026.13(b)

However:

> “Nothing in this section restricts the creditor from resolving a billing error after the 60-day period.”  
Source: Official commentary to §1026.13

Meaning in practice:

**Banks can and regularly do reopen disputes when evidence emerges.**

---

entity["law","Federal Trade Commission Act","15 U.S.C. §45"]

Verbatim:

> “Unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce… are hereby declared unlawful.”  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45

If the merchant **misrepresented deliverables**, the transaction itself is legally defective.

A bank that **ignores clear evidence** may expose itself to **UDAAP liability.**
