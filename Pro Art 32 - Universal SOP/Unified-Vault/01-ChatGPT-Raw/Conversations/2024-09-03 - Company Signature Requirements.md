# Company Signature Requirements

---

## 👤 You

is a company legally required like chase to provide me signatures and original documents showing true accuracy.

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Under U.S. law, a company like Chase is not necessarily legally required to provide **signatures** and **original documents** when you dispute an account or debt. However, the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** does require that they verify the accuracy of any information they report to the credit bureaus. This verification process typically involves ensuring that the data is accurate and up-to-date, but it does not explicitly require the provision of signed documents or original agreements.

You can, however, request validation of debt under the **Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)** if you believe there is an issue with a collection account. In that case, the debt collector must provide proof of the debt, such as an itemized account or evidence of the original creditor, but not necessarily the original signed document.

If you're disputing an item with a bank like Chase directly, their obligation is to verify the debt, but again, they may not be legally required to provide the original signed documents unless the dispute goes to court, at which point more formal evidence might be required.
