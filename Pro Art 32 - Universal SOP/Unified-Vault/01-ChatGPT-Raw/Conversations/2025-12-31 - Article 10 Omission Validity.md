## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer: yes — it’s OK.**  
Nothing is “invalid” or non-compliant just because **Article 10 is skipped**.

### Why this is not a problem
- **Article numbering is not legally required to be sequential.**  
  Colorado law does **not** require Articles of Incorporation to include every numbered article.
- **Articles are identified by content, not numbers.**  
  What matters is that all *required provisions* are present — not that Article 10 exists.
- **This is common and acceptable drafting practice.**  
  Articles are often removed during restatements, and numbers are left unused to avoid renumbering conflicts.

### What your document *does* correctly include
Your Restated Articles contain all core statutory elements:
- Entity name
- Principal office
- Registered agent
- Authorized shares
- Duration
- Lawful purpose
- Director(s)
- Officers
- Amendment authority  

All of that is clearly present in Articles 1–9 and 11–13 fileciteturn0file0

### When a missing article *would* matter
Only if:
- A **specific provision was intended** to be in Article 10 and accidentally omitted (e.g., indemnification, liability limitation), **and**
- You actually need that protection now (investors, lawsuits, board expansion, etc.)

Otherwise, there is **zero operational or legal issue**.

### Bottom line
- ✔ Your filings are valid  
- ✔ Continued use is fine  
- ✔ No retroactive problem created  
- ❌ No requirement to “fix” unless you *want* to add content later
