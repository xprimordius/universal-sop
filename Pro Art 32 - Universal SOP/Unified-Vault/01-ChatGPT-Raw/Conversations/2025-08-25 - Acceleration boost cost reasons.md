Why does an acceleration boost cost $2000 instead of being included

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Tesla charges **$2,000 for the Acceleration Boost** instead of including it because it’s structured as an **aftermarket software upgrade**, not a hardware limitation. Key reasons:  

### 1. **Segmented Product Strategy**  
- Tesla creates tiers (Long Range vs Performance) to capture different budgets.  
- Including the boost for free would blur the line between trims, reducing upsell potential for the higher-priced Performance model.  

### 2. **Software-Unlock Revenue**  
- The hardware (motors, battery) is already capable of the extra speed.  
- Tesla monetizes this latent capability by gating it behind a paid unlock, much like in-app purchases.  

### 3. **Price Anchoring**  
- $2,000 feels like a deal compared to the $10K+ jump to the Performance model.  
- This boosts perceived value while still protecting Performance margins.  

### 4. **Customer Choice & Flexibility**  
- Not everyone wants faster 0–60 at the expense of range, tire wear, or insurance costs.  
- Tesla lets customers decide later, even after purchase.  

### 5. **High-Margin Upsell**  
- Since it’s pure software, Tesla has near-zero cost to deliver.  
- It’s one of their most profitable add-ons, directly feeding into revenue.  

👉 In short: it’s not included because it’s Tesla’s way of **segmenting products, protecting Performance sales, and creating a lucrative upsell funnel**.
