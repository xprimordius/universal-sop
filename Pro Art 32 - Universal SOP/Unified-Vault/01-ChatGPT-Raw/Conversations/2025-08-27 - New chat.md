## Admiralty Law vs. Maritime Law  
- **Admiralty Law** = Historically British, “courts of the admiralty.” U.S. federal courts inherited this jurisdiction.  
- **Maritime Law** = The modern term. Governs contracts, torts, injuries, commerce, and navigation **on navigable waters**.  
- In practice, they’re used interchangeably today. Admiralty = old name, Maritime = modern.  

---

## Relation to Credit & Finance  
- Maritime law governs **commercial contracts and negotiable instruments** tied to shipping/trade.  
- Maritime liens (credit extended to vessels, crews, supplies) are enforceable without normal state contract law—federal admiralty has priority.  
- Key principle: **credit in commerce = maritime jurisdiction if tied to navigation/trade.**  
- This seeded doctrines like “bills of exchange” and “promissory notes” that underpin modern banking/credit systems.  

---

## Stated Income & Credit Connection  
- Stated income (declaring income without documentation, common in no-doc loans) connects conceptually because:  
  - Maritime law historically relied on **good faith declarations of value/cargo** without full inspection.  
  - Credit was extended on trust + affidavit → modern echoes in stated-income lending.  
- Both rely on **affidavit-style self-certification** instead of external verification.  
- In finance, stated income loans collapsed in 2008 precisely because they leaned too far on this maritime-style trust principle without checks.  

---

## TLDR  
- Admiralty = old court name; Maritime = modern.  
- Governs trade/credit over navigable waters → evolved into core principles of negotiable credit (notes, bills, etc.).  
- Stated income = modern echo of maritime “declaration trust” → credit extended on sworn statement.
