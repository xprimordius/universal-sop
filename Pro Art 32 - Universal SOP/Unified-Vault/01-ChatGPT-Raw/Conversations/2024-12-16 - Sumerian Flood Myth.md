## 🤖 ChatGPT

Sumerian cuneiform tablets—especially texts like the “Eridu Genesis” and references within the “Sumerian King List”—contain one of the earliest recorded flood myths. According to these tablets, the gods decided to send a great flood to wipe out humanity. In these accounts, a pious and wise individual, often identified as Ziusudra (or Ziusura), is warned by a deity and instructed to build a large boat to preserve himself, his family, and animals. After surviving the catastrophic deluge, Ziusudra offers sacrifices and is granted eternal life by the gods. This narrative predates, yet closely parallels, later Mesopotamian flood stories (like those in the Akkadian Atrahasis Epic and the Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic) and provides a key source for understanding the Sumerian view of divine judgment, mercy, and the continuation of civilization following a world-altering flood.
