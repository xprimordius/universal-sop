## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Headline: It depends on *which law applies* to the call — location + governing law matter.**

### The black-letter rule
- **Minnesota** = **one-party consent** (Minn. Stat. § 626A.02).
- **Two-party (all-party) states** require **everyone’s consent**.

### Cross-state calls (MN ↔ two-party state)
There is **no single universal rule**. Courts look at:
1. **Where the recording party is located**
2. **Where the recorded party is located**
3. **Which state’s law a court decides governs**
4. **Expectation of privacy**
5. **Choice-of-law analysis** (often unpredictable)

### Practical legal outcomes
- **Recording from MN without consent** *can* be legal **under MN law**.
- **But** you may still face **civil liability** (or worse) **under the two-party state’s law** if:
  - The other party is in a two-party state, **and**
  - That state’s law is applied by the court.

Several two-party states (e.g., **California, Pennsylvania**) have allowed claims **against out-of-state recorders** when their resident was recorded without consent.

### Federal law (not a shield here)
- Federal wiretap law (18 U.S.C. § 2511) is **one-party consent**, **but**
- States may impose **stricter rules**, and courts often allow them.

### The safest, litigation-proof rule
> **If either party is in a two-party consent state → get consent.**

### Bottom line
- **Technically legal under MN law?** Often yes.  
- **Risk-free?** No.  
- **Civil exposure?** Yes, potentially significant.
