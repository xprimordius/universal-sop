---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Yes, an online notary can be used and considered serious for an FTC affidavit. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) accepts notarized affidavits for identity theft reports, and many states in the U.S. have laws that allow for online notarization, also known as remote or electronic notarization. This process involves using video conferencing technology to complete the notarization, ensuring the same level of verification and security as in-person notarization.

When using an online notary, ensure the following:

1. **Legality**: Verify that online notarization is legally recognized in your state or the state where the notary is commissioned.
2. **Platform**: Use a reputable online notarization platform that complies with state regulations.
3. **Verification**: The notary should properly verify your identity using secure methods, typically involving multiple forms of identification and video recording of the session.
4. **Documentation**: The notarized affidavit should include all necessary elements such as the notary's signature, seal, and the date of notarization.

By following these steps, an online notarized FTC affidavit can be as valid and effective as one notarized in person.
