The Bible was originally written in three primary languages, reflecting the cultural and historical contexts of its composition:

---

### **1. Hebrew**
   - **Used in:** Most of the Old Testament
   - **Examples:**
     - The **Torah (Pentateuch)**: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy
     - Historical books: Joshua, Judges, 1 & 2 Samuel, 1 & 2 Kings
     - Prophetic books: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, etc.
     - Wisdom literature: Psalms, Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes
   - **Characteristics:**
     - Ancient Hebrew is a Semitic language written from right to left.
     - It uses an alphabet of 22 consonants (vowels were added later as diacritical marks).

---

### **2. Aramaic**
   - **Used in:** Parts of the Old Testament
   - **Examples:**
     - Sections of **Daniel** (Daniel 2:4–7:28)
     - Portions of **Ezra** (Ezra 4:8–6:18; 7:12–26)
     - A single verse in **Jeremiah** (Jeremiah 10:11)
   - **Context:**
     - Aramaic became the common language of the Jewish people during the Babylonian exile (6th century BCE) and the Persian period.
     - Jesus and His disciples likely spoke a dialect of Aramaic.

---

### **3. Greek**
   - **Used in:** The New Testament
   - **Examples:**
     - All 27 books of the New Testament: Gospels, Acts, Pauline Epistles, General Epistles, and Revelation.
   - **Characteristics:**
     - The New Testament was written in **Koine Greek**, the common language of the Eastern Mediterranean during the Hellenistic and Roman periods.
     - It was chosen for its widespread use, making the Christian message accessible to a broad audience.

---

### **Why Multiple Languages?**
1. **Hebrew:** The original language of the Jewish people and their scriptures.
2. **Aramaic:** A lingua franca during the Babylonian and Persian periods, influencing later Jewish writings.
3. **Greek:** The dominant language of the Roman Empire’s eastern regions, enabling the spread of Christianity.

---

### **Translation History:**
1. **Septuagint (3rd–1st century BCE):**
   - The Hebrew Bible was translated into Greek for the Jewish diaspora in Alexandria, Egypt.
2. **Vulgate (4th century CE):**
   - St. Jerome translated the Bible into Latin, the dominant language of the Western Church.
3. **Modern Translations:**
   - Translations now exist in thousands of languages, derived from ancient manuscripts in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek.

These languages reflect the Bible's diverse historical and cultural roots, making it one of the most significant texts in world history.
