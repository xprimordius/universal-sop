**15 U.S. Code § 1681b - Permissible purposes of consumer reports** under the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** defines **when and to whom** a consumer reporting agency (CRA) may legally furnish a consumer report. Here’s a **precise breakdown**:

---

### ✅ **Permissible Purposes (Summary)**

A CRA **may only** furnish a consumer report under **specific conditions**, including but not limited to:

1. **With written consent** of the consumer.  
   - e.g., applying for a loan, rental, or employment.

2. **For credit transactions**:
   - To a person intending to use the information in connection with a **credit transaction involving the consumer** (including review or collection of the account).

3. **For employment purposes**:
   - If the consumer provides **written authorization** and the report is for **employment decisions** (e.g., hiring, promotion, retention).

4. **For insurance underwriting**:
   - If related to the underwriting of insurance involving the consumer.

5. **For license or benefit eligibility**:
   - Determining eligibility for a **government license or benefit** when a statute requires consideration of financial status.

6. **As a legitimate business need**:
   - In connection with a **business transaction** initiated by the consumer (e.g., renting an apartment, cell phone contract).

7. **Court order or subpoena**:
   - Pursuant to a **court order or federal grand jury subpoena**.

8. **For account review**:
   - Existing creditors may review an account (e.g., for credit limit increases).

---

### ❌ **Unlawful Use Examples**
- Pulling credit without a consumer's knowledge or valid reason.
- Using credit reports for marketing **without consumer initiation**.
- Accessing reports out of curiosity or revenge.

---

### 📜 **Legal Reference: 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(a)**  
> A consumer reporting agency may furnish a consumer report under the following circumstances and **no other**.

Link to official law:  
[15 U.S. Code § 1681b (Cornell Law)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681b)

**Yes, 15 U.S. Code § 1681b** can be used **indirectly** for **late payment removals**—**only if** the late payment was reported or accessed **without a permissible purpose**.

---

### ✅ **When 1681b *Can* Support Removal of Late Payments:**

Use it if:

1. **No Permissible Purpose Exists:**
   - The furnisher (creditor) **no longer has a valid account** with you and continues to pull/report without legal basis.
   - The credit bureau or furnisher **shared your report** without your permission and **not for a listed permissible reason**.

2. **You never authorized** the original creditor, or the debt is from identity theft or fraud.

3. **You revoked authorization** and there's no ongoing relationship, yet they’re still accessing or furnishing data.

4. You requested the **"method of verification"** under 15 USC § 1681i and they **failed to prove** the late payment was accurate **and accessed under a permissible purpose**.

---

### ❌ **When 1681b Alone Cannot Be Used:**

- If the **late payment is accurate**, and the creditor had valid authority to report it (active account, contractual relationship).
- If you **authorized** the transaction or account and they’re simply reporting negative info truthfully.
- If you’re trying to dispute without a legal or factual basis.

---

### 🔧 **What Laws *Can* Be Used for Late Payment Removals:**

1. **15 USC § 1681i (Reinvestigation of Disputes):**
   - If you dispute the late payment, the CRA must verify it within 30 days or delete it.

2. **15 USC § 1681s-2 (Duties of Furnishers):**
   - If the furnisher is reporting inaccurate or incomplete information, they must correct or delete it.

3. **15 USC § 1681e(b):**
   - If the information reported caused harm and was not maintained with maximum accuracy.

---

### 📌 Strategic Tip:

For **removal of late payments**, combine the following:

- **Challenge Permissible Purpose (1681b)** if there's a reason to believe no valid authorization exists.
- **Dispute Inaccuracy (1681i)** and force them to validate.
- **Demand Method of Verification** and original documentation (not e-OSCAR responses).
