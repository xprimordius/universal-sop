Hyperdeflation vs hyperinflation

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Got it — here’s the clean, side-by-side breakdown:  

---

## **Hyperinflation**
- **Definition:** Prices skyrocket uncontrollably because money supply expands far faster than real output.  
- **Cause:** Excessive money printing, collapse of trust in currency, debt monetization.  
- **Effect:**  
  - Currency becomes worthless.  
  - People rush to spend immediately.  
  - Barter, foreign currency, or hard assets (gold, crypto) replace local money.  
- **Examples:** Weimar Germany (1920s), Zimbabwe (2000s), Venezuela (2010s).  

---

## **Hyperdeflation**
- **Definition:** Prices collapse rapidly as money becomes *too scarce* relative to goods and services.  
- **Cause:** Credit contraction, debt defaults, mass saving/hoarding, collapse of liquidity.  
- **Effect:**  
  - Money *gains* purchasing power too fast.  
  - Spending freezes, demand collapses.  
  - Businesses fail, unemployment spikes.  
- **Examples:** Rare historically. The Great Depression (1930s USA) had severe deflationary traits but not “hyper.” Japan’s 1990s “Lost Decade” is a milder case.  

---

## **Key Difference**
- **Hyperinflation:** *Too much money chasing too few goods* → prices explode upward.  
- **Hyperdeflation:** *Too little money chasing abundant goods* → prices collapse downward.  

---

## **Big Picture**
Both extremes = system failure.  
- Hyperinflation destroys trust in money.  
- Hyperdeflation destroys economic activity itself.
