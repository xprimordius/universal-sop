## 👤 You

What is it called when someone puts the sword on the knights shoulders

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Dubbing** or **knighting ceremony**.

The act of touching a sword to a knight’s shoulders is specifically called **"the accolade"** during the **knighting** or **dubbing** ceremony.

**Key Term:**  
- **Accolade** — the ceremonial act of conferring knighthood, traditionally involving the sword on the shoulders.

**Process:**  
1. Sword taps on one or both shoulders.
2. Words of investiture are spoken (e.g., "Arise, Sir...").
3. Knight rises, officially honored.  

**TLDR:** It's called the **accolade** during the **knighting ceremony**.

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**"Accolade"** itself originally meant **the highest honor** or **award of distinction**—especially in the context of knighthood.

However, if you mean *the highest possible honor* more broadly, terms include:
- **"Highest accolade"** (common phrase)
- **"Supreme honor"**
- **"Distinction"**
- **"Decoration"** (in military orders)
- **"Order of Merit"** (in some nations)

If you're thinking of **chivalric ranks**:
- **Knight Grand Cross** (often the highest rank in orders of knighthood)
- **Knight of the Garter** (UK’s highest chivalric order)

**TLDR:** The word is **accolade** for the ceremonial honor. For *highest honor*, it depends on context: **"highest accolade," "supreme honor,"** or specific titles like **"Knight Grand Cross."**

## 👤 You

It’s not accolade it’s kind of like bestow

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The word you’re likely thinking of is **"confer"** or **"bestow."**

In ceremonial language:
- **Bestow** = to formally give (an honor, right, or gift).
- **Confer** = to officially grant (an honor or title).

During knighting, they **bestow** or **confer** knighthood.

Other related words:
- **Invest** (formal granting of rank/office).
- **Grant** (to formally give).
- **Ordain** (mostly religious, but sometimes ceremonial).

**TLDR:**  
Likely words: **bestow** or **confer.**  
Examples:  
- "He was **bestowed** with knighthood."  
- "They **conferred** the title upon him."
