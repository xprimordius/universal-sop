Can you own land and live in a house in a national forest?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**TL;DR:**  
No — you generally **cannot own private land** *inside* a U.S. national forest. However, there are exceptions and legal ways to **live within or near** one.

---

### 🔹 1. Land Ownership in National Forests
- **National forests = federal public land**, managed by the **U.S. Forest Service (USFS)** under the Department of Agriculture.  
- The land is **not for private ownership or sale**; it’s held in trust for the public.  
- You **cannot buy or own** land that’s federally designated as national forest.

---

### 🔹 2. Exceptions (Private “Inholdings”)
- Some **private parcels (“inholdings”)** exist *inside* national forest boundaries.  
  - These were **privately owned before** the forest was established or sold by the government long ago.  
  - You **can legally buy and own** one of these parcels if it’s privately held.  
  - You must **follow local, state, and federal rules** on building, septic, access roads, etc.  

---

### 🔹 3. Living There Without Ownership
- **Long-term residence on public land is illegal** unless:
  - You have a **special use permit or lease** (e.g., for a cabin, resort, ranger housing).  
  - You’re **camping temporarily** (usually 14 days max per location per 30-day period).  
- Permanent or semi-permanent residence without authorization = **trespassing** and can lead to eviction and fines.

---

### 🔹 4. Legal Alternatives
- **Buy private land adjacent to a national forest** → You’ll get forest access and scenery without restrictions.  
- **Lease or permit land use** through the Forest Service (rare and regulated).  
- **Homesteading laws** no longer apply; they ended in the U.S. in 1976 (Alaska extended to 1986).
