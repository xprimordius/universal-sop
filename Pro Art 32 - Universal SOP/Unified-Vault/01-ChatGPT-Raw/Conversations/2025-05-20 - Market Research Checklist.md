Market Research Checklist
1. Land Market Overview
● How many lots sold?
○ Track the total number of lots sold in the area over a specific period (e.g.,
monthly, quarterly). The last 90 days is best.
○ Measure trends (increasing, decreasing, or stable sales volume).
● How many lots for sale?
○ Count the total number of available lots in the market.
○ Compare this to historical data to understand supply levels.
● Average sales time for land:
○ Record how long it takes for lots to sell on average.
○ Analyze if sales time is increasing or decreasing and why.
2. New Construction Overview (For Markets with Both Land and New Builds)
● How many different builders are there?
○ Identify how many active builders are operating in the market.
○ Note any trends in the number of new builders entering or exiting the market.
● How many new construction listings?
○ Track the number of new construction homes listed in the market.
○ Understand the volume and availability of new homes for sale.
● How many new construction houses are sitting compared to selling?
○ Track how many newly built homes are currently sitting on the market without
selling.
○ Compare with the number of homes that are selling to assess the demand and
buyer interest.
● Average sales time for new construction:
○ Measure the average time it takes for new construction homes to sell.
○ Track whether the market is speeding up or slowing down based on sales time.
3. Finding Good Wholesale Markets for Vacant Land
● Market Trends:
○ Population Growth: Look for areas with growing populations, which often
correlate with increased demand for both land and homes.
○ Economic Growth: Investigate areas with expanding job markets, which often
lead to more people needing housing and land.
○ Migration Patterns: Research areas where people are moving to. In some
cases, land in rural or suburban areas near growing cities might become
attractive.
○ Local Infrastructure Developments: Track new infrastructure projects, such as
highways, schools, or hospitals, that could increase land demand.
● Land Demand Indicators:
○ Vacant Land Absorption Rate: Measure how quickly vacant land is being sold
or developed in a specific area. A high absorption rate may signal strong
demand.
○ Zoning Changes/Development Opportunities: Look for areas where land is
being rezoned for new developments (residential, commercial, industrial). These
areas can offer profitable opportunities for wholesale land deals.
○ Land Use Trends: Pay attention to how land is being used and developed in the
area. Agricultural land may be in demand for residential projects, and industrial
land may be growing in suburban locations.
● Local Market Competitiveness:
○ Number of Active Investors: Investigate how many other investors are
operating in the area. Highly competitive markets may require more effort to
secure good deals, but they also indicate high demand.
○ Days on Market for Vacant Land: Track how long vacant land is sitting on the
market. If properties are moving quickly, it could indicate a strong market for land.
○ Price Appreciation: Research the historical land price trends in the area to
understand whether land values have been increasing, decreasing, or remaining
stable.
● Access to Key Amenities and Transportation:
○ Proximity to Major Cities: Vacant land near growing urban centers or with easy
access to transportation routes often appreciates faster.
○ Utilities and Amenities: Land with access to utilities (water, electricity, sewer)
and amenities (schools, shops) tends to be in higher demand and thus, more
profitable for wholesale investments.
● Land Inventory Health:
○ High Inventory vs. Low Inventory: Markets with low land inventory are more
attractive, as they indicate higher demand and potential for profit.
○ Owner Motivation: Identify landowners who may be motivated to sell quickly
(e.g., distressed owners, tax delinquent properties, heirs).
● Legal and Environmental Factors:
○ Title and Ownership Issues: Ensure the land has clear titles. Areas with a
higher number of land with title issues can be risky.
○ Environmental Concerns: Check for environmental restrictions or issues like
flood zones, wetlands, or conservation areas that may reduce a property’s appeal
or development potential.
4. Assessing Competition and Profitability
● Local Real Estate Activity:
○ Real Estate Listings: Track the frequency of land listings in a particular market.
Too many listings can mean oversupply, while few listings can indicate a
high-demand area.
○ Auction Trends: Watch for trends in local land auctions, especially if you’re
considering buying land at auction. Some areas may offer a higher concentration
of deals.
● Financial Trends and Profit Margins:
○ Median Sale Price of Land: Know the average and median sale prices for
vacant land in the area. This will help you identify areas where margins are high.
○ Transaction Volume vs. Price Per Acre: Analyze how much land is being sold
at what price per acre. A higher transaction volume combined with lower per-acre
prices might indicate a good market for wholesale investments.
● Local Government and Market Regulations:
○ Local Zoning and Land Use: Understand the zoning laws in different areas to
identify land that has the potential for development or can be rezoned for
higher-value use.
○ Land Tax Rates and Incentives: Some areas offer property tax incentives,
exemptions, or reductions that make land more attractive for investment.
