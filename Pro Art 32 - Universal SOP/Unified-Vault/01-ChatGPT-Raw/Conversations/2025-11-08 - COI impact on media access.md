If you show up to a venue with an artist to shoot media but don’t have a **Certificate of Insurance (COI)**, a few things can happen depending on the venue’s policies and how strict they are:  

### 1. **You May Be Denied Entry or Shooting Access**
Most venues (especially those run by major promoters like Insomniac, Live Nation, etc.) **require a COI before allowing anyone to shoot**. Without it, security or production management can legally refuse your access to the pit, backstage, or even the general floor if you’re “working.”

### 2. **The Artist’s COI Might Cover You (If Listed)**
If the artist’s insurance explicitly **lists you as an additional insured or crew member**, you may be allowed in under their coverage — but only if their documentation is pre-approved and shared with the venue before the event. Showing up without your name on it will likely not fly.

### 3. **Liability and Risk**
If you shoot without a COI and something happens — e.g., you damage gear, trip a cable, or injure someone — **you (and potentially the artist)** can be held personally liable. Venues use COIs to transfer this liability to an insurer.

### 4. **Workarounds (If You’re Already There)**
If you’re on-site without a COI:
- Sometimes the **artist’s manager or tour rep** can email the venue immediately adding you to their COI.
- If that’s not possible, you can **buy instant event coverage** through Full Frame Insurance, Thimble, or GatherGuard and send proof within minutes.

### 5. **Reputation Impact**
Even if they let you in “this time,” it can affect your professional reputation with that venue or promoter — they’ll expect you to have insurance next time or may blacklist you for not following protocol.
