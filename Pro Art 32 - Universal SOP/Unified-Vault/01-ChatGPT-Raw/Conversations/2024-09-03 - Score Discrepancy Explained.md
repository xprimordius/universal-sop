---

The discrepancy between your VantageScore 3.0 of 804 and your FICO 8 score of 743 can be attributed to several key differences in how these scoring models calculate your credit score:

1. **Different Scoring Models**: VantageScore and FICO use different algorithms and criteria to evaluate your credit data. They weigh factors like payment history, credit utilization, length of credit history, and types of credit differently.

2. **Data Usage**: VantageScore 3.0 tends to be more lenient on certain aspects like recent credit inquiries and short-term credit usage, which might result in a higher score. On the other hand, FICO 8 is more sensitive to high credit card balances and recent credit activity, potentially lowering your score.

3. **Timeframe Considerations**: VantageScore might be quicker to reflect improvements in your credit behavior, while FICO might be slower to adjust, especially in terms of payment history or significant changes in credit utilization.

4. **Credit Report Variations**: The credit report data pulled for each scoring model might slightly differ. Even small discrepancies in the timing of data updates can result in variations between your scores.

5. **Weighting of Factors**: Each model places different emphasis on factors like recent inquiries, the age of accounts, and types of credit. FICO 8, for example, puts more weight on credit card usage, which can significantly impact your score if you have a high balance.

These differences are common and typically explain why your VantageScore might be higher than your FICO score. It's important to monitor both scores, as lenders may use either one depending on the situation.
