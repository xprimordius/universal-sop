### **Who’s at Fault?**
- **Discover** is at fault if they deposited loan proceeds into an account that **does not belong to your aunt**.  
- Your aunt is only liable if the money was deposited into **her account** or if she directly received the funds.  
- If Discover deposited the loan into someone else’s account, then **Discover disbursed incorrectly** and cannot legally enforce repayment against your aunt.  

---

### **Key Laws & Regulations Protecting Your Aunt**
1. **Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1601 et seq.**
   - Requires lenders to make loans only to the actual borrower and properly disburse funds.
   - Misapplied or misdirected funds are the lender’s error, not the consumer’s obligation.  

2. **Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA), 15 U.S.C. § 1666**
   - Protects consumers from being held liable for billing errors, including misapplied payments or funds deposited incorrectly.

3. **Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA), 15 U.S.C. § 1693**
   - Covers electronic transfers into wrong accounts. If funds were deposited into the wrong account electronically, **the financial institution and lender must correct the error**.

4. **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 U.S.C. § 1681**
   - If Discover reports your aunt as delinquent on a loan she never received, that’s inaccurate reporting. She has the right to dispute and have it removed.

5. **Federal Trade Commission Act (FTC Act), 15 U.S.C. § 45**
   - Prohibits unfair or deceptive acts. Trying to force your aunt to repay money she never received could qualify.

---

### **What to Do**
1. **Demand Proof of Disbursement**  
   - Send Discover a written request for the **account number and name** where the $3K was deposited.  
   - If it’s not your aunt’s account, she is not liable.  

2. **File a Written Dispute (FCRA + FCBA)**  
   - Send a dispute letter to Discover stating the loan funds were never received by your aunt.  
   - Keep copies of all correspondence.  

3. **Bank Records**  
   - If they claim it went to your mom’s account (now closed), request official bank records showing no deposit was received.  

4. **Regulatory Complaints**  
   - **CFPB complaint** (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).  
   - **State Attorney General complaint** (consumer protection division).  

5. **Credit Reporting**  
   - If they’ve reported it on your aunt’s credit file, dispute it immediately with all three bureaus under FCRA § 611.
