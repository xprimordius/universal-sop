5 months of iud and sponoloctine and still breaking out a lot of back especially jawline area small white  zits. Why and do I need other medicine?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**TL;DR clarity:**  
Jawline + back acne **5 months into an IUD + spironolactone** usually means your hormones are *still unbalanced* OR the meds are **not addressing the root cause** for *your* body.

Here’s the real breakdown—simple, accurate, no fluff:

---

## **Why it's still happening**
### **1. IUD hormones can *worsen* acne for months**
Especially **Mirena, Kyleena, Skyla** (progestin-based).  
Progestin → increases **androgen activity** → increases **oil production** → more **jawline/back acne**.

For many people, IUD acne **peaks 3–6 months** and can last **9–12 months**.

So: your IUD may still be contributing.

---

### **2. Spironolactone dose may be too low**
Most people need:
- **100 mg/day** to see real acne suppression  
- Some need **150–200 mg/day**

If you're on **25–50 mg**, it’s often not enough for jawline/hormonal acne.

---

### **3. Spironolactone takes 3–6 months—sometimes 9**
You're right in the window where:
- It *should be improving*  
- But if you’re still getting **new whiteheads**, it suggests **underdosing or the IUD overpowering it**

---

### **4. You may need a second medication for the *surface layer***
Spironolactone controls hormones, but **does not exfoliate skin or prevent clogged pores**.

You'll often still break out without:
- **Tretinoin** (retinoid)  
- or **benzoyl peroxide wash**  
- or **azelaic acid**

It's like fixing the plumbing but not clearing the drain.

---

## **What most dermatologists do next**
You can confirm this with your doctor, but **standard next steps** are:

### **1. Increase spironolactone dosage**
Often to **100 mg** (or 150 mg if tolerated).  
This alone fixes 70–80% of hormonal acne.

---

### **2. Add a topical retinoid**
This is almost always recommended:
- **Tretinoin 0.025% or 0.05%**
It stops whiteheads, clogged pores, and texture bumps.

---

### **3. Add benzoyl peroxide 2.5% wash**  
Especially for **back acne**.

This prevents bacteria + inflammation.

---

### **4. If the IUD is the root cause → consider switching**
If acne only started after the IUD:
- Copper IUD = no hormones → acne often improves  
- Birth control pills with **drospirenone** (Yaz, Yasmin) → best for acne  
- Or keep IUD + up spironolactone + tretinoin

---

## **When to definitely change treatment**
If after **6 months** on spironolactone you still have:
- new whiteheads  
- jawline flare-ups  
- back acne  
- painful bumps  

…dermatology guidelines say you likely need:
- **higher dose spironolactone**, AND/OR  
- **a retinoid**, AND/OR  
- **re-evaluation of the IUD**
