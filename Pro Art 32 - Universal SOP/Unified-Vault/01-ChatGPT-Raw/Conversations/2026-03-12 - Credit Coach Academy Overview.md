# 1. Contract Law Reality
Even if a contract says *“no refunds”*, courts and card networks still recognize:

**Failure to deliver contracted services = breach of contract.**

The agreement itself states services must be provided. fileciteturn0file0  

Examples listed in the contract include:

- DFY **Business-in-a-Box systems installed within 7 days**  
- Mentorship calls and support  
- Community access and resources fileciteturn0file0  

If these were **not actually delivered**, that becomes the legal issue.

---

# 2. Chargeback Rules (Visa / Mastercard)
Card networks allow disputes for:

### **Service Not Rendered / Not Received**

Typical code examples:

| Network | Reason Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Visa | 13.3 | Services not received |
| Mastercard | 4855 | Services not rendered |

Source:  
Visa Core Rules / Mastercard Chargeback Guide  
https://usa.visa.com/support/consumer/visa-rules.html

These disputes apply when:

- promised services **never provided**
- promised deliverables **never installed**
- access or fulfillment **not delivered**

Refund clauses **do not override card network rules**.

---

# 3. What Actually Determines Success
Chargebacks depend on **evidence**, not the contract language.

Banks look for:

**1. What was promised**
- sales call recordings
- written marketing claims
- emails / Slack messages

**2. What was delivered**
- proof systems were never installed
- missed calls or no mentorship
- no DFY setup

**3. Timeline**
- date purchased
- date services supposed to start
- lack of fulfillment

---

# 4. Critical Distinction
Banks separate two things:

| Situation | Chargeback Outcome |
|---|---|
| You didn’t like the program | usually denied |
| Service was **never delivered** | often approved |

Example:

- If they gave course access → harder case  
- If DFY system **never installed** → strong case

---

# 5. Important Legal Principle
Even strong liability waivers **cannot protect against:**

- fraud
- deceptive sales
- material non-delivery

Courts consistently rule that **contracts cannot waive fraud or deceptive practices**.

Reference:  
Restatement (Second) of Contracts §195

https://www.law.cornell.edu/restatement/contracts

---

# 6. Evidence That Wins These Disputes
Strong evidence includes:

- **sales call recording**
- Slack messages showing delays
- lack of onboarding
- missed timelines (ex: “7-day install” not done)
- lack of deliverables promised

If **deliverables were not fulfilled**, it does **not block a dispute.**
