## 🤖 ChatGPT

Here’s how this usually plays out:

### 1. **Effective Date Rules**
- Insurance coverage applies **only from the effective date forward**.  
- If your upgraded glass coverage became effective **Saturday the 5th**, anything that happened before that isn’t covered.  

### 2. **Claim Timing & Perception**
- If you noticed the crack **Sunday** (after the effective date), you could argue it occurred during the covered period.  
- But insurers often check whether damage looked “pre-existing.” If the crack was spreading and seems older, they may question whether it started before coverage.  

### 3. **Approval Chances**
- **If you can reasonably show the crack began or was first noticed after coverage started** (Sunday → today), the claim has a fair chance of approval.  
- **If they believe it existed before the upgrade**, they may deny it as pre-existing damage.  

### 4. **What They’ll Look At**
- **Date you reported the damage.**  
- **Photos, inspection notes, or adjuster review** (sometimes they can tell how long a crack has been spreading).  
- **Policy language on pre-existing conditions** (glass claims often have this exclusion).  

---

✅ **Best Practice:**  
When filing, state that the damage was first noticed **after** your new coverage took effect (accurately). If they ask when it happened, emphasize the date you first saw it.  

⚠️ **Risk:**  
If they suspect you upgraded coverage *because* of pre-existing damage, they may deny it.
