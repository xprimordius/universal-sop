Two different laws apply depending on **who is contacting you**.

| Situation | Governing Law |
|---|---|
| **Debt collector (3rd-party collection agency)** | entity["law","Fair Debt Collection Practices Act"] (FDCPA) |

Important corrections for accuracy:

1. **You can request validation of the debt.**  
   FDCPA §809(b) requires the collector to **cease collection until validation is provided.**  
   Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692g

2. **The law does NOT require a “wet ink signature.”**  
   Courts generally accept account records, statements, or electronic agreements.

   Under FDCPA §813, the **maximum statutory damages are $1,000 per lawsuit**, not per call or letter.  
   Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692k

# Professional Debt Validation / Method of Verification Letter

*(Plain text – copy/paste ready)*

Subject: Debt Dispute and Request for Validation

To Whom It May Concern,

This letter serves as formal notice that I dispute the alleged debt referenced in your communications.

Pursuant to the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §1692g(b), I request full validation of the alleged debt. Until such validation is provided, you must cease collection activity related to this account.

Please provide the following documentation so that I may evaluate the validity and accuracy of the alleged obligation:

1. The name and address of the original creditor.  
2. The complete account history, including the amount allegedly owed and how it was calculated.  
3. Documentation demonstrating that I am legally obligated for the debt, including the agreement creating the obligation or other competent evidence of the account.  
4. The date of default and the chain of assignment if the debt has been transferred or sold.  
5. The method of verification used to determine that the information being reported or collected is accurate.

Additionally, if you are reporting this account to any consumer reporting agency, please ensure that the account is reported as disputed as required under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

If you cannot provide sufficient documentation to substantiate the debt, you must cease collection efforts and correct or delete any related credit reporting.

All future communications regarding this matter must be in writing.

This letter is not a refusal to pay, but a request for verification and documentation of the alleged obligation.

Sincerely,  
[Name]  
[Address]

## What the Law Actually Requires

### 1. Debt Validation (Collectors)
Under the entity["law","Fair Debt Collection Practices Act"] (FDCPA):

**15 U.S.C. §1692g(b)** states:

> “If the consumer notifies the debt collector in writing… that the debt is disputed… the debt collector shall cease collection of the debt until the debt collector obtains verification of the debt…”

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692g

**Key point:**  
The statute requires **verification**, not the original signed contract.

Courts generally interpret “verification” as **confirmation the debt exists and the amount is correct**, often through account records.

---

### 2. Credit Reporting Disputes
Under the entity["law","Fair Credit Reporting Act"]:

**15 U.S.C. §1681i(a)(1)** requires credit bureaus to conduct a **reasonable reinvestigation** of disputed information.

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i

Again, the law requires **reasonable investigation**, not original paperwork.

---

## What Evidence Collectors Typically Use

Courts routinely accept:

• Account statements  
• Account history ledgers  
• Bills showing the balance  
• Records from the original creditor  
• Purchase / assignment records for the debt  
• Electronic agreements

Original signed contracts are **rarely required**, especially for credit cards.

---

## When Original Documentation DOES Matter

It becomes more important if the case goes to **court**.

Then the collector must prove:

1. The debt exists  
2. The amount is accurate  
3. You are the debtor  
4. They have the legal right to collect

This proof can include business records and assignment documentation — not necessarily the original contract.

---

## Strategic Reality

The **strongest requests** in a dispute letter are:

• Full **account history**  
• **Chain of assignment** (who owns the debt)  
• **Method of verification** used  
• Documentation showing **you are the responsible party**

These are harder for collectors to produce.

# 1. Debt Collector Verification Requirement
Under the entity["law","Fair Debt Collection Practices Act"]

**15 U.S.C. §1692g(b)**

> “If the consumer notifies the debt collector in writing that the debt… is disputed, the debt collector shall cease collection… until the debt collector obtains verification of the debt…”

Source

### What “verification” legally means
Federal courts have interpreted it narrowly.

Example:

**entity["legal_case","Chaudhry v. Gallerizzo"] (4th Cir. 1999)**

> Verification requires only that the collector confirm the amount of the debt with the creditor.

Source  
https://casetext.com/case/chaudhry-v-gallerizzo

Meaning:  
They often satisfy verification by **checking the creditor’s records**.

# 2. Credit Bureau Disputes (Method of Verification)
If the dispute is with a **credit bureau**, the law is different.

Under the entity["law","Fair Credit Reporting Act"]

**15 U.S.C. §1681i(a)(7)**

Consumers may request the **method of verification**.

Quote:

> “A consumer reporting agency shall provide… a description of the procedure used to determine the accuracy and completeness of the information…”

Source  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i

### What they must give
If requested within **15 days of results**, they must provide:

• The **business name** that verified the data  
• The **address or contact information**  
• A **description of the procedure used**

They still **do not have to identify the individual employee.**

https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i
