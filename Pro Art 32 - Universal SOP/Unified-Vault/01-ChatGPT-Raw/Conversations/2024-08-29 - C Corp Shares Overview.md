What do you mean by non assessable?

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

"Non-assessable" is a legal term used in corporate law to describe shares that, once fully paid for by the shareholder, cannot be subject to any additional calls for payment by the corporation. In other words, once you have paid the agreed-upon price for the shares, the corporation cannot require you to pay more money to cover the corporation's debts or obligations.

### Here’s a more detailed explanation:

- **Fully Paid:** When you purchase or are issued shares in a corporation, you typically pay a certain amount of money per share. Once this amount is paid, the shares are considered "fully paid."
  
- **Non-Assessable:** After shares are fully paid, they are deemed "non-assessable," meaning that the shareholder is not liable to pay any additional amounts to the corporation in the future, regardless of the corporation's financial situation.

This term is important for protecting shareholders from unexpected liabilities. It assures them that their financial obligation to the corporation is limited to the initial amount they invested when purchasing their shares.

### Example:
If you buy 1,000 shares at $1 per share, you pay $1,000. Once this $1,000 is paid, your shares are fully paid and non-assessable. The corporation cannot later demand more money from you to help cover its debts or other financial obligations related to those shares. Your risk is limited to the money you originally invested.
