# Copyright Notice Options

---

## 👤 You

should i keep or remove "all rights reserved" underneath my name without recourse

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The phrase "all rights reserved" is traditionally used to indicate that you retain full copyright over your work and that others must obtain permission to use it. However, the term is somewhat outdated, as international copyright law automatically grants you these rights without needing to explicitly state it. 

If you are looking to present a more modern, professional image or if your work is already covered by a copyright notice elsewhere, you might consider removing "all rights reserved." Instead, you could focus on other legal language that aligns with your specific needs, such as specifying the type of license under which your work is released (e.g., Creative Commons or a custom license).

If you decide to use "without recourse," it typically means that you do not wish to be held responsible for any consequences of others using your work. However, this phrase is more commonly associated with financial documents and may not be necessary or appropriate for general use with copyright statements.

In summary:
- **Keep "all rights reserved"** if you want to make it explicitly clear that you retain all copyright rights.
- **Remove it** if you prefer a cleaner, more modern approach, especially if your work is already protected by copyright law.
- **Evaluate the use of "without recourse"** based on whether it aligns with your legal needs and the context of your work.
