Here’s the simple, accurate breakdown 👇  

### ⚙️ How Charging Stops — Step-by-Step
1. **Constant Current (CC) phase:**  
   The charger pushes a steady current (≈1 A) into the battery while voltage gradually rises toward **8.4 V**.  
2. **Constant Voltage (CV) phase:**  
   Once the battery reaches 8.4 V, the charger holds voltage steady and lets current naturally taper down.  
3. **Cutoff:**  
   When current drops below a small threshold (≈0.05–0.1 A), the **charger’s internal controller** cuts power.  
4. **Battery BMS (backup protection):**  
   Each LP-E6-type battery also has its own protection board (BMS) that will disconnect if voltage ever exceeds the safe limit — a second layer of safety.

### 🧠 So who decides?
- **Primary control:** the **charger** — it constantly monitors voltage and current.  
- **Failsafe protection:** the **battery’s internal circuit** — it ensures safety even if the charger failed.  

✅ **Result:** it automatically stops precisely at full charge — no overcharging, no manual timing required.

How does it know

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Perfect question — and this goes right to how “smart” Li-ion charging actually works.  

Here’s the **real mechanism** behind how your SmallRig (and any quality CC/CV charger) *knows* when to stop:  

---

### ⚡️ 1. Voltage sensing  
Inside the charger is a **microcontroller** (MCU) that constantly measures the battery’s **voltage** in real time.  
- A 2-cell Li-ion pack like LP-E6 hits **8.4 V** when it’s full.  
- The charger is programmed to recognize that exact target.  
- As soon as the pack voltage reaches 8.4 V, it switches from constant current → constant voltage.

---

### 🔋 2. Current monitoring  
After it reaches 8.4 V, the charger keeps feeding just enough current to maintain that voltage.  
- As the battery’s internal chemical reaction completes, it **accepts less current**.  
- The MCU monitors the drop in current — once it falls below a defined threshold (often ~100 mA), it *knows* the battery is fully saturated.  
- At that point it **cuts off power** or shifts to trickle/stand-by.

---

### 🧠 3. Battery protection redundancy  
Each LP-E6-type battery also has its own **BMS (Battery Management System)** — a tiny protection board inside.  
- It monitors individual cell voltage and temperature.  
- If anything exceeds limits (over-voltage, over-current, or over-temp), it opens a protection MOSFET and stops charging.  

So even if the charger were faulty, the battery still prevents overcharge.

---

✅ **In short:**  
The **charger’s microcontroller** does the main decision-making using live voltage/current data, and the **battery’s BMS** acts as a safety net.  
That’s how it *knows exactly when to stop* — automatically, every time.
