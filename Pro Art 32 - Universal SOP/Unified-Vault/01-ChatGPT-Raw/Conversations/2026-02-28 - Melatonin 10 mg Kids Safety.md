## Melatonin 10 mg for Kids — Safe?

**Short answer: No — 10 mg is generally too high for a child unless specifically directed by a pediatrician.**

### What experts typically recommend
- **Preschool (3–5 yrs):** 0.5–1 mg  
- **School age (6–12 yrs):** 1–3 mg  
- **Teens:** 1–5 mg  

Many children respond to **0.5–2 mg**. More is not better.

### Why 10 mg is not ideal
- Significantly higher than common pediatric ranges  
- Can cause: next-day grogginess, headaches, vivid dreams, nausea  
- Long-term hormonal effects in children are **not fully studied**

### Important notes
- Use short term while fixing sleep routine (light exposure, screens, bedtime timing).  
- Always confirm with your child’s pediatrician — especially if the child has ADHD, autism, anxiety, or takes other medications.  
- In the U.S., melatonin is a supplement (not FDA-regulated like prescriptions), so dose accuracy can vary by brand.

### If 10 mg was already given once
- Usually not dangerous, but monitor for excessive sleepiness, vomiting, confusion, or unusual behavior.  
- If concerning symptoms occur, contact Poison Control (U.S. 1-800-222-1222).
