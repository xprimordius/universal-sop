## 🤖 ChatGPT

Here’s the math, simplified:

### Step 1: Annual → Monthly Interest Rate  
- APR = **18% annual**  
- Monthly rate = 18% ÷ 12 = **1.5% per month**

### Step 2: Interest on $1,100 Balance  
- $1,100 × 1.5% = **$16.50 first month interest**

### Step 3: Minimum Payments Effect  
- Most cards set minimum at **2–3% of balance** (≈ $22–$33 on $1,100).  
- Your payment will cover **interest first**, so ~$16.50 goes to interest, only a small amount chips away at principal.  
- As balance slowly falls, monthly interest decreases too.

---

✅ **Answer:** About **$16–$17 in interest the first month**. With minimum-only payments, it would take **years to pay off**, and total interest could exceed the original $1,100.

### Rule of Thumb  
- **Anything above ~3% (minimum)** starts actually hitting principal.  
- **10% of balance per month** is the “sweet spot” → fast payoff, big interest savings, but not too heavy.  
- **20%+ per month** = almost no interest accumulation, balance clears very quickly.

---

### Example on $1,100 at 18% APR  
- **Minimum (≈3%)**: $33 → only ~$16 goes to principal, years to pay off.  
- **10% ($110/month)**: Paid off in ~11 months, **<$100 total interest**.  
- **20% ($220/month)**: Paid off in ~5–6 months, **<$50 total interest**.  
- **50% ($550/month)**: Balance gone in 2 months, **almost no interest**.

---

### Optimal Strategy  
- **Target 10–20% of the balance monthly**.  
- This keeps cash flow manageable while slashing years and thousands in wasted interest.  
- Once you’re at 0, pay in full every month → no interest ever again.
