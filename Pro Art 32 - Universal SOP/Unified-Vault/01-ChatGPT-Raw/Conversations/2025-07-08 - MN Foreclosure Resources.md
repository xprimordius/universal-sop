**How to Check MN Local County Assessor or Sheriff for Foreclosures**

### 1. **Understand Who Handles Foreclosures in MN:**
- **Sheriff’s Office** — Foreclosure sales (NOT the County Assessor).
- **County Assessor** only handles **property values/taxes**, not foreclosures.

---

### 2. **Find Sheriff Foreclosure Sales (Per County):**
Go to the **Sheriff's Office** website of the county you're targeting.

#### Example for Hennepin County:
- Search: **“Hennepin County Sheriff foreclosure sales”**
- Direct Link:  
[Hennepin County Foreclosure Sales](https://www.hennepinsheriff.org/services/foreclosure-sales)
- You’ll find:
  - Property Address
  - Sale Date
  - Judgment Amount
  - Redemption Period

#### Example for Ramsey County:
[Ramsey County Sheriff Foreclosures](https://www.ramseycounty.us/your-government/leadership/sheriff/sheriffs-office-foreclosures)

---

### 3. **Find Pre-Foreclosures via Public Notices:**
- Visit **Minnesota Public Notices**:  
[https://www.mnpublicnotice.com/](https://www.mnpublicnotice.com/)
- Search by county or keywords like “Notice of Foreclosure Sale.”

---

### 4. **Check County Recorder / Treasurer for Tax-Forfeited Properties:**
These are **tax** foreclosures (different from mortgage foreclosures).
- Example:  
[Hennepin County Tax-Forfeited Land Sales](https://www.hennepin.us/residents/property/tax-forfeited-land)

### ✅ Key Facts:
1. **You *can* buy "subject to" a mortgage in foreclosure—but it’s risky and must follow MN law.**
2. Minnesota is a **judicial and non-judicial** foreclosure state.
3. Most MN foreclosures are **non-judicial via Sheriff’s Sale** under **power of sale clause**.

---

### ✅ MN Foreclosure Timeline:
1. **Foreclosure Sale Held by Sheriff.**
2. **Redemption Period (Usually 6 months, sometimes 12 months):**
   - Owner can reclaim by paying full amount.
   - You can buy subject to during this period—but the owner can still redeem.
3. **Possession Rights:**
   - Buyer at Sheriff’s Sale can’t take possession until redemption period ends.

---

### ✅ "Subject To" Strategy in MN:
- You take over **payments** but mortgage stays in seller’s name.
- MN has **strict laws to protect homeowners**:
  - MN Statute 325N — regulates “equity stripping” & foreclosure consultant laws.
  - You may be classified as a “foreclosure consultant” if you approach a homeowner in foreclosure.
  - Violations = Severe penalties (even criminal).

---

### ✅ To Do It Legally:
1. Consult MN attorney familiar with **foreclosure & "subject to" deals**.
2. Provide **full disclosures** to the seller.
3. Comply with **MN Foreclosure Protection Act**:
   - Cooling-off period.
   - Written contracts.
   - No unconscionable terms.
4. Know you may still lose the property if redemption occurs.

---

### ✅ Common Risks:
- Seller redeems or files bankruptcy.
- You can’t evict seller during redemption.
- Due-on-sale clause can trigger foreclosure faster.
- You must comply with MN equity protection laws—strict.

---

### ✅ Bottom Line:
**Yes, it’s possible—but extremely regulated and high-risk in Minnesota.**
