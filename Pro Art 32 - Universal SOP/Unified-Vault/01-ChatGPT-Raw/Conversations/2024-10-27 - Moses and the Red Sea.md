The **Pharisees** were a religious and political group in ancient Judaism during the time of the Second Temple, particularly prominent from around 150 BCE to 70 CE. They were known for their strict adherence to the Torah (Jewish law) and the oral traditions that supplemented it. The Pharisees believed in the importance of both the written and oral law as guiding principles for Jewish life.

Some key points about the Pharisees:

1. **Emphasis on the Law**: They placed great importance on the interpretation and observance of the Jewish laws (Halakha), ensuring that everyday life was in accordance with the Torah. This included rituals, dietary laws, and practices related to purity.

2. **Belief in the Afterlife**: The Pharisees believed in the resurrection of the dead, the existence of angels, and a future judgment. This distinguished them from other groups like the Sadducees, who denied these beliefs.

3. **Influence on Rabbinic Judaism**: After the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE, the Pharisaic tradition largely shaped what became Rabbinic Judaism, which is the foundation of modern Judaism today. Their focus on Torah study and oral interpretation became central.

4. **Relationship with Jesus**: In the New Testament, the Pharisees are frequently portrayed as opponents of Jesus, often criticizing his teachings and actions. Jesus, in turn, rebuked them for their perceived hypocrisy and emphasis on external ritual over internal purity. However, this portrayal reflects the theological and social tensions of that time rather than the full reality of the Pharisees’ beliefs and practices.

The Pharisees were one of several Jewish sects of the time, including the Sadducees, Essenes, and Zealots, but their emphasis on the law and oral tradition was a key influence in shaping the future of Judaism.
