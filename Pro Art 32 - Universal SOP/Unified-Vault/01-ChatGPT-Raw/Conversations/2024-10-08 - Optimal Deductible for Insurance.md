# Optimal Deductible for Insurance

---

## 👤 You

What is the sweet spot for deductible for full coverage car insurance to keep rates low but not have to break the bank in the event something does happen

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

The sweet spot for a deductible on full coverage car insurance typically falls between **$500 and $1,000**. Here's why:

1. **Lower Premiums:** Increasing your deductible to $1,000 can significantly lower your monthly premiums. This is especially beneficial if you're a safe driver and don’t expect to file many claims.
   
2. **Affordable Out-of-Pocket Cost:** A $500 deductible strikes a balance where you won’t be breaking the bank if an accident happens. It's a manageable amount to pay out of pocket.

3. **Risk Tolerance:** If you have enough savings and can comfortably cover a $1,000 deductible, then opting for the higher deductible can save you more in the long run through lower premiums.

So, a **$500 to $1,000 deductible** is generally the sweet spot for maintaining reasonable premiums while ensuring you're not overly burdened with out-of-pocket costs in the event of a claim.

## 👤 You

I was thinking $500 vs $100

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

Choosing between a **$500** and **$100** deductible depends on your priorities regarding monthly premium savings and out-of-pocket costs in the event of an accident. Here's a breakdown:

- **$100 Deductible**: 
  - **Higher premiums**: Your monthly premiums will be significantly higher since the insurance company takes on more risk. 
  - **Minimal out-of-pocket costs**: If you get into an accident, you'll only need to pay $100, which is much easier to handle.

- **$500 Deductible**: 
  - **Lower premiums**: You'll save more on your monthly premiums since you're taking on more risk.
  - **Higher out-of-pocket cost**: In the event of an accident, you'll need to pay $500, which is still manageable for most people.

**Sweet spot recommendation**: In most cases, the **$500 deductible** is better. It offers a balance between saving on premiums while still keeping out-of-pocket costs reasonable if something does happen. If you rarely make claims and can handle the $500 if needed, it's a better choice for long-term savings. The **$100 deductible** is better if you're concerned about any potential out-of-pocket expense, but expect higher premiums month to month.
