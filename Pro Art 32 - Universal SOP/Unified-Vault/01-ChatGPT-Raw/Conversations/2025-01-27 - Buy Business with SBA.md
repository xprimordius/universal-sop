Here’s how to buy a business with no money using the SBA 7(a) loan program and leveraging the target business’s credit:

---

### **1. Understand the SBA 7(a) Loan Program**
The SBA 7(a) loan is a government-backed loan for acquiring, starting, or expanding a small business. Key features include:
- Loan Amount: Up to $5 million.
- Down Payment: Typically 10%–20% (can be creatively structured).
- Repayment Terms: Up to 10 years for non-real estate businesses.

---

### **2. Leverage the Target Business's Credit**
Use the target business's existing financials to qualify for the loan:
1. **Strong Financial Performance:**
   - Ensure the business has stable cash flow, profitability, and good credit history.
   - A Debt-Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) above 1.25 is ideal.

2. **Collateral from the Business:**
   - The target business’s assets (equipment, accounts receivable, inventory) can serve as collateral.
   - Work with a lender who accepts these as sufficient collateral to minimize personal guarantees.

3. **Seller Financing:**
   - Negotiate with the seller to provide financing for a portion (e.g., 10%–15%).
   - This reduces your upfront cash requirement and demonstrates seller confidence in the business.

---

### **3. Structure the Deal for No Money Down**
1. **Combine Seller Financing with SBA Loan:**
   - Use seller financing to cover the SBA-required down payment (consult your lender to ensure compliance).
   - Structure the loan as:
     - 85% from the SBA 7(a) loan.
     - 15% seller financing (subordinated to the SBA loan).

2. **Include Earnouts:**
   - Pay part of the purchase price through future profits based on performance metrics.

3. **Use Equity from Partners or Investors:**
   - Bring in silent partners or investors to fund the down payment in exchange for equity or a return.

---

### **4. Improve Eligibility for SBA Loan**
- **Business Plan:** Develop a detailed plan highlighting the business's potential and your qualifications.
- **Management Expertise:** Show relevant experience or commit to hiring experienced managers.
- **Personal Guarantee:** While SBA loans often require a personal guarantee, mitigate risk by securing strong business financials and collateral.

---

### **5. Find the Right Lender**
- Work with SBA-preferred lenders experienced in business acquisitions.
- Some lenders are more flexible in structuring deals with low or no down payments.

---

### **6. Close the Deal**
1. **Loan Approval:**
   - Provide required documents (financial statements, tax returns, business valuation).
   - Secure lender approval for the SBA loan.

2. **Legal Agreements:**
   - Draft purchase agreements outlining the deal structure (e.g., seller financing, earnouts).
   - Include contingencies for SBA approval.

3. **Transition Planning:**
   - Develop a post-acquisition plan for smooth operations and continued success.

---

### **Key Tips:**
- **Due Diligence:** Ensure the target business has a solid history and clear growth potential.
- **Creative Negotiations:** Leverage seller incentives, such as seller financing or deferred payments.
- **Minimize Risk:** Carefully review the business’s liabilities and cash flow to avoid over-leveraging.

This strategy combines creativity, financial leverage, and structured financing to buy a business with minimal or no personal capital.
