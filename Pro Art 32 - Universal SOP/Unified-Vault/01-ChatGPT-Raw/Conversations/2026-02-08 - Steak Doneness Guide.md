## 👤 You

How to know when a steak is done? I thought it was about the tenderness, it felt raw/rare for so long and had it on there for 10 mins until I cut off an end and it was well done but squishy in the middle still

---

## 🤖 ChatGPT

**Short answer:** tenderness lies. **Temperature + touch + timing** is the truth.

---

## Why your steak fooled you
- **Fat + connective tissue = squishy even when done.**  
  Ribeye, chuck, and thick sirloin can feel soft while already **medium-well to well**.
- **Surface cooks faster than center.**  
  You cut the end (well-done) but the **thermal lag** left the middle hot and soft.
- **Resting finishes the cook.**  
  The center keeps cooking **5–10°F** after it leaves the heat.

---

## The only reliable way (no guessing)
### 1️⃣ Use temperature (gold standard)
- Rare: **120–125°F**
- Medium-rare: **130–135°F**
- Medium: **140–145°F**
- Medium-well: **150–155°F**
- Well: **160°F+**

👉 Pull it **5–10°F early**, then rest 5 minutes.

---

## If you don’t have a thermometer (backup system)
### 2️⃣ Finger test (quick field method)
Press the center with tongs or finger:
- **Very soft, mushy** → Rare
- **Soft, springy** → Medium-rare
- **Firm, springs back** → Medium
- **Hard** → Well

⚠️ This fails with fatty cuts (ribeye, NY strip).

---

## Timing guide (1–1¼" thick steak, high heat)
- Rare: **2–3 min/side**
- Med-rare: **3–4 min/side**
- Medium: **4–5 min/side**
- Well: **5–6+ min/side**

10 minutes total on high heat = **very likely medium-well+**, even if it feels soft.

---

## The squishy-but-well-done paradox (what you experienced)
- Fat hadn’t fully rendered yet → soft center
- Muscle fibers already overcooked → dry edges
- Cutting early released juices → mushy texture

That’s why it *felt* rare but *wasn’t*.
