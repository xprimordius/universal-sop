**Gastroparesis (delayed gastric emptying)**  

- **Definition**: A condition where the stomach muscles don’t move food normally into the small intestine.  
- **Cause**: Often linked to nerve damage (especially the vagus nerve), diabetes, surgery, viral infections, autoimmune conditions, or sometimes unknown (idiopathic).  
- **Symptoms**:  
  - Nausea, vomiting (especially undigested food)  
  - Early fullness, bloating, abdominal pain  
  - Poor appetite, weight loss, malnutrition  
  - Blood sugar fluctuations (in diabetics)  

- **Diagnosis**: Gastric emptying study (scintigraphy), breath test, upper endoscopy, or motility tests.  
- **Treatment**:  
  - **Dietary changes**: small, frequent meals, low fat/fiber, soft/pureed foods.  
  - **Medications**: prokinetics (e.g., metoclopramide, erythromycin), anti-nausea drugs.  
  - **Other**: feeding tubes in severe cases, gastric electrical stimulation, or surgery if unmanageable.  
  - **Lifestyle**: strict blood sugar control if diabetic, avoid smoking/alcohol, upright after meals.  

👉 It’s a **chronic condition**—management focuses on symptom relief, nutrition, and underlying causes (like diabetes).

### **Why This Happened (Causes of Gastroparesis)**
- **Diabetes (most common cause):**  
  High blood sugar over time damages the **vagus nerve**, which controls stomach muscle movement. When this nerve is impaired, food doesn’t move properly.
- **Nerve/muscle damage from surgery:** Operations near the stomach or esophagus can trigger it.  
- **Infections or autoimmune diseases:** Sometimes viral or immune attacks damage stomach nerves.  
- **Medication side effects:** Opioids, antidepressants, and certain anticholinergics can slow stomach emptying.  
- **Idiopathic (unknown):** In many cases, no exact cause is found.  

In her case, **diabetes** is very likely the trigger.  

---

### **Why Surgery is Considered**
Gastroparesis is usually managed with diet + meds, but **surgery becomes necessary** if:
- Nutrition can’t be maintained (weight loss, dehydration).  
- Feeding tube dependence.  
- Severe vomiting and poor quality of life.  

**Types of surgery or procedures:**  
- **Feeding tubes** (into small intestine bypassing stomach).  
- **Gastric electrical stimulation (GES):** small device sends mild pulses to stomach muscles.  
- **Pyloroplasty or G-POEM:** widen/open the valve between stomach and intestine to allow food to pass more easily.  

---

### **What Can Be Done to Resolve**
1. **Stabilize blood sugar** (critical for diabetics).  
2. **Dietary adjustments:** small frequent meals, low fiber, low fat, liquid/pureed foods.  
3. **Medications:**  
   - Prokinetics (metoclopramide, erythromycin) to stimulate stomach contractions.  
   - Antiemetics (ondansetron, promethazine) to reduce nausea/vomiting.  
4. **Nutritional support:** feeding tubes or IV nutrition in severe cases.  
5. **Surgery or gastric stimulator** if nothing else works.
