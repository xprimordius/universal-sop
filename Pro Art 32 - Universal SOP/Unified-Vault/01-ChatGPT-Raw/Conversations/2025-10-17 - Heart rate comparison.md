If your heart rate drifts into **140–150 bpm** during what you’re aiming to keep as a **Zone 2 (aerobic) session**, here’s what happens and how to interpret it:  

---

### 🔹 Physiological Impact  
- **You’re entering Zone 3 (tempo / low anaerobic)** — meaning your body starts relying more on glycogen (stored carbs) instead of primarily fat for fuel.  
- **Fat burning doesn’t shut off**, but it **decreases** relative to carbohydrate use.  
- **Lactate levels rise**, but not dangerously — this range still improves endurance and VO₂ max slightly more than Zone 2 does.  

---

### 🔹 Occasional Spikes Are Fine  
- Briefly going up to 145–150 bpm (e.g., small hills, bursts, excitement) **doesn’t ruin the session**.  
- What matters is the **average HR over the session** — if it stays mostly around 130–140 bpm, you’re still getting Zone 2 benefits.  

---

### 🔹 If It Happens Often  
- You’ll start to lose some of the metabolic efficiency benefits (mitochondrial and fat-oxidation adaptations) if most of the workout lives in 145–150 bpm.  
- You’ll also **recover slower**, since Zone 3 is more taxing on the nervous system and glycogen stores.  

---

### 🔹 Bottom Line  
If you’re **“accidentally between 140–150 bpm” occasionally**, no problem — still a great session.  
If it’s **sustained** in that range, it’s more of a **tempo workout**, not a strict Zone 2.  

You can fix it easily by:  
- Slowing pace slightly or reducing incline.  
- Using nasal breathing as a real-time gauge (if you can’t hold a conversation or breathe nasally, you’re likely above Zone 2).

- **Zone 4–5** = best for performance and glycogen usage (short-term power).
