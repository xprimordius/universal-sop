## 1. Key Principle  
If a creditor **takes adverse action based on a credit report**, U.S. law requires disclosure of the **source of the information** so you can verify or dispute it.

Two federal statutes apply.

---

## 2. Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) — Adverse Action Disclosure

entity["law","Fair Credit Reporting Act","US federal law regulating consumer credit reporting"]

When a creditor takes an **adverse action** (limit reduction, denial, etc.) based on a credit report:

**15 U.S.C. §1681m(a)** requires they disclose:

- The **consumer reporting agency used**
- A **statement that the agency did not make the decision**
- Notice of your right to obtain your report
- Notice of your right to dispute

Source:  
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681m

Important:  
They **do not have to identify the exact creditor line item**, but they **must identify the reporting agency** whose data they relied on.

---

## 3. ECOA / Regulation B — Specific Reasons Required

entity["law","Equal Credit Opportunity Act","US federal law prohibiting discrimination in credit decisions"]

If the action affects credit terms (such as lowering your limit), the creditor must provide **specific reasons**.

**12 CFR §1002.9(a)(2)** requires:

> “A statement of specific reasons for the adverse action.”

Source:  
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/

Examples regulators consider acceptable:

- “Serious delinquency reported on credit report”
- “Recent late payment”

But **vague statements like “credit report information” are not compliant.**
