# **3\) Legal Violations & Enforcement Exposure (With Maximum Civil Penalty Numbers Where Verifiable)**

## **A) Bank dispute handling duties (FCBA / Reg Z — billing error resolution timing)**

Card issuers must acknowledge a billing dispute within **30 days** and complete the investigation within required timeframes (commonly described as within **two billing cycles, not more than 90 days**, depending on circumstances). An FDIC consumer compliance explainer states: **issuer must send a letter within 30 days acknowledging receipt and must complete the investigation within required time**.

**Your demand here is not “customer service.”** It is a compliance demand for the issuer’s **complete file**, reason codes, and lawful investigation.

## **B) CFPB civil penalty exposure (Tiered CMP amounts — inflation adjusted)**

If an entity is within CFPB jurisdiction and conduct is found to violate Federal consumer financial law, CMPs can be assessed (tiered). The CFPB’s inflation adjustment lists maximum daily penalty amounts as:

* **Tier 1:** **$7,034** per day

* **Tier 2:** **$35,169** per day

* **Tier 3:** **$1,406,728** per day

## **C) OCC / bank-regulator CMP exposure (inflation adjusted; very high Tier 3 maximum)**

The OCC’s inflation adjustment document lists maximum civil money penalties under its authority (tiers). The published maximum Tier 3 amount is **$22,513,215** (inflation-adjusted maximum).

Practical meaning: if the file shows systemic or reckless noncompliance in disputes handling, or misleading/defective processes, this can become **enforcement-grade**, not “a billing issue.”

## **D) FTC civil penalty exposure (inflation-adjusted schedule; includes FCRA penalty figure)**

The FTC publishes its inflation-adjusted civil penalty schedule. Among the listed amounts:

* **FCRA (15 U.S.C. 1681s(a)(2)(A))** civil penalty: **$5,046**

(FTC penalty schedule contains multiple civil penalty figures across statutes/rules; the applicable one depends on the violation and statutory hook.)

## **E) State UDAP / Consumer fraud civil penalties (example: Minnesota)**

Minnesota’s Attorney General enforcement statute provides for civil penalties of **up to $25,000 per violation** in specified consumer protection enforcement actions.

If your state differs, the AG recipient should apply the state’s UDAP penalty scheme; the point is that **state enforcement penalties are real, numeric, and per-violation** in many jurisdictions.
