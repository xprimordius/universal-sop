***NOTICE OF INTENT TO SUE***

**Pursuant to the Truth in Lending Act (15 USC 1640), the Fair Credit Billing Act (15 USC 1666), the Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 USC 1681), and applicable state consumer protection laws**, this letter serves as formal notice of the consumer's intent to commence litigation against First Interstate Bank for multiple violations of federal billing error resolution requirements.

This notice is being provided in good faith as a final opportunity for the bank to resolve this matter before suit is filed. The facts, violations, and damages set forth below are supported entirely by FIB's own documents \- including correspondence from Compliance Officer Kelli Asay, Director of Compliance Joey Liskh, and Associate General Counsel Andrea Antillon.

**PATTERN OF WILLFUL NONCOMPLIANCE**

The violations documented herein are not isolated errors. They reflect a pattern of willful noncompliance, reckless disregard for consumer rights, and deliberate obstruction of the billing error dispute process across eight months and five CFPB complaints.

Willful noncompliance is established when a creditor knows or should know that its conduct violates the law and proceeds regardless. **Under 15 USC 1640(a), willful noncompliance entitles the consumer to statutory damages, actual damages, punitive damages, and mandatory attorney's fees.** Under **FCRA 15 USC 1681n, willful noncompliance carries statutory damages of $100 to $1,000 per violation plus punitive damages with no statutory cap.**

**FIB's conduct demonstrates willfulness at every stage**:

**Willful Withholding of Evidence**: FIB withheld merchant representment documents for approximately seven months despite repeated documented requests via certified mail, fax, and recorded phone calls. FIB's own 9/25/2025 letter promises to "promptly provide" these documents upon request. FIB chose not to. This is not an oversight \- it is a deliberate decision to deny the consumer access to the evidence being used against him, sustained across seven months and multiple requests.

**Willful Denial of Meaningful Access**: FIB mailed time-sensitive correspondence to an invalid commercial address it had been notified was no longer in use. The consumer made multiple documented requests to change the address, send correspondence digitally, or deliver to an alternate address. FIB denied every request. FIB also refused to communicate the substance of the dispute over the phone. This created a closed loop designed to produce non-response — and FIB then used that non-response as the sole basis for denial. A creditor that engineers the conditions for failure and then penalizes the consumer for failing is acting willfully.

**Willful Acceptance of Contradictory Evidence**: FIB accepted a "non-refundable" claim from a merchant that had already issued a $7,500 refund. FIB accepted 129.5 billable hours with no signed authorization. FIB accepted an unsigned agreement as binding. FIB accepted retroactive "no refund" terms imposed by text message. These are not judgment calls made in a gray area \- they are internally contradictory positions that any reasonable review would have flagged. FIB accepted all of them without scrutiny.

**Willful Misrepresentation to Regulator**: Attorney Antillon told the Montana Division of Banking that FIB "did not receive any information from the customer disputing that work done by the merchant." This is directly contradicted by certified mail receipts, fax confirmations, CFPB records, and recorded phone calls \- all pre-dating the merchant's response. Making a false statement to a state banking regulator in the course of a regulatory complaint is not negligence. It is willful misrepresentation.

**Willful Misrepresentation of Consumer Rights**: FIB's own 8/21/2025 letter states the claim "does not qualify as a recognizable billing error under consumer protection laws because of the card type." This designation appears on every dispute letter. Yet FIB conducted the entire Regulation Z process and its attorney cited 12 CFR 1026.13(c) and (f) as the authority followed. FIB cannot disclaim Reg Z protections in writing to the consumer while citing Reg Z as authority to the regulator. This is a knowing misrepresentation of the consumer's rights.

**Reckless Disregard Through Repeated Non-Responsive Denials**: Across five CFPB complaints \- each raising distinct, documented, specific regulatory violations \- FIB responded with substantively identical boilerplate. Asay's February 11 letter: "all applicable policies and procedures were properly followed." Liskh's March 2 letter: "all applicable transaction dispute policies and procedures were properly followed." Asay's March 13 letter: "the circumstances surrounding your complaint remain unchanged. No further action will be taken." Antillon's March 13 letter addressed only three narrow claims and did not engage with the unsigned agreement, the documentation withholding, the No-Card Type contradiction, the invalid mailing address, or any other specific allegation. Each denial and each non-responsive appeal response constitutes a separate act of reckless disregard \- because each time, FIB had the documented evidence in its possession and chose to ignore it.

**Willful Violation of the 90-Day Statutory Deadline**: FIB acknowledged receipt of the dispute on 8/14/2025. FIB issued its determination on 11/26/2025 \- 104 days later. The statutory maximum under 12 CFR 1026.13(c)(2) is 90 days. FIB exceeded it by 14 days. A creditor with a legal department and compliance officers is presumed to know the regulatory deadlines governing its own dispute process.

**FORCE MAJEURE AND IMPOSSIBILITY**

The consumer's non-response to the November 14, 2025 letter was not voluntary. It was the direct and unavoidable result of FIB's own conduct:

FIB mailed the letter to an invalid commercial address the consumer had repeatedly asked FIB to change. FIB refused to change the address. FIB refused to send the letter digitally. FIB refused to deliver to an alternate address. FIB refused to communicate the letter's contents by phone. The consumer was traveling in California \- documented by FIB's own fraud block records. One-way USPS transit from Montana to Minnesota consumed 3-5 of the 10 calendar days allotted. Round-trip mail transit made timely response structurally impossible even if the consumer had been home at a valid address.

Under the Restatement (Second) of Contracts §261, a party's duty to perform is discharged when performance is made impracticable by circumstances the non-performing party did not cause. Under the doctrine of equitable estoppel, a party that creates the conditions preventing performance cannot penalize non-performance. Under UCC §1-304, the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing prohibits a party from destroying the other party's ability to receive the benefits of the transaction.

FIB's denial rests entirely on the consumer's failure to do something that FIB made impossible. A creditor that creates the conditions making compliance impossible cannot then penalize the consumer for non-compliance.
