# FIB Yes_No Compliance Questions.md

> [!info] From project: [[_Project Info|Timelog FIB]]

## **January 31, 2:10 AM**

## **Yes / No Compliance Clarification \- Purpose Statement**

## The following questions are presented to establish a clear, factual record regarding First Interstate Bank’s handling of my billing dispute.

## Each question is intentionally framed to be answerable yes or no, based solely on objective facts, records, and actions taken by the bank. These questions are designed to clarify:

* ## what evidence was received,

* ## what investigation steps were taken,

* ## what notice was provided,

* ## and whether statutory and regulatory duties were satisfied. 

## These questions are not argumentative. They are intended to identify **undisputed facts**, **procedural compliance**, and **decision logic** underlying the bank’s denial and closure of my dispute.

## Any inability to answer “yes” to a question that reflects a required consumer protection obligation is relevant to CFPB review, Regulation Z compliance, and UDAAP analysis.

---

## 

##  **YES / NO ADMISSION SERIES**

---

### **A. Services Not Rendered – Objective Facts**

1. **Yes or No:** Did the merchant ever deliver a completed, operational Airbnb property as promised in the transaction?

2. **Yes or No:** Did the merchant ever acquire a lease or property on my behalf?

3. **Yes or No:** Did any Airbnb listing ever go live as part of the paid Done-For-You service?

4. **Yes or No:** Was any revenue ever generated from the promised service?

5. **Yes or No:** Did the merchant issue a **partial refund**, acknowledging non-delivery of services?

*(If “yes” to \#5, non-delivery is legally conceded.)*

---

### 

### **B. Contract & Terms Validity**

6. **Yes or No:** Did the bank receive any **signed contract** showing my agreement to the post-payment terms cited in the denial?

7. **Yes or No:** Did the bank receive any **clickwrap or electronic acceptance record** proving assent to those terms **before payment**?

8. **Yes or No:** Were the terms relied upon in the denial disclosed **prior to or at the time of payment**?

9. **Yes or No:** Does the bank agree that **unsigned, post-payment terms** are not enforceable contracts?

---

### 

### **C. Investigation Duties (Regulation Z)**

10. **Yes or No:** Did the bank receive my complete dispute documentation on **September 5, 2025**?

11. **Yes or No:** Did the bank acknowledge receipt of that documentation?

12. **Yes or No:** Did the bank deny the dispute citing “missing documentation” despite having received it?

13. **Yes or No:** Did the bank rely primarily on the merchant’s rebuttal narrative rather than the documentary evidence?

14. **Yes or No:** Did the bank independently verify whether the contracted service was actually delivered?

---

### 

### **D. Notice & Procedural Fairness**

15. **Yes or No:** Did the bank rely exclusively on **physical mail** to deliver the response deadline notice?

16. **Yes or No:** Was the bank aware that I was traveling at the time the notice was allegedly sent?

17. **Yes or No:** Did I request digital delivery of dispute correspondence?

18. **Yes or No:** Did the bank refuse to provide digital delivery despite using digital delivery for other account communications?

19. **Yes or No:** Has the bank confirmed that I **never received** the November 2025 letter?

---

### 

### **E. Timing & Disparity**

20. **Yes or No:** Was the merchant given approximately **60+ days** to respond to the dispute?

21. **Yes or No:** Was I given **9 days or fewer** to respond to the merchant rebuttal?

22. **Yes or No:** Would the bank agree this created a **procedural imbalance**?

---

### 

### **F. Outcome Logic**

23. **Yes or No:** Is it the bank’s position that temporary access to low-cost third-party software constitutes delivery of a high-value DFY service?

24. **Yes or No:** Does the bank contend that preparatory or token activity cures total non-delivery?

25. **Yes or No:** Would the bank agree that allowing such logic would undermine consumer dispute protections?

---

### 

### **G. Regulatory Exposure Awareness**

26. **Yes or No:** Is the bank aware that closing a dispute without meaningful notice may violate **Regulation Z**?

27. **Yes or No:** Is the bank aware that such conduct may constitute **UDAAP** under Dodd-Frank?

28. **Yes or No:** Is the bank aware that retaliation or adverse treatment following a dispute is prohibited under **ECOA**?

---

### 

### **H. Resolution Capability**

29. **Yes or No:** Does the bank have the authority to correct this dispute administratively?

30. **Yes or No:** Would reversing the charge fully resolve this matter without litigation or regulatory escalation?

---

**I. Additional Questions**

### **31\. Mastercard / Reason Code Admission**

**Yes or No:** Did the bank evaluate this dispute under Mastercard Reason Code 4853 (Goods or Services Not Provided)?

---

### **32\. Receipt vs. Review Distinction**

Banks often claim “received” but not “reviewed.”

Add under **C. Investigation Duties**:

**Yes or No:** Did the bank substantively review the evidence demonstrating non-delivery before denying the dispute?

---

### **33\. Notice Confirmation**

**Yes or No:** Did the bank confirm delivery or receipt of the November 2025 letter before closing the dispute?

---

### **34\. Authority \+ Refusal**

**Yes or No:** Has the bank chosen not to exercise that authority despite evidence of non-delivery?

---

### 

### **J. Enforcement of Unenforceable Merchant Terms**

**35\. Yes or No:** Did the bank rely on a merchant-submitted agreement or terms document that was **unsigned by the consumer** when denying the dispute?

**36\. Yes or No:** Did the bank treat that unsigned document as **binding contractual authority**?

**37\. Yes or No:** Did the bank deny or uphold the dispute **based, in whole or in part, on those unsigned terms**?

**38\. Yes or No:** Did the bank verify whether those terms were **ever presented to or agreed to by the consumer prior to payment**?

**39\. Yes or No:** Did the bank consider whether enforcing **unsigned, post-payment terms** is legally permissible under basic contract law principles?

*These questions force an admission that the bank substituted merchant assertions for lawful agreement.*

---

## **K. Appeals & Repeated Denial After Notice**

**40\. Yes or No:** Did the consumer appeal the dispute after the initial denial?

**41\. Yes or No:** Did the consumer specifically raise the issue that the merchant’s relied-upon terms were **unsigned and unenforceable** during the appeal?

**42\. Yes or No:** Did the bank deny or uphold the dispute on appeal **without addressing the lack of a signed agreement**?

**43\. Yes or No:** Did the bank continue to rule in the merchant’s favor **after being directly notified of this defect**?

---

## **L. Knowledge of Merchant’s Representations & Inducement**

**44\. Yes or No:** Was the bank aware that the merchant publicly advertised **Done-For-You Airbnb services promising $10,000+ per month performance**?

**45\. Yes or No:** Was the bank aware that the merchant represented **expected profits of approximately $3,500+ per month per property**?

**46\. Yes or No:** Did the bank review or receive documentation of these representations **as part of the dispute evidence**?

**47\. Yes or No:** Did the bank consider whether the merchant’s failure to deliver **any property, listing, or revenue** constituted non-delivery of the essential promised service?

**48\. Yes or No:** Did the bank nonetheless rule in the merchant’s favor **despite these representations and the absence of delivery**?

---
