# **1\. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

First Interstate Bank (FIB) denied a $10,500 billing error dispute filed by the consumer on August 13, 2025, involving merchant Steam & Stream (Khaleel Gonzalez). FIB's response to the CFPB \- dated March 13, 2026 from Compliance Officer Kelli Asay and Associate General Counsel Andrea Antillon \- maintains the bank's position is unchanged. This analysis identifies at least seven distinct categories of federal regulatory violations evidenced by FIB's own correspondence.

**The bank's own documents reveal internal contradictions: the 8/21/2025 letter states the claim "does not qualify as a recognizable billing error under consumer protection laws because of the card type," yet the bank proceeded with a dispute investigation, issued provisional credit, and engaged the merchant \- all actions required only under Regulation Z billing error procedures. The bank cannot simultaneously disclaim Reg Z obligations while invoking Reg Z processes.**

# **3\. DETAILED VIOLATION ANALYSIS**

## **VIOLATION 1: Misrepresentation of Billing Error Rights (Reg Z §1026.13(a))**

FIB's 8/21/2025 letter states the claim "does not qualify as a recognizable billing error under consumer protection laws because of the card type." This is the 'No \- Card Type' notation visible on the provisional credit letter (9/25/2025) and the denial letters (11/26/2025, 12/18/2025).

However, FIB proceeded to:

* Request additional information from the consumer (8/21/2025)

* Issue provisional credit of $10,500 (9/25/2025)

* Engage the merchant and receive representment documents (10/30/2025)

* Send a follow-up 'Need More Information' letter with merchant claims (11/14/2025)

* Issue a formal determination that the transaction is valid (11/26/2025)

**These are the exact steps required under 12 CFR 1026.13(c) and (f). The bank cannot disclaim Reg Z billing error protections while simultaneously conducting a Reg Z-compliant investigation process. This constitutes either: (a) a misrepresentation of the consumer's rights under Reg Z, or (b) an admission that the bank understood Reg Z applied and chose to selectively comply.**

**Statutory Authority:** TILA §130(a) \- individual statutory damages up to $5,000 for credit card violations; actual damages; attorney's fees and costs. If the misrepresentation was knowing or willful, liability may also extend under UDAAP (12 USC §5536) with penalties up to $50,000/day per violation (or $1,000,000/day for reckless violations).

## **VIOLATION 2: Failure to Conduct a Reasonable Investigation (Reg Z §1026.13(f))**

Under 12 CFR 1026.13(f), when a creditor determines no billing error occurred, it must have conducted a "reasonable investigation" before making that determination. FIB's investigation exhibits the following deficiencies:

**A. Failure to reconcile contradictory merchant positions.**

The merchant simultaneously claimed: (1) the service was 'non-refundable' per the terms and agreement, and (2) the merchant issued a $7,500 refund. FIB's own 3/2/2026 letter (Liskh) acknowledges both facts. A reasonable investigation would have questioned how a 'non-refundable' service produced a $7,500 refund. FIB did not.

**B. Reliance on 'terms and agreement' never provided to or signed by the consumer.**

The merchant submitted a 'terms and agreement' as part of representment. However, the Consulting Agreement provided in the merchant's own packet (pages 20-22 of the response) contains NO refund/cancellation policy. The merchant's own text message (page 6 of response / iMessage screenshot) admits: "our standard Refund Policy was unintentionally omitted from our Partnership Agreement." FIB relied on a refund policy that was never disclosed to the consumer \- a foundational flaw in the investigation.

**C. Failure to weigh the consumer's evidence.**

Antillon's 3/13/2026 letter states: "We did not receive any information from the customer disputing that work done by the merchant." This mischaracterizes the record. The consumer filed the dispute on the basis of non-delivery of promised services. The bank's framework shifted the burden to the consumer to prove the negative \- that services were NOT delivered \- rather than requiring the merchant to prove the services WERE as described.

**Statutory Authority:** TILA §130(a) \- $5,000 individual statutory damages; actual damages (the $10,500 charge plus accrued interest and fees); attorney's fees. If a pattern of unreasonable investigations is established, the CFPB may pursue civil penalties under 12 USC §5565(c): $5,000/day for violations; $25,000/day for reckless violations; $1,000,000/day for knowing violations.

## **VIOLATION 3: Failure to Provide Requested Investigation Documents (Reg Z §1026.13(f))**

Under 12 CFR 1026.13(f), the creditor must, upon request, provide copies of "documentary evidence of the debtor's indebtedness" including any documents relied upon in making its determination.

FIB's own provisional credit letter (9/25/2025) and denial letter (11/26/2025) both contain the language: "You have the right to request a copy of any document(s) that we relied upon in making our determination. Upon your request, we shall promptly provide a copy of the document(s)."

**Despite repeated documented requests via certified mail, fax, and recorded telephone calls from August 2025 through February 2026, FIB refused to produce the merchant representment documents. These were first provided on 3/2/2026 \- approximately seven months after the dispute was opened \- and only after DBFI intervention.**

Antillon's 3/13/2026 letter claims: "Prior to that date, the Bank previously provided copies of all Dispute Letters to the customer, together with a phone number he could call to request copies of the merchant documents." This is a critical distinction: providing dispute letters is not the same as providing the merchant representment documents relied upon in the determination. Telling the consumer to call a phone number does not satisfy the regulatory obligation to 'promptly provide' documents upon request \- especially when the consumer had already made written requests.

**Statutory Authority:** TILA §130(a) \- $5,000 statutory damages; actual damages; attorney's fees. The 7-month delay is strong evidence of willful noncompliance. Under 15 USC §1640(a)(2)(A), where a creditor fails to comply with billing error resolution requirements, the consumer may recover statutory damages of not less than $500 and not more than $5,000, plus actual damages and attorney's fees.

## **VIOLATION 4: Failure to Provide Proof of Mailing \- November 14, 2025 Letter**

FIB's entire denial rests on the consumer's failure to respond to the November 14, 2025 "Need More Information" letter by the November 24, 2025 deadline (10 days). This is the procedural basis for closing the dispute.

However, FIB has never produced proof that this letter was actually mailed or delivered. Antillon's 3/13/2026 letter states only that the letter "was sent on 11/14/2025 to the same address on file" and "was not returned undeliverable." This is not proof of mailing. The absence of a return does not constitute proof of delivery.

**Under Regulation Z, the creditor bears the burden of establishing that its procedures were followed. The bank's refusal to produce time-stamped mailing logs or certified mail receipts \- despite explicit requests \- is a failure to document its compliance.**

Furthermore, the 10-day response window (11/14 to 11/24) during a holiday period (Thanksgiving) raises questions about whether the consumer was given a meaningful opportunity to respond, as required under the reasonable investigation standard.

**Statutory Authority:** If the November 14 letter was not actually mailed, the entire basis for denying the dispute collapses. The determination would be invalid under 12 CFR 1026.13(f), and the bank would be required to credit the disputed amount. TILA §130(a) damages apply.

## **VIOLATION 5: UDAAP \- Facilitation of Merchant Deception (12 USC §5531, §5536)**

The CFPB's 2023 UDAAP Examination Manual and the FTC's Policy Statement on Deception establish that an act or practice is deceptive if: (1) there is a representation or omission likely to mislead consumers, (2) the consumer's interpretation is reasonable under the circumstances, and (3) the representation or omission is material.

The merchant's own documents reveal the following deceptive conduct:

* The Consulting Agreement (the signed contract) contains NO refund or cancellation policy.

* The merchant admitted via text message that the refund policy was 'unintentionally omitted' from the agreement.

* The merchant then retroactively imposed 'no further refunds' language AFTER the consumer requested a downgrade.

* The merchant's representment to FIB claimed the transaction was 'non-refundable' \- directly contradicting the fact that a $7,500 refund was issued and that no refund policy existed in the contract.

**FIB accepted this contradictory merchant evidence without scrutiny and relied upon it to deny the consumer's billing error claim. Under UDAAP, an issuer that facilitates or ratifies deceptive merchant conduct by failing to conduct a reasonable investigation may itself be engaged in an unfair or deceptive act or practice.**

**Statutory Authority:** 12 USC §5565(c) \- Civil penalties: up to $5,000/day per violation; up to $25,000/day for reckless violations; up to $1,000,000/day for knowing violations. The CFPB has enforcement authority under 12 USC §5564 to pursue injunctive relief, restitution, and civil money penalties.

## **VIOLATION 6: Pattern of Non-Responsiveness to Regulatory Complaints**

Across five CFPB complaints and at least one DBFI complaint, FIB has issued substantially identical responses that do not substantively address the consumer's specific allegations. Each response letter contains the same language: 'all applicable policies and procedures were properly followed' without engaging with the documented deficiencies.

The 3/13/2026 Asay letter states: 'no further action will be taken, and no additional response will be provided by the Bank unless new information is received.' This is a refusal to engage with the regulatory process — particularly notable given that the consumer's complaints have raised new and distinct issues in each filing (documentation withholding, proof of mailing, UDAAP concerns, reasonable investigation deficiencies).

The Antillon letter to DBFI addresses only three narrow claims and does not address the 7-month documentation withholding, the proof-of-mailing issue, or the merchant's contradictory positions.

**Regulatory Concern:** Under 12 USC §5534, supervised entities are required to provide timely and substantive responses to CFPB complaints. A pattern of non-responsive or formulaic responses may constitute a failure to cooperate with CFPB supervisory processes. This can trigger enhanced supervisory review and enforcement priority.

## **VIOLATION 7: Credit Reporting Exposure (FCRA §1681s-2)**

If FIB reported the $10,500 disputed balance as delinquent or past due during or after the dispute process, this would constitute a violation of the FCRA's prohibition on reporting disputed amounts without notation. Under 15 USC §1681s-2(a)(3), a furnisher may not report information that the furnisher knows or has reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate.

The consumer's CFPB complaint requested confirmation that no adverse credit reporting occurred. FIB's responses have not addressed this item.

**Statutory Authority:** Under FCRA §1681n (willful noncompliance): statutory damages of $100 to $1,000 per violation; actual damages; punitive damages; attorney's fees. Under §1681o (negligent noncompliance): actual damages and attorney's fees.

# **6\. STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **A. CFPB Feedback Responses (Complaints 3 & 4\)**

Both complaints should receive substantive feedback responses that:

* Reject the company's response as non-responsive to the specific allegations.

* Identify each violation category with specific evidence from the bank's own response.

* Request supervisory escalation and examination of FIB's billing error procedures.

* Note the UDAAP implications of facilitating merchant deception.

* Reference the pattern across five complaints of substantively identical non-responses.

### **B. Hennepin County Small Claims (Highest Leverage Remaining Action)**

File in Hennepin County, MN conciliation court (limit $15,000). Claim TILA statutory damages ($5,000 per violation x multiple violations), actual damages ($10,500 \+ interest/fees), and attorney's fees. The bank's own documents provide the evidence. Key advantage: the bank must appear or default, and the internal contradictions in their documents are extremely difficult to explain to a judge.

### **C. DBFI Response**

Respond to the DBFI with the full violation analysis, requesting that the Division: (1) complete its investigation of FIB's dispute procedures, (2) issue findings regarding the documentation withholding, and (3) coordinate with the CFPB on enforcement if warranted.

### **D. FDIC Complaint**

If not already filed, submit a complaint to the FDIC (FIB's primary federal regulator as a state-chartered bank) focusing on the Reg Z violations and UDAAP concerns. The FDIC has independent examination authority.

# **7\. IMPORTANT NOTES**

*This analysis is based solely on FIB's own documents provided in their March 13, 2026 CFPB response, the consumer's CFPB complaint filings, and the applicable federal regulations. It is a working analysis document, not legal advice. The penalty figures represent maximum statutory exposure under each cited statute; actual recovery in litigation or regulatory action depends on the specific facts proven and the forum.*

*The 'No \- Card Type' designation is a significant issue that warrants dedicated analysis. If FIB's position is that Reg Z billing error protections do not apply to this card type (e.g., a business/commercial card), then the bank's decision to conduct an investigation, issue provisional credit, and engage the merchant was voluntary \- and the bank cannot then penalize the consumer for non-response to a process the bank chose to undertake outside its legal obligations.*
