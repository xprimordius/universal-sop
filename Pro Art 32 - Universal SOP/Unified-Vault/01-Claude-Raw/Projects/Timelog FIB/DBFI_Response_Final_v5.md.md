# **APPLICABLE LAW AND REGULATORY AUTHORITY**

# *The following federal and state laws, regulations, and enforcement authorities exist specifically to prevent the conduct documented in this case. Each is implicated by the evidence on file:*

# *Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 USC 1601 et seq. \- Enacted to protect consumers in credit transactions and ensure meaningful disclosure. TILA requires creditors to conduct fair billing error resolution procedures and provides consumers a private right of action for violations.*

# *Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.13 \- Implements TILA's billing error resolution requirements. Section 1026.13(a) requires the creditor to acknowledge and investigate billing error claims. Section 1026.13(c) establishes time limits and general procedures. Section 1026.13(f) requires that when the creditor determines no billing error occurred, it must conduct a reasonable investigation, mail its determination to the consumer, and upon request promptly provide copies of documentary evidence relied upon. FIB failed each of these requirements as documented in Binary Tests 1 through 7\.*

# *TILA Statutory Damages, 15 USC 1640(a) \- Provides individual statutory damages of not less than $500 and not more than $5,000 for credit card billing error violations, plus actual damages and mandatory attorney's fees. Multiple distinct violations have been identified.*

# *Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA), 15 USC 1666 \- Establishes the consumer's right to dispute billing errors and requires the creditor to investigate and resolve disputes fairly. The creditor may not restrict or close an account, report the amount as delinquent, or take collection action during the dispute period. The creditor must provide the consumer a meaningful opportunity to respond and must evaluate evidence from both parties.*

# *Consumer Financial Protection Act (CFPA), 12 USC 5531 and 5536 \- Prohibits unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices (UDAAP) by covered persons. FIB's acceptance of materially contradictory merchant evidence, reliance on an unsigned agreement, and misrepresentation of the consumer's billing error rights may constitute UDAAP violations. Civil penalties under 12 USC 5565(c) range up to $5,000 per day for violations, $25,000 per day for reckless violations, and $1,000,000 per day for knowing violations.*

# *Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 USC 1681 et seq. \- Prohibits furnishing information the furnisher knows or has reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate. If FIB reported the disputed $10,500 as delinquent during or after the dispute process, statutory damages of $100 to $1,000 per violation apply under 15 USC 1681n, plus actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees.*

# *Montana Consumer Protection Act, MCA 30-14-103 \- Prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in trade or commerce. The merchant's retroactive imposition of a "no refunds" policy after the transaction, and FIB's reliance on that retroactive policy, implicate this statute. The merchant's bait-and-switch from a DFY property acquisition to hourly consulting without consumer consent is a deceptive act under this provision.*

# *Montana Administrative Procedure Act, MCA 2-4-704(2) \- Provides for judicial review of agency action that is arbitrary, capricious, or characterized by abuse of discretion. Closing a regulatory complaint without reviewing submitted evidence or evaluating documented Reg Z violations may meet this standard.*

# *Montana Banking Code, MCA 32-1-211 \- Establishes DBFI's statutory duty to investigate regulated financial institutions' compliance with applicable law. This obligation requires independent evaluation \- not acceptance and relay of the institution's self-assessment.*

# *FTC Policy Statement on Deception (1983) \- Establishes that a representation or omission is deceptive if it is likely to mislead consumers acting reasonably under the circumstances and is material to the consumer's decision. The merchant's advertising of a DFY property while delivering only consulting hours meets all three elements. FIB's failure to evaluate this in its investigation is a failure of the reasonable investigation standard.*

# *CFPB Supervisory Guidance on Billing Error Resolution (CFPB Bulletin 2013-07 and Examination Manual) \- Establishes that creditors must conduct reasonable investigations that evaluate evidence from both parties, not merely accept the merchant's representment at face value. The creditor must provide the consumer a meaningful opportunity to respond, which requires actual receipt of correspondence and sufficient time to review and reply.*

# *Federal Reserve and FDIC Examination Procedures \- Require state banking regulators to obtain information from both the consumer and the institution, and to independently evaluate whether the institution's response adequately addresses the consumer's specific concerns. DBFI's closure based solely on FIB's self-assessment fails this standard.*

# *These laws exist to prevent exactly what happened here: a financial institution denying a consumer's legitimate billing error claim through procedural manipulation \- mailing time-sensitive correspondence to an invalid address, withholding evidence for seven months, accepting contradictory merchant claims without scrutiny, and then telling the regulator that all procedures were properly followed.*
