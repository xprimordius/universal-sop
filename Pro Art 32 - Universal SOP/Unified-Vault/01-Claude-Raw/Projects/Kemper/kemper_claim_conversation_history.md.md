**"Third-party" doesn't mean "distant." It means you have no contract with Kemper.**

The terminology is counterintuitive. Here's the structure:

## Who's who in insurance language

| Party | Definition | In your situation |
|---|---|---|
| **First party** | The policyholder — the person who pays premiums to the insurer and has the contract | **The at-fault driver who hit you** |
| **Second party** | The insurer | **Kemper** |
| **Third party** | Anyone OTHER than the policyholder who is making a claim against the policy | **You** |

You ARE directly involved with Kemper. They ARE dealing with you directly. But you have **no contract with Kemper**. The at-fault driver does. Your right to recover from Kemper exists only because the at-fault driver injured your property, and Kemper insures the at-fault driver's liability.

## Why this matters legally

| First-party claimant has | Third-party claimant has |
|---|---|
| Contract with the insurer (the policy) | No contract — only tort claim against the at-fault driver |
| Right to sue insurer for bad faith under § 604.18 | No bad faith claim under § 604.18 (expressly excluded) |
| Right to consequential contract damages | Tort damages only (against the driver, paid by their insurer) |
| Insurer owes duty of good faith directly to claimant | Insurer owes duty of good faith to its INSURED, not to you |

The MN Legislature deliberately excluded third-party claimants from § 604.18 when they passed it in 2008 — that's why it doesn't apply to you, no matter how directly Kemper is mishandling things.

## What this changes practically

| What you keep | What you lose |
|---|---|
| All § 72A.201 statutory protections (still apply to "insured **or claimant**") | § 604.18 bad faith remedies ($250K + $100K attorney fees) |
| Right to file Commerce Dept complaint | Right to sue Kemper directly for bad faith |
| Tort recovery against the at-fault driver (Kemper pays as their insurer) | Direct breach-of-contract action against Kemper |
| *Short v. Dairyland* good-faith duty in third-party handling | Most bad-faith leverage tools |
| Right to choose repair shop (Subd. 6(7)) | — |

So your recovery channels are still solid — they just look different from what Gemini was describing.

## The strategic angle worth checking

**Do you have your own auto insurance with collision coverage?**

If yes, you have a **first-party option you might not have used**:

1. File the claim under YOUR collision coverage instead
2. Your insurer fixes the car fast (typical 30–45 days, not 7 months)
3. You pay your deductible
4. Your insurer then **subrogates** (pursues Kemper directly to recover the cost)
5. When your insurer wins subrogation, you get your deductible back

This is the standard play when the at-fault carrier is dragging. Your own insurer:
- Has a contract obligation to you (faster service)

- Is incentivized to recover the full cost from Kemper

**You'd lose:** Maybe the Miami consequentials angle (your insurer covers your direct damage, not all your consequentials)
