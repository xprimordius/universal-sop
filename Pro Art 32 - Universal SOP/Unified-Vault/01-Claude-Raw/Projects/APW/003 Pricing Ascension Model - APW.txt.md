Kai Bax
• 19m
If you want to go down the trial route, I would recommend focusing only on the "strategy" / "general pathway" but don't include the "how".
For example, you could focus the trial on the high-level process of what they would need to do in order to get self funded etc but you wouldn't include the bank lists or specific ways on HOW to actually get the money. That would be saved for the back end. It's kind of like test driving a car, the trial should give the client a feel of the car but if they actually want to take it home and live with the car, they need to buy it. Similar here. The trial should give them a feeling of the general approach, but based on only what they learn in the trial, they should not be able to go and secure the funding which, by default, limits the "risk" of clients taking what they learn and implementing themselves (they can't).
The ascension should then fully focus on execution and actually helping them get the money into their bank account.
The difference should be high-level theory (wet their palate but nothing actionable) - that's the trial. The ascension would be full execution mode and physically getting them the money.
As for the "guarantee" in the trial period, there would be no performance based guarantee since the timeframe is too short to guarantee any tangible, meaningful result in your case. Plus you don't "need" it either. It can either be a "happiness" based guarantee (similar to how we do the "love it or leave it" guarantee) or you could even run no guarantee on the trial. If you run no guarantee on the trial, the "sell" (or the reason why there's no guarantee) IS the trial. I.e They can "test it" for only "X" before they fully commit. That IS the offer for the trial (meaning you don't need an external guarantee if you don't want to use one).
Again, just a reminder as we've spoken about previously, do not get caught up in too many details here. Pick a route and run with it. The longer you spend in this "information overload" stage, the less results you're going to see.
