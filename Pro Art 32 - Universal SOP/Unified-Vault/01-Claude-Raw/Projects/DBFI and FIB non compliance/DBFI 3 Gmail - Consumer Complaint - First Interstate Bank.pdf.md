Dear Mr. Nguyen:
The Division of Banking and Financial Institutions (Division) has received a response from First Interstate Bank (FIB)
regarding your complaint. A copy of the response is attached for your review.
It is the Division’s understanding that this complaint stems from your concerns regarding FIB’s handling of your credit card
billing dispute and your allegation that the bank failed to conduct a reasonable investigation and violated Regulation Z, 12
CFR 1026.13, and UDAAP standards in connection with a “services not rendered” dispute for a Done‑For‑You Airbnb
service.
Your first concern as described in your complaint is that FIB failed to conduct a reasonable investigation under Regulation
Z and improperly denied your services‑not‑rendered billing‑error claim despite your position that no Airbnb property was
acquired, no listing went live, no revenue was generated, and the merchant’s partial refund acknowledged non‑delivery.
Be advised that, according to the bank’s response and supporting timeline, the bank opened your dispute, requested
additional information from you on August 20, 2025, received your response on September 5, 2025, obtained the
merchant’s representment on October 30, 2025, and issued its decision within applicable time frames, documenting that it
reviewed both your materials and the merchant’s evidence (including claimed service hours, property analyses, and
access to software tools) before concluding that a billing error did not occur. Regulation Z requires a timely investigation,
written explanation, and, upon request, access to documentation; it does not require the issuer to reach a particular
substantive outcome or to resolve contractual disputes over the scope or quality of services in the consumer’s favor.
Your second concern as described in your complaint is that FIB failed to provide proper notice by relying solely on
physical mail, including a November 14, 2025 “need more information” or rebuttal‑response letter you state you never
received, and that the bank’s use of mail and internal deadlines resulted in a constructive denial of your rights under
Regulation Z and constituted unfair or abusive practices. Be advised that the bank’s response indicates that all
dispute‑related correspondence was mailed to your address of record and that you had previously received other dispute
letters at that address, and there is no indication that mail was returned as undeliverable or sent outside the regulatory
investigation window. Regulation Z permits issuers to provide required notices by mail to the consumer’s last known
address and does not mandate electronic delivery or require reopening a dispute solely because a consumer later asserts
non‑receipt of a particular letter, absent evidence that the issuer failed to send notice in a timely manner or to the proper
address.
After a thorough review of the information provided by you and by FIB, including the dispute timeline, correspondence,
and the statutory provisions you cited, the Division found no violation of statute or rule and will consider the complaint
closed unless additional information can be provided that would warrant a further review.
This complaint process is a service that is provided to Montana consumers engaging in business with entities under the
Division’s licensing and regulation. Information provided within this letter is specifically related to an individual consumer
complaint and should not be construed as either a legal opinion of the Division or a regulatory action. If you are not
satisfied with the resolution of your complaint, you may wish to consult legal counsel so as to preserve your rights.
