# Tony Robbins Lifeforce Peak Rest timing and melatonin concerns

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The person asked about Tony Robbins' Lifeforce Peak Rest supplement, specifically whether taking it at 5 AM before sleeping until 10 AM would cause grogginess due to its melatonin content. Claude explained that the supplement contains only 300 mcg (0.3 mg) of melatonin total — a micro-dose well below typical OTC supplements — and that at this dose, melatonin clears quickly enough that it would not cause noticeable grogginess after a five-hour sleep window. Claude also noted that the other ingredients in the formula (glycine, L-theanine, magnesium glycinate, GABA) support sleep quality without causing morning sedation.
> 
> The person then raised a concern about circadian rhythm disruption from taking melatonin at 5 AM when daylight is beginning. Claude explained that exogenous melatonin acts as a circadian time cue signaling "nighttime," and that taking it at 5 AM could phase-delay the clock — which would be congruent with the person's then-current noon wake schedule but counterproductive if they wanted to shift earlier.
> 
> The person confirmed a goal of shifting to a 6 AM wake schedule. Claude provided a full protocol: taking Peak Rest around 9 PM targeting a 10 PM bedtime, gradually walking back wake time by 30–60 minutes every two to three days rather than forcing bedtime, using morning bright light exposure as the primary circadian reset signal, and pulling back stimulant use cutoffs earlier as the target bedtime advances. Claude estimated a two-to-three week realistic timeline for the full shift and noted that the non-melatonin ingredients in Peak Rest would support sleep quality throughout the transition.

**Short answer: You're fine. Take it.**

The total melatonin is only **300 mcg (0.3 mg)** — that's a micro-dose. For reference, most OTC melatonin pills are 3–10 mg. This is 10–30x less than that. Research actually shows 0.3 mg is closer to the physiologically ideal dose for sleep onset without next-day hangover.

**5 hours of sleep window (5 AM → 10 AM):**

The melatonin at this dose clears fast — half-life is ~40 min. Even the MicroActive (extended-release) portion is only 150 mcg. You won't feel melatonin grogginess at 10 AM from this.

**The other ingredients are actually the bigger win here.** Glycine (3g), L-Theanine (200mg), magnesium glycinate (120mg), and GABA (100mg) all support deep sleep quality in a compressed window. They don't cause morning sedation — they help you get more restorative sleep per hour.

**Bottom line:** This stack is designed to improve sleep *quality*, not knock you out for 8+ hours. At 300 mcg melatonin with a 5-hour window, you'll wake up cleaner than if you took nothing and slept poorly for the same 5 hours.

One note — if you're taking dextroamphetamine earlier that day, Peak Rest is actually a solid counterbalance for winding down.

>

**Valid concern. Here's the real deal:**

Yes — exogenous melatonin does act as a circadian time cue. It tells your brain "it's nighttime now." Taking it at 5 AM technically signals darkness right when the sun is rising, which *can* phase-delay your clock (push your natural sleep/wake cycle later).

**But here's your context:**

You're already on a ~noon wake schedule. Your endogenous melatonin is probably peaking around 4–6 AM anyway. So 5 AM dosing is actually *aligned* with your current rhythm — not fighting it.

**The real question is: do you want to stay on a noon schedule or shift earlier?**

If **staying ~noon wake** — Peak Rest at 5 AM is perfectly congruent. No conflict.

If **trying to shift earlier** (say, 8–9 AM wake) — then taking melatonin at 5 AM works *against* that goal. You'd want to take it around 1–2 AM instead, and combine with morning bright light exposure right at your target wake time.

**At 0.3 mg though**, the phase-shifting effect is mild compared to the 3–10 mg doses used in jet lag protocols. The sleep quality ingredients (glycine, theanine, mag, GABA) have zero circadian impact.

**Net:** If you're not actively trying to shift your wake time earlier, take it at 5 AM with zero worry. If you *are* trying to shift earlier, the melatonin timing matters more than the dose.

**The shift protocol (noon → 6 AM):**

1. **Move wake time back 30–60 min every 2–3 days** — don't force the bedtime, force the wake time. The bedtime follows.

4. **Hard cutoff on dextroamphetamine by early afternoon** — you're already doing 2:30–3 PM cutoff, which is good. As bedtime moves earlier, you may need to pull that back to 1–2 PM.
5. **No bright screens after 8 PM** once you're near target schedule.

**Realistic timeline:** ~2–3 weeks to fully shift from noon to 6 AM if you're consistent.

The glycine/theanine/mag/GABA in Peak Rest will actually help a lot during the transition — they improve sleep quality even when your body is adjusting.
