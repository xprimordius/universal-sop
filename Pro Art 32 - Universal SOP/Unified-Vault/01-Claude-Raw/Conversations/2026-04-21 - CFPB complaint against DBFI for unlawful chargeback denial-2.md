## Caveats (honoring your accuracy protocol)

- **Legal conclusions vs. facts:** Throughout, I've framed conclusions as "consistent with," "appears to constitute," or "under the doctrine of" rather than asserting final legal adjudication. Merchant fraud is alleged and well-documented, but not yet adjudicated.
- **Tacit acquiescence strength:** The doctrine is most powerful in contract law and in contexts of fiduciary/regulatory duty. Applying it to binary compliance questions is strategically strong and rhetorically unimpeachable, but not a statutorily codified admission. I've framed it as "offered into the record as an admitted fact" — assertive but not overclaimed.
- **TILA damages/fees:** TILA §130 provides for statutory damages and attorney's fees in civil actions. Administrative complaints don't directly award these; they're preserved for litigation. I've framed the relief accordingly.
- **Character limits:** CFPB's portal narrative field accepts roughly 7,500 characters. The condensed version fits. Verify once more when pasting.
