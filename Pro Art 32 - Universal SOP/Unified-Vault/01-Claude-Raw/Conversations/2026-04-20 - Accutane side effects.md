**Short answer: Usually yes, but not always 100%.**

Isotretinoin (Accutane) is the most effective acne treatment available and works on essentially every type — cystic, nodular, inflammatory, and comedonal. It hits acne through four mechanisms at once: shrinks sebaceous glands, cuts oil production, normalizes skin cell turnover, and reduces *C. acnes* bacteria and inflammation.

Most patients see dramatic or complete clearance by the end of a standard course (typically 5–7 months, dosed to a cumulative target). A meaningful minority relapse and need a second course, and a smaller percentage have partial or incomplete response — often tied to cumulative dose being too low, hormonal drivers (especially in women with PCOS or androgen-driven acne), or non-acne conditions that mimic acne (rosacea, folliculitis, fungal acne) that won't respond.

General information, not medical advice — a dermatologist can tell you how likely it is to clear *your* specific acne based on type, severity, and history.

**Most likely: a yeast infection (vaginal candidiasis) triggered by the antibiotic.**

Doxycycline — like most broad-spectrum antibiotics — kills off the protective *Lactobacillus* bacteria in the vagina along with whatever it's targeting. That disruption lets *Candida* yeast overgrow, which commonly causes:

- Thick, white, clumpy discharge (often described as cottage-cheese-like)
- Itching or burning
- Redness or irritation
- Usually no strong odor

This is one of the most common side effects of any antibiotic course in people with vaginas, and doxycycline is a frequent offender.

**What typically helps:**
- Over-the-counter antifungals (miconazole, clotrimazole — 1-day, 3-day, or 7-day options)
- Single-dose oral fluconazole (prescription)
- Probiotics during/after antibiotic course may reduce recurrence

**See a doctor if:**
- Discharge has a strong fishy odor (points to bacterial vaginosis, not yeast — different treatment)
- Discharge is gray, green, yellow, or frothy (possible BV or trichomoniasis)
- You have fever, pelvic pain, or it's your first-ever yeast infection (worth confirming the diagnosis)
- Symptoms don't clear with OTC treatment

General information, not medical advice. If you're unsure what it is, a quick telehealth visit or urgent care can confirm with an exam or swab and get you the right treatment same-day.
