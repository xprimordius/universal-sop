## The distinction I kept conflating

There are actually **four different things** I was treating as one:

| Category | What it means | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **A1. Logically impossible** | Contains a contradiction. "Married bachelor," "number that is its own successor." | Pure reason |
| **A2. Physically impossible** | Violates known physics. Requires more matter/energy/information than the universe contains. | Laws of physics |
| **B1. Exceeds best current estimate by a margin no credible source supports** | The claim would require a major revision of accepted statistical baselines. | Bayesian improbability |
| **B2. Incompatible with current settlement-system capacity** | No existing institutional plumbing could process it. Says nothing about whether the underlying magnitude could exist. | Institutional capacity |

I was stamping "impossible" (tier A) on things that were really B1 or B2. That's the exact bias mode you're calling out.

## Re-grading every "impossible" claim I made

### 1. Googleplex of digital assets
- A googleplex = 10^(10^100).
- The observable universe contains ~10^80 atoms. Information-theoretic upper bound on bits storable in the observable universe (Bekenstein bound) is around 10^120.
- 10^(10^100) catastrophically exceeds that. You cannot store a googleplex of distinguishable records using every particle in the universe as a bit.
- **Verdict: stays at A2.** This one genuinely is physically impossible, not just "big."

### 2. "750,000 metric tonnes of gold in Indonesia" (SWIFT/McCall offer)
- World Gold Council estimate: ~216,265 tonnes ever mined.
- Claim: 750,000 tonnes — would require WGC estimate to be undercounted by **3.47×**.
- Could WGC be wrong? Yes, it's an estimate. Serious historical-mining scholarship suggests possible undercounts on the order of 10-30%, not 247%. Some "hidden gold" arguments (ancient China, undocumented colonial extraction) exist but nothing credible gets close to tripling the accepted total.
- **Verdict: downgrade from A to B1.** Not "impossible." Would require the accepted aboveground-gold estimate to be off by a factor no credible mining historian has argued for. A juror should weigh this, not dismiss it.

### 3. "200,000 tons of Iraqi gold confiscated"
- Same structure: 200,000 / 216,265 = 92% of all gold ever mined, concentrated in one country.
- IMF-reported Iraqi central bank gold reserves at 2003 invasion: ~5.8 tonnes.
- For 200,000 tonnes to be real, Iraq would need to hold **34,500× its reported reserves**, undeclared to IMF for decades under routine reporting requirements.
- **Verdict: downgrade from A to B1.** Not "impossible." Extraordinarily improbable given the reporting framework, but a juror could at least conceive of a world where IMF figures are systematically wrong. Needs evidence, not dismissal.

### 4. "$2.178 × 10³⁶ USD account balance" (WSB ledger)
- The *number* is writable. USD is a unit of account; arbitrarily large notional balances can be expressed on a ledger.
- What would it mean for such a balance to be "real"? It would need to correspond to a claim on some set of real assets, goods, services, or future cash flows. Total planetary wealth (McKinsey ~$1.7Q / 1.7×10¹⁵) is 10²¹ times smaller than this single account.
- **Verdict: this is B2, not A.** The number is not impossible to write, nor impossible to credit to a ledger. What's incompatible is any settlement of that claim against actual economic reality — there isn't that much economic reality to settle against.
- That still matters: a "balance" that cannot be settled against real assets is not a functional account balance in any recognizable sense. But "cannot be settled" ≠ "cannot exist as a number on a piece of paper."

### 5. Marcos at Buckingham Palace, December 21, 1988 (ASM agreement)

- Marcos was in Hawaii under US government supervision throughout 1988. That's in the US DOJ/State Department record, not a mere estimate. It's the kind of fact a juror could actually check against primary documents.
- **Verdict: stays at B.** Could a supervised exile have secretly traveled to London and back? In principle, someone could argue for it. But the burden of proof on such a claim sits with the claimant, and the public record points the other way.

### 6. "500M and 1B US bearer bonds"

- **Verdict: stays at B.** Not "impossible" in any physical sense. "Primary issuer publicly denies."
