## 🤖 Claude

Yeah, that's frustrating—you're paying premium prices and getting bare-minimum responsiveness. Let me break down what you'd actually need operationally to deliver the service level you're describing.

For a true daily, multi-touch account management model with dedicated onboarding, you're looking at staffing that's genuinely lean but present. You'd need one full-time onboarding specialist—someone whose sole job is intake, orientation, getting clients ramped smoothly. Then separately, one account manager per maybe eight to twelve active clients, depending on complexity. That person handles daily check-ins, problem escalation, quick turnaround on questions.

Here's the operational reality: at ten to twenty thousand dollars per client, you need actual human availability. Real account managers aren't juggling thirty clients. They're managing a tight roster so they can respond within hours, not days. They're proactive—spotting friction before clients complain. They're taking notes, remembering context, not asking you to repeat yourself.

The places you paid and got let down? They were probably treating those roles as outsourced, high-touch theatre—hiring cheap labor, minimal training, no accountability structure. No daily performance metrics on response time. No consequence for missing what you actually said.

If you're building APW with this service model, the math is simple: one onboarding manager covering intake and first week, then account managers at a four to one ratio minimum per cohort. That's your non-negotiable operating cost if you want to genuinely deliver. Anything less is just saying you offer it without actually resourcing it.

What's your vision—is this a core differentiator for APW, or more of a nice-to-have?
