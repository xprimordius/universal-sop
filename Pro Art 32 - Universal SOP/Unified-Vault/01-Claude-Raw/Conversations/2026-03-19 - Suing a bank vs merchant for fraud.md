## Forum Selection Clause — Challengeable on Multiple Grounds

---

### The Merchant's Argument

He's citing **browsewrap doctrine** — the idea that using a service = agreeing to its terms, even without a signature. Texas courts have upheld this *in some cases*, but it's **not automatic and has hard requirements**.

---

### Where It Falls Apart

| Challenge | Strength |
|---|---|
| **No actual/constructive notice** | If terms weren't prominently displayed before you engaged, browsewrap is unenforceable — even in Texas |
| **Fraud voids the contract** | You can't enforce a clause *inside* a contract that was induced by fraud. The forum clause goes down with the fraudulent contract. |
| **Unconscionability** | Buried forum clause in a consumer context = strong unconscionability argument, especially cross-state |
| **You're in Minnesota** | MN courts are not required to enforce TX forum clauses if enforcement is unreasonable or against MN public policy |
| **You never signed** | Mere participation only holds if terms were clearly presented *before* participation — if not, no meeting of the minds |

---

### The Fraud Exception Is Your Best Weapon

Courts consistently hold:

> **A forum selection clause cannot be used to shield a fraudulent transaction.** If the contract itself was procured by misrepresentation, the clause is unenforceable.

This is especially true for bait-and-switch and fraudulent inducement claims — the entire agreement is voidable, not just the bad terms.

---

### Practical Reality

- **Small claims court in Minnesota** — just file there. Force HIM to object and prove TX jurisdiction applies. Most small-claims merchants won't fly to MN to contest venue.
- If he tries to get it dismissed on venue grounds, you counter with fraud exception + lack of proper notice of terms.
- His text admitting the omitted refund policy **directly undermines** any claim that you had full disclosure of terms.

---

### Bottom Line

His argument has surface-level legitimacy but breaks down under scrutiny — especially with fraud in play. File in MN small claims. Make him come to you.
