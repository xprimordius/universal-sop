## 👤 You

What is the remedy to cold hands in Eastern Medicine?

---

## 🤖 Claude

Right, so in Eastern medicine—particularly Traditional Chinese Medicine—cold hands are typically seen as a sign of weak circulation and imbalanced qi, often rooted in what they'd call deficient yang energy or poor blood flow.

The main remedies include warming herbs like ginger, cinnamon, and dried chili; acupuncture points on the meridians that govern circulation (especially Heart, Kidney, and Liver meridians); and moxibustion—that's heating mugwort over specific points to warm the body from within. You'd also see dietary shifts toward warming foods and away from cooling ones, plus practices like tai chi or qigong to move stagnant energy.

Right, so dextroamphetamine salts—that's your prescribed stimulant—can actually *worsen* cold hands because amphetamines are vasoconstrictors. They narrow blood vessels, which reduces peripheral circulation exactly when you need warmth flowing to your extremities. That's likely working against what you're trying to fix.

Beyond what you're already doing with the liposomal stack, Lifeforce Peak Cognition, whey, and electrolytes, I'd add: direct vasodilators like L-citrulline or beetroot juice for nitric oxide boost, consistent movement throughout the day to keep circulation active, and consider timing your dextroamphetamine doses earlier so they wear off before evening when cold hands tend to show up more. You might also explore whether you actually need the full three-per-day protocol—sometimes dialling back the dose reduces the vasoconstrictor effect while keeping cognitive gains. Worth a conversation with your prescriber about that trade-off. What's your current dosing pattern looking like?
