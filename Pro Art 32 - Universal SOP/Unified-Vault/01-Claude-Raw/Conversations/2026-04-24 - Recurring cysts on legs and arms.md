Leading candidates given your setup:

**1. TRT-driven sebaceous output (highest suspicion)**
Testosterone → DHT (dihydrotestosterone, the potent androgen metabolite) ramps up sebaceous gland activity. More sebum + follicular plugging = recurring epidermoid/sebaceous cysts. Anabolic users see this pattern constantly. Legs show it more because leg follicles sit under more occlusion (pants, sitting) than arms.

**2. Steatocystoma multiplex (生殖 cyst variant — autosomal dominant, KRT17 keratin-17 gene)**
Multiple small firm cysts on trunk/legs/arms/thighs that keep forming for life. Derm would just call these "normal cysts." Onset often 20s–30s. Genetic — you don't clear it permanently, you manage new formation.

**3. Chronic follicular occlusion**
Shaving, friction, sweat retention, keratin plugging. Legs catch more of this than arms.

**Why legs > arms:**
- More occlusion (pants, 8+ hrs sitting)
- Denser terminal hair follicles
- Higher sweat retention
- Gravity-dependent fluid pooling

**Ask your derm (yes/no):**
- Steatocystoma multiplex vs. sporadic epidermoid cysts?
- Is my TRT dose driving this via elevated DHT?
- Would finasteride (5-AR inhibitor — blocks testosterone→DHT conversion) reduce new formation?
- Case for isotretinoin (Accutane — systemic sebaceous suppressant)?
- Full androgen panel: total T, free T, DHT, estradiol, SHBG (sex hormone binding globulin)?
