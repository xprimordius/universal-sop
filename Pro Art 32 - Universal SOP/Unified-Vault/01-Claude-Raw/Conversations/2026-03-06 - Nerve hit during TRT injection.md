# Nerve hit during TRT injection

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
>

## 👤 You

When I was injecting TRT today I went more towards the outer middle of my leg vs the outer upper middle of my leg, and everything was fine, until I let go of my hand that was stretching the skin out for the needle to go in. Then came a sharp sensitive pain that would not go away. Aspirating was good, but any micro movement hurt and even slow injection still was sharp and painful. Once I used my hand to stretch out the skin again the pain relieved, and then I finished injecting as normal. Pulling out was more painful then normal, with just a little bit of blood slowly came out to form a small to medium sized bubble

What was that? What happened

---

## 🤖 Claude

What you likely hit was a **sensory nerve branch** — here's what happened step by step:

**The core issue: needle placement hit a nerve**

Going lower and more toward the middle of the outer thigh moves you closer to where branches of the **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** and other sensory nerve branches run. These are the nerves responsible for skin sensation in that area.

**Why stretching the skin relieved the pain**

When you used your hand to stretch the skin, you were essentially doing what's called a **Z-track technique** — it shifts the tissue laterally, which physically moves the nerve branch slightly away from the needle tip. That's why the pain disappeared the moment you re-stretched. The needle hadn't moved, but the tissue around it had, creating just enough space between needle and nerve.

**Why aspiration was clean**

Good news — no vascular involvement. The pain was purely neurological, not from hitting a vessel. The small blood bubble on withdrawal was just minor capillary bleeding from the needle path, which is normal.

**Why withdrawal hurt more**

On the way out, the skin recoils back to its natural position, pulling nerve tissue back toward the needle track right as it exits — brief but sharp.

**The small blood bubble**

Totally benign. That's subcutaneous capillary bleed. Apply light pressure for 30–60 seconds next time and it won't pool.

---

**Going forward:**

Your normal upper-outer zone (vastus lateralis, upper third of the outer thigh) is safer because nerve density is lower there. If you ever go slightly lower again, maintain skin stretch throughout the entire injection *and* withdrawal. Some guys permanently adopt Z-track for this reason — it minimizes nerve contact and reduces post-injection soreness in general.

You didn't damage anything. Nerves don't like being poked but they recover quickly from a single needle graze.
