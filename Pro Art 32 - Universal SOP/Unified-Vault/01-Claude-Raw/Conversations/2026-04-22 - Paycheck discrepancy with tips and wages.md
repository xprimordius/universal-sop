**Why that breaks down:**

1. **The 03/20/26 period is the one where she worked 9 out of 9 scheduled days** — the *most* hours of any period on his list. Paying that period at the same flat $1,568.08 as periods where she worked 1 day out of 8 is mathematically indefensible. The insurance average was designed to cover *missed* work, not to cap *actual* work.

2. **$1,568.08 gross ÷ $30/hr ≈ 52.3 hrs of implied pay** — but only if you assume zero tips and zero ETs inside that number. His breakdown says tips were $660 for that period. If tips are inside the $1,568.08, then hourly = $1,568.08 − $660 − $45 (ETs) = **$863.08, or 28.8 hrs at $30** — when she logged 43.5.

3. **Tips cannot lawfully be absorbed into a flat wage figure without itemization.** Under the FLSA, tips are the property of the employee and must be reported separately (W-2 Box 7). Rolling them into "Shutdown Pay" isn't a formatting preference — it changes the tax character of the money and affects her Social Security tip reporting and any tip-related deductions.
