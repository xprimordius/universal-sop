## Short answer

**Yes, depreciation is baked into the standard mileage rate.** That's why you don't see a separate depreciation line. You can't claim both.

## What's happening on your Schedule C

Line 9 shows **$7,662 in car/truck expenses**. Given your numbers, that looks like the **standard mileage method** (2025 rate: 70¢/mile → roughly 10,946 business miles).

The IRS standard mileage rate already includes:
- Depreciation
- Gas
- Maintenance & repairs
- Insurance
- Tires
- Registration

So adding a separate depreciation entry would be double-dipping and is not allowed.

## The two methods (you pick one)

| | Standard Mileage | Actual Expenses |
|---|---|---|
| **What you track** | Business miles only | Every receipt + total miles for business % |
| **Depreciation** | Built into the rate | Claimed separately (Form 4562) |
| **Gas, repairs, insurance** | Built in | Claimed separately, prorated by business use % |
| **Best for** | Fuel-efficient cars, high mileage | Expensive vehicles, low MPG, heavy repair costs |

**Important rule:** If you want to use standard mileage, you generally must choose it the **first year** the vehicle is placed in service for business. If you used actual expenses year one, you're locked into actual going forward for that vehicle. If you used standard year one, you can switch between methods later (with some restrictions on depreciation recapture).

- If you pick **actual expenses** → it will walk you through gas, insurance, repairs, lease payments, **and** a separate depreciation section (basis, date placed in service, business use %, MACRS/Section 179/bonus depreciation options).

## The lock-in rule (critical)

**Year 1 choice determines your options forever:**

| Year 1 method | Can you switch later? |
|---|---|
| **Standard mileage** | ✅ Yes — can switch to actual in any later year (but depreciation must use straight-line going forward, not MACRS) |
| **Actual expenses** | ❌ No — locked into actual for the life of that vehicle |

**Translation:** If 2025 is **not** the first year you used the RS4 for business, I need to know what method you used the very first year before we go further.

## Why "depreciated more" alone doesn't mean actual wins

Two different concepts get conflated here:

1. **Market depreciation** (what the car is worth now vs. what you paid) — this is what you're feeling.
2. **Tax depreciation** (what the IRS lets you deduct each year based on basis × business use % × MACRS schedule) — this is what actually hits your return.

Tax depreciation is capped hard for passenger vehicles by **luxury auto limits** (Section 280F). For a vehicle placed in service in 2025, the caps are roughly:

- Year 1: ~$12,400 (with bonus depreciation) or ~$20,400
- Year 2: ~$19,800
- Year 3: ~$11,900
- Year 4+: ~$7,160/year

These caps apply to passenger autos under 6,000 lbs GVWR. The **RS4 is under 6,000 lbs**, so it's subject to these limits. You can't just deduct "the car lost $25K in value this year" — the IRS caps it.

I should verify the exact 2025 caps before you file — they adjust annually for inflation. Want me to pull the current IRS figures?

## Critical second issue: §30D vs §45W

Here's something most people miss, and I want to flag it because it's not fully settled in your situation without more info:

- **§30D Clean Vehicle Credit ($7,500)** = consumer credit, for personal-use or mixed-use vehicles
- **§45W Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (up to $7,500)** = business credit, for vehicles used in a trade or business

**You cannot claim both on the same vehicle.** And the rules around which one applies to a vehicle used partly for personal and partly for Schedule C business are nuanced. Generally:

- If the vehicle is **predominantly personal** with some business use → §30D consumer credit, basis reduced by credit, then you depreciate the business-use portion
- If the vehicle is **predominantly business** → §45W may apply instead
