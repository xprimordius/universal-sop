# Responding to Regulation Z investigation letter

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The user is engaged in an ongoing consumer financial dispute against First Interstate Bank (FIB) related to a credit card chargeback for a "Done-For-You Airbnb" service where no property was delivered. The user has been navigating complaints through the CFPB portal and the Montana Division of Banking and Financial Institutions (DBFI), corresponding with Faith Franklin, a paralegal at the Division. The user had already drafted a detailed response email to Faith Franklin and asked Claude to help add on to that existing draft — specifically to address a Reg Z quote Faith cited in her letter stating that Reg Z does not require issuers to reach a particular substantive outcome.
> 
> Claude produced an add-on section to append to the user's existing email, structured around three arguments: that FIB violated Reg Z §1026.13(f) by refusing to provide merchant documentation upon request despite multiple verbal and written requests; that FIB's sub-7-day response window imposed on a traveling consumer — while granting the merchant 60–90+ days — violated the reasonable investigation standard; and that FIB's "need more information" letter failed to specify what information was needed despite substantial evidence already submitted. The add-on was framed to concede the accuracy of Faith's Reg Z citation while pivoting to the specific statutory obligations FIB did fail, rather than arguing against the quote directly.
> 
> The user's communication style in this context is assertive and detailed, with a strong command of the factual record including certified mail receipts, CFPB portal submissions, call logs, and merchant marketing materials. The user's key clarification at the end of the conversation was that they wanted an addition to their existing draft, not a full replacement — an important distinction Claude had not initially honored in a prior response.

Regarding your statement that *"Regulation Z requires a timely investigation, written explanation, and, upon request, access to documentation; it does not require the issuer to reach a particular substantive outcome or to resolve contractual disputes over the scope or quality of services in the consumer's favor"* —

I agree with that statement as written. However, it does not exonerate FIB, because FIB has failed the very requirements Reg Z *does* impose:

**1. Access to documentation upon request — directly violated.**
Reg Z §1026.13(f) requires the issuer to provide documentation upon the consumer's request. I have requested merchant documentation repeatedly — verbally on recorded calls, in writing, and through the CFPB portal. FIB has refused every time. This is not a gray area. This is a named, specific statutory obligation FIB has willfully not fulfilled.

**2. Timely investigation — violated by procedural manipulation.**
FIB issued a denial based on a sub-7-day alleged response window while I was traveling — travel FIB had on record through multiple card-unlock calls, recordings, and their own fraud monitoring system. The merchant received 60–90+ days. I received fewer than 7, with no verified proof the letter was ever sent or received. That is not a timely and reasonable process — it is a procedural trap.

**3. Written explanation — incomplete and evasive.**
FIB's "need more information" letter did not specify what information was needed, despite me having already submitted substantial documented evidence. A written explanation that requests unspecified information while ignoring existing submitted evidence does not satisfy the spirit or letter of Reg Z's written explanation requirement.

This dispute is not about FIB being required to rule in my favor. It is about FIB being required to actually conduct the investigation Reg Z mandates — using the evidence provided, supplying documentation as required, and affording the consumer a reasonable opportunity to respond. FIB has failed all three.
