# No-doc funding roadmap to $500K-$1M

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The person is building a comprehensive no-doc business funding playbook for what appears to be a financial education or consulting business (Ascend Prime Wealth, ascendprimewealth.com). The conversation centered on designing and iterating a visual operational guide explaining a wave-based business credit funding strategy targeting a range from several hundred thousand dollars to over one million dollars across multiple entities and institutions.
> 
> The core strategy involves stacking business credit cards across national banks, mid-tier lenders, ELAN-network institutions, and a foreign filing/Florida domicile approach, all powered by a three-entity circular payment system that generates automated deposit activity for bank seasoning. A critical rule throughout is keeping the personal guarantee profile completely untouched by using business cards only, which enables infinite entity scaling. The person explained that touching the personal profile halts both personal and business funding for extended periods, whereas keeping it pristine allows continuous funding of new entities indefinitely.
> 
> The conversation evolved significantly through multiple iterations. Early versions were criticized as too static, too text-heavy, and not intuitive enough for beginners. The person progressively clarified the wave-based application mechanics (up to nine hard inquiries per wave across three bureaus, with rounds of one pull per bureau escalating to three per bureau based on results), the inquiry removal and cooldown cycle between waves, a critical thirty-day reporting window after first approvals, and two parallel funding tracks: Group 1 for immediate fast-start funding on a primary entity in Month 1, and Group 2 for a secondary entity requiring eight to twelve weeks of seasoning before entering the same wave-round funding sequence. A parallel Florida domicile track opens one new bank account per week from Week 1, targeting eight institutions at roughly fifty thousand dollars each. Bureau pull mapping was clarified with Experian shown in blue, TransUnion in yellow, and Equifax in crimson, with example institution-to-bureau mappings noted as geography-dependent and requiring individual research before execution. The final deliverable was a four-page PDF playbook with visual flow diagrams, decision gates, round-by-round left-to-right flows with arrows, a Gantt-style seasoning timeline, parallel track visualization, institution reference table, and cumulative milestone totals. The person consistently pushed for more visual elements, icons, and reduced text density to make the material accessible to both beginners and experienced practitioners with minimal cognitive effort.
