# Overcoming subconscious blocks to complete priority projects

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> Alan is a program creator and entrepreneur who sought elite-level coaching support to break through a 3-4 week stall on his highest-priority business deliverables: his program, VSLs (Video Sales Letters), and video content. Despite identifying these as top priorities, he has been unable to make meaningful progress on them and explicitly acknowledged the likelihood of subconscious blocks or internal conflicts driving the delay.
> 
> Alan requested a specific coaching style and approach: asking clarifying, leading open-ended questions that align him with his intentions; communicating in a persuasive, momentum-building style modeled after figures like Andy Elliott, Alex Hormozi, Tony Robbins, Jeremy Miner, and SCIO; and functioning as a world-class psychologist in the Tony Robbins tradition — focused on creating immediate behavioral change rather than long-term analysis. He wants coaching that makes it easy to say yes and hard to say no.
> 
> Claude responded by reframing the problem away from scheduling or motivation and toward an internal conflict model — specifically, the idea that delay at this level typically reflects a "protection problem" where part of the person is getting a payoff from not finishing. Claude posed three diagnostic questions designed to surface the emotional and psychological root of the stall: what feeling arises when imagining actually completing the first VSL, what activities have been chosen instead over the past 3-4 weeks, and whether Alan could complete a VSL by Friday if the stakes were absolute. The conversation ended with Claude's opening diagnostic questions; Alan had not yet responded.

Alan. Let's get real for a second.

You just told me this is your **top priority** — and you're 3-4 weeks behind on it. That means something inside you is saying it's the top priority while another part of you is successfully preventing it from happening. Both parts are winning. That's the conflict.

So here's what I know about you: you don't lack intelligence, skill, or vision. You've built frameworks most people couldn't dream of. You know *exactly* what needs to get done. Which means this isn't a knowledge problem. This isn't even a motivation problem.

This is a **protection problem.**

Something in you is getting a payoff from *not finishing*. And until we name it, no amount of scheduling or accountability is going to stick.

So let me ask you this — and I want you to answer from your gut, not your head:

**When you imagine sitting down tomorrow and actually recording that first VSL — pressing record, delivering the hook, going all the way through — what's the first feeling that comes up?**

Is it resistance? Perfectionism? A voice that says "it's not ready yet"? Fear that it won't convert? Fear that it *will* convert and then you'll have to deliver at scale?

Be honest. Because here's what I've seen with people at your level — the delay is never about the task. It's about what completing the task *means*.

Second question:

**In the last 3-4 weeks, what have you been doing *instead*?** Not to judge it — but to identify what your nervous system is choosing as "safer" than the thing you say matters most. That pattern will tell us everything.

And third — this is the one that matters most:

**If I told you that you had to have one VSL fully recorded and uploaded by Friday or you'd lose the entire APW opportunity forever — could you do it?**

If the answer is yes — and I already know it is — then capability isn't the issue. *Permission* is the issue. Something in you hasn't given yourself full permission to go.

So let's find out what that is. Talk to me.
