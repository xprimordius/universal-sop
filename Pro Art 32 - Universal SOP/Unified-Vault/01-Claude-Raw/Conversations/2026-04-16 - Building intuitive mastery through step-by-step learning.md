## The Theory — Steelmanned in 7 Moves

1. A mortgage = promissory note + lien. Lien dies when note is satisfied.
2. Fed publications (*Modern Money Mechanics*, *Two Faces of Debt*) admit banks "create money" when lending.
3. Therefore your signature IS the money — you funded your own loan.
4. Bank then sells the note (securitization / Fed) and receives cash for it.
5. Bank never disclosed #3 → **non-disclosure = fraud**.
6. Legal maxim: "**fraud vitiates all things**" → contract void from inception.
7. Remedy: home free and clear + refund of every payment.

**Delivery:** send letters → bank stays silent → claim "tacit acquiescence" → file civil suit → demand the original note (bank usually can't produce because it was securitized) → settlement.

## Where Each Move Actually Breaks

| # | Claim | Reality |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Mortgage = note + lien; lien dies with note | **True.** Uncontroversial black-letter. |
| 2 | *Modern Money Mechanics* says banks create money | **Partially true.** It describes fractional-reserve deposit creation. It does **not** say borrowers fund their own loans or that mortgages are fraudulent. That's a reader's interpolation. |
| 3 | Your signature = money | **False.** UCC 1-201 defines "money" as a government-authorized medium of exchange. A promissory note is a **negotiable instrument** under UCC Article 3 — a *promise to pay money*, not money itself. The pitch conflates the two. |
| 4 | Bank sells the note for cash | **True and legal.** Securitization is disclosed in every closing package (TILA, transfer-of-servicing notices, assignment records). Not a secret. |
| 5 | Non-disclosure = fraud | **False.** Mortgage disclosure is exhaustively regulated (TILA, RESPA, Reg Z). "You funded your own loan" is not a required disclosure because it isn't true. |
| 6 | Maxim voids the contract | **Maxim exists, premise fails.** Fraud requires proof of every element (false statement of material fact, knowledge of falsity, intent, justifiable reliance, damages, etc.). The theory meets none. |
| 7 | Free home + full refund | **Has never occurred in a contested case on this theory.** Occasional procedural wins on foreclosure *standing* defects are a different doctrine and do not produce free homes. |

## The "Tacit Acquiescence" Trick

Pitch says: send letters, bank stays silent X days → they've "admitted" everything.

**This is not how US law works.** Silence in response to private correspondence is not admission of anything. The only place silence can create admission is a **formally served Request for Admission under Rule 36 (or state equivalent) inside active litigation** — and even then, only to specific factual admissions, never to legal conclusions. "Conditional acceptance" letters are a sovereign-citizen artifact; courts read them as noise.

| Citation | Pitch's claim | What it actually says |
|----------|---------------|------------------------|
| **12 USC 1813(i)(1)** | "Promissory notes must be treated as legal tender" | 12 USC 1813 is the **FDIC Act definitions section** (banks, deposits, insured institutions). Does not say notes are legal tender. *(The real legal-tender statute is 31 USC 5103: coins and Federal Reserve notes — not promissory notes.)* |

## What's Actually True in the Mortgage-Dispute Space

Legitimate frameworks that sometimes produce real relief:

- **Standing / chain-of-title defenses in foreclosure** — if the foreclosing party can't prove it holds note + mortgage, case can be dismissed. Does *not* equal free home; the real holder usually refiles.
- **TILA rescission (15 USC 1635)** — within 3 years for specific disclosure violations on refinances/HELOCs (not purchase-money mortgages).
- **RESPA Qualified Written Requests (12 USC 2605)** — servicer nonresponse creates statutory damages.
- **State UDAP / HOEPA / state foreclosure defenses** — real, case-specific, narrow.

None produce free homes. All require specific facts and usually counsel.
