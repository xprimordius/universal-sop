# Business score dropped unexpectedly

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> Alan is the CEO of Skyward USA, Inc., a management consulting firm. He came to Claude with concerns about his Experian business credit score, which sits at 55 (Low-Medium Risk) against an industry benchmark of 44, significantly below the 88 he expected. Claude analyzed the report and identified the core issues: dormant and closed tradelines (including a closed account ending in 9387 and a stale account ending in 962C), an extremely thin file with only two payment tradelines and one SBFE trade, and 8 business credit inquiries with no corresponding new open accounts — a pattern that signals risk to scoring models. The SBFE Financial Stability Risk Rating is 5 (High Risk), compounding the problem.
> 
> Alan pushed back on Claude's characterization of management consulting as an industry risk factor, noting he had been told it was favorable. Claude clarified the distinction between how lenders view the industry versus how Experian's proprietary scoring algorithm weights SIC 8742, and acknowledged the industry factor is the smallest drag compared to the thin file and dormant tradelines. Claude recommended opening three net-30 vendor accounts (Uline, Quill, Grainger) that report to Experian Business at no cost to open, making small purchases monthly, and paying on time to rebuild active tradeline reporting within 60–90 days.
> 
> The latter portion of the conversation focused on disputing unauthorized business credit inquiries. Claude drafted multiple versions of a dispute letter, iterating based on Alan's feedback — first incorporating FCRA citations with a firm tone, then softening the language at Alan's request for a less hostile approach toward Experian Business, then incorporating Alan's own preferred draft language. The final version preserved Alan's framing around fraudulent inquiries and cited FCRA §1681b(c) and Section 611, with one key adjustment: replacing "Social Security number" with "business identification number" to accurately reflect that this is an Experian Business file tied to BIN 428222169. The final letter came in at 812 characters, within Alan's 1000-character limit.
