Quick read — and first: **Alan or Josephine?**

The phrase means the AI leader isn't just a technologist. They've got working fluency in:

- **Client-side reality** — what law firm clients (GCs, corporate legal, in-house procurement) actually want, how they buy legal services, what pain points drive their spend.
- **Industry workflows** — how lawyers actually do the work day-to-day: matter intake, conflicts checks, document review, drafting, e-discovery, billing, KM, compliance reviews. The unglamorous plumbing.

Translation: they can build/deploy AI that fits *into* real legal practice instead of forcing lawyers to reshape their work around the tool. It's a hire signal that the firm wants someone who speaks both "transformer" and "billable hour" — not a pure data scientist parachuted into a partnership culture they don't understand.

In job-post language, it usually screens for: prior legal-tech, BigLaw ops, consulting at a legal vertical (Thomson Reuters, Harvey, iManage, Relativity, Axiom, etc.), or in-house legal innovation roles.
