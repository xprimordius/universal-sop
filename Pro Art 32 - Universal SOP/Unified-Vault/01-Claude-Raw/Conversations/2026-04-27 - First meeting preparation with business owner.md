## 🤖 Claude

Rotowelds are **automated pipe welding robots** — basically machines that weld pipe joints automatically instead of a human welder doing it by hand.

**The simple version:**
- You load a piece of pipe onto rollers
- The machine spins the pipe while a robotic arm holds the welding torch in place
- The arm uses cameras and AI to adjust in real-time as it welds
- One operator can supervise instead of welding manually

**Why shops want them:**
- Skilled welders are scarce and expensive
- A Rotoweld can do ~150–300 diameter inches per day vs. a manual welder doing far less
- Quality is more consistent — every weld looks the same
- An operator can be trained in about a week vs. years for a certified pipe welder

**Made by:** Tecnar, a Canadian company. The current model is the Rotoweld 3.0. Each unit runs roughly $500K–$1M+ depending on configuration.

**In plain terms:** It's the Tesla factory robot version of pipe welding. Replaces the bottleneck of finding human welders. That's why his whole business plan hinges on having three of them — it's the productivity engine.

**Easy line for the meeting:** *"So the Rotowelds are basically the production engine — three of them, that's a serious commitment. What drew you to that platform versus going heavier on manual welders?"*

## 🤖 Claude

**EPC = Engineering, Procurement, and Construction.**

It's a type of large contractor that handles a project end-to-end — they design it (engineering), buy all the materials and equipment (procurement), and build it (construction). One firm, one contract, one point of accountability.

**Why it matters for him:**

On big industrial projects — data centers, pharma plants, power facilities — the owner often hires an EPC firm to deliver the whole thing. That EPC then subcontracts the pipe fabrication out. So instead of chasing 20 individual data center owners, he could land *one* EPC relationship and get fed steady work across multiple projects.

**Examples of EPCs in his world:** Fluor, Bechtel, Jacobs, Kiewit, Burns & McDonnell, Black & Veatch, DPR, Holder, Turner Industries.

**How to use it in conversation:**

*"Are you thinking about going direct to data center owners, or building EPC relationships that feed you across multiple projects?"*

That single question signals you understand how the work actually flows in his industry. EPC anchor relationships are often more valuable than individual client wins because they compound.
