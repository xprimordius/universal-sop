## 🤖 Claude

No — opposite. Higher HR doesn't help the donation, and the reasoning has a flaw worth correcting.

**The mechanics:**

Blood flow into the collection bag is driven by **venous pressure and gravity**, not heart rate. The needle's in a vein, not an artery. Your heart rate doesn't push blood out faster through a venous draw. What actually controls draw speed:

- Vein size and quality
- Hydration (blood volume)
- Squeezing the stress ball (muscle pump moves venous blood)
- Gravity (arm position)

**What dex actually does that's relevant:**

- **Vasoconstriction** — narrows blood vessels, including veins. Can make veins *harder* to access and draw from, not easier.
- **Raises BP** — can push you over the 180/100 cutoff and get you deferred at intake.
- **Raises HR** — Red Cross typically wants pulse roughly 50–100. Stimulant tachycardia can flag you.
- **Masks symptoms** — if something goes sideways during or after (lightheadedness, pre-syncope, chest pressure), dex can blunt your awareness of it. That's the part that matters most given your morning.

**Net:** Dex doesn't make donation easier. It makes the vitals screen harder to pass and masks warning signs. The org cleared you on the prescription — that's about eligibility, not about it being *helpful*. Different question.

**The thing that actually makes donation easier:** hydration. Drink water now.
