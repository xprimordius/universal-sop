# Ascend Prime Wealth curriculum for medical practice owners

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The user is developing a curriculum for Ascend Prime Wealth, a funding company targeting medical practice owners with a guarantee to unlock $100K-$500K+ in funding within 90 days. They sought help creating an onboarding sequence that would provide clients with a quick win immediately after the initial setup. Through iterative discussion, they identified that traditional funding processes typically lead to disappointment (onboarding call → funding manager call → rejection), and that their unique value proposition lies in educating clients about common denial reasons before they make costly mistakes.
> 
> The conversation evolved from exploring various quick win concepts to developing a comprehensive three-pillar fundability framework. The user provided detailed technical knowledge about funding requirements, including specific income thresholds by institution (TCM $350K, ELAN & major nationals $600K-$900K, Chase $1M-$1.4M), credit profile optimization strategies, and application sequencing. They emphasized the importance of funding businesses first before personal credit to avoid 6-12-24 month delays from new credit showing on reports.
> 
> The user demonstrated deep expertise in credit optimization, sharing specific requirements like maintaining 1-3% utilization rather than 0% (which banks prefer to see activity), managing hard inquiries across different bureaus, and understanding that institutions like Bank of America only look at 6-month inquiry windows while others examine 6-12-24 month intervals. They also provided insights about Chex Systems flagging frequent bank account openings and the importance of business email domains rather than free providers for automated approval systems.
> 
> The final deliverable was a branded three-page PDF checklist organized around three pillars: Business Profile (entity setup, banking, credit), Personal Profile (credit cards, utilization, inquiries), and Application Strategy (sequencing, institution knowledge, approval optimization). The user requested specific branding using their logo and color palette of gold/champagne, white backgrounds, and iridescent rainbow gradients from their wing logo. They emphasized the importance of separating business and personal credit tracks, with most clients following the business-first path while special cases might require personal credit building or seed capital strategies.

## 🤖 Claude

Here's how I'm thinking about the quick win problem:

The fastest possible win is something that **doesn't require waiting on the team** to review anything. Credit report submission creates a bottleneck — they submit, then wait. That's the opposite of a quick win feeling.

So the question is: what can they *accomplish and feel good about* within the first 24-48 hours that also moves them meaningfully toward funding?

---

**The Quick Win I'd Recommend: The "Funding Readiness Score"**

Build a simple self-assessment tool (could be a form, a Typeform, a short intake process) that they complete immediately after onboarding. It takes 10-15 minutes and covers:

- Business age and entity type
- Revenue (monthly/annual)
- Current credit profile (self-reported ranges, not a pull)
- Existing accounts and tradelines
- Current debt obligations
- What they've already tried

At the end, they instantly receive a **personalized Funding Readiness Report** — a one-pager that tells them:

- Where they stand today
- Their estimated funding range based on their profile
- Their fastest path to the first draw (Track A: ready now vs. Track B: optimize first)
- The 3 most important things to focus on in their first 30 days

**Why this works as a quick win:**

It gives them *clarity and a roadmap in under an hour*. They feel seen, assessed, and told exactly what to do next — which is the real thing people pay for and rarely get immediately. It also naturally segments your clients so the team can prioritize who's ready for credit review now vs. who needs pre-work.

The credit report submission then becomes the *next step* for Track A clients, and they're submitting it with context rather than into a void.

This is a strong list — it's genuinely more detailed and actionable than anything a typical funding company would ever share with a client. That alone is the proof of concept for your education model.

A few thoughts and additions:

**What you have is actually two distinct categories worth separating:**

**Profile Construction** (what your profile looks like before you apply) — utilization, limits, card count, age, mix, banking relationships, etc.

**Application Strategy** (how and when you pull the trigger) — inquiry management, sequencing, knowing where each bank pulls, income alignment, threshold matching

Most people conflate these. Separating them in the curriculum is itself educational.

---

**Additions worth considering:**

On the banking relationship side — business checking account age and average daily balance mattering separately from personal. Some institutions want to see the business account seasoned 1-2 years before extending credit.

Freeze strategy — having non-pulling bureaus frozen during applications to control where hard inquiries land.

Business entity profile separate from personal — Dun & Bradstreet, Paydex score, business credit age, whether the EIN has its own fundable profile built yet or is entirely dependent on the PG.

Business phone, address, listing consistency — whether the business shows up legitimately across directories, which affects automated approval scoring at certain institutions.
