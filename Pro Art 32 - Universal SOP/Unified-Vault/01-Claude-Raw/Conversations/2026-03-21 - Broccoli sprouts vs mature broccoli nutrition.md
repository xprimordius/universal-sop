**Quick answer while you confirm:**

Broccoli sprouts contain roughly **10–100x more sulforaphane** (the key bioactive compound) than mature broccoli, depending on the specific measurement and growth stage. The most commonly cited figure is **20–50x more** by weight.

**Key details:**

- The compound that matters most here is **glucoraphanin**, the precursor to sulforaphane. It's highly concentrated in 3–5 day old sprouts and dilutes as the plant matures.
- Mature broccoli still has solid fiber, vitamin C, vitamin K, and folate — sprouts aren't a full replacement nutritionally, just dramatically superior for sulforaphane specifically.
- Sulforaphane is the compound linked to Nrf2 pathway activation (master antioxidant switch), detoxification support, and anti-inflammatory effects.

**Bottom line:** If your goal is sulforaphane intake (cellular defense, detox, inflammation), sprouts win by a massive margin. If you want broad micronutrient coverage, eat both.
