**You received this because someone owes you money and filed bankruptcy.**

Here's the breakdown:

**Who filed:** Laura Lan Pham (Minneapolis, MN) — Chapter 7 bankruptcy, Case No. 26-40221.

**What Chapter 7 means:** The debtor is liquidating assets to pay off debts. Most unsecured debts get discharged (wiped out).

**Why you got this notice:** You're listed as a creditor — meaning Laura Lan Pham owes you money. Initially the court said there were no assets to distribute, but now it appears there *may* be money available to pay creditors ("payment of a dividend appears possible").

**What you need to do (if you want to get paid):** File a **Proof of Claim** (Official Form 410) by **June 22, 2026** at [www.mnb.uscourts.gov](http://www.mnb.uscourts.gov). No login needed.

**If you do nothing:** You forfeit any right to receive a share of whatever assets are distributed.

**Key context:** Even when a dividend is possible in Chapter 7, creditors often receive only pennies on the dollar — but it's better than zero. Filing the claim is free and straightforward.

Do you know what debt this person owes you? That'll determine whether it's worth pursuing and how to categorize the claim on the form.

> [!note]- Claude's thinking
> Alan is saying his mom's bankruptcy attorney isn't doing a good job. Let me give him practical guidance on this.


That's frustrating but common with budget BK attorneys — they file and disappear.

**What matters right now:** The fact that the trustee found assets worth distributing is the one thing that could actually affect her. She needs to make sure those assets are properly exempted (protected) — that's literally the attorney's job.

**Two moves:**

**1. Get clarity from the current attorney.** Have her (or you on her behalf) send a direct message:

*"The court issued a Notice to File Claims dated 3/17/26 indicating a possible dividend. What asset(s) did the trustee identify, and are they fully covered by my exemptions? If not, what's the exposure?"*

If they dodge, delay, or give a vague answer — that tells you everything.

**2. If they're truly dropping the ball — consult a second BK attorney.** Most will do a free or low-cost review mid-case. In Minnesota, look for attorneys who specifically handle Chapter 7 trustee disputes or exemption issues. She can switch attorneys even during an active case with court approval.

**The risk if nobody's paying attention:** The trustee liquidates an asset your mom assumed was protected, and she loses property or money she didn't have to lose — simply because exemptions weren't properly claimed or defended.

Want me to help you draft that message to the attorney, or look into BK attorneys in Minneapolis who handle this?
