## 🤖 Claude

> [!note]- Claude's thinking
> This is a straightforward tipping question about a nail salon redo—the user paid $160 initially, had to return within a week due to chipping, and the correction took about 20 minutes. The core issue is whether tipping applies when you're getting faulty work fixed rather than a new service.
> 
> The standard practice is that salon touch-ups within their warranty period (usually 3-7 days) are free and don't require a tip, since they're correcting their own mistake. That said, many people do leave a small tip of $5-10 as a courtesy, especially if the technician handles it gracefully. Since the user already tipped on the original service, they're not obligated here—but a modest tip is a nice gesture if they want to acknowledge the quick fix. I should also note that chipping within a week suggests a quality issue worth questioning, since a proper gel or acrylic manicure should last longer than that.


**No tip required — this is a redo of faulty work.**

A manicure chipping in a week is below standard. The salon is correcting their own mistake, not providing a new service. You already tipped on the original $160.

**If you want to tip anyway:** $5 is a goodwill gesture for the tech's 20 minutes — but it's genuinely optional and no one would fault you for skipping it.

**Worth considering:** Quality gel should hold 2–3 weeks minimum. If this keeps happening, it's the product or application, not you. Might be time for a new salon.
