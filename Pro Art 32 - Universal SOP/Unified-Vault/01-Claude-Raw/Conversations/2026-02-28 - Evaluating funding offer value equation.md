# Evaluating funding offer value equation

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The user is developing a business funding education program and sought Claude's help to optimize their value proposition using the value equation framework: Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). They wanted to score each component and refine the messaging to achieve maximum impact. The user demonstrated strong business instincts throughout, frequently correcting Claude's suggestions when they felt inauthentic or weakened the original messaging.
> 
> The user has significant personal credibility in this space, having gone through over 40 funding company sales processes, signed up with multiple services, and personally secured substantial funding starting from a damaged credit profile. They emphasized the importance of truth-based messaging over "trust me bro" claims and insisted all statements be rooted in indisputable facts rather than subjective opinions. Their program teaches clients to obtain funding independently rather than remaining dependent on funding companies that charge percentage fees.
> 
> The conversation involved extensive refinement of four key areas: the dream outcome messaging (emphasizing repeatability and practical applications), perceived likelihood of achievement (incorporating industry credibility and personal proof), time delay promises (balancing speed with realistic expectations), and effort requirements (distinguishing between "Done With You" and "Done For You" service levels). The user consistently pushed back on language that felt manufactured or overly promotional, preferring direct, confident statements that could withstand scrutiny.
> 
> A significant portion focused on developing guarantee structures to differentiate their offer in the marketplace. They wanted both outcome-based and money-back guarantees with appropriate legal protections, particularly for "special approved cases" who might need credit optimization before beginning the funding process. The user's sales manager provided feedback that certain urgency tactics felt "cheesy and gimmicky," leading to refinements that emphasized natural consequences of delay rather than artificial scarcity.
> 
> The user demonstrated a pattern of preferring concise, punchy messaging over elaborate explanations, frequently asking Claude to "say less" or simplify complex statements. They showed particular sensitivity to language that might invite legal scrutiny or create undefendable claims, consistently choosing more conservative but truthful positioning over aggressive marketing language. Their communication style favored direct challenges to Claude's suggestions when they didn't align with their vision, showing confidence in their own judgment about what would resonate with their target audience.

## 🤖 Claude

Here's how your offer ranks across the value equation:

---

**Overall Score: ~7.2 / 10** — Solid foundation, but leaving significant value on the table primarily because of a weak Dream Outcome.

---

**1. Dream Outcome — 6.5/10** *(Your instinct is right — this is the weakest link)*

You already flagged this yourself. "$100K-$500K in funding" sounds like a *tool*, not a transformation. People don't dream about funding — they dream about what funding *unlocks*. The dream outcome needs to be the life on the other side of the money. Right now it reads like a feature, not a destination.

The repeatability angle ("for clients, family, partners") is genuinely strong and hints at a wealth-building or income opportunity framing — but it's buried. That's actually a *second* dream outcome hiding inside the first one. A person could build a funding consulting business with this. That's enormous and completely undersold.

**Fix:** Lead with the transformation. What does someone *do* with $100K-$500K? Buy a business? Fund a real estate deal? Launch a product? Escape their job? Layer that identity shift on top of the funding mechanism.

---

**2. Perceived Likelihood of Achievement — 8.5/10** *(Your strongest variable)*

This is well-constructed. You're doing almost everything right — social proof, proven system, longevity ("decades"), authority transfer (underwriters, trainers), and the bold unreasonableness framing ("UNREASONABLE not to succeed"). That's a genuinely powerful reframe. The damaged credit mention is a strong objection crusher.

What would push this to a 10: a specific success rate or case study number. "Countless others" is softer than "312 clients funded in the last 18 months." Quantify wherever possible.

---

**3. Time Delay — 8/10** *(Fast, but could be crisper)*

The timeline is competitive. 90 days to capital is a strong anchor. The support structure (async, same-day, live Q&A, 1-on-1) signals low abandonment risk, which reinforces likelihood of achievement as a bonus. 

The weakness here is presentation — the numbered list of delivery mechanics reads like a features sheet, not a promise. Reframe it around speed: *"Most clients are funded within 60-90 days. You'll spend a few hours upfront, then just a couple hours a week following the system."* Clean, fast, confident.

---

**4. Effort & Sacrifice — 8/10** *(Solid, especially DFY)*

The DWY/DFY distinction is smart and the effort framing is honest without being dismissive. "As close to little effort as legally possible" is actually a great line — it's specific, credible, and slightly cheeky in a way that builds trust. 

The risk here is that listing *what the client still has to do* (sign documents, open accounts, season accounts) could feel like effort to someone who came in expecting pure DFY. Make sure the framing around those actions emphasizes how *guided and simple* each step is, not just that they exist.

---

**The Core Problem in One Sentence:**

You have a strong delivery machine wrapped around a mediocre dream. Fix the Dream Outcome — make it visceral, identity-level, and outcome-focused — and this offer could easily jump to a 9/10. Right now the numerator of your value equation is underperforming while the denominator is well-optimized. That's an imbalanced equation.

## 🤖 Claude

Yes, this works — and it's actually a legitimate and commonly used credibility frame. Here's how to think about it cleanly:

---

**The Core Logic Holds**

Bank underwriting criteria don't change based on who teaches them. A credit score threshold is a credit score threshold. If the *system* is proven and you've learned it from verified sources, the system's track record is real — you're just the new delivery vehicle for it. This is no different than a new CPA saying "I was trained on the same tax code that saved thousands of clients millions of dollars." The code works regardless of who applies it.

So yes — "$100M+ funded using these same systems" is defensible *if* you frame it correctly.

---

**How to Frame It Without Legal or Credibility Risk**

Say something like: *"This program is built on the same underwriting approval systems that have helped funding companies collectively facilitate over $100M+ for clients — the same strategies used industry-wide, because bank approval criteria are standardized and proven."*

That framing attributes the track record to the *system and industry*, not falsely to yourself. It's honest, it's strong, and it's not a personal claim you can't back up.

---

**On Naming Other Companies**

Avoid it — even the legitimate ones. Here's why: you don't have their permission, you can't verify their exact numbers, and one of the names you listed has a founder you yourself described as a scammer. Even guilt by association in passing can damage your credibility. You don't need their names. The *industry result* is the proof point, not the brand names behind it.

---

**The One Thing to Be Careful About**

Don't imply you personally generated that $100M+. The moment someone asks "where's that number from?" your answer needs to be clean and immediate: *"That's the collective industry track record of the underwriting systems this program is built on."* If you hesitate or it sounds retrofitted, it erodes trust fast. So build the explanation into the framing upfront rather than leaving it as a defense.

---

**Bottom line:** Yes, use it. Frame it as system-level proof, not personal proof. Keep the names out. And make sure your explanation is ready and rehearsed so it adds to your credibility rather than creating a question mark.
