**Counter 1: § 325M.19(a)(4) — "We need to retain the data to defend legal claims"**

BP could argue they're exempt from deletion because the statute allows controllers to retain data to "investigate, establish, exercise, prepare for, or defend legal claims."

**Why it fails — and how to pre-counter:**

The exemption requires a **nexus between the specific data and a specific legal claim**. It's designed for litigation holds — a company preserving communications relevant to an active or reasonably anticipated lawsuit. It doesn't authorize blanket retention of all user-generated content forever just because lawsuits theoretically exist.

Your forum post is about a third-party business (Freedom Funders). It has zero relevance to any legal claim BP is defending, establishing, or investigating. BP isn't a party to any dispute involving that content. The exemption can't be stretched into a universal content-retention shield — that would swallow the deletion right entirely and render § 325M.14, Subd. 1(d) meaningless, which violates basic statutory construction principles (no provision should be interpreted to nullify another).

Also important: even if BP invoked this exemption, **§ 325M.14, Subd. 4(f) still requires them to provide written reasons for denial and appeal instructions.** They haven't done that. The exemption doesn't excuse the procedural violations — it would only potentially excuse the substantive deletion obligation, and only if they actually cited it with specificity.
