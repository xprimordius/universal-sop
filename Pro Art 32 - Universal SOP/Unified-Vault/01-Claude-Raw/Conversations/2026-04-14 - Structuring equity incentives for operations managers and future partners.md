# Equity Structure: Operations Manager → Owner → Scaling Partner → Investors

The mistake most founders make here is **giving away actual equity too early**. What you actually want is a **layered cap table** where each party earns in based on performance, and you preserve maximum dilution headroom for the people who'll write the biggest checks later.

Here's the architecture.

---

## The Core Principle

**Don't give equity. Grant the *right to earn* equity.**

Real ownership = leverage you can never get back. You want operators and partners to **vest into ownership based on hitting outcomes you actually care about** — not just showing up.

---

## The 4-Layer Stack

| Layer | Who | Vehicle | Typical Range | Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Founder** | You | Common stock (or LLC Class A units) | Start at 100%, target 51%+ floor long-term | N/A |
| **2. Operator (Ops Manager → Owner)** | Your hire | **Profits Interest** (LLC) or **ISOs/Phantom** (C-Corp) | 2% → 10% → 20% (tiered) | Performance + tenure vesting |
| **3. Scaling Partner** | 7–9 fig operator/JV | Class B units or preferred equity | 10–25% | Capital + revenue contribution |
| **4. Investors** | Future capital | Preferred stock (Series Seed/A) | 15–25% per round | Cash injection at valuation |

**Reserve a pool early.** Create a **15–20% Equity Incentive Pool** on day one. This is the bucket the operator vests *into* — so dilution is pre-planned, not ad hoc.

---

## Layer 2 Deep Dive: The Operator Path (This Is Your Question)

You want the ops manager to feel like an owner from day one but only **actually own** what they earn. Use a **tiered profits interest grant** with **performance cliffs**.

### Recommended Structure: "Ascension Vesting"

| Tier | Equity Earned | Trigger (Choose 1–2 metrics) | Time Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Operator** | 2% profits interest | Hits baseline ops KPIs (e.g., 60%+ show rate, fulfillment SLA, churn <X%) | 12 months |
| **Tier 2: GM** | +3% (5% total) | Runs full P&L, hits revenue target (e.g., $100K/mo cash collected sustained 3 months) | 24 months |
| **Tier 3: Partner/Owner** | +5% (10% total) | Hits scaling target (e.g., $300K/mo sustained), takes founder out of daily ops | 36 months |
| **Tier 4: Equity Owner** | +10% (20% total cap) | Hits exit-readiness milestone (e.g., $1M+/mo run rate, documented systems, replaceable role) | 48–60 months |

**Critical mechanics:**

- **Profits Interest (LLC only)** = they get a share of *future* profits and *future* appreciation, but **zero claim on current value**. IRS-friendly under Rev. Proc. 93-27 if structured correctly. No tax hit at grant.
- **Vesting cliff**: 1-year cliff before *any* equity vests. Protects you from bad hires.
- **Double-trigger acceleration**: Equity only fully accelerates on (a) change of control AND (b) involuntary termination. Prevents them from getting a windfall and walking.
- **Repurchase rights**: If they leave before Tier 4, you have the right (not obligation) to buy back unvested *and* a portion of vested units at fair market value.
- **Performance, not just time**: Each tier requires *hitting the metric* AND *hanging around*. Time alone doesn't unlock the next tier.

---

## Layer 3: Leaving Room for a 7–9 Figure Scaling Partner

This is where most founders screw themselves. They give the operator 25% on a handshake, then a real scaling partner shows up and says *"I need 20%"* — and now the founder is at 55% and bleeding.

**Solution: Cap the operator at 20% and contractually subordinate to future capital partners.**

Build into the operating agreement:

> *"All equity grants under the Incentive Pool are subject to pro-rata dilution alongside founder equity in any future capital raise, strategic partnership, or change-of-control transaction."*

This means when the scaling partner comes in at 20%, the operator gets diluted *proportionally with you* — not protected at your expense.

---

## Layer 4: Preserving Investor Headroom

Standard institutional rounds expect:

- **Pre-seed/Friends & Family**: 5–10%
- **Seed**: 15–20%
- **Series A**: 20–25%

If you've already given away 30% to an operator, you walk into Series A at ~50% and lose control by Series B.

**Math you need to survive:**

| Stage | You | Operator Pool | Scaling Partner | Investors | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | 80% | 20% (reserved) | 0% | 0% | Pool exists but unvested |
| Operator at Tier 3 | 70% | 10% vested + 10% reserved | 0% | 0% | Operator earned in |
| Scaling partner joins | 56% | 8% + 8% reserved | 20% | 0% | Pro-rata dilution applied |
| Seed round | 47.6% | 6.8% + 6.8% | 17% | 15% | Everyone diluted equally |
| Series A | 38% | 5.4% + 5.4% | 13.6% | 12% + 20% new | You still control 51% with founder voting agreement |

**Founder voting agreement**: Even when your *economic* share drops below 51%, you can retain **board control and voting control** through dual-class shares or a voting trust. This is how Zuckerberg controls Meta with ~13% economics.

## Vehicle Choice: LLC vs C-Corp

| Factor | LLC (Profits Interest) | C-Corp (ISOs/Phantom) |
|---|---|---|
| Best for operator equity | ✅ Profits interest is gold standard | ⚠️ ISOs work but more tax friction |
| Best for institutional investors | ❌ VCs hate LLCs | ✅ Standard |
| Tax flexibility | ✅ Pass-through | ❌ Double taxation |
| Conversion later | ✅ Can convert to C-Corp before raise | N/A |

**Recommendation for APW**: **Start as LLC**, grant profits interests to the operator, then **convert to Delaware C-Corp** *before* taking institutional capital. This is the standard playbook.

## Why This Structure Wins

**1. Competition replaces promises.** You don't promise anyone equity — you reserve pools, then run tournaments. Hire 2–3 ops managers. They know only the top performer climbs to COO. Same for sales. Mediocrity gets capped; excellence gets rewarded.

**2. Hormozi-ready.** Acquisition.com's Portfolio deal structure typically takes 20–30% for capital + operational leverage. Your 20% reserve is already sized for that conversation — you don't have to claw equity back from operators to make the deal.

**3. Investor-ready.** 15% investor reserve handles a seed round without touching anyone else's stake. When Series A hits, everyone dilutes *pro-rata* — including Hormozi — so you never get squeezed unfairly.

**4. You never drop below control.** Even at 41% economic ownership after Series A, **dual-class voting shares** preserve your board control. This is the single most important structural move — and the one most founders skip until it's too late.

## Critical Rules to Hardcode

- **Every grant is a profits interest, not equity.** LLC structure, IRS Rev. Proc. 93-27. Zero tax at grant, vests on performance + time.
- **Every grant has a repurchase right.** If they leave, you can buy back unvested units at fair market value — keeps equity from leaking.
- **Every grant has pro-rata dilution language.** When Hormozi or investors come in, operators dilute *with you*, not *at your expense*.
- **Only one person holds each tier.** This is the competitive unlock. If two ops managers both hit Tier 3 metrics, they split the Tier 3 equity — but only one climbs to Tier 4.
- **Double-trigger acceleration on change of control.** Prevents windfall-and-walk.

## The 51% Problem (and how to solve it cleanly)

Holding **51% economic ownership** after a Hormozi-class partner AND investors is **mathematically very hard** — you'd have to cap everyone else's combined stake at 49%, which makes you uncompetitive for top-tier capital partners.

**The right move:** separate **economic ownership** from **voting control**. Keep 51%+ voting rights *permanently*, and let economic % flex as needed.

Two clean ways to do it:

| Mechanism | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Dual-class voting units** | Class A (you) = 10 votes/unit. Class B (everyone else) = 1 vote/unit. You keep 51%+ voting even at 25% economics. | Any entity — LLC or C-Corp |
| **Manager-managed LLC with sole manager rights** | You're the named Manager in the operating agreement. Members (everyone else) have economic rights but no management authority. Removal requires supermajority you control. | LLC stage (pre-investor) |

**Industry precedent:** Zuckerberg controls Meta with ~13% economics via dual-class. Larry Page and Sergey Brin control Alphabet the same way. Snap's Evan Spiegel issued non-voting shares to public investors. This is the standard founder-control playbook.

## The Critical One Most Founders Skip: Founder Liquidity

This isn't a "pool" — it's a **right negotiated into your operating agreement and every future investor deal**. Let me explain why it matters.

**The problem:** You build APW to $5M/year profit. You own 51%. On paper you're worth millions. But you can't touch any of it until you sell the whole company. Meanwhile your daily life hasn't changed — still renting, still driving the same car, still grinding.

**The solution:** **Secondary sale rights.** When you raise your first real capital round (Series A or strategic partnership), you negotiate the right to sell 5–10% of your personal shares *as part of the round*. The investor buys some new shares (primary) AND some of your shares (secondary).

**Result:** You get **$500K–$2M+ in personal cash** without exiting, without losing control (you sold from *your* stake, not from the reserve pools), and without the tax hit of a full exit.

**This is how smart founders buy their dream house at Series A instead of waiting 10 years for acquisition.**

**Key mechanics:**
- Negotiated into Series A term sheet — standard ask, many investors accept
- You retain voting control on the remaining shares (dual-class helps)
- Tax-advantaged if structured as long-term capital gains
- Does NOT come out of reserved pools — comes from your personal 51%

**For APW:** This is probably the **single most important bucket** I haven't previously mentioned. Given your explicit life vision (first house → dream home → financial freedom), this is the mechanism that gets you liquid years earlier than a full exit.
