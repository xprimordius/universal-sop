Two different programs to know about here:

**Unemployment Insurance — likely NO.** Traditional UI in MN requires you to be "able and available" to work. If she's recovering from surgery and can't work, she typically won't meet that requirement.

**Minnesota Paid Leave (MPL) — likely YES.** This is the better path. MN's new Paid Leave program took effect January 1, 2026, and covers medical leave for surgery and recovery. Key details:

- Up to 12 weeks of medical leave for your own serious health condition
- Most people receive between 55% and 90% of their regular wages, with a max of $1,423/week
- To be eligible, she must have earned at least $3,900 in the past year
- The condition must require her to be away from work for at least seven days
- She can apply online or by phone through DEED

**Bottom line:** She should apply for **Minnesota Paid Leave**, not unemployment. It's specifically designed for exactly her situation — surgery recovery with a plan to return to the same employer.

She can apply at [pl.mn.gov](https://pl.mn.gov).

Yes. The medical certification doesn't have to be completed before the surgery. Her healthcare provider can fill it out after the procedure — it just documents the condition, the surgery performed, and the expected recovery period.

If the leave is foreseeable (planned surgery), she should notify her employer at least 30 days in advance of her intent to take leave. But the actual medical certification paperwork from the provider can be submitted after.

**Practical move:** Have her provider complete it at her first post-op follow-up appointment. That's standard.

> [!note]- Claude's thinking
> The user is asking about additional financial assistance options for someone (likely Josephine or someone they know) recovering from heel surgery in Minnesota. Let me think about what programs might be available.


A few options worth looking into:

**Short-Term Disability Insurance** — If she has a policy through her employer or a private plan, this would pay in addition to or instead of MN Paid Leave. Check if her employer offers it — many do as a standard benefit.

**FMLA (Federal)** — Doesn't pay anything, but protects her job for up to 12 weeks if her employer has 50+ employees and she's been there 12+ months. Worth confirming so she has federal job protection on top of the state protection.

**Minnesota Economic Assistance (MFIP / GA)** — If her income drops low enough during recovery, she may qualify for General Assistance or emergency cash assistance through her county. Apply at [mn.gov/dhs](https://mn.gov/dhs) or her local county human services office.

**SNAP (Food Assistance)** — Reduces grocery costs during reduced income. Apply at [MNbenefits.mn.gov](https://mnbenefits.mn.gov).

**Energy Assistance (LIHEAP)** — If she's struggling with utility bills during recovery, MN has a program that helps cover heating/electric costs.

**Medical Assistance / MinnesotaCare** — If she doesn't have insurance or her coverage is thin, she may qualify for state healthcare coverage while income is reduced.

**Negotiate with creditors** — If she has bills, many creditors will offer hardship deferments for medical situations. A quick call before falling behind goes a long way.

**Biggest moves right now:**
1. Confirm whether her employer offers short-term disability
2. Apply for MN Paid Leave
3. Apply for SNAP if income will be tight
4. Notify any creditors proactively
