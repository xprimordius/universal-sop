# Change Management — Plain English

**Definition:** The structured process of helping people, teams, and organizations move from how they work today to how they need to work tomorrow — so that a new system, process, or strategy actually gets adopted instead of rejected.

It's the "human side" of any transformation. The technology or process is usually the easy part. Getting people to actually use it is where 70%+ of transformations fail.

---

## The Core Problem It Solves

You can buy the best software, design the best process, or restructure the best org chart — but if employees:

- Don't understand *why* the change is happening
- Don't know *what* they're supposed to do differently
- Don't have the skills to execute the new way
- Don't believe leadership is serious
- Feel threatened or ignored

…they will quietly sabotage it, revert to old habits, or leave. Change management is the discipline of preventing that.

---

## What It Actually Looks Like in Practice

| Phase | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Diagnose** | Assess current state, stakeholders, culture, readiness, risks |
| **Vision & Case** | Define why the change matters in terms employees care about |
| **Sponsor & Align** | Get executives visibly committed and speaking the same message |
| **Communicate** | Clear, repeated, two-way messaging — not one town hall and done |
| **Train & Enable** | Build new skills, provide tools, remove blockers |
| **Reinforce** | Measure adoption, recognize wins, correct backsliding, embed in culture |

---

## Why It Matters to Amanda / Wonder Services

In procurement specifically, companies spend millions on platforms like Coupa, SAP Ariba, or Oracle — and then watch adoption stall because nobody changed the behaviors, incentives, or workflows around the tool. Change management is what turns a $5M software purchase into actual ROI instead of expensive shelfware.

That's why Amanda's entire thesis is built on it: **the tech isn't the bottleneck — the humans are.** Her "Discover. Design. Delight." framework and her focus on 90%+ adoption rates are basically change management as a productized service.

---

## Well-Known Frameworks (in case she references one)

- **Kotter's 8-Step Process** — classic, urgency → coalition → vision → communicate → empower → wins → consolidate → anchor
- **ADKAR (Prosci)** — Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement (most common in procurement/enterprise world)
- **McKinsey 7-S** — structure, strategy, systems, skills, style, staff, shared values

If you want a one-liner for the conversation: *"Change management is the bridge between 'we bought the tool' and 'people actually use it' — and it's usually where the money gets won or lost."*
