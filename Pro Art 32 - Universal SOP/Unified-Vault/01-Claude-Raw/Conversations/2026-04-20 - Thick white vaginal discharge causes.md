**Most likely: yeast infection (vaginal candidiasis)**

Thick, white, cottage cheese-like discharge is the classic sign. Usually comes with itching, burning, or irritation. Typically no strong odor.

**Other possibilities to rule out:**
- **Bacterial vaginosis** — usually thinner, grayish-white, fishy odor
- **Trichomoniasis** — often frothy, yellow-green, with odor
- **Normal discharge** — can thicken mid-cycle or before a period, but shouldn't be chunky or itchy

**What to do:**
- If it's the first time, see a clinician to confirm — self-diagnosis is wrong ~2 of 3 times per studies
- If confirmed yeast before: OTC fluconazole (Diflucan, 1 pill) or miconazole/clotrimazole cream (1, 3, or 7-day)
- See a doctor if: fever, pelvic pain, pregnant, recurrent (4+ times/year), or symptoms don't clear in a week

**Common triggers for yeast infections**

**Top causes:**
- **Antibiotics** — kill protective vaginal bacteria, letting Candida overgrow
- **High blood sugar / diabetes** — yeast feeds on glucose
- **Hormonal shifts** — pregnancy, birth control, menstrual cycle, menopause
- **Steroids** — oral or inhaled corticosteroids suppress immune response
- **Weakened immunity** — illness, stress, poor sleep, HIV, chemo

**Lifestyle triggers:**
- Tight/non-breathable underwear or wet clothing (leggings, swimsuits left on)
- Scented soaps, douches, bubble baths — disrupt pH
- Sitting in damp workout clothes
- High-sugar diet
- Sex (not technically an STI, but can trigger or pass back and forth)

**Less obvious:**
- Recent antibiotic course (even weeks prior)
- New detergent or fabric softener
- Hormonal IUD or new birth control
- Chronic stress (cortisol disrupts immune + microbiome)

**The mechanism:** Candida yeast lives in small amounts in most vaginas normally. Anything that disrupts the balance — killing off *Lactobacillus* bacteria, raising pH, or feeding the yeast — lets it overgrow.
