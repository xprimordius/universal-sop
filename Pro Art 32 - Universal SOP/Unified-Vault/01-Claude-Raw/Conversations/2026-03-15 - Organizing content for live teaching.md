# Organizing content for live teaching

> [!info]- Conversation summary
> **Conversation Overview**
> 
> The person is building a structured video course on business funding, specifically focused on a methodology called the 7 Day Fast Start (7DFS). They are developing teaching materials intended for use in live video sessions, with the goal of keeping instruction on track and ensuring all important points are covered. The conversation involved organizing raw content into a scannable, numbered, labeled teaching outline, then narrowing focus specifically to the 7DFS module.
>

The person clarified that most of these topics are either covered in prerequisite foundational lessons, handled case-by-case during bank research, or intentionally deferred to later modules. Four items were confirmed as needing integration into the 7DFS: a manual review lesson, a data point lesson, soft pull pre-qualification guidance, and a 30-day account reporting window warning.
> 
> The final task was generating bullet content for an introductory section of the 7DFS covering important disclaimers and what to watch out for. Claude produced two sections — Section A (Important Disclaimers) and Section B (What to Watch Out For) — covering topics such as sequence integrity, hard inquiry management, manual review triggers, profile monitoring, income question handling, soft pull limitations, and round timing. The person's working style reflects a preference for modular, sequenced content with clear labels, tags, and visual hierarchy, and they are building the course incrementally, starting with the 7DFS before addressing post-funding content.

## 🤖 Claude

Let me think through the 7DFS end-to-end against what a real funding sequence requires.

**Gaps I'm spotting:**

**Before the sequence starts**
- No mention of business profile verification — EIN, DUNS/Experian BIZ, Secretary of State standing, business address (not home), phone listed in 411/directory. These get checked by underwriters and can auto-deny.
- No mention of business bank account age requirement — many institutions want 90+ days of history before approving lines.

**During the sequence**
- No guidance on application timing windows — how long to wait between rounds (typically 30–90 days depending on bureau refresh cycles).
- No mention of the "ChexSystems" factor — deposit account openings pull ChexSystems; too many in a short window can flag you or get accounts denied.
- No coverage of what to do if an application triggers a manual review call — what to say, what not to say, how to handle banker questions about the business.
- Revenue/income question handling — even on "no doc" applications, some institutions ask verbally or on the app. No guidance on how to answer accurately and strategically.
- No mention of application channel strategy — online vs. in-branch vs. phone. Some institutions approve higher limits in-branch due to relationship weighting.

**Post-funding**
- No mention of utilization management after funding — critical for keeping limits and protecting the PG profile for the next round.
- No coverage of payment timing strategy — when to make payments relative to statement dates to protect reported utilization.
- No "round 2 prep" framework — what changes between funding rounds, when the profile is ready to go again.

**Missing entirely**
- Secondary bureau monitoring (Innovis, LexisNexis, etc.) — often overlooked but referenced in underwriting.
- A "what to do if denied" decision tree — denied at which point? Which bureau? Which institution type? The response should be different each time.
- Soft pull pre-qualification step — some institutions allow this and it should be used before committing a hard pull.

3. "If denied" decision tree (after Step 7)
