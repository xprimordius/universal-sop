The restriction isn't about the Achilles — it's about **anesthesia recovery and pain medications**.

**24–48 hours post-op:** Avoid greasy/spicy food due to nausea risk from general anesthesia. This is the strictest window.

**While on opioid painkillers (typically 3–7 days):** Greasy food worsens the constipation and GI slowdown opioids already cause. Spicy food can irritate an already sensitive stomach. This is likely the window your doctor meant.

**Once you're off opioids and tolerating normal meals comfortably**, you're clear to eat red meat, greasy food, etc. For most people that's **3–7 days post-surgery**, sometimes sooner.

If you're only on Tylenol/ibuprofen now and feeling no nausea, you're likely fine to eat what you want. Just ease in — don't go straight to a massive greasy meal if your gut's been quiet for days.

## 🤖 Claude

> [!note]- Claude's thinking
> He's asking if you can't eat greasy/red meat while on oxycodone specifically.


You *can*, but it's not ideal. Here's why:

**Opioids like oxy slow your entire GI tract.** Greasy and heavy foods (like red meat, fried food) are already harder to digest. Combining them with oxy can cause or worsen constipation, bloating, nausea, and general gut discomfort.

It's not a hard medical prohibition — it's a "you'll probably feel like crap" warning.

**If you want to eat it while still on oxy:**

- Keep portions moderate
- Stay hydrated
- Take a stool softener (docusate/Colace) — most surgeons recommend this anyway while on opioids
- Add fiber if tolerable

Once you're off the oxy, eat whatever you want.
