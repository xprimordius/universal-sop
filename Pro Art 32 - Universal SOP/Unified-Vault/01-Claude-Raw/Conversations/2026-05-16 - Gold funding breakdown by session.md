## 2. Credit Card Act of 2009 — what it actually is

- Real law: the **Credit CARD Act of 2009** (Credit Card Accountability Responsibility and Disclosure Act) — a consumer-protection law (rate-hike limits, 45-day notice, fee restrictions, statements 21 days before due)
- The transcripts reference it for one specific thing — *household income on card applications* (March ~768, May ~1827)
- **Their claim is broadly accurate, with nuance:** the 2009 Act required issuers to assess your *individual* ability to pay; a **2013 CFPB amendment** (to Regulation Z) lets applicants **21+** include income they have a *"reasonable expectation of access to"* — which can include a spouse's or household income
- **Legal version:** income you genuinely have access to (joint account, spouse who'd actually give you money)
- **The line:** inventing income you have no real access to is misrepresentation. The law permits *household* income, not *fictional* income.
