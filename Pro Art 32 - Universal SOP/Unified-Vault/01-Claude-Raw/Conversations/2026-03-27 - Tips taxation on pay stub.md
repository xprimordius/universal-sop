## What MN §181.032 Actually Requires on a Pay Stub

The earnings statement must include: the rate or rates of pay and basis thereof, the total hours worked, the total gross pay, a list of deductions, net pay, the pay period end date, and employer identification info.

That's it. No statutory requirement to separately itemize tips vs. hourly wages on the stub itself. The ADP formatting is not a violation.

**Where tip accuracy does matter:** The W-2. The IRS requires tips to be properly reported in specific W-2 boxes — Box 7 for Social Security tips, and included in Box 1 for total compensation. If the W-2 misreports tip income, that's the real problem — but that's a year-end issue, not a per-paycheck formatting violation.

## Major Update You Asked About: Reducing Taxable Tip Income

This is the big one. **P.L. 119-21 ("No Tax on Tips") is now law.** Signed July 4, 2025.

Effective for 2025 through 2028, employees may deduct qualified tips received in occupations that customarily and regularly received tips before 2025. Maximum annual deduction is $25,000.

The deduction effectively exempts qualified tip income from income tax. However, that tip income is still subject to payroll taxes (such as for Social Security and Medicare).

**What this means for your mom:** Her tip income (massage therapy is a qualifying occupation — the law expanded the FICA tip credit to beauty service businesses including body and spa treatments) can be deducted up to $25,000/year on her federal return. She still pays FICA/Medicare on tips, but **no federal income tax on them.**

Since Forms W-2 for 2025 will not have dedicated boxes for qualified tips, the IRS allows alternative reasonable methods — including Box 7 of Form W-2, total tips reported to employer, or amounts on Form 4137.

**This is exactly why her own daily tip log matters.** She can use her records to substantiate the full deduction amount even if the employer's reporting is sloppy.

## Red Flags Found

### 1. Employee Handbook — Payroll Section Is Silent on Tips

The handbook's Section III (Payroll Practices and Compensation) says: *"MES will communicate an employee's hourly wage or salary to him or her at the time the job offer is made"* and *"Each employee will complete and sign a wage agreement with their Clinic Administrator upon being hired."*

**What's missing:** Zero mention of tips anywhere in the compensation section. No tip policy, no tip reporting procedure, no explanation of how tips are processed through payroll. For a business where service providers regularly receive tips, this is a significant omission. The handbook covers taxes, overtime, pay periods, and payroll corrections — but is completely silent on tip handling.

**Why this matters:** MN §181.032(d) requires employers to provide a written notice at the start of employment disclosing the rate of pay and basis thereof. If tips are part of the compensation structure, the employer should have a documented tip policy. The absence of any tip policy gives the owner unchecked discretion over how tips are categorized, reported, and distributed — which is exactly what's happening here.

### 2. Employee Handbook — Payroll Correction Policy Actually Helps Her

The handbook states: *"In the unlikely event that there is an error in the amount of pay, that an improper deduction has been taken, or another improper payroll practice is occurring, the employee should promptly bring the issue to the attention of MES personnel so that corrections can be made as quickly as possible."*

This is the employer's own policy. If the owner is claiming "overpayment" justifies withholding $400 in tips, the employee handbook itself says the process is: bring it to attention → review → reimburse. It does **not** authorize the employer to unilaterally deduct or withhold future earnings.

### 3. Restrictive Covenant — Entity Name Mismatch

The agreement is between the employee and **"Sweetman - Edina LLC"** but the signature block says **"Massage Envy of Vadnais Heights."** The pay stub says **"Sweetman - Edina LLC DBA Massage Envy"** with a Maple Grove address. The handbook references **"Sweetman-Vadnais Heights LLC, D.B.A. Massage Envy® Vadnais Heights."**

This is sloppy — potentially multiple entities under one owner (Scot Ziessman). It's not directly about tips, but it creates ambiguity about which entity is the actual employer, which matters if this escalates to a wage complaint or lawsuit. The employer entity on the W-4, pay stub, and agreements should all match.

### 4. Restrictive Covenant — Blank Fields

The geographic region, county for venue, and governing state law fields are all left blank. This weakens the agreement's enforceability if the owner ever tried to use it against your mom — but that's a separate issue from pay.

### 5. W-4 Forms — Filed Correctly, Not Relevant to the Tip Issue

She filed Single/Married Filing Separately on the federal W-4 and appears to have filled out the MN W-4MN correctly with 1 allowance. The W-4s don't create any red flags for the tip issue — they just determine withholding rates.

### 6. Standards of Practice — Important Note at the Bottom

Page 8 contains: *"The Standards of Practice are not designed to interfere with, restrain, or prevent employee communications regarding wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment."*

This is a legally required NLRA disclaimer. It means the employer cannot retaliate against your mom for discussing wages, tips, or pay practices with coworkers — or for filing a complaint about them.

### 7. ESST Requirement the Employer May Be Violating

The handbook says ESST accrual *"must be indicated on the employee's earnings statement."* Look at the pay stub from the first upload — is there any ESST/PTO balance shown? If not, that's a separate MN law violation (§181.9447).
