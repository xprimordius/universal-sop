That dark object looks like a **blood clot or a piece of vein/blood vessel** — very common in chuck cuts. Chuck has a lot of connective tissue, veins, and capillaries running through it, and when the meat thaws, clotted blood can collect and look exactly like that.

**Less likely but possible:** a small piece of organ tissue or lymph node that wasn't fully trimmed.

**Very unlikely it's a worm.** Beef parasites (like tapeworm cysts) are extremely rare in USDA-inspected commercial beef, and they typically look like small white/translucent cysts embedded in the muscle — not a dark, soft mass on the surface.

**Is the rest safe to eat?** Yes — cook to 145°F minimum (160°F for ground/restructured cuts). That kills any potential pathogen or parasite. If the meat smells normal and the color looks right (no grey/green slime), you're good.

If you want peace of mind, you can always save that piece in a sealed bag in the fridge and bring it back to Aldi — they have a no-questions-asked return policy, even on opened food.

OK — with this closer shot, I can see why you're concerned. That's a **blood vessel segment** (vein or small artery). You can see it's tubular and hollow, which is the giveaway.

Chuck is one of the most vascular cuts on the cow — tons of veins, arteries, and connective tissue run through the shoulder. When you're patting down or trimming the meat, these can pull right out and look alarming, especially when they're dark from clotted blood inside.

**Why it's not a parasite:** Beef roundworms (rare in the US) are smooth, white/cream-colored, and solid — not hollow tubes. Tapeworm cysts are small translucent sacs embedded in muscle. This has the classic look of a vessel — dark, tubular, rubbery, hollow.

**Rest of the meat is safe.** Cook it to temp and you're good. This is a trimming issue, not a safety issue.

That said — save the photos and the piece. Aldi will refund or replace it no questions asked if the quality bothers you. Totally reasonable to bring it back.
