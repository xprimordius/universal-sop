## Short answer: No functional difference on Heavy Duty foil.

Both sides cook, wrap, and seal identically. Use whichever side you want — food-side, out-side, doesn't matter.

**Why the two sides look different**
Reynolds mills two layers of foil against each other during production. The side touching another sheet comes out dull; the side touching the roller comes out shiny. The performance of the foil is the same, whichever side you use.

**Effect on food (Heavy Duty specifically)**
Both sides reflect heat and heat food at virtually identical rates. The shiny and dull appearance is a byproduct of manufacturing, not functionality. No measurable difference in cook time, browning, moisture retention, or food safety.

**The one real exception (not Heavy Duty)**
Reynolds Wrap Non-Stick Foil and Grill Foil have a food-safe non-stick coating on the dull side — that side must face the food. Heavy Duty has no coating, so this doesn't apply to what you're asking about.

**Minor technical footnote (not practically relevant)**
Some sources cite reflectance of ~88% shiny vs ~80% dull. In real cooking, this ~8-point gap doesn't translate to a noticeable result — Reynolds and the Aluminum Association both confirm it doesn't affect food outcomes.

**Bottom line:** Roll it whichever way is convenient. If you ever switch to Non-Stick or Grill Foil, then orientation matters — dull side touches the food.
