Good draft, Alan. The structure is strong, the 4 regulatory questions at the end are your best weapon, and the point-by-point rebuttal is solid. Here's my assessment — what's working, what to cut/fix, and what to add.

---

**What's strongest:**

The Reg Z §1026.13(f) argument is airtight. FIB withheld merchant representment docs for 4+ months after repeated requests, then produced them only after denial and CFPB complaints. That's not a contractual dispute — that's a documented regulatory violation DBFI is authorized to evaluate. Your 4 closing questions force DBFI to either engage on the merits or explain in writing why they won't. That's excellent positioning.

The "partial refund = downgrade from 2 properties to 1" argument, backed by FIB's own 11/14 letter language, directly destroys the "services were provided" narrative. Keep hammering that.

---

**What to cut or fix:**

**Remove the AI accusation.** "I can also see that you are using A.I. to respond" — this undermines your credibility with the regulator. It reads as emotional, it's unprovable, and it gives them a reason to dismiss you as difficult rather than engage on substance. Delete it entirely.

**Tighten the opening.** The MCPA / MCA §31-3-141 citations in the intro are somewhat loosely connected. The MCPA applies to the merchant's conduct, not directly to DBFI's review. §31-3-141 relates to dispute statements on credit reports, not regulatory complaint handling. If you want legal pressure in the opening, keep it focused on what's squarely on point: DBFI's statutory duty under §32-1-211, the arbitrary closure standard under §2-4-704(2), and 12 CFR 1026.13(f). Those three are your cleanest hooks against DBFI's handling.

**Reduce repetition.** The doc withholding point appears in multiple sections. Consolidate it — state it powerfully once in Section II (FIB's claim about providing docs), then reference it in the closing questions. Right now it dilutes impact through repetition.

**Soften the "impossible timeline" point slightly.** Your argument that FIB couldn't have reviewed your 3/13 submission and responded the same day is strong on logic. But frame it as a factual impossibility, not an accusation of bad faith — let the reader draw that conclusion. Something like: "FIB's response is dated 03/13/2026, the same date I submitted extensive documentation at 8:54 PM CST. The timeline raises a serious question as to whether FIB's response incorporates any review of that submission."

---

**What to add to enforce action:**

**1. Formal response deadline.** End with a specific ask: "I respectfully request written acknowledgment of receipt within 5 business days and a substantive determination within 30 calendar days." Without a deadline, this gets shelved.

**2. Parallel enforcement notice.** Add a line making clear that CFPB escalation, Montana AG referral, and potential federal litigation are live parallel tracks — not threats, just facts. Something like: "This matter is also pending before the CFPB under complaint numbers [list them], and I am evaluating all available federal and state remedies, including private right of action under TILA. I am providing the Division a good-faith opportunity to fulfill its regulatory role before those processes advance further."

**3. Emphasize DBFI's independent duty.** Add one clean paragraph early: "The Division's role is not to accept or relay FIB's characterization of its own conduct. Under MCA §32-1-211, the Division has an independent obligation to investigate whether a regulated institution complied with applicable law. To date, the Division's closure appears to rest entirely on FIB's self-assessment, without independent evaluation of the consumer's submitted evidence or the specific regulatory compliance questions raised."

**4. The "no signed contract" point needs more emphasis.** FIB and DBFI both frame this as a "contractual dispute." Your strongest counter: there IS no signed contract. No signed terms. No signed billing authorization. No signed mutual agreement. If there's no contract, there's no "contractual dispute" for DBFI to deflect to. Make this a standalone, clearly labeled point — it's currently buried across multiple sections.

**5. Request the specific FIB review log.** Ask DBFI to obtain and produce documentation of what FIB actually reviewed during its investigation — call logs, internal case notes, the specific evidence FIB evaluated before denial. If FIB can't produce evidence that it reviewed your submission, that's another Reg Z procedural failure.

---

**On enforcing the refund specifically:**

DBFI can't order FIB to refund you directly. But here's the leverage path: if DBFI finds or acknowledges a Reg Z violation, that creates documented regulatory findings that massively strengthen your CFPB escalation and any federal court claim under TILA (which provides a private right of action + statutory damages + attorney's fees). FIB's cost-benefit calculus shifts fast once there's a regulatory finding on record.

Your best enforcement pressure is the combination of: DBFI finding → CFPB escalation with DBFI finding attached → FIB realizing litigation exposure exceeds the refund amount → settlement/refund.

- File size is typically smaller than equivalent DOCX with images

- Only if you need the recipient to edit the document (you don't — you want this locked)

**Bottom line:** PDF for anything going to a bank, regulator, court, or attorney. Always. Especially evidence packets.
