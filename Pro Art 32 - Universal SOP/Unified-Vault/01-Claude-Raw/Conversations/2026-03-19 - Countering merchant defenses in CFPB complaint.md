## MERCHANT DEFENSE DISMANTLING — POINT BY POINT

### 1. Merchant Claims "129.5 Service Hours" at $45/hr

**Fatal problem:** No signed billing authorization exists anywhere in the record. The Consulting Agreement on pages 40-42 of the PDF is unsigned — the DocuSign envelope history (page 43) shows status "Waiting for Others" with Alan never signing. The signature lines on page 42 are blank.

The merchant unilaterally declared an hourly rate and billed against it without the consumer's signed consent. Under the merchant's own Consulting Agreement Section 7 (Compensation), payment terms must be agreed in writing prior to commencement. This never happened. FIB accepted this claim without verifying a single signed document authorizing hourly billing.

**Rebuttal-proof framing:** The merchant cannot simultaneously claim (a) a binding agreement existed AND (b) the DocuSign for that agreement was never executed. Their own exhibit proves no mutual assent.

### 2. Merchant Claims "93 Professionally Underwritten Properties" Delivered

**What this actually is:** A shared Google Sheets spreadsheet of property leads. Not acquired properties. Not furnished properties. Not listed properties. Not revenue-generating properties.

**What was sold:** The merchant's own invoice (page 25) describes the purchase as "90-Day Accelerator Enterprise Program — $21,000 Enterprise Program for two (2) Properties" with the scope explicitly including "Underwriting, Property Acquisition, Full in-person set up, (ex. custom theater build and assembly project management) & Listing/SEO Optimization for two (2) properties."

The merchant's own Consulting Agreement (page 40) Section 2 titles the scope: "Underwriting, Property Acquisition, Listing Setup & Optimization: 90-Day Done-for-You Program – 2 Properties."

A spreadsheet of leads is preliminary research activity. It is not delivery of an acquired, furnished, listed, revenue-generating property. If I pay a builder $18,000 to construct a house and they hand me a list of 93 potential lots, they have not built a house.

**Rebuttal-proof framing:** The merchant's own invoice and their own unsigned Consulting Agreement both describe the deliverable as property acquisition and DFY setup — not consulting hours. The merchant's recharacterization of the service as "consulting" after failing to deliver contradicts their own sales documents.

### 3. Merchant Claims Alan "Expressed Satisfaction" and "Actively Participated"

**Context the merchant omits:** Alan's participation in weekly meetings was understood as progress updates toward the contracted DFY property delivery. Asking questions and acknowledging research during the fulfillment timeline of a 90-day program is normal client engagement — it is not affirmative consent to replace the entire contracted deliverable with hourly consulting.

The Slack screenshot (page 36) shows Alan saying "I appreciate all the work you and the team are doing. And thank you for the detailed updates & research!" This is a courtesy acknowledgment during an active engagement. The merchant frames this as acceptance of consulting-only terms. That framing is deceptive.

**The merchant's own timeline destroys this argument:** On 8/20/2025 (page 38-39), the Slack messages show Alan expressing dissatisfaction, stating he is 11 weeks deep (77 days) and still at week 2-3 of the original timeline. He states: "I am requesting a cancellation and refund, again." The merchant responded by proposing two options — including refunding the difference of service hours. Alan's dissatisfaction is documented by the merchant's own exhibits.

### 4. Merchant Claims Partial Refund Was for "Downgrade" Not Defective Service

**This actually proves the consumer's case.** The $7,500 refund was explicitly for downgrading from a 2-property Enterprise Package to a 1-property Pro Package. FIB's own 11/14/2025 letter (page 12) confirms this.

This means:
- The deliverable was PROPERTIES, not hours
- The pricing was per-property, not per-hour
- The refund acknowledged that the unit of delivery was a property

After the downgrade, the remaining $10,500 was for ONE DFY Airbnb property. Zero properties were acquired. Zero listings went live. Zero revenue was generated. The partial refund does not constitute satisfaction — it confirms the remaining deliverable was never fulfilled.

### 5. Merchant Claims Refund Policy Was "Unintentionally Omitted"

**Page 26 — the merchant's own text message admits:** "Sure, given that our standard Refund Policy was unintentionally omitted from our Partnership Agreement, I will honor a partial refund..."

This is a direct admission that no refund terms existed at the time of the transaction. Any "no refund" policy the merchant later attempted to impose was introduced after the sale, without mutual assent, and is therefore unenforceable. The merchant cannot retroactively impose terms that were absent at the point of sale.

The merchant then states "From here on, all sales are final. No further refunds will be issued." This is a unilateral declaration, not a negotiated term. Alan never signed or affirmatively consented to this new condition.
