## The Maxim

**Latin (two accepted forms):**
- **Fraus omnia vitiat** — "Fraud vitiates everything"
- **Fraus omnia corrumpit** — "Fraud corrupts all" / "Fraud vitiates everything it touches"

Both forms are used interchangeably in modern jurisprudence. *Vitiat* is more common in US contract doctrine; *corrumpit* is more common in UK, Commonwealth, and European usage.

## Seminal Modern Statement — Lord Denning MR

From **Lazarus Estates Ltd v Beasley [1956] 1 QB 702 at 712–713** (Court of Appeal, England) — the most-cited modern formulation:

> "No court in this land will allow a person to keep an advantage which he has obtained by fraud. No judgment of a court, no order of a Minister, can be allowed to stand if it has been obtained by fraud. Fraud unravels everything."

And completing the passage:

> "The court is careful not to find fraud unless it is distinctly pleaded and proved; but once it is proved it vitiates judgments, contracts and all transactions whatsoever."

Lord Parker LJ in the same case added that fraud vitiates all transactions known to the law of however high a degree of solemnity.

## Modern House of Lords Affirmation — Lord Bingham

In **HIH Casualty & General Insurance Ltd v Chase Manhattan Bank [2003] UKHL 6**:

> "Fraud is a thing apart. This is not a mere slogan. It reflects an old legal rule that fraud unravels all: fraus omnia corrumpit. It also reflects the practical basis of commercial intercourse. Once fraud is proved, it vitiates judgments, contracts and all transactions whatsoever."

| Instrument affected | Result once fraud is proved |

| Judgments | Subject to being set aside / rescinded |

## US Application

The doctrine is embedded in American contract law, especially around fraudulent inducement and merger clauses. Representative citations:

- **Italian Cowboy Partners v. Prudential Ins. Co.** (Tex. Sup. Ct.) — "Fraus omnia corrumpit: fraud vitiates everything it touches"
- **ABRY Partners V, L.P. v. F&W Acquisition LLC** (Del. Ch.) — applying the maxim to deny contractual protection to a party who fraudulently induced the agreement
- **Calamari & Perillo, *The Law of Contracts*** §9.21 — the standard US treatise treatment
- **United States v. Throckmorton**, 98 U.S. 61 (1878) — foundational US Supreme Court precedent on fraud and finality of judgments

## EU / Continental

The European Court of Justice and national high courts apply the same principle: a person can never rely on a fraudulent act to justify the application of a rule of law to his own profit; the principle operates as a corrective mechanism to the absolute and unlimited application of a rule of law through reliance on fundamental moral standards such as good faith, fairness, or justice.

## Why It Matters for Your Dispute Work

Three practical implications for your active matters (FIB, BiggerPockets, and any future Done-For-You / bait-and-switch claims):

1. **Merger / integration clauses don't protect fraudulent inducement.** When a contract contains a valid merger clause, parol evidence is still admissible for purposes of demonstrating that the agreement is void or voidable or for proving an action for deceit — fraus omnia corrumpit. Any "this is the entire agreement" boilerplate collapses the moment pre-contractual fraud is proved.

2. **Finality doctrines yield to fraud.** Judgments, settlements, arbitration awards, and administrative orders can be reopened where fraud in the procurement is proved. This is why CFPB / state AG records built contemporaneously matter — they preserve the evidentiary record.

3. **Pleading standard is high, effect is total.** Courts will not casually find fraud — it must be *"distinctly pleaded and proved"* (Denning) — but once proved, the remedy is complete unraveling, not partial adjustment. This is why your instinct to build tight evidence matrices (FTVL Protocol, Evidence Matrix) is strategically correct: you're meeting the high pleading standard so the maxim can deliver its full remedy.

**Bottom line phrasing you can cite directly in a demand letter or complaint:**

> *"It is a settled maxim of law that fraus omnia corrumpit — fraud vitiates everything it touches. As Lord Denning stated in Lazarus Estates Ltd v Beasley [1956] 1 QB 702, and as reaffirmed by the House of Lords in HIH Casualty v Chase Manhattan [2003] UKHL 6: 'Once fraud is proved, it vitiates judgments, contracts and all transactions whatsoever.'"*
